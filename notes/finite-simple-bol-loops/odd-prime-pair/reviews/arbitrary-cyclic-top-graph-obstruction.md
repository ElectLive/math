# Audit of the longer signed Jordan construction: index failure and an axial no-go theorem

**Disposition:** the proposed arbitrary-prime-power extension fails. The sign calculation and avoidance address a genuine partial transversal, but the proposed subgroup does not have the required index. The structural no-go below rules out fixing it within the same split elementary purely axial architecture, even with arbitrary multiplicities and an arbitrary signed upper elementary module. It does not prove anything about mixed weights or non-elementary cores. The [reviewed ternary order-nine construction](../manuscripts/third-degree-ternary-order-nine-top-construction.md) is unaffected. A separate read-only model audit accepted §2 at scientific source `b16e57d1` under the canonical two-axis reduction; it checked the cases $`b=0`$, $`\delta=0`$, the sign displacement and the actual top lift. This does not audit the mixed bound corollary or any higher-top existence.

## 1. The exact failed count

In the attempted longer-block extension let $`d=p^{k-1}-1`$, let $`C=\mathbb F_p[u]/(u-1)^d`$ additively, and let the rotation act by multiplication by $`u`$. The sign is $`g(u)\mapsto-g(u^{-1})`$, well defined on this local ring: inversion sends $`u-1`$ to a unit times $`u-1`$. Its square is the identity, it inverts the rotation, and the graded line of degree $`\ell`$ has sign $`(-1)^{\ell+1}`$. There are $`d/2`$ negative dimensions. The two moving axial modules have dimension $`f`$ each, with $`f=\mathrm{ord}_q(p)`$, and the top rotation has order $`p^k`$, where $`p^k\mid f`$.

The proposed $`W=H\cap N`$ is a graph over a hyperplane in the first axial module, so $`|W|=p^{f-1}`$. The proposed stabilizer has upper image $`B_2\rtimes C_{p^k}`$ and thus order $`qp^{f-1+k}`$. Its top lift really does have $`p^k`$th power in the graph: the vector is the terminal $`L^{p^k-1}x`$, and its graph image is $`J^{p^k-2}1=0`$, where $`L=R-1`$ on the first axial module and $`J=U-1`$ on $`C`$. Therefore
```math
|D|=q^2p^{2f+d+k},\qquad [D:H]=q p^{f+d+1}.
```

The number of inverted marks, computed by fixed-point lifting through the true elementary core and the swapped axes and inverted top, is instead $`q p^{f+k+d/2}`$. These numbers agree only when $`d/2+1=k`$, hence only at $`p=3,k=2`$ in the odd range $`k\ge2`$. In particular the examples $`p=5,q=101,k=2`$ and $`p=3,q=109,k=3`$ have candidate mark/index exponents $`29/30`$ and $`34/36`$ respectively. Neither is a constructed loop by this recipe.

### What the phase and strict-cut arguments actually check

There is no hidden collision accounting that could close the size defect. A pure nonidentity stabilizer-axis element cannot be inverted by an outside sign. Above identity in $`D/N`$, two signs have equal quotient and their moving negative spaces coincide; a sum with zero second coordinate therefore has zero first coordinate, leaving only the identity in the proposed graph. At nonzero top $`j`$, physical conjugation by $`B_2`$ makes the projected product pure $`h^j`$, since this power is fixed-point-free on the nontrivial $`q`$-axis. The inverting signs then have no axis shift. Their moving negative graphs make a pure first moving coordinate lie in $`(R^j-1)A_1=L^sA_1`$, for $`s=p^{v_p(j)}`$. If $`s=1`$, the candidate stabilizer vector is nonzero modulo its hyperplane. Otherwise, writing $`a=j/s`$, its upper vector has leading term $`-aJ^{s-2}1`$ modulo $`J^{s-1}C`$ by the geometric sum and graph map. On the other hand the two upper negative contributions are in the negative spaces of $`U^i\epsilon`$ and $`U^{i+j}\epsilon`$. They agree modulo $`J^sC`$ because $`s\mid j`$ and $`U^j\equiv1\bmod J^s`$. Thus a leading term of degree $`s-2<s`$ would have to be negative. But this graded line is positive (since $`s`$ is odd), a contradiction. This establishes avoidance, including the terminal case $`s=d+1`$; it does not establish existence of an element in each coset.

The proposed marks do generate $`D`$: $`h`$, the negative generator of $`C`$, and the axis anti-diagonal generate the upper quotient, and normality of the group generated by the single outside class's products then gives all moving commutators. The proposed subgroup is core-free by the two distinct irreducible axial modules, the proper first hyperplane, the zero intersection with $`C`$, and the fact that $`C_D(N)=N`$. The signed-normal subgroup calculations also give **the inequalities that would be required** of a true strict-cut folder. Inside $`C`$ a nonzero stable subspace contains the terminal positive line and misses the proposed $`W`$. An invariant subgroup of $`N`$ with moving part contains both irreducible axes by sign and has form $`M\oplus C_0`$; its intersection with $`W`$ has dimension $`f-1-d+\dim C_0<f+\dim C_0^+`$. Above $`N`$ a nontrivial signed-normal image contains both axes; coprime Sylow conjugacy then gives actual $`B`$ and $`M`$, and the proposed $`H`$ covers the remaining quotient through its surjection onto the upper $`p`$-group. But the strict-cut criterion requires a genuine sharp folder to conclude simplicity; it cannot create the missing coset representatives. The asserted arbitrary-top existence is rejected.

## 2. A no-repair theorem for the split elementary purely axial architecture

**Proposition.** Let a finite simple proper left Bol loop of odd two-prime order have its canonical signed group in the following split elementary purely axial form. Its core is $`N=A_1\oplus A_2\oplus C`$ elementary abelian over $`\mathbb F_p`$. The $`q`$-group is $`B=B_1B_2\cong C_q^2`$; each axis acts fixed-point-freely on its own moving module $`A_i`$, and trivially on the other and on $`C`$. The sign exchanges the axes and modules and fixes $`C`$ setwise; the upper cyclic quotient has order $`p^k`$, $`k\ge2`$, acting faithfully on each $`q`$-axis. The $`q`$-normalizer is $`B\rtimes S`$, $`S/C`$ this cyclic top, and the sign can be taken to stabilize $`S`$. For the canonical quotient map $`\pi:D\to D/N`$, require $`\pi(H)\cap B=B_2`$ (identifying $`B`$ with its injective image) and $`\pi(H)`$ surjective onto the cyclic top. These are the actual stabilizer properties in the audited canonical two-axis reduction. These conditions hold for the attempted envelope, and the proof allows arbitrary equal dimensions and multiplicities on the two axial parts. Then necessarily $`p=3,k=2`$.

Let $`F=\dim A_1=\dim A_2`$, $`b=\dim C^+`$, and $`c_-=\dim C^-`$. Use the actual point stabilizer $`H`$, normal-generate $`D`$ by it, and write $`W=H\cap N`$. In the canonical two-axis reduction the projection of $`H`$ covers the top and meets $`B`$ in one axis, chosen as $`B_2`$. For the orbit and taboo arguments arrange literal $`B_2\subset H`$ by coprime conjugacy and normalize its projected top lift by diagonal $`B`$ shift, as in the reviewed ternary argument. In particular a lifted generator of the top in $`H`$ acts on the elementary core by the genuine rotation; its additional core component acts trivially.

Coprime averaging under $`B_2`$ splits off the $`A_2`$ projection of $`W`$ as $`W\cap A_2`$. This subspace is invariant under the genuine rotation because $`H`$ covers the top, and under both axes (the first axis is trivial on $`A_2`$). It is therefore normal in $`D`$ and contained in the core-free $`H`$, so vanishes. Likewise $`W\cap C`$ is invariant under the rotation, fixed by $`B`$ and $`N`$, and normal in $`D`$, so vanishes. Thus $`W`$ is a graph $`\eta:K\to C`$ over a rotation-invariant subspace $`K\subseteq A_1`$; this does not use irreducibility or a one-dimensional multiplicity space. Equivariance is for the true rotation on both factors.

The physical full-mark count is $`[D:H]=q p^{F+c_-+k}`$. The exact core orbit $`[N:W]=p^{F+c_-+k}`$, since the projected stabilizer has index $`q`$. Comparing with $`\dim N=2F+b+c_-`$ gives
```math
\dim W=F+b-k,\qquad \delta:=\mathrm{codim}_{A_1}K=k-b\ge0.
```

In particular $`b\le k`$. This missing dimension constraint is independent of the chosen stabilizer graph.

Choose an inverted lift $`h`$ of a generator in $`S`$ (a sign displacement of a lift is enough), and let $`U`$ be its action on $`C`$, with $`J=U-1`$. The space $`\ker J`$ is sign-stable and cannot contain any negative vector: the span of such a vector would be an actual central signed-normal line of $`D`$, disjoint from $`H`$ by faithfulness, with true orbit equal to its sign fiber $`p`$. The strict-cut criterion forbids it. Thus $`\ker J`$ is entirely positive. In particular $`h^{p^k}\in C`$ is trivial, since it is both fixed by $`h`$ and inverted by the sign.

If $`C\ne0`$, choose a sign eigenvector $`v`$ attaining a largest $`J`$-chain of length $`\ell`$. Such a vector exists by splitting any maximal-chain generator into its sign eigenspaces. The relation $`\epsilon J\epsilon=-JU^{-1}`$ shows the associated one-dimensional grades alternate sign, and the terminal sign is positive. Thus this stable chain subspace uses $`\lceil\ell/2\rceil`$ positive dimensions, at most $`b`$. So $`J^{2b}=0`$. If $`b=0`$ then $`C=0`$, because any nonzero nilpotent module has nonzero $`\ker J`$. This proof does not assume any particular Jordan decomposition of the upper module or that it is cyclic.

Put $`s=p^{k-1}`$ and $`L=R-1`$ for the action of the actual $`h`$ on $`A_1`$. The quotient $`A_1/K`$ has dimension $`\delta`$, hence $`L^\delta A_1\subset K`$. A true $`H`$ lift of $`h`$ can be taken in $`N_H(B_2)`$ because $`H=W N_H(B_2)`$ by coprime normalizer conjugacy in $`H\cap NB`$. After removing its $`B_2`$ factor, it has form $`(x,c h)`$ with $`x\in A_1,c\in C`$, since its moving part centralizes $`B_2`$. Taking the $`s`$th power in the actual group gives $`(L^{s-1}x,\,(J^{s-1}c)h^s)`$ by the geometric-sum identity in characteristic $`p`$. If $`s\ge k+b+1`$, then $`s-1-\delta\ge2b`$, and equivariance gives
```math
\eta(L^{s-1}x)=J^{s-1-\delta}\eta(L^\delta x)=0,
\qquad J^{s-1}c=0 .
```

Cancel its first component by the genuine $`W`$ element. The original stabilizer now contains $`h^s\ne1`$, itself inverted, contradicting sharp transitivity. We conclude $`s\le k+b\le2k`$, forcing $`(p,k)=(3,2)`$ for odd $`p`$ and $`k\ge2`$.

This rules out resizing the upper Jordan block, adding signed elementary blocks, or changing the stabilizer graph while retaining these split purely axial hypotheses. It does not rule out higher cyclic tops in a mixed-weight or non-elementary-core envelope. No theorem about higher-top existence outside this architecture is inferred from the failed partial transversal.

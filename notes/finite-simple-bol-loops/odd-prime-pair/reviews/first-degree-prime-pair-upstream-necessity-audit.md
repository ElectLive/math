## Physical signed input

The Bol sandwich law gives actual generating marks $`T`$ closed under inverses and sandwiches. The Bol autotopism conjugates each left-nuclear translation back into that set of translations and gives equality of the left and middle nuclei with a normal subloop. Thus the left nucleus in a simple nonassociative loop is trivial. In C0007's word test, a word $`k_1\cdots k_m`$ whose letterwise inverse word is trivial sandwiches every mark into a mark, and hence acts as a left-nuclear translation. Triviality of the nucleus gives triviality of that word. Applying the same observation to inverses shows that sending each mark to its inverse gives a well-defined automorphism $`t`$ of the faithful canonical group $`D=\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)`$. In particular the signed doubled group projects isomorphically onto $`D`$ in this simple case: it is the graph of $`t`$. Applying the general double argument to a faithful simple case does not add a kernel.

The outside involutions in $`Dt`$ are one $`D`$-class by Sylow 2-conjugacy; $`Tt`$ is closed under conjugation by its own members, contains $`t`$ and generates the signed group, so $`T`$ is the full inverted set. For any $`t`$-stable normal $`A`$, projection of $`T`$ has equal fibers $`b_A=[A:C_A(t)]`$ by the outside-class argument in $`At`$. The original orbit size $`\Delta_A=[A:H\cap A]`$ is the number of physical left $`H`$-cosets per projected stabilizer coset, so $`b_A\mid\Delta_A`$. Equality gives a genuine loop quotient by the projected transversal operation; no unproved quotient is used to infer the equality. A proper nontrivial normal Sylow subgroup cannot occur, since its equal-fiber ratio is both a power of its prime and prime to it (compute by projected stabilizer and fixed-group orders). Also $`H`$ normally generates $`D`$: killing its normal closure makes the original marks multiply as the quotient group, a group image of the simple nonassociative loop, which is necessarily trivial.

## A replacement for the rank-one prime-descent dependency

Write $`N=O_p(D)`$, $`C=O_q(D)`$ and $`F=N C`$. The physical orbit of any $`q`$-subgroup divides $`p^a q`$, so the cut forces $`t`$ to centralize $`C`$: otherwise $`b_C=\Delta_C=q`$ and simplicity fails. The signed group is generated by conjugates of $`t`$, hence $`C`$ is central. As in the threshold proof, $`N`$ is nontrivial but is not Sylow, and $`X=D/F`$ has $`O_p(X)=1`$ and $`p\mid |X|`$. Indeed a central Hall $`q`$-subgroup in the preimage of a normal $`p`$-subgroup splits off a unique Sylow $`p`$-subgroup, so this preimage would add nothing above $`F`$. If $`N=1`$, then $`F`$ is central and Fitting self-centralization forces $`D=F`$, incompatible with simple nonassociativity.

Let $`R=O_q(X)`$; it is nontrivial by solvability and $`O_p(X)=1`$. If $`t`$ centralizes $`R`$, then $`R`$ is central in $`X`$ and Fitting self-centralization forces $`X=R`$, impossible. Its outside-class fiber divides the remaining mark count, whose $`q`$-valuation is one since $`F=N C`$ and $`t`$ fixes $`C`$. Thus $`[R:C_R(t)]=q`$. The marks projected to $`X/R`$ have $`p`$-power cardinality, generate, are twisted, and are 2-divisible by odd order. FKP Theorem 4.17 makes $`X/R`$ a $`p`$-group. Take its $`t`$-stable Sylow lift $`P`$ in $`X`$.

For each conjugate sign $`s`$ the $`q`$ inverted elements of $`R`$ form a subgroup $`[R,s]`$ of order $`q`$, central in the $`q`$-group $`R`$: the powers of any nonidentity inverted element exhaust them, and the displacement identity makes the subgroup normal. Their span is signed-normal, central elementary abelian, and on the quotient by it the generating signs act trivially on $`R`$. Thus $`M=[R,P]`$ is central elementary abelian. Averaging the coprime $`P`$-displacements in this central group gives $`R=M C_R(P)`$; then $`M=[M,P]`$, and averaging on $`M`$ gives $`M^P=0`$ and a direct product. The action of $`t`$ on $`M`$ is nontrivial, or signed generation would centralize $`M`$, contrary to $`[M,P]=M\ne1`$. The single negative dimension is already spent on $`M`$, so $`t`$ fixes $`C_R(P)`$. This second factor is signed-normal, hence central and then absent since all generating marks project trivially to it. Therefore $`R=V`$ is elementary abelian, $`V^P=0`$, and the action of $`P`$ is faithful by Fitting self-centralization. Over $`\mathbb F_q`$, Maschke applies to $`P\rtimes\langle t\rangle`$. Each nontrivial summand has a negative $`t`$-eigenvector because the conjugates of $`t`$ generate; the total negative dimension is one and $`V^P=0`$, so $`V`$ is irreducible. **No dimension bound or monomiality theorem has yet been used.**

Choose a $`t`$-stable Sylow $`q`$-group $`B`$ of the preimage of $`V`$, so $`B/C=V`$. Its normalizer in the signed group surjects onto the signed quotient modulo $`F`$ by Sylow conjugacy in that preimage. Since $`C`$ is central, a nonzero commutator of $`B`$ would yield a nonzero signed-invariant scalar alternating form on $`V`$ (take a linear functional on the exponent-$`q`$ central commutator group). By irreducibility this form would be nondegenerate. But the negative eigenspace of any symplectic involution in odd characteristic is itself nondegenerate and even-dimensional, whereas this sign has negative dimension one. Thus $`B`$ is abelian **in arbitrary dimension**. Transfer to abelian Sylow $`B`$ sends $`c\in C`$ to $`c^{[D:B]}`$, so $`C\cap D'=1`$. As $`D`$ is generated by inverted marks, $`t`$ acts by inversion on $`D/D'`$; no nontrivial fixed odd element from $`C`$ can survive there. Hence $`C=1`$.

Only now use the original stabilizer. In $`X=D/N=V\rtimes P`$, $`J=HN/N`$ covers $`P`$ because $`H`$ normally generates $`D`$ and a subgroup normally generating a finite $`p`$-group is that whole group. The $`q`$-part of $`[X:J]`$ is $`q`$ since $`N`$ is a $`p`$-group. Thus $`J\cap V`$ is a $`P`$-invariant hyperplane. Its annihilator and its sign-mate span a signed-invariant subspace of $`V^*`$ of dimension at most two, and hence the whole dual. Dimension one cannot support a nontrivial faithful odd $`P`$: the determinant of the signed module is generated by reflection determinants, so its restriction to $`P`$ is trivial. In dimension two the two dual lines are distinct; $`P`$ is diagonal of determinant one and injects into $`\mathbb F_q^\times`$. Hence $`P=C_{p^k}`$ for $`k\ge1`$, $`p^k\mid q-1`$, its two characters are faithful reciprocals, and $`t`$ inverts $`P`$. This proves the necessary split conclusion without the threshold's monomial route or its dimension-three dispatch.

## All-core minimum and the stated first-degree interface

Let $`f=\mathrm{ord}_q(p)`$ and $`d_k=\mathrm{lcm}(p^k,f)`$. The actual signed outside fibers on $`N,V,P`$ give $`a=s+k`$, where $`p^s=[N:C_N(t)]`$. Choose $`B_2`$ on the original stabilizer axis and conjugate $`H`$ by $`N`$ when needed only for orbit and avoidance calculations. Put $`W=H\cap N`$, $`U=N/\Phi(N)`$, $`M=[N,B]`$. The source's point-stabilizer passage is valid in the original group: $`M`$ is $`D`$-normal by the commutator identity and Frattini normalizer argument; $`MB`$ is $`D`$-normal; $`H`$ covers the $`p`$-group $`D/(MB)`$. A Sylow $`p`$-group of $`H\cap NB`$ surjects to the image $`N/M`$ and lies in $`N`$, giving the claimed surjection from $`W`$.

If an axial weight occurs on $`U`$, its full $`P`$-Frobenius packet and the sign-exchanged packet each have size $`d_k`$, so $`s\ge d_k`$ and $`a\ge d_k+k`$. Without axial weights, $`C_U(B_2)=C_U(B)`$. The stabilizer surjection and coprime averaging put this fixed part in $`\bar W`$, the image of $`W`$ in $`U`$, and leave no fixed part in $`U/\bar W`$. That is an actual $`J`$-module; every nontrivial rational $`B_2`$-packet has size $`d_k`$ under the full top covered by $`J`$. Hence $`\dim(U/\bar W)`$ is a nonzero multiple of $`d_k`$. If $`a=d_k`$, the entire Frattini lies in the point stabilizer and is trivial by faithfulness. Then $`N`$ is elementary and $`C_N(B)\subseteq W`$. The normalizer of $`B_2`$ equals the normalizer of $`B`$, by the coprime fixed-space identity and the original axis action. The stabilizer part in this normalizer covers the top and contains $`C_N(B)`$, hence contains a Sylow $`p`$-group there. Conjugate to a signed one: inversion on its nontrivial top forces a nonidentity inverted element. This contradicts mark avoidance in every conjugate of $`H`$. Thus $`a\ge1+d_k\ge1+\mathrm{lcm}(p,f)`$ for both branches, without abelian-core assumptions.

I also checked the structural steps before the separately imported axial uniqueness at $`p\nmid f`$, $`a=1+pf`$. Then $`k=1`$. In the axial case the two irreducible reciprocal axial modules consume all negative mass; the remaining weights are fixed under the signed group and $`t`$ fixes the Frattini, making it central. A nonzero scalar commutator on either axial module would be nondegenerate on both (pairing across axes vanishes and $`t`$ exchanges them). The true isotropic stabilizer projects onto either all or none of the moving $`B_2`$-module. In either case the isotropic dimension contradicts such nondegeneracy. The two axial modules therefore lift to the central commutator-of-$`B`$ factor in $`N`$; its complement $`C_N(B)`$ is fixed by $`t`$ and eliminated in the generated inverted quotient $`D/(MB)`$. It remains to identify the unique marked axial folder by the separate axial argument, not by equating dimensions.

In the nonaxial case the actual stabilizer quotient has dimension $`d`$ and is irreducible, leaving Frattini orbit order $`p`$. A nontrivial $`t`$ action on this Frattini would yield equality in the physical cut and a forbidden proper normal subloop. So the Frattini is central and, by faithfulness, has order $`p`$. The abelian escape fails: if $`N`$ were abelian, its coprime fixed direct summand for $`B_2`$ equals that for $`B`$ already modulo $`pN`$, hence also in $`N`$ itself. Coprime fixed-point lifting from the surjection of the physical $`W`$ onto $`N/M`$ then gives $`C_N(B)\subseteq W`$, leading to the same Sylow avoidance contradiction. Thus $`N'=\Phi(N)=C_p`$; this verifies the interface used in the extraspecial continuations. This is a theoretical proof audit with no claim of independent finite formula verification or unrestricted classification.

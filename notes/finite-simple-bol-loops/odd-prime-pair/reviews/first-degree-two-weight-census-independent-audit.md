## Scope and checks

- **Exhaustive signed normalization at the stated interface.** Normalizing the actual $`q`$-complement, then using the odd abelian Sylow $`p`$-lift with $`t`$-inverted complement, removes the nonsplit ambient extension. Central shears kill by averaging over the two-axis normal subgroup, because it fixes no vectors in the dual. In normalizing the actual point stabilizer, a diagonal $`t`$-fixed translation handles its upper projection and $`N=W C_N(t)`$ ensures the Sylow-$`q`$-lift conjugation is achievable by a $`t`$-fixed factor, not merely by isotope. The horizontal stabilizer has no central graph over $`W`$ because its $`q`$-axis fixes no linear functional there. Its upper order-$`p`$ tilt must be nonzero by actual sharp transitivity. Normalizing that tilt fixes the generator of the center and its corresponding scalar on the form.
- **Physical folder, not merely a linear chart.** Expanded multiplication of two outside involutions in the Heisenberg group. A nontrivial element of the single marked $`q`$-axis cannot be inverted by an outside involution, since every outside sign exchanges the axes. Above the identity or a nontrivial rotation, the required normalization is actual conjugation by the axis inside the stabilizer. At a rotation both signs then have zero translation parts, and their negative vectors with sum in $`L`$ have opposite $`tL`$ coordinates. The cross terms vanish since any symplectic involution satisfies $`\omega(sy,y)=0`$ in odd characteristic. The resulting central phase is zero, incompatible with the actual nonzero top tilt in $`H`$. The proof uses $`L\oplus tL`$, not an extra assumption that $`L`$ is stable under the other axis. For every proper signed normal cut inside $`N`$, projection from $`Y\cap L`$ into $`Y^+`$ is injective, giving a strict deficit in the actual marked fiber. Beyond $`N`$ a nontrivial signed normal image contains both axes, and commutators force all of $`N`$; the sole proper cut $`NV`$ has the stated strict deficit. This checks the use of the cited strict normal-cut interface without re-proving that interface.
- **Isotypes, not a cohort of examples.** In a fixed $`V_2`$-weight orbit, its restrictions have total multiplicity two. Free top cycles and Frobenius give exactly two graphs, including repeated graphs. Transposition sends the graph label $`\lambda`$ to $`\xi(\lambda)^{-1}`$; common axis scaling shifts just its $`\alpha`$-exponent. For a repeated graph the negative fixed type would force a one-dimensional weight to pair with itself in the pulled-back alternating form, which is impossible. For distinct fixed graphs a negative graph would require a scalar of norm both $`1`$ and $`-1`$; for the positive graphs the opposite reversal signs are necessary and sufficient (the ratio is a signed site shift in characteristic $`p`$). Norm-one scalar changes remove the residual graph intertwiner. This gives one repeated plus $`p-1`$ fixed-distinct extraspecial classes.
- **Exchanged case and no extra quotient.** Order $`h_i=f/\gcd(f,i)`$ gives the stated norm of $`T^2`$, with transversality exactly for odd $`h_i`$ or $`4\mid h_i`$. Interchanging the names of the two graphs reduces $`i`$ to $`1\le i<f/2`$; none of the remaining allowed common axis scalings interchanges the normalized distinct Frobenius exponents. The signed stabilizer-preserving isomorphism fixes the oriented center; on the weight blocks the gauge consists of the common Frobenius and field-scalar adjustments stated in the source. Evaluating these on primitive-element exponents gives the affine phase map $`j\mapsto pj+(p-1)/2`$ after the norm-one translations have been divided out. Fixed points of its $`k`$th power solve the indicated one-variable linear congruence, proving the stated Burnside summand. The right nucleus and the G-loop assertion follow separately from the normalizer of the original stabilizer and the transitive sign centralizer; they do not identify distinct based classes.
- **Computational cross-check.** Independent integer code compared the phase filters against [the separate arithmetic formulation](../manuscripts/exact-first-exponent-orbit-count.md) and counted direct affine permutation cycles versus Burnside fixed-point sums. It covered 89 parameter families $`p\in\{3,5,7,11,13\}`$, even $`2\le f\le42`$, $`p\nmid f`$. Of 866 index rows, 861 with modulus at most 250,000 were explicitly cycled, totaling 555,694 phases. The sample totals at $`(p,f)=(3,10),(7,6),(3,16),(5,12)`$ were respectively 8, 12, 11, 20. These are arithmetic checks, not exhaustive native multiplication-table runs; parameter-only rows need not have an admissible $`q`$.

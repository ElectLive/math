# At the second odd prime-pair degree, axial weights are impossible

**Research proof; see the scope of verification.** [Verification and provenance](../VERIFICATION.md).

**Theorem.** Let $`p,q`$ be distinct odd primes, $`p\mid q-1`$, $`f=\mathrm{ord}_q(p)`$, $`p\nmid f`$ and $`d=pf`$. If a finite simple proper left Bol loop has order $`p^{d+2}q`$, then the Frattini quotient of its canonical $`p`$-core has **no axial weight** for the two-axis $`q`$-group in the canonical signed reduction. Here “axial” means a nonzero character whose restriction to one of the two axes is trivial. This is an exclusion of the entire axial branch for these parameters, not a proof that a nonaxial loop exists.

## 1. Axial weights would force an elementary core

Use $`D`$, the canonical inverting automorphism $`t`$, the original stabilizer $`H`$, the upper quotient $`D/N=V\rtimes P`$, $`V=C_q^2`$, $`P=C_p`$ and the sign-stable Sylow $`q`$-group $`B=B_1B_2`$ from the [signed reduction](../reviews/first-degree-prime-pair-upstream-necessity-audit.md). Its axes are exchanged by $`t`$, and $`P`$ acts on them by reciprocal scalars of order $`p`$. Let $`N=O_p(D)`$, $`F=\Phi(N)`$, $`U=N/F`$, $`W=H\cap N`$, and $`\bar W=WF/F`$. By [Frattini sign rigidity](second-prime-pair-frattini-sign-rigidity.md), $`F`$ is central in the signed group and $`U`$ has negative dimension $`d+1`$. In particular $`H\cap F=1`$ by faithfulness, and the actual $`\bar W`$ is totally isotropic for each scalar component of the alternating commutator form on $`U`$ (the form has elementary central target because $`N^p\le F`$).

Suppose there is an axial weight. Its rational top/Frobenius orbit and the sign mate on the other axis furnish two $`d`$-dimensional rational modules $`A_1,A_2`$ and contribute $`d`$ negative dimensions. Each is irreducible for its axis and the full $`p`$-top: the top and Frobenius act transitively on the distinct weight packet. No second pair of axial packets or extra multiplicity fits. Nor does a mixed packet fit. In a mixed orbit the coordinates are nonzero and distinct top/Frobenius exponents give $`pf=d`$ distinct weight pairs since the coordinate ratio determines the top exponent. Only at most $`f`$ are fixed under a swap (at most one top exponent, times $`f`$ Frobenius exponents). Thus any signed collection of mixed packets contributes at least $`(d-f)/2\ge f\ge3`$ negative dimensions. Here $`f\ge3`$ because $`q>p`$ and odd. Therefore
```math
U=A_1\oplus A_2\oplus U^B,
```
with all active weights on the two axial modules and $`t`$ exchanging them.

Invariance under $`B`$ makes different axes pair trivially under the commutator form, and makes the moving part pair trivially with the fixed part. If some scalar component is nonzero on either axial module, it is nondegenerate there by irreducibility and on the other by sign invariance. Normalize $`H`$ by an $`N`$-conjugation to contain $`B_2`$, only for orbit and avoidance arguments. Projection onto $`A_2`$ by the coprime $`B_2`$ averaging operator and its complement decomposes $`\bar W`$, and its moving projection is either $`A_2`$ or zero by irreducibility for the actual stabilizer image $`B_2\rtimes P`$. If it is $`A_2`$, isotropy already fails. If it is zero, let $`e=\log_p|F|`$ and $`z=\dim U^B`$. The original orbit $`[N:W]=p^{d+2}`$ gives $`\dim\bar W=d+z+e-2`$. Its intersection with $`A_1`$ then has dimension at least $`d+e-2>d/2`$, again impossible for an isotropic subspace of a nondegenerate alternating space. Thus the entire moving part lifts to the center of $`N`$.

Put $`M=[N,B]`$ and $`C=C_N(B)`$. The moving commutator factor $`M`$ is central in $`N`$ by the preceding result. Coprime action gives $`N=M C`$, $`M=[M,B]`$, and $`M\cap C=1`$, since averaging on the central abelian $`M`$ makes its fixed subgroup trivial. Since $`F`$ is $`B`$-fixed, also $`M\cap F=1`$, so $`M`$ is elementary abelian and identifies with $`A_1\oplus A_2`$. We have $`N=M\times C`$ and $`F=\Phi(C)`$. The actual $`W\to N/M=C`$ is onto by the physical stabilizer calculation: $`H`$ normally generates $`D`$, hence covers the $`p`$-group $`D/(MB)`$, and a Sylow $`p`$-subgroup of $`H\cap NB`$ still covers the kernel $`N/M`$. Therefore $`\Phi(W)`$ maps onto $`\Phi(C)=F`$, contrary to $`\Phi(W)\subset W\cap F=1`$ unless $`F=1`$. In particular $`N`$ and $`C`$ are elementary abelian, and the negative rank of $`t`$ on $`C`$ is exactly one.

## 2. The signed rotation lifts would form a single Heisenberg group

Take $`L=N_D(B)`$ and a $`t`$-stable Sylow $`p`$-group $`S`$ of $`L`$. We have $`D=M\rtimes L`$, $`L=B\rtimes S`$, $`S/C=P`$, and $`S\cong D/(MB)`$ because $`M\cap C=1`$. So $`S`$ is generated by images of inverted marks. Choose a $`t`$-inverted lift $`h`$ of a generator of $`P`$ (take a displacement $`x^{-1}t(x)`$ from a top generator), and let $`\alpha`$ be conjugation by $`h`$ on $`C`$. Its order divides $`p`$ since $`h^p\in C`$ and $`C`$ is abelian. Put $`C_0=\mathbb F_p[\alpha] C^-`$. It is signed-normal in $`S`$, as $`t`$ inverts $`h`$. In $`S/C_0`$ the sign fixes $`C/C_0`$ pointwise, so conjugation by $`h`$ acts on $`C/C_0`$ as its own inverse and must be trivial, having odd order. This quotient is then abelian, generated by inverted images of marks, and cannot have a nontrivial sign-fixed subgroup. Hence $`C=C_0`$ is one cyclic Jordan block for $`\alpha`$, generated by a negative vector. The successive one-dimensional layers for powers of $`\alpha-1`$ alternate signs, with the generator's layer negative, because $`t(\alpha-1)t=\alpha^{-1}-1\equiv-(\alpha-1)\pmod{(\alpha-1)^2}`$. As $`\dim C^-=1`$, $`\dim C`$ is 1 or 2.

Dimension one makes $`C`$ central in all of $`D`$ and inverted by $`t`$. It avoids the faithful point stabilizer, so its true orbit size and sign fiber are both $`p`$, yielding a forbidden proper normal subloop. Thus $`\dim C=2`$, $`\alpha`$ is a nontrivial size-two Jordan block, and its fixed line $`Z`$ is sign-positive. Because $`h^p\in C`$ commutes with $`h`$, it lies in $`Z`$; it is also inverted, so $`h^p=1`$. In particular $`S`$ is the extraspecial exponent-$`p`$ group of order $`p^3`$ with center $`Z`$, and the sign induces inversion on $`S/Z`$ (the images of $`h`$ and the negative generator of $`C`$). For any nonzero vector in this quotient a displacement has that vector as its image, so it has an inverted lift. The conjugacy class of any noncentral element exhausts its central coset, since its commutators in $`S`$ give all of $`Z`$. Thus every element of $`S`$ with nonzero projection in $`P`$ is conjugate to a nonidentity inverted mark. The line $`Z`$ is central in $`D`$: it is central in $`S`$, and $`C`$ commutes with $`B`$ and with $`M`$.

## 3. The original stabilizer cannot dodge this Heisenberg group

Now $`\dim N=2d+2`$ and $`\dim W=d`$. The original $`W`$ surjects onto $`C`$, so it cannot contain the $`d`$-space $`A_2`$. Its moving projection is irreducible, as above, so $`W\le A_1\oplus C`$. Its intersection with $`C`$ is invariant under the top action: $`H`$ covers $`P`$, and conjugation on $`C`$ factors through that top. Every nonzero invariant subspace of the single nontrivial size-two Jordan block $`C`$ contains its fixed line $`Z`$. This line is central in $`D`$ and misses $`H`$. Therefore $`W\cap C=1`$, and projection of the $`d`$-dimensional $`W`$ onto the $`d`$-dimensional $`A_1`$ is a bijection.

Use a sign-fixed diagonal element of $`B`$ to normalize the image of $`H`$ in $`D/N`$ to $`B_2\rtimes P`$ with respect to the chosen $`h`$; it moves the first-axis shift by an arbitrary scalar, because the top acts nontrivially on that axis. This preserves $`B_2\subset H`$ and the sign. Sylow conjugacy in $`H\cap NB`$ gives $`H=W N_H(B_2)`$, so there is a lift of a nontrivial top element in $`N_H(B_2)`$. In the actual split $`D=M\rtimes (B\rtimes S)`$ the $`M`$ component of an element normalizing $`B_2`$ belongs to $`C_M(B_2)=A_1`$. Removing a $`B_2`$-factor leaves the lift in $`A_1\rtimes S`$, with still nontrivial top. Multiplication on the left by the unique element of $`W`$ with opposite $`A_1`$ coordinate cancels that coordinate. The added $`C`$ component lies in $`S`$ and commutes with $`A_1`$, so the resulting element truly lies in $`S\cap H`$ with nontrivial top. This is conjugate to a nonidentity inverted mark by Section 2, contradicting sharp transitivity on the original point set. The axial hypothesis is impossible.

The argument handles all $`p\nmid f`$, not only odd $`f`$. The remaining nonaxial branch has central Frattini of order $`p^2`$ by the preceding source, and no assertion about its eventual construction or classification is made here.

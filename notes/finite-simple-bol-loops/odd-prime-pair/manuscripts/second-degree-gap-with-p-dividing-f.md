# The next-degree gap also holds when p divides the character degree

**Research proof; see the scope of verification.** [Verification and provenance](../VERIFICATION.md).

**Theorem.** Let $`p,q`$ be distinct odd primes with $`p\mid q-1`$, and let $`f=\mathrm{ord}_q(p)`$ be divisible by $`p`$. No finite simple proper left Bol loop has order $`p^{f+2}q`$. Combined with the independently audited $`p\nmid f`$ gap, for **every** admissible odd prime pair the minimum exponent is $`1+\mathrm{lcm}(p,f)`$ and its immediate successor $`2+\mathrm{lcm}(p,f)`$ is impossible. This does not decide any subsequent exponent.

## 1. The unequal roles of axial size and mixed size

Use the physical signed notation $`D,t,H,N=O_p(D),F=\Phi(N),U=N/F,W=H\cap N`$. The original quotient $`D/N=V\rtimes P`$ has reciprocal axes $`V=C_q^2`$ and top $`P=C_{p^k}`$. Its mark equation is $`a=s+k`$, where $`[N:C_N(t)]=p^s`$, and the Frattini quotient has negative dimension at most $`s`$. We use here the signed group as an actual permutation folder, not a free choice of stabilizer in the quotient. The sign-stable lift $`B=B_1B_2`$ of $`V`$ cannot act trivially on $`U`$: otherwise it acts trivially on $`N`$ by the coprime automorphism kernel on a Frattini quotient, contrary to Fitting self-centralization and $`F(D)=N`$. The axis $`B_2`$ is the one in the projected actual stabilizer.

An axial packet, together with its sign-mate, costs $`d_k=\mathrm{lcm}(p^k,f)\ge f`$ negatives. A mixed packet of nonzero coordinate characters $`(a,b)`$ has exactly $`p^k f`$ elements:
```math
(\alpha^i p^j a,\alpha^{-i}p^j b),\qquad
0\le i<p^k,\quad 0\le j<f,
```
with $`\alpha`$ of order $`p^k`$ modulo $`q`$. The ratio determines $`i`$ (the order is odd), and either coordinate then determines $`j`$. Within a packet at most $`f`$ individual weights are fixed by the coordinate swap. Thus every nonzero signed-invariant sum of mixed packets costs at least $`f(p^k-1)/2\ge f`$ negatives. These observations prove $`s\ge f`$, so $`a=f+2`$ forces $`k=1`$ or $`2`$. We also retain the stronger audited bound $`a\ge1+d_k`$.

## 2. A top of order p

For $`k=1`$, the **axial packet size is $`d_1=f`$**, while **mixed packet size is $`pf`$** and each nonzero signed mixed part costs at least $`f(p-1)/2\ge f\ge3`$. These are precisely the needed substitutions in the earlier second-degree reductions.

First transfer the [Frattini sign theorem](second-prime-pair-frattini-sign-rigidity.md) with its budget $`d+1`$ replaced by $`f+1=s`$. Its stabilizer quotient in the nonaxial case has positive dimension divisible by $`f`$: a weight of $`B_2`$ now has joint top/Frobenius orbit of size $`\mathrm{lcm}(p,f)=f`$, not $`pf`$. Thus $`\dim(U/\bar W)=f`$ because $`a=f+2<2f`$; the true Frattini orbit is $`p^2`$. In the axial case the paired packets already spend $`f`$ negatives, so with a hypothetical nontrivial sign on $`F`$ exactly one remains there. In either case the hypothetical sign fiber on $`F`$ is $`p`$: nonaxially use simplicity and the orbit $`p^2`$, axially use the budget. The proof eliminating this hypothesis continues without any coprimality requirement on $`f`$ and $`p`$:
- No sign can exchange two nontrivial $`B`$-Frobenius cycles on $`F/\Phi(F)`$, since that would cost at least $`f`$ negatives. The same exponent-two argument in the signed $`q`$-normalizer forces $`B`$ to centralize $`F`$; the original $`H`$ surjects onto the resulting $`p`$-group action on $`F`$, giving $`H\cap F=1`$.
- Axially there are exactly two $`f`$-dimensional axis packets (no other packet fits). If $`\bar W`$ contains the moving $`B_2`$-module, the physical $`[W,B_2]`$ exhausts $`[N,B_2]`$ by fixed lifting and coprime action, putting it inside $`W`$ and outside $`F`$. Its sign-mate does the same on the other axis, both are elementary, and the physical $`W\twoheadrightarrow C_N(B)`$ forces $`F=1`$, a contradiction. If the projection is zero, the codimension is at least $`f`$, so the Frattini orbit size is exactly $`p^2`$ and $`\bar W=U^{B_2}`$. The nonaxial case already has the same orbit and $`U^{B_2}=U^B\subseteq\bar W`$.
- At that point both branches give $`F\cong C_p^2`$, $`C=C_N(B)=(W\cap C)F`$, and $`H\cap N_D(B)`$ still covers the top (in the axial case remove the actual $`W`$-part from a lift in $`N_H(B_2)`$ using $`\bar W=U^{B_2}`$). The signed Sylow quotient and the index-$`p^2`$ physical mark bound force $`C=F`$, then the signed group of order $`p^3`$ is extraspecial and all nonzero-top elements are conjugate to inverted marks. This is unchanged, since it uses only a top of order $`p`$, not the prime divisibility of $`f`$. It contradicts the original stabilizer.

So $`F`$ is central in the signed group also here. For axial weights the [axial exclusion](second-prime-pair-axial-exclusion.md) now applies with $`d`$ replaced by $`f`$: the two $`f`$-modules are irreducible under their full axis and top (the same joint transitivity argument), no mixed packet fits the one spare negative, and $`f+e-2>f/2`$ for $`e\ge0`$ in the isotropy test except at $`f=3`$, $`e=0`$. In that exception the stabilizer argument is only used to exclude a **nonzero** commutator, which implies $`e\ge1`$, so the strict inequality still holds. The central elementary core follows. The fixed-space signed top, its single size-two Jordan block, and the final physical $`W\to A_1`$ bijection use no weight arithmetic after that step, so the contradiction to the inverted top goes through. (The stated axis dimensions have simply become $`f`$ instead of $`pf`$.)

In the nonaxial case, $`F`$ is central of order $`p^2`$ and the actual irreducible quotient $`U/\bar W`$ has dimension $`f`$. The [mixed radical argument, Section 1](second-odd-prime-pair-degree-gap.md#1-the-actual-stabilizer-forces-a-nonsingular-alternating-pencil-on-the-moving-part) goes through with **stabilizer codimension $`f`$** and **mixed packet size $`pf`$**: $`W`$ misses $`F`$, covers the fixed part, $`N^p=F^p`$, $`F=[N,N]`$ by the finite abelian Frattini property, and each nonzero scalar form has radical in $`\bar W`$. Its actual orbit is $`p^2`$, permitting at most one negative direction in that radical. A nonzero signed mixed part would spend at least $`f(p-1)/2\ge3`$, so the form is nondegenerate on the moving part $`A=[U,B]`$. But isotropy of its subspace $`\bar W\cap A`$ of codimension $`f`$ forces $`\dim A\le2f`$, whereas any nonzero mixed packet costs $`pf\ge3f`$ dimensions. This rules out the order-$`p`$ top in the entire stated range, including $`p=3`$.

## 3. A top of order p squared

Suppose $`k=2`$. The audited bound $`f+2\ge 1+\mathrm{lcm}(p^2,f)`$ implies $`p^2\mid f`$: if $`v_p(f)=1`$, the right side is $`1+pf>f+2`$. Now $`s=f`$, and a mixed part costs at least $`f(p^2-1)/2>f`$. Thus the active support is a single axial pair of $`f`$-dimensional modules, sign-exchanged, which exhaust the negative budget. These modules are irreducible for full axis plus top by transitivity on their joint character packets (here the rotation is an order-$`p^2`$ Frobenius multiplier). Hence $`t`$ fixes both $`F`$ and the $`B`$-fixed part of $`U`$, and $`F`$ is central in the signed group. The two-module isotropy proof above gives $`M=[N,B]`$ central elementary, $`N=M\times C_N(B)`$ and $`F=1`$: the same dimension comparison $`f+e-2>f/2`$ holds for $`f\ge p^2`$, and the same physical $`W\twoheadrightarrow C_N(B)`$ yields the Frattini contradiction before imposing the top order.

Put $`C=C_N(B)`$, $`L=N_D(B)`$ and choose a signed Sylow $`p`$-group $`S\le L`$. The group $`S\cong D/(MB)`$ is generated by inverted images; $`t`$ fixes $`C`$ and inverts $`S/C=C_{p^2}`$. The action of a top generator on abelian $`C`$ therefore equals its own inverse (conjugating by elements of $`C`$ acts trivially); its order is odd and it is trivial. Then $`S`$ is abelian and generated by inverted elements, so $`C=1`$. Consequently $`S=P=C_{p^2}`$ and $`N=A_1\oplus A_2`$ elementary. The real $`W`$ has dimension $`f-2`$, lies inside $`A_1`$ by the projection onto irreducible $`A_2`$, and is invariant under the action of the top. Arrange $`B_2\subseteq H`$ by coprime Sylow conjugacy and normalize the projection of $`H`$ to $`B_2\rtimes P`$ by diagonal shift in $`B`$, as in the axial exclusion; these preserve the required actual orbit and taboo tests.

Choose a generator $`h\in S`$, which $`t`$ inverts. By $`H=W N_H(B_2)`$, the actual $`H`$ contains a lift $`(x,h)`$ after discarding a $`B_2`$-factor; its $`A_2`$ coordinate is zero since it normalizes $`B_2`$. Write $`R`$ for the action of $`h`$ on $`A_1`$. In characteristic $`p`$ its $`p`$th power is $`((1+R+\cdots+R^{p-1})x,h^p)=((R-1)^{p-1}x,h^p)`$. On the two-dimensional quotient $`A_1/W`$ the action is unipotent, so $`(R-1)^2A_1\subseteq W`$; $`p-1\ge2`$ makes the vector coordinate lie in $`W`$. Cancel it by a genuine element of $`W`$. The stabilizer then contains $`h^p\ne1`$, itself an inverted mark, impossible. This rules out the last top order and proves the gap.

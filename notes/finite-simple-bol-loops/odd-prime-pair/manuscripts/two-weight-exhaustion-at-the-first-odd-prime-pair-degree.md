# The complete two-character census at the first odd prime-pair degree

**Research proof.** [Scoped verification](../VERIFICATION.md). We classify the extraspecial residual itself, including nonsplit envelopes and non-invariant graph stabilizers at input, and obtain an explicit count using the *actual* signed orbit law. Via the named earlier reductions this finishes the remaining first-degree branch at p^a q. It does not classify the higher exponents or unrestricted simple Bol loops. “Simple” below means simple nonassociative left Bol, hence proper at odd order.

Let p,q be distinct odd primes with p|(q−1), f=ord_q(p) **even and prime to p**, d=pf, a=d+1. For 1≤i<f/2 put h_i=f/gcd(f,i) and retain i if h_i is odd or is divisible by four. Write
```math
  m_i=\gcd(p^{f/2}+1,p^i-1),\qquad
  D_i=\#\big((\mathbb Z/m_i)/[j\mapsto p j+(p-1)/2]\big),
```
where the notation counts orbits of that permutation. Equivalently average over k=0,…,f−1: the k-summand is gcd(m_i,p^k−1) if this divides (p^k−1)/2, and zero otherwise (k=0 gives m_i). Our total number of simple loop isomorphism classes at p^(d+1)q is
```math
                         p+1+\sum_{i\ {\rm retained}}D_i.        \tag{1}
```
All are G-loops; in particular this is also the number of isotopy classes represented there. Of the total, exactly one is the preceding abelian-core matched-pair form. Every other class has extraspecial p-core, full native right nucleus C_p, and its nuclear right translations are exactly the center of its faithful LMlt. At 3³¹·61 the count is **8**, and at 3⁴⁹·193 it is **11**. The enumerated isotypes and their distinctness also give these numbers as constructive lower bounds using the explicitly named existence inputs, separately from exhaustion or minimum. Arithmetic examples in the checker that specify only p,f do not assert that a q with such data exists.

**Dependency boundary.** [The all-core lower bound at 268180f2](all-core-prime-pair-minimum.md), [the conditional extraspecial residual](extraspecial-first-degree-rigidity.md), the [abelian-core endpoint](abelian-core-minimum-and-nonabelian-defect.md) and [split prime-pair structure](two-prime-divisibility-existence.md) are substantive inputs, not byproducts of the new character calculation. Specifically they say that at this degree either the canonical split abelian case holds or the faithful native D has N=O_p(D) extraspecial of exponent p, |N|=p^(2d+1), Z=N′=C_p fixed by the whole signed group; X=D/N=V⋊⟨h⟩ with V=F_q², |h|=p, reciprocal faithful characters and sign τ interchanging axes; the projected actual stabilizer is over V₂ with top p and H∩Z=1. On U=N/Z the signed symplectic action is faithful on V, the V₂-fixed space is zero, and L=(H∩N)Z/Z is a J=HN/N-invariant Lagrangian, U/L is an irreducible d-dimensional moving J-module, U=L⊕τL. ## 1. Normalizing genuine splittings and genuine stabilizers

Choose a τ-stable Sylow q-subgroup B of the preimage of V (there are oddly many). Its normalizer in the signed group surjects onto X⋊⟨τ⟩ by the Frattini argument. Its intersection with N is C_N(B)=Z (use U^V=0, and N normal with N∩B=1). In its even subgroup select a τ-stable Sylow p-subgroup S; this has order p², contains Z and projects isomorphically modulo Z onto a p-complement to V in X. Thus S is abelian; splitting S into τ-eigenspaces gives a negative cyclic order-p factor lifting that complement, since the action on its image modulo V is inversion. Hence D=N⋊X₀ with a genuine signed complement B⋊⟨h⟩. Choose h so the first axis is acted on by a fixed scalar α of order p. The sign inverts h and literally interchanges the axes in compatible coordinates. Identify Z with additive F_p. The usual extraspecial model has (x,s)(y,t)=(x+y,s+t+ω(x,y)/2): in N the class-two addition uv[u,v]^(−1/2) is elementary abelian and a linear section over Z gives this formula. The initial complement action on N can differ from the linear action on U by a central linear shear, a 1-cocycle with values in U*. This cocycle is principal: average first over normal V, making its restriction zero by a coboundary; then its value on every element of X⋊⟨τ⟩ is V-fixed by normality, and (U*)^V=0. A change of Heisenberg coordinates therefore makes the **whole signed complement** act linearly, not only V or τ separately.

Conjugate by a τ-fixed element of V to put J=V₂⋊⟨h⟩: any lift of its nontrivial top differs from h by an element of V, V₂ is present, and conjugation by the diagonal fixed line gives all first-axis translations via 1−α. Write W=H∩N. To put the chosen V₂ itself inside H, conjugate a Sylow q-lift above V₂ by N. This is an admissible *signed isomorphism*, not just an isotope or an arbitrary change of origin: L∩U⁺=0 and dim L=dim U⁺=d by L⊕τL (an element of L∩U⁺ or L∩U⁻ lies in τL), so N=W C_N(τ). Thus the same conjugation on H is achieved by its τ-fixed factor. Now W is the horizontal L: its possible linear graph to Z is V₂-invariant and L has no trivial V₂-constituent. By the Frattini argument in the normal H∩NV₂ we have H=W N_H(V₂). Since N_D(V₂)=Z X₀ (V₂ has no fixed vectors on U and is normalized by X₀), the remainder is the graph of a map J→Z trivial on V₂, hence
```math
               H=(L\times\{0\})\rtimes\langle V_2,z^\kappa h\rangle.
```
Here κ≠0 because h is itself an inverted mark. Choose the generator z so κ=1; this also fixes the scalar orientation for the symplectic form. The upper construction [e562c132](frobenius-tilted-lagrangians-in-simple-odd-bol-loops.md) proves that **any** such faithful symplectic representation with no V₂-fixed vector, τL complementary, and L J-stable gives a simple proper loop: its mark count, every physical two-sign phase, every proper signed-normal cut, core-freeness and generation use just these hypotheses. In particular the fixed-distinct models below need not be X-stable on L. The phase proof works with any symplectic signed action: after conjugating an actual top collision by a *physical* V₂-element it involves signs h^kτ exchanging L with τL, exactly as in e562c132.

The centralizer of τ has order p^(d+1)q=|H|, and H∩C_D(τ)=1: on X the J-intersection with the fixed diagonal line is zero, on N use L∩U⁺=0. As |D|=|H|², D=H C_D(τ). Every conjugate of H is therefore conjugate by a τ-fixed element. An arbitrary loop isotope changes the generating marks to a point conjugate of K a⁻¹, a∈K, and K a⁻¹=a^(1/2)K a^(−1/2) by sandwich closure. After this conjugation the only additional freedom relative to ordinary signed-folder isomorphism is conjugating the point stabilizer; conversely rebasing the same sharp marks gives an actual isotope. Thus these loops really are G-loops. (For the separately named abelian input it is Nagy's matched-pair G-loop construction.) Finally N_X(J)=J, since a first-axis translation must vanish to normalize its top modulo V₂. If (v,s)∈N normalizes W then v∈L^⊥=L; hence N_D(H)=HZ (cancel its projection by H). The actual right nucleus is N_D(H)/H=C_p via the permutation centralizer at the base point. Its nuclear right translations are represented by Z, which is exactly Z(D) by faithfulness on V and U^V=0.

We record **all** identifications between normalized models. Any signed isomorphism preserving H preserves the characteristic N, then V=O_q(X), and V₂=J∩V. It therefore also preserves V₁=τV₂ and scales both axes by the same δ∈F_q^×. It fixes h in X: its top power must be 1 by its action on V₁, its translation correction must belong to V₂ since it preserves J, and τ-inversion then forces this correction to be zero (the two axes are distinct). The image of our complement X₀ may initially have an affine U-shift over this base map. Its shifts form a 1-cocycle of X with values in U, principal by the V-averaging argument. Its unique principal vector is τ-fixed because both complements are signed and U^X=0. On V₂ all the image lifts belong to H, hence this vector is fixed modulo L by V₂. There are no fixed vectors in U/L by the hypothesis, so the vector belongs to L∩U⁺=0. The remaining central complement twist is a homomorphism X→Z respecting signs, hence zero (X_ab is generated by the negative h). The induced linear map on U must actually intertwine the signed X-actions, with no residual shear on N since a shear would now be X-invariant. Finally z h∈H forces z↦z' rather than an arbitrary nonzero power: the normalized top is fixed. Thus our gauging group consists of just common axis scalings and exact prime-field symplectic intertwiners preserving τ and L, and conversely all of these are genuine signed-folder isomorphisms. This step is why allowing general unsigned changes of central scale, primitive top or origin would give incorrect counts.

## 2. The only two-sheet character possibilities

Fix k=F_(p^f), ζ∈k of order q, star the involution x↦x^(p^(f/2)); −1=p^(f/2) mod q. Set S=⟨p,α⟩ inside F_q^×. Because p∤f its order is d, each r∈S has a unique expression p^n α^j modulo f,p, and ξ(p^n α^j)=p^n α^(−j) is an involution. Over k the V₂-weights of U/L consist of exactly one orbit of size d under h and field Frobenius. Indeed every nonzero orbit has size d; any additional orbit or multiplicity would exceed the dimension. The symplectic identification L≅(U/L)* gives **the same** orbit, since −1 is in ⟨p⟩. A common axis scaling normalizes this orbit to S, now on each axis by τ. The full V-weights hence form a multigraph over S×S with exactly two weights (with multiplicity) in every column. h acts by reciprocal scalars, Frobenius by simultaneous multiplication by p. The action on a single column is free/transitive under their product; consequently the complete multigraph is exactly two graphs
```math
                  O_\lambda=\{(\lambda\xi(r),r):r\in S\},
                  \qquad \lambda\in S ,
```
with repeats allowed. Transpose, induced by τ, sends λ to ξ(λ)⁻¹. Scaling both coordinates by δ preserves S only if δ∈S and sends graph constants to δλ/ξ(δ), changing only the α-exponent by any even shift, hence by any shift mod p.

The representation on a single graph is canonical as an X-module: write A_λ=k^(Z/p), with (a,b) acting at site j by the scalar ζ^(λ α^j a+α^(−j)b) and h by (Rx)_j=x_(j+1). To see completeness, decompose into rational V₂-orbits (Frobenius cycles); h acts freely/transitively on the p rational sites, so choose a k-coordinate at one and transport a basis around the h-cycle, which has no monodromy since h^p=1. On a single graph V₁ then acts by the indicated eigenvalue, and on a repeated graph it is scalar on each whole multiplicity space. Distinct graphs split as X-modules by their V-weights, without any assertion that all characteristic-p representations of X are semisimple. Restricted to J all single graphs are the same irreducible A as just described, with endomorphism field k: a commuting map preserves each V₂ rational site, is a field scalar there and h forces all scalars equal. With multiplicity two, L is one such J-copy: in each V₂-column it has one eigenline by transitivity and the dimension. In the distinct case each graph is symplectically nondegenerate and the two are orthogonal: opposite weights remain on their own graph and can pair with no other. On each graph the invariant alternating form is
```math
               g_c(x,y)=\sum_j\operatorname{Tr}_{k/\mathbb F_p}(c x_j y_j^*),
               \qquad c^*=-c\ne0 .
```
For justification, V₂-invariance pairs a rational site only with itself since inversion is field star; trace duality and commuting with a primitive q-action leave precisely the displayed trace coefficient. Shift invariance makes it site-independent. All such coefficients are equivalent by nonzero field scalars and surjectivity of the multiplicative norm from k to its star-fixed subfield k₀=F_(p^(f/2)).

## 3. Repeated and fixed-distinct graphs

A repeated graph is τ-stable, so λ=±α^l. L is now X-invariant, because V₁ is scalar on every fixed V₂-column, and U=L⊕τL. Its entire form is specified by nondegenerate b(x,y)=ω(x,τy) on L, which is alternating by symplectic involutivity. Over k it pairs a weight v only with −vᵀ. If λ=−α^l there is a weight v=−vᵀ (choose r=α^j, 2j=l); its one-dimensional eigenspace in L would be orthogonal to everything including itself, impossible. Normalize λ=+1 by common scaling. In coordinates on L=A₁ the pairing b has the form ΣTr(c x_j y_(−j)^*), c^*=−c: weight pairing forces the opposite site and star, and h balances j with −j. Multiplying both L and τL coordinates by one common field scalar transports any nonzero coefficient to any other by its norm, commuting with τ and fixing the oriented central generator. This is exactly one class, realized by the earlier duplicated-weight extraspecial construction.

For two **distinct fixed** graphs each λ=±α^l. On the individual nondegenerate symplectic X-module τ has to transport the weights by transposition. Its site map takes j to −j−l and is multiplied by a fixed field scalar s by h-equivariance. If λ=−α^l it also applies star at each site. Then τ²=1 requires ss^*=1, but form invariance requires ss^*=−1 since c^*=−c. Impossible. For positive λ, τ is just site reversal j↦−j−l with sign ±1. Normalize both forms to the same g_c independently; τ commutes with such scalar changes. In A_l⊕A_m the projected L is a graph x↦η x: either coordinate projection from its irreducible J-module is an isomorphism, since L cannot be one of the nondegenerate blocks. Isotropy requires ηη^*=−1. The intersection L∩τL is zero precisely when the two reversal signs are opposite. Indeed their ratio acts by that sign times a site shift; a shift has only eigenvalue 1 in characteristic p and always has a nonzero fixed vector, so only opposite signs avoid intersection. Once this holds, all η are equivalent by independent norm-one scalar rescalings on the blocks. Distinguish the plus and minus graph intrinsically by τ acting on the h-fixed constant site vectors of each. Common axis scalings translate the two exponents l,m together. Normalize the plus one to 0: the negative one's remaining offset r can be any nonzero value mod p, and no isomorphism can change it. This yields exactly **p−1** additional classes, all genuine by §1. They do not have the same V-spectrum range as the exchanged case below.

## 4. Exchanged graphs and the residual physical phase count

The last case consists of O_λ and its **distinct** transpose. Normalize their constants to p^i,p^(−i) with 1≤i<f/2 by a common scaling and naming the first graph (a bookkeeping choice, not an extra equivalence once i is fixed). In the coordinates of [e562c132](frobenius-tilted-lagrangians-in-simple-odd-bol-loops.md) use A=A_(p^i) first, its τ-twist second, with literal exchange as τ. This normalization is always possible by choosing the second coordinates as the τ-images of the first. The two forms are therefore equal g_c. L again projects isomorphically to both copies. The complete set of J-intertwiners from the first to the second consists exactly of scalar multiples of site reversal followed by φ(x)=x^(p^i), because the displayed map is an intertwiner and End_J(A)=k. Thus
```math
                 L=\{(x,T x)\},\qquad
                 (Tx)_j=\eta(x_{-j})^\varphi,\qquad
                 \eta\eta^*=-c^\varphi/c .
```
The full field-norm proof in e562c132 gives L∩τL=0 exactly if h_i is odd or divisible by four. For clarity, T² at each site is ηη^φ times φ²; its fixed-point test is the φ²-norm of ηη^φ. For h_i odd this is n² with n=Norm_φ(η) satisfying nn^*=−1, so n²≠1. For h_i even, φ^(h_i/2)=star and the norm equals n=(−1)^(h_i/2+1), which is different from 1 exactly in the retained case.

It remains to count η without an extra quotient. The two different Frobenius exponents modulo f cannot be interchanged by common axis scaling, which can shift only α-powers. Between normalized swapped models, δ must have δ/ξ(δ)=1, so δ is a Frobenius power p^k. The corresponding intertwiner on the first normalized block is scalar ρ times a field Frobenius (up to the inverse convention for δ), with no site shift. On the second it must be the **same** map by τ. These are all such intertwiners: scalar is the full X-endomorphism, and distinct graphs cannot mix since δ does not exchange them. After fixing c once, preservation of the oriented symplectic form requires ρρ^*=c^(p^k)/c. The induced change of parameter is
```math
               \eta\longmapsto\rho^{1-\varphi}\eta^{p^k}.
```
There is no further central multiplier or signed conjugacy quotient, by §1.

Here is an elementary evaluation. Choose a primitive v∈k, write q₀=p^(f/2) and m=q₀+1, and normalize c=v^(m/2), which has c^*=−c. All η have exponents
```math
                \eta=v^{E_0+(q_0-1)j},\quad
                E_0=(p^i+q_0-2)/2,\quad j\bmod m .
```
The k=0 maps send j to j+(1−p^i)s arbitrarily, so their orbits are j mod m_i. For k=1 choose ρ=v^((p−1)/2). Substitution gives exactly j↦p j+(p−1)/2 modulo m_i: the constant follows from (p−1)E₀−(p^i−1)(p−1)/2=(p−1)(q₀−1)/2. Frobenius and the k=0 subgroup generate all the maps. The permutation has order dividing f as p^f−1 is divisible by 2m_i. Its powers give exactly the fixed-point counts stated above. This proves (1) with no duplication and no scalar-inversion ambiguity.

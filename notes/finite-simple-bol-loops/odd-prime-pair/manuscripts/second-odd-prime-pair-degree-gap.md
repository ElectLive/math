# The first odd prime-pair degree is followed by a gap when $`p\nmid f`$

**Research proof; see the scope of verification.** [Verification and provenance](../VERIFICATION.md).

**Theorem.** Let $`p,q`$ be distinct odd primes, $`p\mid q-1`$, $`f=\operatorname{ord}_q(p)`$ and $`p\nmid f`$. Then there is no finite simple proper left Bol loop of order $`p^{pf+2}q`$. In particular the occurrence at the [first degree $`p^{pf+1}q`$](../reviews/first-degree-odd-prime-pair-assembly.md) does not extend to the very next $`p`$-exponent.

Assume a loop at this degree exists and put $`d=pf`$. We use the canonical signed notation $`D,t,H,N=O_p(D),F=\Phi(N),U=N/F`$ and $`W=H\cap N`$, $`\bar W=WF/F`$. The quotient $`D/N`$ has the two reciprocal axes $`V=C_q^2`$ and top $`P=C_p`$. Choose a $`t`$-stable Sylow $`q`$-group $`B=B_1B_2`$ lifting $`V`$, with $`B_2`$ the axis in the image of the physical stabilizer. As in the previous arguments, conjugate $`H`$ by $`N`$ if needed so $`B_2\subset H`$; the comparisons are of actual normal orbits and avoidance under all conjugates, not of based isomorphism types. All nonzero weights on $`U`$ are mixed by the axial exclusion, i.e. nontrivial on both axes. The previous theorem gives
```math
F\le Z(D\rtimes\langle t\rangle),\quad |F|=p^2,\quad H\cap F=1,\quad \dim U^-=d+1,\quad \dim(U/\bar W)=d .
```
Write $`U=U_0\oplus A`$, where $`U_0=U^B=U^{B_2}\subseteq\bar W`$ and $`A=[U,B]`$. The quotient $`U/\bar W`$ is irreducible for the actual projected stabilizer: its $`B_2`$-weights are nontrivial, and each joint top/Frobenius weight orbit has size $`d`$, so any nonzero submodule would already cost all $`d`$ dimensions.

## 1. The actual stabilizer forces a nonsingular alternating pencil on the moving part

The alternating commutator takes values in central $`F`$, all $`p`$th powers of $`N`$ are central, and each commutator has order dividing $`p`$ by $`[x,y]^p=[x^p,y]`$. Thus $`x\mapsto x^p`$ from $`N`$ into $`F`$ is a homomorphism, since the class-two binomial term is trivial at odd $`p`$. It is fixed by $`B`$ and kills $`M=[N,B]`$. Let $`C=C_N(B)`$. Coprime action gives $`N=MC`$; fixed-point lifting and $`U_0\subseteq\bar W`$ give $`C=(W\cap C)F`$. The subgroup $`W`$ is elementary abelian since it embeds into $`U`$. Hence $`N^p=C^p=F^p`$, so $`F=F^p[N,N]`$ and $`[N,N]=F`$ by the finite abelian Frattini property. In particular $`F`$ is elementary abelian and $`N`$ has exponent $`p`$.

Fix a nonzero scalar functional $`F\to\mathbb F_p`$. The resulting alternating form on $`U`$ is nonzero since commutators generate $`F`$. It is signed-invariant and $`\bar W`$ is totally isotropic (the physical $`W`$ meets $`F`$ trivially). Its radical $`R`$ lies in $`\bar W`$: otherwise its image in the irreducible $`U/\bar W`$ would be onto, giving $`U=R+\bar W`$ and a zero form. The actual preimage of $`R`$ in $`N`$ is a signed-normal subgroup; its orbit size is exactly $`p^2`$ since $`W`$ covers $`R`$ above the disjoint $`F`$. The sign fiber has size $`p^{\dim R^-}`$ since $`F`$ is fixed, and it must be strictly smaller than this orbit, or the physical cut gives a proper nontrivial normal subloop. Thus $`\dim R^-\le1`$.

Each rational mixed packet has exactly $`d=pf`$ weights under the reciprocal order-$`p`$ top and simultaneous field Frobenius: the ratio determines the top exponent and either coordinate then determines the Frobenius exponent. Within a packet there are at most $`f`$ weights with equal coordinates, since there is at most one eligible top exponent. So a signed-invariant nonzero mixed part contributes at least $`(d-f)/2\ge f\ge3`$ negative dimensions, counting exchanged weight spaces of equal multiplicity. This estimate also holds if distinct packets are exchanged. Hence $`R`$ has no mixed weight and $`R\subseteq U_0`$. Conversely $`U_0`$ pairs trivially with $`A`$ by $`B`$-invariance and with itself by isotropy, so $`R=U_0`$. We have proved that **every nonzero scalar commutator form on $`A`$ is nondegenerate**. In particular $`C`$ is central in $`N`$ and elementary abelian, $`[M,M]=[N,N]=F`$, and $`C\cap M=F`$ (their images in $`U`$ are disjoint). No simplicity criterion for an arbitrarily chosen abstract pencil was used.

## 2. Exactly two moving packets, including at $`p=3`$

For any of the nondegenerate scalar forms, $`\bar W\cap A`$ has dimension $`\dim A-d`$ (as $`U_0\subseteq\bar W`$) and is isotropic, so $`\dim A\le2d`$. This removes a possible third packet even at $`p=3`$, where the unsigned count alone could allow it. Also $`\dim A`$ is a positive multiple of $`d`$.

Take $`L=N_D(B)`$ and a $`t`$-stable Sylow $`p`$-group $`S`$ of $`L`$. Then $`S/C=P`$. Since $`C_N(B_2)=C`$ by coprime fixed-point lifting, $`N_D(B_2)=L`$, and $`H=W N_H(B_2)`$ gives $`H\cap L`$ covering $`P`$. As $`C=(W\cap C)F`$ and $`H\cap F=1`$, its Sylow $`p`$-subgroup has index $`p^2`$ in a Sylow of $`L`$. Conjugate its Sylow into $`S`$; each inverted element of $`S`$ is a true mark, so there are at most $`p^2`$ of them by sharp transitivity against that conjugate stabilizer. The inverted top costs a factor $`p`$, and $`F`$ is fixed, so
```math
\dim U_0^-\le1 .
```
If $`\dim A=d`$, its one mixed packet contributes at most $`(d+f)/2`$ negatives (the same fixed-weight bound), too few for $`\dim U^-=d+1`$ even with one more on $`U_0`$. Thus $`\dim A=2d`$. The true moving stabilizer $`\bar W\cap A`$ is a common Lagrangian for the entire pencil.

If $`f`$ is odd, $`d`$ is odd. The two moving packets are distinct and opposite, since the opposite of a mixed weight is not in its own joint orbit: reciprocity forces the top exponent zero and $`-1`$ is not a Frobenius multiplier for odd $`f`$. Nondegeneracy requires both opposite packets, and each has multiplicity one. The two sign eigenspaces of a symplectic involution on $`A`$ have even dimension. Therefore $`d+1`$ even and $`\dim U_0^-\le1`$ force $`\dim U_0^-=0`$ and $`\dim A^-=d+1`$. If $`t`$ exchanges the packets, $`\dim A^-=d`$. If it preserves both, there are exactly $`f`$ fixed weights on each. Indeed a swap in the joint orbit requires the square of its Frobenius multiplier to be one (multiply the coordinates); for odd $`f`$ that multiplier is one, and the order-$`p`$ ratio admits exactly one aligning top exponent. The fixed weights on each packet are one Frobenius cycle, so their one-dimensional sign values agree. Perfect sign-invariant pairing of opposite weights forces the same sign on both cycles; the signature is $`+2f`$ or $`-2f`$ and $`\dim A^-=d-f`$ or $`d+f`$. None equals $`d+1`$ since $`f\ge3`$. This excludes odd $`f`$.

## 3. Even $`f`$: the actual center again produces a forbidden normal cut

For even $`f`$, the even-dimensional negative symplectic space on $`A`$ and odd $`d+1`$ force $`\dim U_0^-=1`$ and $`\dim A^-=d`$. Let $`H_0`$ be a Sylow $`p`$-subgroup of $`H\cap L`$, conjugated into the signed $`S`$ as above. If desired conjugate the whole $`H`$ along with it; $`L`$ preserves the two axes and all the sharpness and normal-orbit calculations remain valid. This $`H_0`$ is an actual complement to central $`F`$ in $`S`$, since it has index $`p^2`$ and avoids $`F`$. In particular $`H_0\cap C`$ is a complement to $`F`$ in $`C`$, stable under $`H_0`$.

There is an isomorphism $`S/F\cong D/(MB)`$ because $`N=MC`$ and $`C\cap M=F`$; this quotient is generated by images of inverted marks. Choose an inverted lift $`h`$ of the top in $`S`$ and write $`\alpha`$ for conjugation by $`h`$ on central elementary $`C`$. Its order divides $`p`$. The $`\alpha`$-span of $`(C/F)^-`$ is all of $`C/F`$: after quotienting by that span, the induced sign fixes the residual elementary subgroup, so the odd top action equals its own inverse and is trivial; the resulting abelian group generated by inverted images has no nontrivial fixed odd elements. Thus $`C/F`$ is one cyclic Jordan block and its successive signs alternate starting with a negative generator. The negative dimension one gives $`\dim(C/F)=1`$ or $`2`$.

The complement $`H_0\cap C`$ is $`\alpha`$-stable: $`H_0`$ covers the top and $`C`$ is abelian, so a lift of its generator induces a generator of the same action as $`h`$. If $`\dim(C/F)=1`$, then $`\alpha`$ is trivial on this complement and on $`F`$, hence on $`C`$. The negative line of $`C`$ is then signed-normal in $`D`$ (since $`C`$ centralizes $`N`$ and $`B`$), and misses the point stabilizer because its nonidentity elements are marks. Its actual orbit size and sign fiber both equal $`p`$, impossible.

If $`\dim(C/F)=2`$, let $`v`$ span the negative line of $`C`$ and let $`w=(\alpha-1)v`$. The $`\alpha`$-invariant complement above shows $`(\alpha-1)^2=0`$ on $`C`$, since it vanishes on both that complement and $`F`$. The vectors $`v,w`$ therefore span a genuine complement $`C'`$ to $`F`$, with one size-two Jordan block, $`tv=-v`$ and $`tw=w`$. In particular $`C'`$ is signed-normal in **the original group $`D`$**, not just in a quotient: $`N,B`$ centralize it and $`S`$ preserves it. The element $`h^p\in C`$ belongs to the $`\alpha`$-fixed subspace $`F+\langle w\rangle`$, all sign-positive; it is also inverted, so $`h^p=1`$. Hence $`S=F\times S_0`$ where $`S_0=\langle C',h\rangle`$ is extraspecial of order $`p^3`$, exponent $`p`$, with derived line $`\langle w\rangle`$.

Inside this direct product the actual complement $`H_0`$ is the graph of a homomorphism $`S_0\to F`$, which kills $`\langle w\rangle`$. Thus it contains that physical line in $`C'`$. It cannot contain all of $`C'`$ because the negative line consists of actual inverted marks, giving $`|H_0\cap C'|=p`$. As $`C'`$ is normal in $`D`$, the $`p`$-subgroup $`H\cap C'`$ is normal in $`H\cap L`$ and is contained in its Sylow $`p`$-subgroup; thus the computed intersection is the **original** one, up to the harmless conjugation preserving $`C'`$. The true $`C'`$-orbit has size exactly $`p`$, equal to its sign fiber. This yields a forbidden proper normal subloop, excluding even $`f`$ as well.

The argument is uniform, theoretical, and does not rely on enumerating groups or loops at a fixed $`(p,q)`$. The input lower bound already excludes smaller exponents; the theorem here excludes precisely the exponent after the attained minimum, not every larger exponent.

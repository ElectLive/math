# One exponent above the first odd prime-pair degree: no negative Frattini direction

**Research proof; see the scope of verification.** [Verification and provenance](../VERIFICATION.md).

**Theorem.** Let $`p,q`$ be distinct odd primes, $`p\mid q-1`$, $`f=\mathrm{ord}_q(p)`$, $`p\nmid f`$, and $`d=pf`$. If a finite simple proper left Bol loop has order $`p^{d+2}q`$, put $`D=\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)`$, $`N=O_p(D)`$, and $`F=\Phi(N)`$. Then its canonical sign $`t`$ fixes $`F`$ pointwise; indeed $`F\le Z(D\rtimes\langle t\rangle)`$. The negative eigenspace on $`N/F`$ has dimension $`d+1`$. In the nonaxial branch $`|F|=p^2`$. In the axial branch with no sign action on $`F`$ we do not determine $`|F|`$ or the residual folder.

## 1. A hypothetical negative sign on the Frattini implies a prime fiber and a trivial q-action

Use the actual stabilizer $`H`$ and full inverted mark set $`T`$. The audited lower bound $`a\ge 1+\mathrm{lcm}(p^k,f)`$ forces $`k=1`$, as $`a=d+2`$ and $`p\nmid f`$. Thus $`D/N\cong V\rtimes P`$, $`V\cong C_q^2`$ with reciprocal axis action by $`P=C_p`$; the sign exchanges the axes and inverts the top. The physical sign equation gives $`[N:C_N(t)]=p^{d+1}`$. Write $`W=H\cap N`$, $`U=N/F`$ and $`\bar W=WF/F`$. As in the first-degree proof, choose a sign-stable Sylow $`q`$-group $`B=B_1B_2`$ over $`V`$ and conjugate $`H`$ by $`N`$ if needed so that it contains the original stabilizer-axis lift $`B_2`$. This conjugation is used for orbit and avoidance tests, not as a marked isomorphism.

Assume for contradiction $`t`$ is nontrivial on $`F`$. The **axial** case means that some $`B`$-weight on $`U`$ is supported on a single axis. Then the paired axial packets consume at least $`d`$ negative dimensions, leaving at most one on $`F`$ by coprime fixed-point lifting. In the **nonaxial** case, the first-degree stabilizer calculation still gives that the actual $`J=HN/N`$-module $`U/\bar W`$ has no $`B_2`$-fixed weights, and positive dimension divisible by $`d`$. Since its codimension accounting is $`a=\dim(U/\bar W)+\log_p[F:H\cap F]`$ and $`a=d+2<2d`$, $`\dim(U/\bar W)=d`$ and the physical Frattini orbit has size $`p^2`$. Its sign fiber cannot equal that orbit, since equality would give a proper nontrivial normal subloop. Thus in either branch $`[F:C_F(t)]=p`$.

I claim that $`B`$ centralizes $`F`$. The negative dimension on $`F/\Phi(F)`$ is at most one. Work over a splitting field for its $`B`$-weights. In the signed $`q`$-normalizer $`N_D(B)\rtimes\langle t\rangle`$, each conjugate of $`t`$ normalizes $`B`$ and has the same negative dimension. It cannot exchange two distinct Frobenius orbits of nonzero weights: these each have $`f\ge3`$ members, and the exchanged weight spaces contribute at least $`f`$ negative dimensions. Each conjugate thus preserves every Frobenius orbit, on which it acts as a power of the Frobenius cycle of order at most two. The group generated by these signs acts on each orbit through an elementary group of exponent at most two. Its image in the signed quotient modulo $`N`$ is the whole signed quotient: the normalizer surjects onto it by the Frattini argument, and in the quotient the conjugates of the sign generate by the full inverted mark property. Hence some odd element of the generated group induces the nontrivial top rotation on $`B`$. It acts trivially on each Frobenius orbit by odd order, yet that rotation fixes no nonzero weight on $`B`$ (both eigenvalues on the two axes are nontrivial). Therefore only the trivial $`B`$-weight occurs on $`F/\Phi(F)`$. The kernel of the action of $`\mathrm{Aut}(F)`$ on its Frattini quotient is a $`p`$-group (equivalently, use its free action on all ordered generating lifts of a fixed basis). The $`q`$-group $`B`$ consequently centralizes $`F`$ itself.

The action of $`D`$ on $`F`$ now has $`p`$-group image: $`D=N B S`$ for a Sylow $`p`$-group $`S`$ in the $`q`$-normalizer, and $`B`$ acts trivially (also its order accounts for the entire $`q`$-part of $`D`$). Since $`H`$ normally generates $`D`$, it maps onto that $`p`$-group. Thus $`H\cap F`$ is $`D`$-invariant and trivial by the faithful action. Also $`M=[N,B]`$ centralizes $`F`$, since the action of $`B`$ on $`F`$ is trivial. These conclusions do not require $`F`$ to be abelian.

## 2. Reduction to the same index-$`p^2`$ signed Sylow problem

In the axial case, the paired $`d`$-dimensional active axial modules $`A_1,A_2`$ use all the negative rank on $`U`$, since one unit was used on $`F`$. There may also be a $`B`$-fixed part $`U_0`$; all other active weight packets would cost another negative dimension under the generating signed group. Thus $`U=A_1\oplus A_2\oplus U_0`$. The actual $`B_2\rtimes P`$-action on its moving axial summand $`A_2`$ is irreducible. Coprime projection to this summand makes $`\bar W`$ either contain $`A_2`$ or have zero intersection and projection to $`A_2`$.

First suppose $`A_2\subseteq\bar W`$. Put $`M_i=[N,B_i]`$, normal in $`N`$. Since $`W`$ is $`B_2`$-stable, the image of $`[W,B_2]`$ in $`U`$ is all $`A_2`$, the image of $`M_2`$. Hence $`M_2=[W,B_2](M_2\cap F)`$. The second factor is fixed by $`B_2`$ and commutes with $`M_2`$ since $`M`$ centralizes $`F`$. Coprime commutator action gives $`M_2=[M_2,B_2]\le[W,B_2]`$, so $`M_2\le W`$ and $`M_2\cap F=1`$. It is elementary abelian, and the sign gives the same facts, except for membership in $`W`$, for $`M_1`$. Their images are on distinct axes, so their intersection is trivial and they commute by normality. Thus $`M=M_1M_2`$ is elementary abelian and disjoint from $`F`$. Its normality and the trivial action of $`N`$ on $`U`$ show that $`N`$ centralizes $`M`$. With $`C=C_N(B)`$, coprime action then gives $`N=M\times C`$ (the intersection has trivial image in $`U`$ and is disjoint from $`F`$). In particular $`F=\Phi(C)`$. The actual $`W\to N/M=C`$ is onto by the first-degree stabilizer argument, which uses the $`p`$-group $`D/(MB)`$. Hence $`\Phi(W)`$ maps onto $`\Phi(C)=F`$, impossible since $`\Phi(W)\subseteq W\cap F=1`$. This excludes the case $`A_2\subseteq\bar W`$ under the hypothetical sign action.

If the axial projection onto $`A_2`$ is zero, then $`\dim(U/\bar W)\ge d`$ and the Frattini orbit exponent is at least two by physical simplicity (its sign fiber is $`p`$). Thus $`\dim(U/\bar W)=d`$, $`\bar W=A_1\oplus U_0=U^{B_2}`$ and $`[F:H\cap F]=p^2`$. In the nonaxial branch we already established the same codimension and Frattini orbit size, and $`U^{B_2}=U^B\subseteq\bar W`$. In both surviving cases $`|F|=p^2`$, since $`H\cap F=1`$. It must be elementary abelian: an automorphism of a cyclic odd order-$`p^2`$ group with order two is inversion and has sign fiber $`p^2`$, not $`p`$.

Now let $`L=N_D(B)`$ and $`C=C_N(B)\le L`$. We have $`F\le C`$, and $`U^B\subseteq\bar W`$ in both cases. Since fixed points lift and $`F\le C`$, it follows that $`C=(W\cap C)F`$, so $`[C:W\cap C]=p^2`$. Moreover $`H\cap L`$ covers $`P`$. To see this in the nonaxial case, fixed lifting gives $`C_N(B_2)=C`$ and then $`N_D(B_2)=L`$; use $`H=W N_H(B_2)`$. In the axial case the normalizer of $`B_2`$ is $`C_N(B_2)L`$, since $`D=NL`$ and $`L`$ preserves both axes. A rotation lift in $`N_H(B_2)`$ has the form $`n\ell`$ with $`n\in C_N(B_2)`$, $`\ell\in L`$. Here $`C_N(B_2)=(W\cap C_N(B_2))F`$ because $`\bar W=U^{B_2}`$ and $`F`$ is $`B_2`$-fixed. Removing the corresponding $`W`$ factor gives a lift of the same rotation in $`H\cap L`$.

## 3. The resulting signed Sylow contradicts physical sharpness

Take a $`t`$-stable Sylow $`p`$-group $`S`$ of $`L`$. It contains $`C`$, and $`S/C=P`$. A Sylow $`p`$-group of $`H\cap L`$ projects onto $`P`$ and can be conjugated into $`S`$ with index $`p^2`$, by the computed $`[C:W\cap C]`$. Every inverted element of $`S`$ is an actual mark. Sharp transitivity gives at most one such mark per coset of this conjugate subgroup of $`S`$ contained in a conjugate point stabilizer. Thus $`[S:C_S(t)]\le p^2`$. The top $`P`$ costs $`p`$ and the sign fiber on $`F`$ costs $`p`$ by assumption, so coprime fixed-point lifting forces $`t`$ to fix $`C/F`$ pointwise.

The quotient $`C/F`$ is abelian, and $`t`$ inverts the cyclic $`S/C`$. Thus conjugation by a lift of its generator acts on $`C/F`$ by the same automorphism as conjugation by its inverse, an automorphism of odd order; it is trivial. It follows that $`S/F`$ is abelian. Since $`N=M C`$ and $`C\cap M\subseteq F`$ (their respective images in $`U`$ are the fixed and moving parts), $`D/(MBF)\cong S/F`$. This quotient is generated by inverted images, so its sign-fixed odd part $`C/F`$ is trivial. We conclude $`C=F`$ and $`|S|=p^3`$.

The action of $`D`$ on $`F`$ now factors through $`S`$ because $`M`$ and $`B`$ centralize $`F`$. The action is nontrivial: otherwise the sign-negative line of elementary $`F`$ is signed-normal in $`D`$, would have true orbit size $`p`$ (it avoids $`H`$), and would equal its sign fiber, yielding a forbidden proper normal subloop. A nontrivial $`p`$-subgroup of $`\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)`$ is unipotent of order $`p`$ with a single common fixed line $`Z`$. This line is signed-normal. The sign must fix it, since otherwise the same forbidden order-$`p`$ cut applies. Thus the sign on $`F/Z`$ is negative.

Choose an inverted lift $`h`$ of a generator of $`S/F=P`$; an element $`x^{-1}t(x)`$ from a top generator suffices. The power $`h^p\in F`$ is fixed under $`S`$ (it commutes with $`h`$ and $`F`$), so $`h^p\in Z`$. But it is inverted by $`t`$, which fixes $`Z`$, and has odd order. Hence $`h^p=1`$. Thus $`S`$ is the extraspecial group of order $`p^3`$ and exponent $`p`$, center $`Z`$, and $`t`$ acts as $`-I`$ on $`S/Z`$ (the images of $`h`$ and $`F/Z`$ are two independent inverted generators). Every noncentral vector of $`S/Z`$ has an inverted lift, again by $`x^{-1}t(x)`$ and invertibility of 2. Conjugation in the extraspecial group reaches the whole central coset of each noncentral element. Therefore **every noncentral element of $`S`$** is conjugate to a nonidentity mark. This contradicts the conjugate original stabilizer containing a subgroup of $`S`$ projecting onto $`P`$. The hypothetical sign action on $`F`$ is impossible.

The sign-fixed conclusion makes $`F`$ central in the full signed group by conjugate-sign generation. Fixed-point lifting then gives the full $`d+1`$ negative dimensions on $`U`$. In the nonaxial branch, the Frattini orbit calculation used above did not assume a nontrivial sign, so faithfulness and centrality give $`|F|=p^2`$ there also.

## Finite control (not a source assumption)

In ordinary Heisenberg coordinates for $`S`$ the final sign is $`(x,y,z)\mapsto(-x,-y,z)`$. Direct exact enumeration for $`p=3,5,7,11`$ checked respectively $`9,25,49,121`$ inverted elements, $`18,100,294,1210`$ elements with nonzero top coordinate, and reached $`25,121,337,1321`$ elements by conjugating inverted elements. In every case all nonzero-top elements were reached. The computation checks the terminal group model only; the group reduction and all-prime proof above are not consequences of that enumeration.

# Higher cyclic tops cannot saturate the odd prime-pair axial budget

**Research proof; see the scope of verification.** [Verification and provenance](../VERIFICATION.md).

**Theorem.** Let a finite simple proper left Bol loop have order $`p^a q`$ for distinct odd primes $`p,q`$. Put $`f=\mathrm{ord}_q(p)`$. In its canonical signed reduction write $`C_{p^k}`$ for the cyclic top above the normal $`p`$-core and the two $`q`$-axes. If $`p\mid f`$ and $`k\ge2`$, then
```math
a\ge \mathrm{lcm}(p^k,f)+k+1.
```

In particular at the third exponent $`a=f+3`$ only a top of order $`p`$ or $`p^2`$ can occur; the second option requires $`p^2\mid f`$.

Let $`N=O_p(D)`$ be the canonical $`p`$-core, $`F=\Phi(N)`$ and $`U=N/F`$. The canonical sign is $`t`$, the actual point stabilizer $`H`$, and the marked core intersection $`W=H\cap N`$. Retain the two-axis lifted group $`B=B_1B_2`$, with $`B_2`$ the stabilizer axis, and let $`d_k=\mathrm{lcm}(p^k,f)`$. The original sign count is $`a=s+k`$, $`[N:C_N(t)]=p^s`$. The $`B`$-action on $`U`$ is nontrivial by the coprime automorphism kernel for the Frattini quotient and Fitting self-centralization, as in the audited second-degree proof.

An axial packet and its sign-mate cost $`d_k`$ negative dimensions. Each genuinely mixed packet costs at least $`f(p^k-1)/2`$ negatives in the signed sum, by the ratio and fixed-weight count of §1 of the second-degree argument. As $`g=\gcd(p^k,f)\ge p\ge3`$ and $`p^k\ge9`$,
```math
f(p^k-1)/2 > fp^k/g=d_k.
```

Thus always $`s\ge d_k`$. At $`s=d_k`$ only one paired axial packet can occur; it uses all the negative dimensions on $`U`$ and leaves both $`F`$ and the $`B`$-fixed part of $`U`$ sign-fixed. The sign conjugates generate the signed group, so $`F`$ is central in it.

Write $`U=A_1\oplus A_2\oplus U^B`$, where the two axial modules each have dimension $`d_k`$ and are irreducible under their full axis plus top by joint top/Frobenius transitivity. They are sign-exchanged. The actual $`\bar W=WF/F`$ is isotropic for every scalar commutator form, since $`H\cap F=1`$ by faithfulness. As before, the moving $`B_2`$-projection is all of $`A_2`$ or zero. The two axial modules pair trivially with each other and with the fixed part under any scalar form. So if any scalar form is nonzero on one axial module it is nondegenerate there (irreducibility), and also on the other (the sign). If the $`A_2`$ projection of $`\bar W`$ is full, coprime averaging puts $`A_2`$ itself in $`\bar W`$, violating isotropy. Otherwise, writing $`e=\log_p|F|`$ and $`z=\dim U^B`$, the actual orbit $`[N:W]=p^{d_k+k}`$ gives $`\dim\bar W=d_k+z+e-k`$. Its intersection with $`A_1`$ has dimension at least $`d_k+e-k>d_k/2`$, since $`d_k\ge p^k>2k`$. This too violates isotropy. Thus the moving part centralizes the original core.

Put $`M=[N,B]`$ and $`C=C_N(B)`$. Coprime action gives $`N=M\times C`$, with $`M`$ the central elementary lift of $`A_1\oplus A_2`$ (it misses $`F`$ since it has no $`B`$-fixed part). The true $`W`$ surjects to $`N/M=C`$: $`H`$ normally generates $`D`$, thus covers the $`p`$-group $`D/(MB)`$, and a Sylow $`p`$-subgroup of $`H\cap NB`$ covers its kernel $`N/M`$. Consequently $`\Phi(W)`$ maps onto $`\Phi(C)=F`$, impossible unless $`F=1`$, since $`\Phi(W)\subseteq H\cap F`$. This recovers the elementary core without assuming it.

In $`L=N_D(B)`$ take a signed Sylow $`p`$-group $`S`$. It contains $`C`$ and $`S/C=C_{p^k}`$. The quotient $`S\cong D/(MB)`$ is generated by inverted marks and the sign fixes $`C`$. Conjugation by a lift of the cyclic top on abelian $`C`$ therefore equals its inverse; being of odd order it is trivial. Thus $`S`$ is abelian, its sign acts by inversion, and $`C=1`$. In particular $`S=C_{p^k}`$, and $`N=A_1\oplus A_2`$. The actual $`W`$ has dimension $`d_k-k`$, lies in $`A_1`$ since its dimension is less than $`d_k`$ and its moving projection to $`A_2`$ is irreducible, and is invariant under the top.

Normalize the actual stabilizer as in the audited order-$`p^2`$ step: arrange $`B_2\subset H`$ by coprime conjugacy, preserving the orbit and taboo tests, and use a sign-fixed diagonal shift to normalize $`HN/N`$ to $`B_2\rtimes S`$. Because $`H=W N_H(B_2)`$, there is a lift $`(x,h)\in H`$ of a generator $`h\in S`$ after removing a physical $`B_2`$-factor, with $`x\in A_1`$ since the lift normalizes $`B_2`$. Put $`R`$ for its action on $`A_1`$. The action on the $`k`$-dimensional $`A_1/W`$ is unipotent, so $`(R-1)^k A_1\subseteq W`$. Raising the actual lift to $`p^{k-1}`$ gives
```math

(x,h)^{p^{k-1}}=\bigl((R-1)^{p^{k-1}-1}x,\,h^{p^{k-1}}\bigr)
```

by the exact geometric-sum identity in characteristic $`p`$. Since $`p^{k-1}-1\ge k`$ for odd $`p`$ and $`k\ge2`$, its vector part lies in $`W`$. Cancel that part by a genuine element of $`W`$. The nonidentity $`h^{p^{k-1}}`$ is then in $`H`$, but it is itself inverted by the sign and hence is a mark. This contradicts sharp transitivity. Equality $`s=d_k`$ is impossible, proving the claim.

At $`a=f+3`$, the bound for $`k\ge2`$ implies $`k\le2`$ since $`d_k\ge f`$. If $`k=2`$ and $`p^2\nmid f`$, then $`d_2=pf`$ because $`p\mid f`$, which would give $`a\ge pf+3>f+3`$. This makes the two remaining third-degree top regimes explicit, without addressing whether either contains a genuine loop.

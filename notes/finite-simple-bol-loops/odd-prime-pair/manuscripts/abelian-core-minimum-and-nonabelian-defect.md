# The exact first two-prime degree with abelian $`p`$-core

**Proposed, not independently reviewed.** Fix distinct odd primes $`p,q`$ with $`p\mid q-1`$. Write $`f=\operatorname{ord}_q(p)`$, $`d=\operatorname{lcm}(p,f)`$. Among finite simple nonassociative left Bol loops of order $`p^a q`$ for which the canonical left multiplication group's $`p`$-core is abelian, the **least exponent is $`a=d+1`$** and the loop there is **unique up to based isomorphism**. Consequently, if any loops occur below the known construction at this exponent, they must have a genuinely nonabelian $`p`$-core, and satisfy the specific fixed-point/Frattini obstruction below. This settles this first-exponent problem even when $`p\nmid f`$, but **does not exclude that nonabelian-core escape**. The [split prime-pair and construction theorem](two-prime-divisibility-existence.md), source $`\mathtt{2625d36bc331e3ec7157d7266dca390baf46233e}`$, is a dependency.

For a simple proper left Bol loop the canonical inverting automorphism on the faithful left multiplication group exists by [C0007, Theorem 1.1(1)](../../sources/foundations/C0007.md), with its scoped source at $`\mathtt{932ead5db5f39045e2085caa6b062f6b981b2813}`$. A simple nonassociative odd loop is proper by FKP's odd Moufang solvability. Thus the earlier doubled group $`D`$ is exactly the graph of that automorphism, and its first projection identifies its $`p`$-core with the canonical one. All the arguments below also work directly in the doubled presentation with the physical core-free proviso on signed-stable normal groups.

## The obstruction before any linearity assumption

Use $`N=F(D)=O_p(D)`$, the signed extension by $`t`$, and the split upper quotient $`X=D/N=V\rtimes P`$ proved in the prime-pair source. Here $`V=C_q^2`$, $`P=C_{p^k}`$ for $`k\ge1`$, its two reciprocal rational axes are exchanged by $`t`$, and $`P`$ acts faithfully on each axis. Choose a $`t`$-stable Sylow $`q`$-subgroup $`B`$ of the preimage $`NB`$ of $`V`$; $`B\to V`$ is an isomorphism. Put $`L=N_D(B)`$, $`S`$ a $`t`$-stable Sylow $`p`$-subgroup of $`L`$, and $`M=[N,B]`$. The Frattini argument gives $`D=NL`$, $`L=B\rtimes S`$, where $`S\to P`$ is onto with kernel $`C_N(B)`$. In detail the action of $`L`$ on $`B`$ preserves the two axes, and its image in $`X`$ is all $`X`$. The kernel of $`L\to X`$ in $`N`$ consists of elements normalizing $`B`$, therefore centralizing it since $`N\cap B=1`$.

The actual stabilizer projects to an index-$`q`$ group $`J`$ above one axis $`V_2`$ extended by $`P`$; label the axes accordingly. Choose $`B_2\le B`$ above that axis. For necessary arguments we may conjugate $`H`$ by $`N`$ to arrange $`B_2\le H`$: a Sylow $`q`$-subgroup of $`H`$ maps onto $`V_2`$, and all such complements in $`NB_2`$ are $`N`$-conjugate. This change need **not** preserve $`T`$ as a set, so we use it only for the conjugacy-invariant conditions $`H\cap(Tt)^2=1`$ and normal generation, not as a classification gauge. The standard projection to a group quotient shows $`\langle H^D\rangle=D`$ by simplicity and nonassociativity.

We claim that if
```math
C_N(B_2)=C_N(B),                                                       \tag{1}
```
then one must have
```math
 C_M(B)\not\subseteq\Phi(S).                                           \tag{2}
```
Here $`M`$ is normal in $`D`$ and $`t`$-stable: it is normalized by $`N`$ by the commutator identity (equivalently it is the least normal subgroup of $`N`$ making the $`B`$-action trivial), and by $`L,t`$; together these generate. The product $`MB`$ is normal in $`D`$ since $`N/M`$ centralizes the image of $`B`$. Thus $`D/(MB)`$ is a $`p`$-group; the normal-generation argument forces $`H`$ to surject onto it. This quotient is naturally $`S/C_M(B)`$, because $`D=NL`$, $`N=M C_N(B)`$ by coprime action, and $`N\cap L=C_N(B)`$. One can see the coprime equality directly by lifting fixed points in $`N/M`$: by Schur–Zassenhaus any complement to the normal $`p`$-subgroup inside $`MB`$ is $`M`$-conjugate to $`B`$.

Write $`W_H=H\cap N`$. This group maps onto $`N/M`$. Indeed $`H\cap NB`$ maps onto the kernel $`N/M`$ of $`D/(MB)\to P`$; its Sylow $`p`$-subgroup still does, and lies in $`N`$, the unique Sylow $`p`$-subgroup of $`NB`$. Moreover $`C_{W_H}(B_2)`$ already maps onto $`N/M`$: $`W_H`$ is $`B_2`$-stable, its quotient image is fixed by $`B_2`$, and coprime fixed-point lifting on $`W_H/(W_H\cap M)`$ applies. Under (1) these representatives belong to $`H\cap L`$. Also $`H=W_H N_H(B_2)`$ by Sylow conjugacy in $`H\cap NB`$, and (1) gives $`N_D(B_2)=L`$: each $`d`$ is $`n\ell`$, $`\ell\in L`$ already normalizes $`B_2`$, and $`N_N(B_2)=C_N(B_2)`$. It follows that $`H\cap L`$ surjects onto $`S/C_M(B)`$.

If (2) failed, the image of $`H\cap L`$ in $`S=L/B`$ would be all of $`S`$ by the Frattini property. Hence $`H\cap L`$ would contain a full Sylow $`p`$-subgroup of $`L`$, conjugate to our $`t`$-stable $`S`$. But $`S`$ contains a nonidentity $`t`$-inverted element: $`t`$ inverts its nontrivial cyclic quotient $`P`$, so take a nontrivial $`s^{-1}t(s)`$. This element belongs to the full inverted set $`T\subseteq (Tt)^2`$. The latter square is conjugacy invariant and avoids $`H`$, a contradiction. This proves (2). The distinction between $`C_M(B)`$ and $`M`$ is essential for a nonabelian core; for instance a fixed center in an extraspecial group may survive.

When $`N`$ is abelian, coprime averaging splits $`N=M\times C_N(B)`$, so $`C_M(B)=1`$. Therefore (1) **cannot** hold in the abelian-core case.

## Numerical cost of a genuine axial character

Choose a finite signed-invariant filtration of $`N`$ by characteristic elementary abelian sections (for example the lower exponent-$`p`$ central series). Inner $`N`$ acts trivially on each section. The $`t`$-negative ranks sum to $`s=\log_p[N:C_N(t)]`$ by coprime fixed-point lifting. If $`C_N(B_2)>C_N(B)`$, some section has a $`B_2`$-fixed, $`B`$-nontrivial character after extension of scalars: for the two coprime acting groups, the sizes of their fixed subgroups are the products of the elementary fixed-section sizes. In our split weight coordinates this is an actual weight on a single axis. Its $`P`$- and Frobenius-translates on that axis have cardinality
```math
 d_k=\operatorname{lcm}(p^k,f).
```
Indeed their scalars generate the unique order-$`d_k`$ subgroup in the cyclic $`\mathbb F_q^\times`$. The conjugate axis is distinct; $`t`$ exchanges the two sets, so the section alone costs at least $`d_k`$ negative dimensions. The exact physical equation $`a=s+k`$ from the two-prime theorem gives $`a\ge k+d_k\ge1+d`$. This applies whenever (1) fails, including for nonabelian $`N`$. Thus below $`d+1`$ every occurrence must satisfy both (1) and (2).

## Unique marked group and stabilizer for abelian $`N`$ at equality

The construction at $`a=d+1`$ in the prime-pair source has $`p`$-core $`U\times U`$, elementary abelian: its quotient is the faithful affine $`C_q^2\rtimes C_p`$, with no normal $`p`$-subgroup. Suppose now $`N`$ is abelian and equality holds. A coprime group acting on a finite abelian $`p`$-group splits off its fixed subgroup; the nested fixed subgroups of $`B_2`$ and $`B`$ are distinguished already on $`N/pN`$ if they differ on $`N`$. More explicitly $`C_N(B_2)=C_N(B)\times C_{[N,B]}(B_2)`$; the latter factor is an abelian direct summand of $`[N,B]`$, which is a direct summand of $`N`$. Its nonzero reduction modulo $`p`$ supplies the axial character there. Therefore $`k=1`$, $`s=d`$, and the whole negative mass is spent by one paired axial orbit on $`N/pN`$, with multiplicity one. There are no other $`V`$-active character orbits: any nontrivial orbit sees some nontrivial action of the fixed-point-free $`P`$ on its weights, so cannot be pointwise fixed by $`t`$ and its conjugates. The $`V`$-trivial summand is $`t`$-fixed. Also $`pN`$ is $`t`$-fixed and therefore central in the signed group. The active $`M=[N,B]`$ satisfies $`C_M(B)=1`$ and $`pM=0`$, so is exactly the elementary sum of the two paired modules. Write $`N=M\times Z`$ where $`Z=C_N(B)`$ is fixed pointwise by $`t`$ and central (all negative mass is on $`M`$). Since $`D=NL`$ and $`M\cap L=1`$, $`D=M\rtimes L`$. Its signed quotient $`L/B`$ is a central extension of the cyclic $`P=C_p`$ by $`Z`$, hence abelian, and is generated by inverted elements. Therefore its fixed odd subgroup $`Z`$ is trivial. In particular the entire abelian $`p`$-core at equality is elementary abelian, and $`D=M\rtimes(B\rtimes P)`$ with the chosen $`t`$-stable factors.

The two coordinates $`A_1,A_2`$ of $`M`$ have dimension $`d`$. To justify uniqueness of the signed modules, the $`B_1`$-weights of $`A_1`$ form, up to choosing a generator of $`B_1`$, exactly $`\langle p\bmod q,\alpha\rangle`$ with multiplicity one, for $`\alpha`$ the order-$`p`$ eigenvalue of a generator of $`P`$. Set coordinates on $`B_2,A_2`$ by transport under $`t`$. If $`p\mid f`$ the representation is the field-semilinear model already normalized in [fixed-pair minimum rigidity](fixed-pair-minimum-rigidity.md). If $`p\nmid f`$, the $`B_1`$-module has $`p`$ pairwise distinct irreducible rational character spaces $`E=\mathbb F_{p^f}`$. The element $`h`$ generating $`P`$ cyclically permutes them. Choose a field coordinate in the first space (fixing a primitive $`q`$-th root) and transport it around the $`p`$ spaces by $`h`$. The cyclic consistency is exact since $`h^p=1`$. Thus its action is the plain $`p`$-cycle, with $`B_1`$ acting by the $`p`$ Frobenius orbits of eigencharacters $`\zeta^{\alpha^j}`$; this is the very rational model used in the constructive prime-pair proof. We may pick the same generator of the order-$`p`$ subgroup of $`\mathbb F_q^\times`$ in both models by changing the generator of $`P`$. Each $`A_i`$ is irreducible under $`B_i\rtimes P`$, even when it is reducible under $`B_i`$ alone.

Use only genuine $`t`$-preserving gauges from now on. The projected stabilizer $`J`$ is $`B_2\rtimes P`$ after swapping axes if necessary and conjugating by an element of $`B^t`$: its image modulo $`B_2`$ can be normalized by a shift in the first axis, and a $`t`$-fixed diagonal shift realizes it. Put $`W_H=H\cap M`$; its dimension is $`d-1`$ since $`[M:W_H]=p^{d+1}`$. Its projection to $`A_2`$ is a $`J`$-invariant subspace, hence zero by the preceding **full $`B_2\rtimes P`$** irreducibility. Therefore $`W_H`$ is a $`P`$-invariant hyperplane of $`A_1`$. It contains $`(h-1)A_1`$. For $`p\nmid f`$, the quotient by this image is the one-dimensional $`E`$-space obtained by summing the $`p`$ field coordinates; matched scalar $`E^\times`$ acts transitively on its prime-field hyperplanes by the nondegenerate field trace. For $`p\mid f`$ the same holds using scalars in the fixed field of the order-$`p`$ Frobenius and relative trace. Thus all these $`W_H`$ are equivalent under marked-group automorphisms.

The actual sections over $`J`$ have quotient module $`M/W_H=\mathbb F_p\oplus A_2`$. Averaging over the normal $`B_2`$ shows the $`A_2`$-cocycle is principal, because $`A_2^{B_2}=0`$, regardless of reducibility on $`B_2`$ alone. Its required translation is realized by matched $`t`$-fixed coordinates as in [the boundary proof](two-prime-boundary-folder-rigidity.md). The remaining scalar homomorphism has nonzero value on $`h`$, or $`H`$ contains a nonidentity inverted mark. All nonzero values are equivalent under simultaneous prime-field scaling. These are **based** folder isomorphisms, proving the asserted uniqueness. No normalization of $`H`$ used only for the necessary obstruction was used as a marked isomorphism here.

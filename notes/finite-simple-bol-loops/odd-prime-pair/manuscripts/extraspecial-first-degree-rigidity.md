# A genuinely extraspecial endpoint and odd character-degree uniqueness

**Research proof.** [Scoped verification](../VERIFICATION.md). This necessary result refines the class-two residual of [268180f2](all-core-prime-pair-minimum.md). It neither assumes nor proves a new construction for even character degree. We use the standing faithful simple signed group and the node 268180f2 with its stated independent dependency limits.

Suppose $`p,q`$ are distinct odd primes, $`p\mid q-1`$, $`f=\operatorname{ord}_q(p)`$, $`p\nmid f`$, $`d=pf`$, and a finite simple nonassociative left Bol loop has order $`p^{d+1}q`$. Every noncanonical possibility in the cited reduction has the following much stronger properties:

* $`N=O_p(D)`$ is **extraspecial of exponent $`p`$ and order $`p^{2d+1}`$**; its center is the $`t`$-fixed subgroup $`Z=\Phi(N)=[N,N]`$ of order $`p`$.
* On $`U=N/Z`$ the commutator is nondegenerate symplectic, and the stabilizer image $`L=(H\cap N)Z/Z`$ is a Lagrangian. Its two $`t`$-eigenspaces on $`U`$ are nondegenerate of dimension $`d`$ each, and $`U=L\oplus tL`$.
* In particular $`d`$ and $`f`$ must be even. Thus if $`p\nmid f`$ and $`f`$ is odd, the **standard abelian-core loop is the only isomorphism class at the least degree** for this prime pair, subject to the cited first-degree reduction and actual canonical-folder uniqueness. This is not derived by equating numerical order profiles.

Indeed the preceding source gives central $`\Phi=N'=C_p`$, $`[N:H\cap N]=p^{d+1}`$ and an irreducible $`d`$-dimensional moving module $`U/L`$ for the physical projected group $`J=B_2\rtimes P`$. On $`U`$ the alternating commutator form $`\omega`$ has coefficients in $`Z`$, which the signed group fixes. Its radical $`K`$ is $`J`$-stable. If $`K`$ maps nontrivially to the irreducible $`U/L`$, then $`U=K+L`$, impossible because $`L`$ is totally isotropic and $`\omega`$ is nonzero. The isotropy follows from the actual lift in $`H`$ and $`H\cap Z=1`$. Therefore $`K\subset L`$. Its preimage is precisely $`Z(N)`$. Since $`H\cap N`$ maps onto $`L`$, every vector of $`K`$ has a central lift in $`H`$, so
```math
                 [Z(N):Z(N)\cap H]=p .
```
This is a true orbit size. The proper signed-normal subgroup $`Z(N)`$ cannot have its $`t`$-negative mark fiber of the same size $`p`$, or the [physical normal cut](../../odd-degree/manuscripts/odd-cubefree-normal-tower.md) would give a proper normal subloop of order $`p`$. Thus $`t`$ fixes $`Z(N)`$ pointwise. Its conjugates generate the signed group, so $`Z(N)`$ is central there; its intersection with the core-free $`H`$ is trivial. We conclude $`Z(N)=Z`$ and $`\omega`$ is nondegenerate.

The $`B_2`$-fixed space on $`U`$ is the $`B`$-fixed space by the nonaxial assumption, and lies in $`L`$. It has zero pairing against the $`B_2`$-moving space by averaging, and zero pairing against itself by isotropy. Nondegeneracy implies $`U^{B_2}=0`$. The $`p`$-power map $`U\to Z`$ is $`\mathbb F_p`$-linear in a class-two group with central commutator of order odd $`p`$ (the binomial commutator factor has exponent divisible by $`p`$), and is $`B_2`$-invariant. It vanishes since $`U^{B_2}=0`$. Hence $`N`$ has exponent $`p`$.

Put $`m=\dim U`$. Its $`B_2`$-weights under $`J`$ and Frobenius form orbits of size $`d`$, as in 268180f2. Since $`L`$ is isotropic of codimension $`d`$, $`m\le2d`$, so $`m=d`$ or $`2d`$. The entire negative rank in $`N`$ is $`d`$ and the center is fixed. If $`m=d`$, then $`t=-I`$ on $`U`$; conjugation by $`t`$ would act trivially on the image of $`B`$ there. The action of $`B`$ on $`U=N/\Phi(N)`$ is faithful: a coprime automorphism in its kernel centralizes $`N`$ by the Frattini property for automorphisms of a $`p`$-group, and a nontrivial $`B`$-element cannot centralize the self-centralizing Fitting subgroup $`N`$ of $`D`$. But $`t`$ actually swaps the two distinct $`q`$-axes. Contradiction. Hence $`m=2d`$, $`L`$ is Lagrangian and each $`t`$-eigenspace has dimension $`d`$. The two eigenspaces are orthogonal by $`t`$-invariance of $`\omega`$ and are nondegenerate since their sum is all of $`U`$. In particular $`d`$ is even.

For the asserted transversality consider the conjugacy-invariant taboo set $`(Tt)^2`$, containing $`ab^{-1}`$ for any two $`t`$-inverted elements of $`N`$. Every $`u`$ in the negative eigenspace $`E_-`$ has an inverted lift: choose any lift $`v`$, then an appropriate power of $`v^{-1}t(v)`$ projects to $`u`$ (multiplication by $`-2`$ is invertible). This lift is unique, since distinct central lifts differ by a $`t`$-fixed scalar of odd order. In the class-two exponent-$`p`$ group one may use additive Heisenberg coordinates $`(u,c)`$ with product central term $`\omega(u,v)/2`$ and linear $`t`$ action: the abelian addition $`xy[x,y]^{-1/2}`$ on $`N`$ is elementary abelian and $`t`$-equivariant, and an invariant linear splitting over $`Z`$ exists by averaging over $`t`$. Here $`\omega`$ uses the convention $`xy=yx[x,y]`$. Thus inverted lifts are exactly $`(u,0)`$ for $`u\in E_-`$. At a prescribed nonzero $`u\in E_-`$ their two-fold differences range over the **entire** central fiber above $`u`$, since the scalar as the decomposition varies contains $`\omega(u,E_-)/2=\mathbb F_p`$. Avoidance by $`H`$ therefore gives $`L\cap E_-=0`$. Projecting the Lagrangian $`L`$ along $`E_-`$ makes it the graph of an anti-isometry $`E_+\to E_-`$ between two nondegenerate spaces of equal dimension; in particular $`L\cap E_+=0`$. Any vector in $`L\cap tL`$ has both $`t`$-eigenprojections in $`L`$, so is zero. This proves $`U=L\oplus tL`$. The odd-$`f`$ uniqueness conclusion uses only necessity, together with the separate previously proven standard axial endpoint; it asserts nothing about the number of even-$`f`$ endpoints.

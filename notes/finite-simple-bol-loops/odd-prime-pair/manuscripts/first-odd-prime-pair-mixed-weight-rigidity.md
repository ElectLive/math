# Mixed-weight rigidity at the first odd two-prime degree

**Research proof; see the scope of verification.** [Verification and provenance](../VERIFICATION.md).

Let $`p,q`$ be distinct odd primes with $`p\mid q-1`$. Set $`f=\operatorname{ord}_q(p)`$, assume $`p\nmid f`$, and put $`d=pf`$. At order $`p^{1+d}q`$ the cited predecessor leaves, besides its unique axial class, a possible nonaxial nonabelian $`p`$-core $`N`$ in the faithful canonical left group $`D`$, with central $`\Phi(N)=[N,N]\cong C_p`$.

**Theorem.** In that remaining case $`C_N(B)=\Phi(N)`$ for a $`t`$-stable Sylow $`q`$-group $`B`$, the core has exponent $`p`$, and $`|N|=p^{2d+1}`$. If $`f`$ is odd, the remaining case is impossible. Thus for odd $`f`$ prime to $`p`$ the cited construction gives exactly one first-degree loop isomorphism class, under the stated inputs. For even $`f`$ the commutator form on $`N/\Phi(N)`$ has rank either $`d`$ or $`2d`$; this is not a classification in even $`f`$.

## 1. The physical Sylow cut removes the entire fixed weight

Use the notation and exact physical identities of the predecessor: $`W=H\cap N`$, $`U=N/\Phi`$, $`D/N\cong (C_q\times C_q)\rtimes C_p`$, with reciprocal order-$`p`$ action on the two axes, and $`t`$ exchanging them and inverting the top. The absence of an axial weight gives $`C_U(B_2)=C_U(B)`$ for the axis $`B_2`$ met by the original stabilizer, and equation (3) of the predecessor gives $`C_U(B)\subseteq\bar W`$. Also $`W\cap\Phi=1`$, $`\dim(U/\bar W)=d`$, and the number of inverted elements of $`N`$ is $`p^d`$. As there, conjugate $`H`$ by $`N`$ when necessary to contain $`B_2`$; the following arguments only use the sizes of intersections with all conjugate point stabilizers, not a change of marked isomorphism data.

Write $`C=C_N(B)`$ and $`L=N_D(B)`$. The inherited Frattini and centralizer arguments give $`N_D(B_2)=L`$, $`D=NL`$, and $`H=W N_H(B_2)`$, so $`H\cap L`$ covers the top $`C_p`$ of $`D/NB`$. Coprime fixed-point lifting gives $`C_W(B_2)`$ mapping onto $`C_U(B_2)`$ and gives $`C_N(B_2)=C_N(B)`$ because their images in $`U`$ coincide and both contain $`\Phi`$. Hence $`C=(W\cap C)\Phi`$ and $`[C:W\cap C]=p`$. As $`W\cap\Phi=1`$ and $`N/\Phi`$ is abelian, $`W\cap C`$ is abelian; $`C`$ is abelian too.

Take a $`t`$-stable Sylow $`p`$-subgroup $`S`$ of the odd group $`L`$ (the involution acts on an odd set of Sylows). It contains $`C`$, and $`S/C\cong C_p`$. A Sylow $`p`$-subgroup $`H_0`$ of $`H\cap L`$ has order $`|C|`$, so after conjugation in $`L`$ it lies in $`S`$ with index $`p`$. Every inverted element of $`S`$ belongs to the actual sharply transitive mark set $`T`$. Its elements have distinct images of the point whose stabilizer contains this conjugate of $`H_0`$. Equivalently they occupy distinct cosets modulo that index-$`p`$ subgroup of $`S`$, so there are at most $`p`$ of them. For an involutory automorphism of an odd group the inverted set has size the fixed-subgroup index: the map $`x\mapsto x^{-1}t(x)`$ has precisely that many images, and every inverted element occurs by its unique square root. Fixed points lift under a coprime automorphism on invariant quotients. Thus the fixed index in $`S`$ equals the fixed index in $`C`$ times the fixed index in $`S/C`$; the latter is $`p`$. This proves that $`t`$ fixes $`C`$ pointwise.

Conjugation by a generator of $`S/C`$ induces on abelian $`C`$ the same automorphism as conjugation by its image under $`t`$, but the latter induces the inverse automorphism. The automorphism has odd order, so it is the identity. Thus $`S`$ is abelian, and its plus part under $`t`$ is precisely $`C`$ (the top is inverted).

Let $`M=[N,B]`$. The image of $`M`$ in $`U`$ is $`[U,B]`$, disjoint from the image $`C_U(B)`$ of $`C`$. Hence $`C\cap M\subseteq\Phi`$. In fact $`C\cap M=\Phi`$: coprime action gives $`N=MC`$, and if $`M\cap\Phi=1`$, both $`[M,M]`$ and $`[M,C]`$ lie in $`M\cap\Phi`$ while $`[C,C]=1`$, contrary to $`[N,N]=\Phi\ne1`$. The signed quotient $`D/(MB)`$ is therefore $`S/\Phi`$: $`S`$ maps onto it via $`D=NL`$, $`N=MC`$, and $`L=BS`$, with kernel $`S\cap MB=C\cap M`$. The quotient is abelian and generated by inverted images of $`T`$, so its entire plus part must be trivial. That plus part is $`C/\Phi`$. This proves $`C=\Phi`$ and $`C_U(B)=0`$.

The $`p`$-power map $`U\to\Phi`$ is linear, because the group is class two, $`p`$ is odd, and its commutators have order dividing $`p`$. It is $`B`$-invariant because $`\Phi`$ is central in $`D`$. Since $`[U,B]=U`$, the map vanishes. Thus the core has exponent $`p`$.

## 2. Mixed packets and an actual center orbit

Over a splitting field, each nonzero weight of $`B`$ on $`U`$ has both coordinates nonzero: there are neither trivial nor axial weights. For $`\alpha`$ of order $`p`$ in $`\mathbb F_q^\times`$, the joint orbit of a weight $`(a,b)`$ under the top and prime-field Frobenius is
```math
\{(\alpha^i p^j a,\alpha^{-i}p^j b):0\le i<p,\ 0\le j<f\}.
```
Every orbit has size $`d`$: equality of weights forces equality of the ratios and hence of $`i`$ since $`\alpha^2`$ still has order $`p`$, and then equality of $`j`$. Multiplicities are constant on an orbit. Choose the two axes so that $`t`$ swaps weight coordinates. Within each orbit at most $`f`$ individual weights are fixed by that swap, since equality of the coordinates determines $`i`$ uniquely if it has a solution. Each exchanged pair of weight spaces contributes half its total dimension to the negative eigenspace. Therefore in any signed invariant subquotient $`Y`$ of $`U`$,
```math
d\mid\dim Y,\qquad \dim Y^-\ge \frac{p-1}{2p}\dim Y.
```
This is an estimate of eigenspace dimensions, not an integer trace inference from characteristic $`p`$.

Coprime fixed-point lifting and $`t`$ fixing $`\Phi`$ give $`\dim U^-=d`$. Thus $`\dim U\le 2dp/(p-1)`$. Since any nonzero packet has some exchanged weights, its contribution to the positive eigenspace is nonzero. Therefore $`\dim U=d`$ cannot occur. For $`p\ge5`$ only $`\dim U=2d`$ remains; for $`p=3`$ the bound still permits $`3d`$.

Let $`R`$ be the radical of the nonzero alternating commutator form $`U\times U\to\Phi`$, and $`r=\dim(U/R)`$ its rank. This form is invariant under $`D,t`$ (their action on $`\Phi`$ is trivial), and $`R`$ is the image of the actual characteristic subgroup $`Z(N)`$. The inherited stabilizer $`\bar W`$ is totally isotropic, since its representatives in $`W`$ have commutators in $`W\cap\Phi=1`$. Its projection to $`U/R`$ has dimension at most $`r/2`$. Thus
```math
[Z(N):H\cap Z(N)]\le p^{1+d-r/2}.
```
Indeed $`\dim(\bar W\cap R)\ge \dim U-d-r/2`$, this is the dimension of $`W\cap Z(N)`$, and $`|Z(N)|=p^{1+\dim U-r}`$. The inverted elements of $`Z(N)`$ are marks, and so cannot outnumber that orbit of the original basepoint. Coprime lifting and $`t`$ fixing $`\Phi`$ express their number as $`p^{\dim R^-}`$.

If $`p=3`$ and $`\dim U=3d`$, the mixed-packet bound on $`R`$ now implies $`(3d-r)/3\le1+d-r/2`$, or $`r\le6`$. But $`U/R`$ has nonzero dimension divisible by $`d`$, and $`d=3f\ge9`$ (the multiplicative order cannot be 1 or 2 since $`q>p`$ is odd). This contradiction removes $`3d`$. Consequently $`\dim U=2d`$ in all remaining cases. The possible ranks are $`d`$ and $`2d`$.

## 3. Odd order of the Frobenius multiplier closes the alternative

If $`f`$ is odd, no mixed packet contains the negative of one of its weights. Indeed that would force $`\alpha^i=1`$ by the ratio, and then $`p^j=-1`$ in the odd-order group generated by $`p\bmod q`$. An invariant nondegenerate bilinear form pairs each weight space with its opposite, so the nondegenerate quotient $`U/R`$ contains both full packets of any of its weights. Hence $`r\ge2d=\dim U`$, so the form is symplectic on all of $`U`$. The positive and negative eigenspaces of a symplectic involution in odd characteristic are mutually orthogonal and each is nondegenerate: orthogonality follows from the product of opposite eigenvalues, and a vector orthogonal to its eigenspace is then orthogonal to all of $`U`$. Both eigenspaces therefore have even dimension, contradicting $`\dim U^-=d=pf`$ odd.

This excludes the nonaxial case for odd $`f`$, but not the possibilities in even $`f`$. The uniqueness conclusion imports the predecessor's axial classification and the separately cited construction, rather than re-proving either of them.

## Finite arithmetic control and review boundary

An author-side exact modular replay enumerated all 900 weights for $`(p,q,f)=(5,31,3)`$ and 500 seeded weights each for $`(3,1093,7),(7,2801,5),(11,2069,47),(17,307,3)`$; it checked packet sizes, swap fixed counts, and disjoint opposite packets. At $`(3,61,10),(5,601,12),(7,43,6)`$, 502 mixed weights each (including equal and opposite coordinate controls) instead have opposite weights in the same packet, as they should. These 4,406 packet checks do not certify the normalizer or center arguments and do not count actual loops. No claim of historical novelty or independent acceptance is made.

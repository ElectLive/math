# Third-degree order-p-squared tops with fixed Frattini

**Supporting proof draft; no separate end-to-end review asserted.** [Verification and provenance](../VERIFICATION.md).

**Theorem.** Let $`p,q`$ be distinct odd primes with $`p\mid q-1`$, write $`f=\operatorname{ord}_q(p)`$, and suppose $`p^2\mid f`$. There is no finite simple proper left Bol loop of order $`p^{f+3}q`$ whose canonical cyclic top has order $`p^2`$ and whose sign fixes its canonical $`p`$-core's Frattini, if $`p\ge5`$. At $`p=3`$ the same hypotheses force an elementary abelian core, two axial $`f`$-modules and a two-dimensional fixed-for-$`B`$ Jordan block. The original stabilizer's lift of a top generator must have the nonzero fixed-line phase specified below.

Use the actual group $`D`$, sign $`t`$, stabilizer $`H`$, core $`N`$, Frattini $`F`$, quotient $`U=N/F`$, and $`W=H\cap N`$. The sign budget on $`N`$ is $`s=f+1`$ because $`a=f+3`$ and the top exponent is $`k=2`$. The coprime group $`B=B_1B_2`$ acts nontrivially on $`U`$. A mixed packet of joint top and field Frobenius has size $`p^2 f`$ and uses at least $`f(p^2-1)/2>f+1`$ negative dimensions in its signed orbit. Thus only two paired axial packets occur, each of dimension $`f`$ (since $`p^2\mid f`$); no second pair or extra multiplicity fits. They give $`A_1,A_2`$, irreducible under their full axis and top, interchanged by sign. The remaining $`U^B`$ has negative dimension one because the sign fixes $`F`$.

The fixed $`F`$ is central in the signed group by generation by sign conjugates, so $`H\cap F=1`$. The actual $`\bar W=WF/F`$ is isotropic for every scalar commutator form. Distinct axes and the fixed part cannot pair with an axial module by $`B`$-invariance. A nonzero scalar form on either axial module would be nondegenerate there by irreducibility and on the other by the sign. The original stabilizer projection to $`A_2`$ is either the whole module or zero, by irreducibility and coprime averaging. In the former case it contains $`A_2`$, violating isotropy. In the latter, $`\dim(\bar W\cap A_1)\ge f+e-3>f/2`$, with $`e=\log_p|F|`$ and $`f\ge p^2\ge9`$, also violating isotropy. Hence the moving part centralizes $`N`$. Coprime action gives $`N=M\times C`$ with $`M=[N,B]`$ elementary lifting $`A_1\oplus A_2`$ and $`C=C_N(B)`$. The actual $`W`$ surjects onto $`N/M=C`$ by the $`p`$-group quotient normal-generation argument; thus $`\Phi(W)`$ maps onto $`\Phi(C)=F`$ while $`\Phi(W)\subseteq W\cap F=1`$. Consequently $`N`$ is elementary.

Let $`L=N_D(B)`$ and $`S`$ a signed Sylow $`p`$-group of $`L`$. Then $`S/C=C_{p^2}`$, and $`S\cong D/(MB)`$ is generated by inverted marks. Choose an inverted lift $`h`$ of a generator of this cyclic top; let $`\alpha`$ be conjugation by $`h`$ on $`C`$. Its order divides $`p^2`$. The $`\alpha`$-span of the one-dimensional $`C^-`$ is all $`C`$: quotienting by that span makes the residual $`C`$ sign-fixed, so the action of the cyclic generator equals its inverse and is trivial; abelian generation by inverted elements then kills that residual $`C`$. Thus $`C`$ is one cyclic Jordan block and the consecutive signs alternate from minus. As its negative dimension is one, $`\dim C=1`$ or $`2`$. Dimension one would make $`C`$ a signed-normal inverted central line disjoint from $`H`$, giving a forbidden physical order-$`p`$ normal cut. Therefore $`\dim C=2`$, $`J=\alpha-1`$ has $`J^2=0`$ but $`J\ne0`$, and its fixed line $`Z`$ is sign-positive. The power $`h^{p^2}\in C`$ lies in $`Z`$ (it commutes with $`h`$), yet is inverted, so it is trivial. Thus $`S=C\rtimes\langle h\rangle`$ with $`|h|=p^2`$.

Now $`\dim W=f-1`$. Arrange $`B_2\subset H`$ by coprime conjugacy and use the actual physical stabilizer throughout; such a change preserves the taboo tests. The moving projection to $`A_2`$ is zero since $`\dim W<f`$, so $`W\le A_1\oplus C`$. Its intersection with $`C`$ is $`\alpha`$-invariant because $`H`$ covers the top; any nonzero invariant subspace of the two-dimensional Jordan block contains $`Z`$, central in $`D`$ and disjoint from $`H`$. Thus $`W`$ is a graph over its projection $`K`$ to $`A_1`$. The space $`K`$ is a top-invariant hyperplane, and its graph map $`\eta:K\to C`$ is equivariant for the actual top because $`N`$ is abelian and $`B_2`$ acts trivially on both components.

Use a sign-fixed diagonal shift to normalize $`HN/N`$ to $`B_2\rtimes\langle hN\rangle`$. A physical lift of $`h`$ in $`N_H(B_2)`$ exists by $`H=W N_H(B_2)`$; discarding a $`B_2`$-factor yields a lift $`(x,ch)`$ in $`A_1\rtimes S`$ since its moving part centralizes $`B_2`$. Put $`R`$ for the action of $`h`$ on $`A_1`$. Then $`(R-1)A_1\subset K`$ by unipotence on the one-dimensional quotient. The actual lift's $`p`$th power is
```math
((R-1)^{p-1}x,\; h^p),
```

because the $`C`$ norm is $`J^{p-1}c=0`$. The vector lies in $`K`$. After canceling it by a genuine element of $`W`$, the stabilizer contains the pure signed-upper-group element
```math
(-\eta((R-1)^{p-1}x))\,h^p,\qquad
\eta((R-1)^{p-1}x)=J^{p-2}\eta((R-1)x).
```

For $`p\ge5`$ the displayed phase vanishes since $`J^2=0`$. Then $`h^p\ne1`$ is a mark in the stabilizer, impossible. For $`p=3`$ the phase lies in $`Z`$, and it must be nonzero or the same contradiction applies. A linear control explains why this proof cannot simply set the phase to zero: with a length-nine unipotent Jordan chain $`e_8,\ldots,e_0`$ and hyperplane $`K=\langle e_7,\ldots,e_0\rangle`$, an equivariant graph map can send $`e_7\mapsto v`$, $`e_6\mapsto Jv`$ and the lower vectors to zero. For $`x=e_8`$ the phase is $`Jv\ne0`$. This checks only the linear residual, not existence, full transversal avoidance or physical simplicity.

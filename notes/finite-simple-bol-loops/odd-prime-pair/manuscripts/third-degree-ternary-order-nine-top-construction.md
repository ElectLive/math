# An actual third-degree ternary loop with cyclic top of order nine

**Research proof; see the scope of verification.** [Verification and provenance](../VERIFICATION.md).

**Theorem.** Let $`q`$ be an odd prime with $`9\mid f=\mathrm{ord}_q(3)`$. There is a finite simple proper left Bol loop of order $`3^{f+3}q`$ with canonical cyclic top of order nine and elementary abelian canonical $`3`$-core. In particular $`q=19`$, $`f=18`$ gives order $`3^{21}\cdot19`$. This is exactly two exponents above the minimum $`f+1`$, after the independently audited absence at $`f+2`$.

## 1. An actual marked group and the physical stabilizer

Take $`E=\mathbb F_{3^f}`$, a primitive $`q`$th root $`\zeta\in E`$, and $`R:E\to E`$ given by $`R(x)=x^{3^{f/9}}`$. This has order nine, and its action on $`\zeta`$ raises it to $`\alpha=3^{f/9}\bmod q`$ of order nine. Put $`A_1=A_2=(E,+)`$, $`M=A_1\oplus A_2`$, and $`B=B_1\times B_2\cong C_q^2`$. The two axes act by $`\zeta`$-multiplication on their own coordinates alone. A rotation $`h`$ of order nine acts on $`M`$ by $`(R,R^{-1})`$ and on $`B`$ by $`(\alpha,\alpha^{-1})`$.

Let $`C`$ be elementary abelian with basis $`v,w`$ over $`\mathbb F_3`$ and $`J(v)=w,\ J(w)=0`$. Define $`S=C\rtimes\langle h\rangle`$ with $`h`$ acting on $`C`$ by $`1+J`$. In $`D=M\rtimes(B\rtimes S)`$ let $`C`$ act trivially on both $`M`$ and $`B`$. The sign $`t`$ exchanges $`A_1,A_2`$ and $`B_1,B_2`$, inverts $`h`$, and sends $`v\mapsto -v,\ w\mapsto w`$. These prescriptions define an automorphism of order two because they invert each rotation action. Put $`N=M\times C`$. The quotient $`D/N=B\rtimes\langle hN\rangle`$ has no normal $`3`$-subgroup, since a normal $`3`$-subgroup centralizes normal $`B`$ and the top acts faithfully on it. Therefore $`N=O_3(D)`$.

Over the fixed field of $`R`$, the normal basis theorem gives one permutation cycle of length nine, hence one nilpotent Jordan block of length nine for $`R-1`$ in characteristic three. Over $`\mathbb F_3`$ this yields a direct sum of such blocks, by choosing a prime-field basis of the fixed field. Choose a chain $`e_8,\ldots,e_0`$ on $`A_1`$ satisfying $`(R-1)e_i=e_{i-1}`$ for $`i\ge1`$. Let $`K`$ be the hyperplane spanned by $`e_7,\ldots,e_0`$ and the other blocks, and put $`x=e_8`$. Define $`\eta:K\to C`$ by $`e_7\mapsto v`$, $`e_6\mapsto w`$, all other chain basis vectors to zero. This is onto and equivariant for $`R`$ and $`1+J`$.

In the actual elementary core $`N`$ take $`W=\{(y,0,\eta(y)):y\in K\}`$. Let $`H`$ be generated by $`W`$, $`B_2`$, and the true lift $`(x,h)`$, with $`x`$ in $`A_1`$. The lift normalizes $`W`$ by equivariance and normalizes $`B_2`$, which centralizes $`W`$. Its ninth power is $`((R-1)^8x,1)=(e_0,1)\in W`$. Therefore $`H\cap N=W`$, its image in $`D/N`$ is $`B_2\rtimes C_9`$, and
```math
|H|=3^{f+1}q,\qquad |D|=3^{2f+4}q^2,\qquad [D:H]=3^{f+3}q.
```

The image of $`H`$ in $`D/(MB)=S`$ is all of $`S`$: $`\eta(K)=C`$ and the lift supplies $`h`$.

## 2. All pair products of signs avoid the original stabilizer

Let $`T=\{u\in D:t(u)=u^{-1}\}`$. By odd order, $`Tt`$ is the unique outside involution class in the signed group. Its size is $`[D:C_D(t)]=3^{f+3}q`$ by the sign fibers: $`f`$ negative dimensions on $`M`$, one on $`C`$, top size nine, and one $`q`$ factor from the swapped axes. We show $`(Tt)^2\cap H=\{1\}`$.

Project a proposed collision to $`X=D/N`$. The product of two outside involutions is inverted by either outside involution in that pair. A nonidentity pure element of $`B_2`$ is never so inverted, since the signed involutions interchange the two axes and inner conjugation in $`X`$ preserves each. If the projected product is identity, the two projected signs coincide; their moving contributions on $`M`$ sum in the negative space of that same sign. That negative space is a graph between $`A_1`$ and $`A_2`$ (the sign exchanges the two), so a sum with zero $`A_2`$ coordinate is zero. But an element of $`H`$ over identity in $`X`$ is in $`W`$, determined by its $`A_1`$ coordinate. Thus that collision is identity.

The remaining projected $`X`$ product is $`b_2h^j`$ with $`j\not\equiv0\pmod9`$. Conjugate by a genuine element of $`B_2\le H`$ to make it $`h^j`$; the specified top power acts fixed-point-freely on $`B_2`$. Both projected signs that invert $`h^j`$ must now have zero $`B`$-shift, because $`h^j`$ acts fixed-point-freely on all of $`B`$. They are $`h^i t,h^{i-j}t`$. In the abelian semidirect extension by $`M`$, a lift of a sign has its moving part in that sign's negative space. The second moving contribution after multiplication by the first sign lies in the negative space of $`h^{i+j}t`$, since conjugating the second sign by the first changes its rotation exponent to $`i+j`$. These spaces are the graphs $`(u,-R^{-i}u)`$ and $`(u',-R^{-i-j}u')`$ respectively. If the total $`A_2`$ coordinate is zero then $`u'=-R^ju`$, so its $`A_1`$ coordinate lies in $`(R^j-1)A_1`$. Here we used only the actual upper projection, so it applies even if the signs have additional $`C`$ coordinates.

After the same $`B_2`$ conjugation, an element of $`H`$ above the pure top power $`j`$ can be written as
```math
(y+\Sigma_j x,\ \eta(y)h^j),\qquad
y\in K,\quad \Sigma_j=1+R+\cdots+R^{j-1}.
```

It has zero $`A_2`$ coordinate. If $`j\not\equiv0\pmod3`$, its first coordinate is nonzero modulo $`K`$ (it is $`jx`$ there), whereas $`(R^j-1)A_1\subset K`$. This excludes all such $`j`$.

If $`j=3`$ or $`6`$, write $`j=3r`$ for $`r=1`$ or $`2`$. We have $`(R^j-1)A_1=(R-1)^3A_1`$, since after factoring $`(R-1)^3`$ the residual polynomial has nonzero constant term in $`R-1`$. And $`\eta`$ kills $`(R-1)^3 A_1`$ by its definition (this lies in $`K`$). A putative collision must therefore satisfy
```math
\eta(y)=-\eta(\Sigma_{3r}x)=-r w,
```

using $`\Sigma_{3r}=(R-1)^2\sum_{\ell=0}^{r-1}R^{3\ell}`$ and the chosen chain. Its $`S`$ projection is $`(-rw)h^{3r}`$, which is impossible for a product of two outside signs in the signed $`S`$ itself: an inverted element of $`S`$ with top $`i`$ has $`C`$ coordinate $`av+(ia/2)w`$ (apply $`t(c)=- (1+J)^{-i}c`$). In a product of two inverted elements of total top $`j`$, the resulting $`C`$ coordinate is
```math
(a+b)v+\bigl(i(a+b)+jb\bigr)w/2.
```

If $`3\mid j`$ and the $`v`$ coordinate is zero, this $`w`$ coordinate is zero. Products of two outside signs give exactly such products ($`T_S`$ is inverse-closed). The contradiction excludes $`j=3,6`$ and proves full avoidance.

The set $`T`$ is inverse- and sandwich-closed, and $`(Tt)^2`$ is conjugation invariant. Avoidance now gives at most one element of $`T`$ in each coset of each conjugate of $`H`$, hence exactly one by the count. These are the actual sharply transitive marks of a left Bol folder, not only a transversal at one point.

## 3. Generation, faithfulness and every signed normal cut

The image of $`T`$ in $`D/M`$ generates it: $`h`$ and $`v`$ are inverted and generate $`S`$ by their commutator $`w`$, and the anti-diagonal of $`B`$, together with its conjugate by $`h`$, generates $`B`$ since the two eigenvalues $`\alpha,\alpha^{-1}`$ are distinct. The subgroup generated by $`T`$ is normal in $`D`$ because it is generated by the conjugacy-invariant $`(Tt)^2`$ (which includes $`T`$). Therefore its commutators with $`M`$ include all $`M`$ from the two axes, and $`T`$ generates $`D`$. In particular no larger ambient group than the actual left multiplication group is invoked after faithfulness.

The core of $`H`$ is trivial. Any nontrivial $`D`$-normal subgroup of $`N`$ lying in $`W`$ would contain either a nonzero subspace of $`C`$ or a whole $`A_i`$: the $`B`$-action is coprime and the two rational axis modules are distinct and irreducible, each by the primitive-root degree $`f`$. But $`W\cap C=0`$, it has no $`A_2`$ component, and its image in $`A_1`$ is a proper hyperplane. If a normal subgroup intersects $`N`$ trivially it centralizes $`N`$; however $`C_D(N)=N`$, because the action of $`B\rtimes C_9`$ on $`M`$ is faithful (a nontrivial power of the Frobenius automorphism is not scalar multiplication, as evaluating at $`1`$ confirms). Thus no nontrivial normal subgroup can lie in $`H`$. The loop's true canonical left group is $`D`$.

Let $`I`$ be any nontrivial proper signed-normal subgroup of $`D`$. If $`I\le N`$, the rational coprime module decomposition and exchange under $`t`$ give either $`I\le C`$, or $`I=M\oplus C_0`$ for a signed rotation-invariant $`C_0\le C`$. In the former case $`I`$ is either the fixed positive line or all of $`C`$, and $`W\cap I=1`$ while the sign fiber is at most three, strictly below the orbit size. In the latter case, $`\eta`$ is onto $`C`$, so $`\dim(W\cap I)=f-3+\dim C_0`$. The orbit is therefore $`3^{f+3}`$, while its sign fiber is at most $`3^{f+1}`$. Both are actual strict cuts.

If the image of $`I`$ in $`X`$ is nontrivial, it contains $`B`$: inside $`B`$, a nonzero signed invariant subspace contains both axes by the distinct top eigenvalues and their exchange; a nonzero top image already forces both axes by commutators with $`B`$. Then $`I\cap NB`$ maps onto $`B`$, its Sylow $`q`$-subgroup does too, and Sylow conjugacy in $`NB`$ plus normality of $`I`$ gives the actual $`B\le I`$. So $`M=[N,B]\le I`$ as well. Since $`H`$ maps onto $`D/(MB)=S`$, the projected stabilizer now has index one. The projected mark set has size greater than one, because it generates the nontrivial group $`D/I`$. This is the remaining strict cut. The reviewed criterion gives simplicity in all cases. The nontrivial point stabilizer excludes associativity; odd finite simple nonassociative Bol loops cannot be Moufang by FKP Proposition 6.11. This proves the stated proper loop construction.

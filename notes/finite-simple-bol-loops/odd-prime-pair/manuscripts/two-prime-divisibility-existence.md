# Exact prime-pair existence when the second prime occurs only once

**Provisional, not independently reviewed.** For distinct odd primes $`p,q`$, there exists a finite simple nonassociative left Bol loop of order $`p^a q`$ for **some** $`a`$ if and only if $`p\mid q-1`$. Write $`f=\operatorname{ord}_q(p)`$. If the divisibility holds, there is a construction at
```math
 a=1+\operatorname{lcm}(p,f).
```
When $`p\mid f`$, this is the **minimum possible exponent for this fixed pair**: $`a_{\min}=f+1`$. Otherwise the stated construction is an upper bound, not asserted optimal. Every loop in the necessary direction has a split two-dimensional $`q`$-layer and a cyclic $`p`$-complement of order dividing $`q-1`$, not the nonsplit torus or a noncyclic 3-group allowed by linear data alone. This extends the [proposed exponent boundary](../../odd-degree/manuscripts/two-prime-exponent-threshold.md) at $`\mathtt{6f180cf5e0ef93a03fbe2849200a54d67d9cf0d4}`$. We do not deduce that larger-exponent loops all come from the displayed construction.

## Eliminate the invisible $`q`$-center, then use the original stabilizer

Recall from that source the signed, odd, generating double $`D`$, $`F=O_p(D)\times C`$ with $`C=O_q(D)`$ central and $`t`$-fixed, and
```math
 X=D/F=V\rtimes P,\qquad V\ \text{elementary abelian over }\mathbb F_q,
```
where $`P`$ is a nontrivial $`p`$-group and $`V`$ is an absolutely irreducible faithful reflection module of dimension 2 or 3 for $`P\rtimes\langle t\rangle`$. The proved prime ordering is $`q>p`$. Take a $`t`$-stable Sylow $`q`$-subgroup $`B`$ of $`D`$ (there are oddly many); $`C\le B`$ and $`B/C\cong V`$. Hence the commutator of $`B`$ is a signed-equivariant alternating bilinear map from $`V\times V`$ to a trivial elementary abelian subgroup of $`C`$: $`C`$ is central, and $`q`$-th powers are central, so commutators have exponent $`q`$. To justify full equivariance despite the choice of $`B`$, its normalizer in the signed group projects onto the signed quotient of $`D`$ by $`F`$. Indeed $`B`$ lies in the normal preimage $`Y`$ of $`V`$, and any conjugate is a Sylow $`q`$-subgroup there, conjugate by an element of $`O_p(D)`$ as $`Y=B O_p(D)`$. The representatives normalizing $`B`$ induce exactly the stated action on $`V`$ and fix $`C`$.

Any nonzero linear functional detecting the commutator image would give a nonzero invariant alternating scalar form on $`V`$. In dimension two the reflection $`t`$ has determinant $`-1`$, so no such form is invariant. In dimension three a nonzero alternating form has a nonzero proper radical; this contradicts irreducibility. Therefore $`B`$ is abelian. For $`c\in C`$, transfer from $`D`$ to the abelian Sylow $`B`$ sends $`c`$ to $`c^{[D:B]}`$, since every coset is fixed by the central $`c`$ and every term in transfer is $`c`$. Consequently $`C\cap D'=1`$. For completeness the transfer homomorphism is the product of the elements $`r_i g r_{\sigma_g(i)}^{-1}`$ in $`B`$, using right coset representatives $`r_i`$ and the permutation induced by $`g`$; multiplicativity is obtained by reindexing in the abelian product. On the other hand $`t`$ inverts all of $`D/D'`$ since $`D`$ is generated by its $`t`$-inverted marks. A fixed odd central $`c`$ must have trivial image in this abelianization. Thus $`C=1`$, and $`F=O_p(D)`$.

The projected original stabilizer $`J=HF/F`$ maps onto $`P=X/V`$. Indeed $`H`$ normally generates $`D`$, since the group quotient by its normal closure is a homomorphic image of the simple nonassociative loop. A subgroup normally generating a finite $`p`$-group must be the whole group, as every proper subgroup lies in a normal maximal subgroup. Meanwhile $`F`$ is a $`p`$-group, so $`[X:J]`$ has $`q`$-part exactly $`q`$. Thus $`J\cap V`$ is a $`P`$-invariant hyperplane in $`V`$. Its annihilator is an actual $`\mathbb F_q`$-line in $`V^*`$. Together with its $`t`$-translate it spans a $`P,t`$-stable subspace: $`t`$ normalizes $`P`$. Irreducibility forces $`\dim V=2`$ and the two distinct rational eigenlines span. Odd rotations have determinant one on this reflection representation. Thus faithful $`P`$ is cyclic diagonal of order $`p^k\mid q-1`$, its two eigencharacters faithful and reciprocal, and $`t`$ inverts it. In particular $`p\mid q-1`$.

The character budget argument of the preceding source now applies in every characteristic $`p`$, including $`p=3`$: the free cyclic $`P`$-orbits have size $`p^k`$, all nonzero Frobenius cycles have size $`f\ge3`$, and
```math
 a\ge k+\max\{p^k,f\}\ge f+1.
```
Here $`\min(p^k,f(p^k-1)/2)=p^k`$ also at $`p^k=3`$.

## An honest construction for every $`p\mid q-1`$

Choose $`\alpha\in\mathbb F_q^\times`$ of order $`p`$, let $`S=\langle p\bmod q,\alpha\rangle\le\mathbb F_q^\times`$, $`d=|S|=\operatorname{lcm}(p,f)`$, and take a primitive $`q`$-th root $`\zeta\in\mathbb F_{p^f}`$. On basis vectors $`e_s`$, $`s\in S`$, over $`\mathbb F_{p^f}`$, define
```math
 b e_s=\zeta^s e_s,\qquad h e_s=e_{\alpha^{-1}s},\qquad
 \mathcal F(c e_s)=c^p e_{ps}.
```
Both operators commute with the semilinear $`\mathcal F`$; $`hbh^{-1}=b^\alpha`$. The fixed space $`U`$ of $`\mathcal F`$ is defined over $`\mathbb F_p`$ and has dimension $`d`$: each Frobenius cycle of length $`f`$ contributes one free coordinate in $`\mathbb F_{p^f}`$, giving $`f`$ prime-field dimensions and spanning that cycle after scalar extension (finite-field descent, or the independent field embeddings). It is an irreducible faithful module for $`B=\langle b,h\rangle=C_q\rtimes C_p`$. Indeed $`b`$'s eigenvalues are all distinct; a rational invariant submodule's weight support is stable under both multipliers $`\alpha,p`$, transitive on $`S`$. No nonidentity $`h`$-power acts diagonally, and $`b`$ acts faithfully. In particular $`b-1`$ is invertible on $`U`$. This argument does not assume that $`U`$ stays irreducible on $`C_q`$ alone.

Put $`Z=U\rtimes B`$ and $`Y_1=B`$. Choose a hyperplane $`W`$ containing $`(h-1)U`$, possible since $`h`$ is a $`p`$-element in characteristic $`p`$, and $`u\notin W`$. Set $`Y_0=\langle W,(u,h)\rangle`$; $`W`$ is $`h`$-stable and the $`p`$-th power of this affine generator lies in $`W`$ since its norm is zero on the trivial $`U/W`$. Then $`|Y_0|=p^d`$, and $`Y_0`$ acts regularly on $`U`$: for $`1\le j<p`$, elements in $`W(u,h)^j`$ translate by $`ju\ne0`$ on $`U/W`$, and nontrivial translations have no fixed point. Therefore $`Z=Y_0Y_1`$, $`Y_0\cap Y_1=1`$, both core-free because any nonzero normal subgroup contains $`U`$ (faithful irreducible affine action). Neither $`Y_i`$ contains $`U`$. Moreover $`Z'=U\rtimes C_q`$, and both $`Y_i`$ map onto $`Z/Z'=C_p`$.

Apply the [exact-factorization folder](../../sources/foundations/C0002.md) to this concrete triple. Its canonical group, stabilizer and marks are
```math
 D_0=\{(x,y)\in Z^2:xy\in Z'\},\qquad
 H_0=D_0\cap(Y_0\times Y_1),\qquad T_0=\{(x,x^{-1}):x\in Z\},
```
with $`t`$ swapping coordinates. The marks give a loop of order $`|Z|=p^{d+1}q`$; this interface includes generation and faithfulness. We now verify simplicity rather than assuming every exact factorization produces a simple loop. Let $`M=U\times U`$; $`C_{D_0}(M)=M`$ by the faithful module. Every nontrivial normal subgroup of $`D_0`$ meets $`M`$. Every intersection that is also $`t`$-stable is all of $`M`$: the two $`C_q`$ axes act fixed-point-freely on their own coordinate and trivially on the other. Commuting an element with nonzero coordinate with its own axis isolates a nonzero element of that coordinate. The full projection of $`D_0`$ on that coordinate acts irreducibly, and $`t`$ exchanges coordinates.

Modulo $`M`$ the group is $`(C_q\times C_q)\rtimes C_p`$ with reciprocal distinct eigenvalues $`\alpha,\alpha^{-1}`$, the stabilizer image is the second $`q`$-axis extended by $`C_p`$, and the signed involution swaps the axes. An invariant normal subgroup inside the two-dimensional $`q`$-space stable under both $`C_p,t`$ is zero or the whole space. A normal subgroup projecting nontrivially to $`C_p`$ contains both axes by commutators, hence is everything. So the only proper nontrivial $`t`$-stable normal cuts of $`D_0`$ are $`M`$ and the preimage of the two axes together. At $`M`$ the projected marked set has $`pq`$ elements (all inverse pairs in $`B`$) versus stabilizer index $`q`$; at the larger cut it has $`p`$ elements versus index 1. Both strict inequalities imply simplicity by the [odd invariant-cut criterion](../../packet/manuscripts/odd-folder-radicals.md#candidate-simplicity-criterion-using-τ-stable-normal-subgroups), independently reviewed at $`\mathtt{65a3e58df517df59656ab948ac9d122208f62c4a}`$. The nontrivial canonical stabilizer gives nonassociativity; an odd simple nonassociative loop is not Moufang by [FKP Proposition 6.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0208231v2).

Thus each admissible prime pair actually occurs. If $`p\mid f`$, then $`d=f`$, so the constructive exponent meets the universal lower bound. For $`p\nmid f`$, the proof only gives $`1+\max(p,f)\le a_{\min}\le1+pf`$. It does not settle whether intermediate exponents work. Neither historical priority nor exhaustive classification of higher orders is claimed.

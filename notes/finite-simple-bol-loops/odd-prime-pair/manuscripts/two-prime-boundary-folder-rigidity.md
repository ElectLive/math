# Rigidity at the first possible two-prime degree

**Provisional exact-source classification; independently unreviewed.** For distinct odd primes $`p,q`$, every finite simple nonassociative left Bol loop of order $`p^{p+1}q`$ is isomorphic to the existing semilinear $`Q_{p,q}`$, and these loops exist exactly when $`\mathrm{ord}_q(p)=p`$. Combined with the [separate tentative minimum theorem](../../odd-degree/manuscripts/least-odd-simple-bol-order.md), this gives a **unique** isomorphism type at the minimum odd order 1053. The construction, its order and its isomorphisms *within the original construction* come from [C0003, Theorem 1.1](../../sources/foundations/C0003.md). The proposed new implication is that **arbitrary** simple left Bol folders at this degree have that form. The [two-prime exponent theorem](../../odd-degree/manuscripts/two-prime-exponent-threshold.md) at $`\mathtt{6f180cf5e0ef93a03fbe2849200a54d67d9cf0d4}`$ is a dependency, not an independent review of this result.

## The actual module at equality

Use the signed doubled folder $`(D,H,T,t)`$ of the two-prime proof, its characteristic cores $`N=O_p(D)`$, $`C=O_q(D)\le Z(D\rtimes\langle t\rangle)`$, $`F=N\times C`$, faithful $`X=D/F`$ on $`W=N/\Phi(N)`$, and its $`X=V\rtimes P`$ with $`V=O_q(X)`$. At equality $`f=\mathrm{ord}_q(p)=p`$, $`P=C_p`$, and $`V=\mathbb F_q^2`$ with faithful reciprocal eigencharacters for $`P`$, interchanged by $`t`$. For $`p=3`$ the preceding source gives $`k=\log_p[P:C_P(t)]=1`$: the three inverted elements generate $`P`$ and any nonidentity one's powers already exhaust them, so indeed $`P=C_3`$. Extend scalars from $`\mathbb F_p`$ for the $`V`$-weight calculation. We have
```math
r=\dim W^-(t)=s=\log_p[N:C_N(t)]=p .
```
For $`p\ge5`$ this is proved in the boundary argument. At $`p=3`$ it follows from $`r\ge f=3`$ and $`s+k=4`$.

A nonzero $`P`$-orbit of $`V`$-weights has size $`p`$. If $`t`$ exchanges two such orbits their union has $`2p`$ weights and consumes all of $`r`$, each with multiplicity one. Frobenius preserves this pair (or another disjoint pair would cost another $`p`$); on the resulting regular dihedral orbit it is a permutation of order $`p`$ commuting with $`P,t`$. In the opposite regular dihedral group it is a nontrivial rotation, preserving each $`P`$-orbit. Choose dual coordinates with generator of $`P`$ acting by $`(x,y)\mapsto(\alpha x,\alpha^{-1}y)`$, $`\alpha`$ of order $`p`$. Frobenius multiplies weights by the residue $`p\bmod q`$. If it sends $`(x,y)`$ to $`(\alpha^i x,\alpha^{-i}y)`$, both coordinates cannot be nonzero: that would give $`p^2\equiv1\bmod q`$. Thus the two orbits sit on the **two different eigenaxes**, exchanged by $`t`$. Each is the Frobenius orbit of one character and gives a rational module of dimension $`p`$.

If a nonzero $`P`$-orbit is $`t`$-stable, its $`p=f`$ distinct Frobenius translates are likewise stable with one fixed weight each. They cost at least $`p(p-1)/2`$ negatives. This is impossible for $`p\ge5`$. For $`p=3`$ they consume all three negatives with multiplicity one; the weights must have both coordinates nonzero since $`t`$ swaps the axes. Their nine values are $`(x\alpha^i,y\alpha^j)`$ for all $`i,j\bmod3`$: Frobenius is scalar multiplication by a generator of $`\langle\alpha\rangle`$, while the rotation multiplies reciprocally, and 2 is invertible modulo 3. In either pattern additional nonzero $`V`$-weights cannot occur: any further $`P,t,\mathrm{Fr}`$-orbit has strictly positive negative cost by the same cases. The $`V`$-trivial space is fixed by $`t`$, hence by the generating conjugates. Write $`W=U\oplus W^V`$ over $`\mathbb F_p`$ for the active and inactive spaces, using coprime averaging on $`V`$. In **both** patterns no two active weights are negatives of one another: on either nonzero coordinate the ratio would be $`-1`$ in the odd cyclic group $`\langle\alpha\rangle`$.

## No hidden central or Frattini layers

Equality $`r=s`$ makes $`t`$ fix $`\Phi(N)`$ pointwise by odd-kernel centralizer lifting. This characteristic subgroup is therefore central in the signed group. Consequently $`N`$ has class at most two, and its commutator defines a bilinear alternating map $`W\times W\to[N,N]`$ to a trivial elementary abelian $`p`$-space: $`x^p\in\Phi(N)`$ gives $`[x,y]^p=1`$. For $`V`$-weight arguments the map is equivariant since the conjugation actions on both source and target factor through $`X`$. A nonzero pairing in the scalar extension needs opposite weights. We just excluded every opposite to an active weight, even among the trivial weights. Thus **every lift into $`N`$ of an element of $`U`$ is central in $`N`$**.

Let $`Y`$ be the preimage of $`V`$ in $`D`$; $`N`$ is normal Sylow $`p`$ there and $`Y/N`$ is a $`q`$-group. Choose a $`t`$-stable Sylow $`q`$-subgroup $`B`$ of $`Y`$ (the number of Sylow subgroups is odd). It contains $`C`$. Put $`A=[N,B]`$. Its image in $`W`$ is $`[W,V]=U`$, so $`A\le Z(N)`$. Coprime action gives $`N=A\,C_N(B)`$: indeed the displacements of any $`x\in N`$ are a $`B`$-cocycle into the central abelian $`p`$-group $`A`$, so coprime averaging gives a translate fixed by $`B`$. It follows that $`A=[A,B]`$ and $`C_A(B)=1`$ by coprime averaging on $`A`$. Thus $`A\cap\Phi(N)=1`$ since $`B`$ centralizes $`\Phi(N)`$. In particular $`A\cong U`$ is elementary abelian and $`N=A\times N_0`$ with $`N_0=C_N(B)`$. All Sylow $`q`$-complements to $`N`$ in $`Y`$ are $`N`$-conjugate. Conjugating $`B`$ by $`N`$ changes neither $`A`$ nor $`N_0`$, both normal in $`N`$, so these two factors are normal in the signed group. All negative mass in $`N`$ occurs on $`A`$; hence $`N_0`$ is $`t`$-fixed and therefore central in the signed group.

Put $`L=N_D(B)`$, a $`t`$-stable subgroup. Frattini's argument and Sylow conjugacy in $`Y=BN`$ give $`D=NL`$. Since $`A\cap L=C_A(B)=1`$ (a normalizing translation's commutators lie in $`A\cap B=1`$), $`D=A\rtimes L`$, with $`N_0\le L`$. The quotient $`L/B`$ is a central extension of $`P=C_p`$ by $`N_0`$, hence abelian. The image of $`T`$ along the $`t`$-equivariant projection $`D\to L\to L/B`$ consists of inverted generators, so $`t`$ inverts this whole abelian quotient. But $`N_0`$ was $`t`$-fixed and embeds there, so $`N_0=1`$.

Now $`B/C=V`$ with $`C`$ fixed central. Its commutator pairing $`V\times V\to C`$ has elementary image by centrality of $`q`$-th powers. The involution $`t`$ has determinant $`-1`$ on the two-dimensional $`V`$, so it negates $`\bigwedge^2 V`$ but fixes the target. Hence the pairing vanishes and $`B`$ is abelian. Choose a $`t`$-stable Sylow $`p`$-subgroup of $`L`$ projecting isomorphically to $`P`$ and use the same letter for it. Coprime action on $`B`$ splits it as $`[B,P]\times C_B(P)`$. The latter is exactly $`C`$ since $`V^P=0`$. These factors are $`t`$-stable; $`C`$ is a fixed direct factor of $`L=([B,P]\rtimes P)\times C`$. As $`L`$ is the image of an inverted generating set, $`C=1`$. We have proved the **actual split group with involution**
```math
 D=A\rtimes(V\rtimes P),\quad t(V)=V,\quad t(P)=P,
```
not just its chief factors. The signs and weights remain those determined above.

## The physical stabilizer and allowed gauges

The actual prime cut at $`A`$ has fiber $`p^p`$; simplicity forces its orbit size strictly greater. Thus $`[A:H\cap A]=p^{p+1}`$, and $`J=HA/A`$ has index $`q`$ in $`V\rtimes P`$. It has order $`pq`$, meets $`V`$ in a $`P`$-invariant line (conjugation by a lift of a generator of $`P`$ gives its actual action on abelian $`V`$), and so that line is one of the two distinct eigenaxes $`V_1,V_2`$. We can use $`t`$ itself as an automorphism preserving the mark set to choose $`V_2`$. Any complement part of $`J`$ is obtained by conjugating the standard $`P`$ by a translation of $`V`$; modulo $`V_2`$ every such translation has a representative fixed by $`t`$ because $`t`$ exchanges the axes. Hence conjugation by a **$`t`$-fixed** element puts $`J=V_2\rtimes P`$ while preserving the marks. Here and below mark preservation follows from $`T`$ being the full $`t`$-inverted set.

Write $`W_H=H\cap A`$. It is $`J`$-invariant since $`A`$ is abelian. In the mixed nine-weight case $`p=3`$, each weight on $`V_2`$ is nontrivial; every nontrivial $`\mathbb F_3 C_q`$-constituent has dimension $`f=3`$. But $`\dim W_H=9-4=5`$, not divisible by three. The mixed case is excluded, without isotopy or unrelated graph assumptions.

In the remaining paired case write $`A=A_1\oplus A_2`$ with $`A_i`$ acted on irreducibly by $`V_i`$ alone. Then $`W_H`$ has dimension $`p-1`$; it must lie in $`A_1`$ since projection onto the irreducible $`V_2`$-module $`A_2`$ cannot have that small positive dimension. Normalize the representation concretely as follows. Use a generator $`h`$ of $`P`$ acting on $`V_1`$ by the residue $`p\bmod q`$, and choose coordinates on $`V_2`$ via $`t`$. On $`A_1=\mathbb F_{p^p}`$ a suitable generator of $`V_1`$ acts by a fixed primitive $`q`$-th root, and $`h`$ acts by a scalar times $`x\mapsto x^p`$. The scalar has norm 1 since $`h^p=1`$; it can be removed by rescaling the field coordinate since the image of $`x\mapsto x^{p-1}`$ is the norm-one subgroup. Transport this coordinate to $`A_2`$ by $`t`$, so $`t`$ swaps the $`A_i`$ and $`h`$ acts on $`A_2`$ by inverse Frobenius. These are all choices up to marked-group isomorphism: $`V_1,V_2`$ have independent generators, with the second dictated by the choice of the first and $`t`$. Frobenius on $`A_1`$ has order $`p`$ and one-dimensional fixed space; on dimension $`p`$ it has a single unipotent Jordan block. Hence $`W_H=(h-1)A_1=\ker\mathrm{Tr}`$ is its unique invariant hyperplane.

Finally $`H/W_H`$ is a section over $`J`$ in the split group $`(A/W_H)\rtimes J`$; here $`A/W_H=\mathbb F_p\oplus A_2`$ with trivial first summand. Its $`A_2`$ cocycle is principal: averaging over normal $`V_2`$ removes the restriction, and the residual cocycle then takes values in $`A_2^{V_2}=0`$. The required translation in $`A_2`$ can be realized by a **$`t`$-fixed** translation in $`A`$ using the matched $`A_1`$-coordinate; this latter coordinate has no effect in the trivial quotient $`A_1/W_H`$. Remaining sections are homomorphisms $`J\to\mathbb F_p`$; they vanish on $`V_2`$ and take $`h`$ to an arbitrary $`\lambda\in\mathbb F_p`$. If $`\lambda=0`$ the actual $`H`$ contains $`h\in P\setminus1`$, an inverted mark, impossible by original transversality. Every nonzero $`\lambda`$ is equivalent under simultaneous nonzero $`\mathbb F_p`$-scalar multiplication on $`A_1,A_2`$, an automorphism centralizing $`t`$. The resulting stabilizer is exactly the one in C0003: $`W_H=\ker\mathrm{Tr}`$ on the first field, the second scalar axis, and the lift of $`h`$ with nonzero trace on the first field. All reductions were marked-group isomorphisms or actual $`t`$-preserving gauges, not arbitrary stabilizer conjugations. Since the loop product is determined by $`ab\in cH`$, they are based loop isomorphisms. Existence and simplicity come from C0003; the deduction here gives necessity and uniqueness.

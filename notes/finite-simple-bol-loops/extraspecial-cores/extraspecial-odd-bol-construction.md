# An explicit family of simple odd Bol loops with extraspecial cores

**Mira Cult**

The construction does not require a classification or a minimal-order theorem. The [signed normal-cut test](signed-cut-test.md) and [independent verification](verification.md) are given separately.

**Theorem.** Let $`p,q`$ be odd primes with $`p\mid q-1`$, and $`f=\operatorname{ord}_q(p)`$. Put $`b=f`$ for even $`f`$ and $`b=2f`$ for odd $`f`$. For every $`m\ge1`$ write $`d=p b m`$. There is a finite simple nonassociative (thus non-Moufang) left Bol loop of order
```math
                         p^{d+1} q
```
whose faithful left multiplication group's $`p`$-core is **extraspecial of exponent $`p`$ and order $`p^{2d+1}`$**. For example, $`(p,q)=(3,61)`$ gives $`f=10`$ and an example of order $`3^{31}\cdot61`$.

## 1. The physical symplectic module and subgroup

Choose $`\alpha\in\mathbb F_q^\times`$ of order $`p`$. We need a symplectic $`\mathbb F_p`$-space $`(E,g)`$ of dimension $`bm`$ with a fixed-point-free order-$`q`$ isometry $`z`$. When $`f`$ is even, take one block $`\mathbb F_{p^f}`$, let $`z`$ be multiplication by a primitive $`q`$-th root $`\zeta`$, and put $`x^*=x^{p^{f/2}}`$. Then $`\zeta^*=\zeta^{-1}`$; for any nonzero $`c`$ with $`c^*=-c`$ use $`g(x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}(cxy^*)`$, with trace to $`\mathbb F_p`$. This is nondegenerate by trace duality and alternating by the star. For odd $`f`$ take a block $`\mathbb F_{p^f}\oplus\mathbb F_{p^f}`$ with its usual trace alternating pairing and reciprocal multipliers $`\zeta,\zeta^{-1}`$. Take the orthogonal sum of $`m`$ blocks in either case. All nonidentity powers of $`z`$ are fixed-point-free.

Set $`A=E^{\mathbb Z/p}`$ of dimension $`d`$, $`U=A\oplus A`$ and
```math
 B(x,y)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z/p}g(x_j,y_{-j}),\qquad
 \omega((x,y),(x',y'))=B(x,y')+B(y,x').
```
Both are nondegenerate alternating forms. On $`A`$ let $`(Rx)_j=x_{j+1}`$. Then $`B(Rx,y)=B(x,Ry)`$, so every power of $`R`$ is self-adjoint for this alternating form. The two copies of $`A`$ are Lagrangian in $`U`$. The actual extraspecial group $`N=U\times\mathbb F_p`$ has law
```math
        (u,c)(v,e)=(u+v,c+e+\omega(u,v)/2).
```
Its center, commutator subgroup and Frattini subgroup are precisely the last line, and its exponent is $`p`$.

Take $`V=\mathbb F_q^2`$, and let $`X=V\rtimes\langle h\rangle`$ with $`h`$ of order $`p`$ acting on $`V`$ by $`(a,b)\mapsto(\alpha a,\alpha^{-1}b)`$. Let $`t`$ have order two, exchanging axes of $`V`$, inverting $`h`$ and exchanging the two copies of $`A`$ in $`U`$. On the first copy the action of $`(a,b)`$ in site $`j`$ is $`z^{\alpha^j a+\alpha^{-j}b}`$; on the second it is $`z^{\alpha^j b+\alpha^{-j}a}`$. On $`U`$ set $`h=(R,R^{-1})`$. These formulas satisfy the stated relations: shifting the first set of sites forward multiplies the first scalar by $`\alpha`$ and the second by $`\alpha^{-1}`$; the reverse shift gives the corresponding swapped formula. The powers acting on $`x_j`$ and $`y'_{-j}`$ in the cross-pairing agree, so $`V`$ acts symplectically; the self-adjointness of $`R`$ and the exchange symmetry give the same for $`h,t`$. They act on $`N`$ with the center fixed. The $`X`$-action on $`U`$ is faithful: a nontrivial $`h`$-component permutes sites; within $`V`$, vanishing of the first-copy exponents at $`j=0,1`$ forces $`a=b=0`$ since $`\alpha\ne\alpha^{-1}`$. The second $`q`$-axis alone has no fixed vectors on $`U`$.

Write $`D=N\rtimes X`$, with signed extension $`D\rtimes\langle t\rangle`$. Let $`V_2=0\times\mathbb F_q`$, let $`w`$ be the central generator $`(0,1)`$ in $`N`$, and fix $`\lambda\in\mathbb F_p^\times`$. Our **actual stabilizer** is
```math
 H=\{((x,0),0):x\in A\}\rtimes\langle V_2,\,w^\lambda h\rangle ,
```
where the notation describes its internal subgroup product, not a quotient-only choice. The indicated $`p`$-th power is one; the center is fixed. Thus every element has form $`((x,0),\lambda j)b_2 h^j`$ with $`x\in A`$, $`b_2\in V_2`$, $`j\bmod p`$. The center meets $`H`$ trivially.

## 2. Count, generate, and test every marked-square collision

Let $`\mathcal C`$ be the conjugacy class of $`t`$ in the signed group and $`T=\mathcal C t`$ in $`D`$. The signed group has odd-order subgroup $`D`$ of index two, so these are all outside involutions, and $`T`$ is exactly the $`t`$-inverted set of $`D`$. We have $`|C_X(t)|=q`$ and $`|C_N(t)|=p^{d+1}`$, whence
```math
          |T|=[D:C_D(t)]=p^{d+1}q=[D:H].
```
The subgroup $`D_0=\langle T\rangle`$ is normal in $`D`$: $`\langle\mathcal C\rangle`$ is normal in the signed group and its even part is generated by products of two members of $`\mathcal C`$, equivalently by $`T`$. Its image contains $`h`$ and the anti-diagonal in $`V`$, whose $`h`$-conjugates span $`V`$ because $`\alpha\ne\alpha^{-1}`$. Thus $`D_0`$ surjects onto $`X`$. The image of $`[N,D_0]`$ on $`U`$ contains $`[U,V]=U`$, whence $`N\subset D_0`$ by the Frattini property. We have proved generation, not only the mark count.

We prove $`H\cap\mathcal C^2=1`$. Each actual involution above a sign $`a\in Xt`$ is of form $`(u,0)a`$ with $`a u=-u`$; indeed the square equation fixes the vector sign and gives $`2c=0`$ in the center since $`\omega(u,-u)=0`$. Only involutory projected signs are relevant. In the product of two such lifts above $`a,b`$, the vector terms to add lie in $`E_a`$ and $`a E_b=E_{aba}`$ respectively, where $`E_s=\ker(s+1)`$, and their central term is half their $`\omega`$-pairing. These are the true physical lift phases.

* If the projection of a stabilizer element is $`b_2\in V_2\setminus\{1\}`$, it cannot be a product of two projected involutions: the first would invert $`b_2`$, but each outside sign takes the second axis onto the first.
* If the projection is the identity, both projected signs are the same $`a`$. The product vector belongs to $`E_a`$. Since every $`a\in Xt`$ exchanges the two Lagrangians while $`X`$ preserves them, $`E_a`$ has zero intersection with $`A\oplus0`$. A possible stabilizer vector must therefore be zero, in which case the two summands are negatives and their central term vanishes.
* Otherwise the projection is $`b_2 h^j`$ with $`j\ne0`$. Conjugating by $`V_2\subset H`$ reduces it to $`h^j`$, without changing membership in $`H`$ or its conjugacy-invariant taboo test. Both projected involutions normalize $`\langle h^j\rangle=\langle h\rangle`$. Its normalizer in $`X\rtimes\langle t\rangle`$ is $`\langle h,t\rangle`$, because $`C_V(h)=0`$. Thus write $`a=h^i t`$, $`b=h^{i-j}t`$, $`aba=h^{i+j}t`$. The negative space of $`h^s t`$ consists of $`(R^s v,-v)`$. For two vectors in $`E_a,E_{aba}`$ to have sum in $`A\oplus0`$, they must therefore be
```math
               (R^i v,-v),\qquad (-R^{i+j} v,v).
```
Their pairing is $`B(R^i v,v)+B(v,R^{i+j}v)=0`$, since $`B`$ is alternating, every $`R^s`$ is self-adjoint, and $`p`$ is odd. Thus **all** marked products with such a stabilizer vector have central phase zero. The actual stabilizer above $`h^j`$ requires central phase $`\lambda j\ne0`$. This handles every projected decomposition and every lift, not just one choice of signs or the zero vector.

The class-square avoidance and exact mark count make $`T`$ a transversal to every conjugate of $`H`$. In detail $`T^{-1}T=\mathcal C^2`$ (since $`t\mathcal C t=\mathcal C`$), so every coset of every conjugate contains at most one mark, hence exactly one. As permutations of $`D/H`$ the marks are sharply transitive. They contain the identity, and $`T`$ is closed under $`uvu`$ since $`t`$ inverts each mark. Assign the unique mark sending $`H`$ to each coset as its left translation: evaluating $`uvu`$ at $`H`$ gives the left Bol identity. The action is faithful: any nontrivial normal subgroup meeting $`N`$ contains its center (a noncentral point in the intersection has a nontrivial central commutator); a normal subgroup disjoint from $`N`$ centralizes it, whereas $`C_D(N)=Z(N)`$ by the faithful linear $`X`$ action. Because $`H\cap Z(N)=1`$, its core is trivial. Thus $`D`$ is genuinely the generated faithful left multiplication group. Finally $`O_p(X)=1`$ since a normal $`p`$-subgroup must centralize $`V`$ and $`C_X(V)=V`$. Hence $`O_p(D)=N`$.

## 3. All signed-normal cuts

Use the [signed normal-cut test](signed-cut-test.md): it suffices that for every proper nontrivial $`t`$-stable normal $`C\triangleleft D`$ its physical orbit size $`[C:C\cap H]`$ exceed its mark fiber $`[C:C_C(t)]`$. Here is the full audit of these cuts.

* If $`1<C\le N`$, normality gives $`Z(N)\subseteq C`$. Let $`Y=C/Z(N)\subset U`$ be its $`t`$-stable subspace. Since $`C`$ is the entire preimage, the physical index is $`p^{1+\dim Y-\dim(Y\cap(A\oplus0))}`$. The mark fiber is $`p^{\dim Y^-}`$ since the center is fixed, and $`(A\oplus0)\cap U^-=0`$. Their dimensions give strict inequality, with the extra central factor $`p`$. No irreducibility, nor any classification of the potentially many subspaces $`Y`$, is assumed.
* Outside $`N`$ the projected nonzero normal subgroup of $`X`$ stable under $`t`$ contains $`V`$: $`h`$ has distinct eigenaxes on $`V`$, swapped by $`t`$, and any nontrivial projection to the prime-order top has commutators spanning $`V`$. Hence $`[C,N]`$ projects onto $`[V,U]=U`$, which gives $`N\le C`$ by the Frattini property. The only proper outside case is the full inverse image of $`V`$. Its physical index is $`p^{d+1}q`$, and its signed mark fiber is $`p^d q`$, again strict.

Thus the native loop is simple. It is nonassociative: the stabilizer in its faithful left multiplication group is nontrivial whereas a group's left translations form a regular group. It is non-Moufang by [Foguel–Kinyon–Phillips, Proposition 6.11, odd-order Moufang solvability](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0208231). The nonabelian $`p`$-core distinguishes these examples up to isotopy from any same-order example with abelian $`p`$-core, since the group of row differences is invariant up to conjugacy under isotopy. No claim is made about the least degree.


## 4. A structural distinction from the faithful exact-factorization family

In this whole family **every nontrivial normal subgroup of $`D`$ contains its central subgroup of order $`p`$**, as proved in the faithfulness argument. In particular $`D`$ has no pair of disjoint nontrivial normal subgroups. This separates every constructed loop, even up to loop isotopy, from all loops given directly by the faithful exact-factorization construction of [G. P. Nagy, *A class of simple proper Bol loops*, Definition 3.1 and Proposition 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0703919). Indeed, for a faithful exact factorization of a group $`Y`$ the marks in $`Y^2`$ are $`s(a)=(a,a^{-1})`$. They generate a group containing $`1\times Y'`$: the elements $`s(a)s(b)s(ab)^{-1}`$ give the elementary commutators in the second coordinate. Coordinate exchange preserves the generated group, so it also contains $`Y'\times1`$. Both are normal there and have trivial intersection. The generated group really is the faithful left multiplication group: the action of $`Y^2`$ on the product of the two coset spaces is faithful by the core-free factors, and the generated subgroup is transitive via its marks. A nonabelian $`Y`$ therefore cannot give our $`D`$; an abelian $`Y`$ gives only a regular, associative loop. This comparison is to that **direct faithful construction**, not to arbitrary quotients of it, to nonfaithful modifications, or to an exhaustive historical literature search.

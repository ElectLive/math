# Axial cotangent blocks and unboundedly many distinct extraspecial Bol loops at one order

**Mira Cult**

The [nonaxial construction](extraspecial-odd-bol-construction.md) makes the two distinguished order-$`q`$ subgroups act without fixed vectors. Here each fixes a substantial subspace. Orthogonal sums give an unbounded number of nonisotopic loops at the same order. The strongest statements below are existence and lower bounds, not minimum orders or exhaustive counts.

Let p,q be odd primes with p|q−1, f=ord_q(p), l=lcm(p,f). Put a=2l and b=p f if f is even, or b=2pf if f is odd. For every pair i,j≥0 with i+j>0 put d=ia+jb. There is a finite simple nonassociative, non-Moufang left Bol loop of order p^(d+1)q whose faithful left multiplication group has extraspecial p-core of order p^(2d+1), exponent p. On its characteristic p-core Frattini quotient, either of the two (unordered) normal q-axes in the quotient of the left multiplication group fixes exactly ia dimensions. Therefore **at d=k lcm(a,b)** there are at least k+1 pairwise nonisotopic such loops, for every k≥1. This is a lower bound on multiplicity, not an exhaustive isomorphism or isotopy classification.

### Exchange construction

Write V=F_q² and X=V:C_p, h acting by (α,α^−1) on the two coordinates, where α has order p. The order-two automorphism θ exchanges axes and sends h to h^−1. Suppose X acts faithfully on a symplectic F_p-space U with [V,U]=U, and an involution t inducing θ acts symplectically, exchanges two complementary Lagrangians L,M, and X preserves each. Denote dim L=dim M=d. The Heisenberg group N=U×F_p has product (u,c)(v,e)=(u+v,c+e+ω(u,v)/2); its order-p center is generated by w=(0,1). Let D=N:X, t fixing the center, and H be the actual subgroup generated by the zero lifts of L, the second axis V₂, and w^λh, 0≠λ∈F_p. Then every H element is ((l,λj),v₂h^j) with l∈L. This is a subgroup because the lifted L is abelian, X-invariant and contains no center; w^λh has order p. The t-inverted set K translated by t is the full outside involution class. Its size is p^(d+1)q=[D:H] from the centralizers: swap has d fixed vector dimensions, the center is fixed, and C_X(t) is the diagonal of V.

Here the collision proof does not assume *any single axis* acts fixed-point-freely. Every involutory top sign s interchanges L,M and preserves ω. Its negative vector space is {(s y,−y): y∈M}, using L,M as separate summands (equivalently sy−y). For any y, ω(sy,y)=0 by involutivity, skew symmetry and odd characteristic. An actual involution above s has central coordinate zero, since its square has vector zero precisely in that eigenspace and then has central coordinate twice the original one. For a product above signs u,v the two contributing vectors belong to E_u,E_uvu. If their sum lies in L they are uy−y and −(uvu)y+y for the SAME y∈M. Their pairing is ω(uy,y)+ω(y,(uvu)y)=0. Thus every such product has central phase zero over L, for every projected product, whereas every possible vector above v₂h^j in H requires λj. This excludes j≠0. At j=0 a nonidentity second-axis element cannot be inverted by an outside sign since it is carried to the first axis; at identity the two signs coincide, the possible E_s vector in L vanishes, and the zero-sheet test disposes of the center. Consequently H intersects the class square only at identity.

For clarity the remaining sufficient hypotheses are no stronger than stated. The class-square taboo and the exact index make K sharply transitive on D/H, and sandwich closure gives a left Bol loop. The mark-generated group is normal and surjects onto X from the anti-diagonal, h and h-translates. Its commutators with N cover U modulo Φ(N) since [V,U]=U; thus it contains N and is D. The faithful X action gives C_D(N)=Z(N), each nontrivial normal subgroup meets that prime center, and H misses it. Hence LMlt really is D. The p-core of X is trivial by normality of V and its faithful h action, so O_p(D)=N. In a nontrivial signed-normal subgroup inside N, the center is contained, and for Y its image on U the physical exponent 1+dim Y−dim(Y∩L) exceeds dim Y^− because Y∩L is disjoint from the negative eigenspace. In any nonzero signed-normal top image, h-irreducibility *of each of the two distinct axes*, θ exchanging them, and the full commutator of V with a nontrivial h power force V into the image. The preimage then contains N by [V,U]=U. The only proper outside case NV has physical index p^(d+1)q vs inverted index p^d q. Thus the [signed normal-cut test](signed-cut-test.md) gives simplicity. The faithful stabilizer is nontrivial, so this is not an associative loop; odd Moufang solvability excludes Moufangness.

### The new axial rational representation

Produce an l-dimensional F_p-module W of X, with the first axis fixed-point-free and the second acting trivially. If p|f, choose α=p^(f/p) modulo q (order p). On W=F_(p^f), (a₁,a₂) multiplies by ζ^a₁ for ζ of order q, and h is the genuine Frobenius^(f/p). It has order p in characteristic p and conjugates the multiplier to its α-th power; no semisimplicity of h is assumed. If p does not divide f, choose any α of order p. Take W=(F_(p^f))^p; at coordinate k the first-axis element acts by ζ^(α^k a₁), and (h w)_k=w_{k+1} (indices modulo p), giving the same semidirect relation and dimension pf=l. For both constructions the first axis is faithful on W and no nontrivial h-power acts trivially on that axis.

Let W'=W∘θ. The symplectic cotangent representation on W⊕W* has form η(x)−ξ(y) on ((x,ξ),(y,η)), using the true contragredient action on the dual. Take the orthogonal direct sum U of this module with the identical cotangent construction on W'⊕W'*. The twist t switches the two copies and the two dual copies via their common underlying spaces; it is symplectic, induces θ on X, and has order two. The X-invariant Lagrangian L=W⊕W'* is carried to its complementary M=W'⊕W*. Here dim L=2l=a. V is faithful (each separate axis active in one cotangent summand) and the action of X is faithful since a nonzero h-power acts nontrivially on V. There is no common V-fixed vector, equivalently [V,U]=U by coprimality; alternatively (1,1) is already fixed-point-free. Importantly, V₂ alone fixes W⊕W*, of dimension 2l=a, and symmetrically V₁ fixes W'⊕W'*. This does not violate any of the sufficient cut or sheet tests above.

Take i orthogonal copies of this module plus j copies of the [signed nonaxial module](extraspecial-odd-bol-construction.md) with the SAME chosen α, with L the sum of their respective Lagrangians. The original construction works for every generator α of its order-p subgroup. Its nonaxial half-module of dimension $`b`$ contributes **zero** fixed dimension for each nonidentity axis, by the nonzero α^k axis coefficients at every coordinate. Orthogonal sums preserve every hypothesis of the exchange construction, including faithfulness and [V,U]=U in the pure-axial and pure-nonaxial cases. The two order-q normal subgroups of X are intrinsic as an unordered set: any q-subgroup lies in its normal Sylow V, and the h action has exactly its two distinct eigenlines. The conjugation action of D/N on N/Φ(N) is intrinsic, since inner automorphisms of N are trivial there. Any isomorphism of the native left multiplication groups therefore preserves the unordered pair of fixed dimensions, here {ia,ia} counting both axes. Oriented loop isotopy conjugates left multiplication groups (all row differences generate the group in each loop). This proves the multiplicity statement by choosing i=u D₀/a, j=(k−u) D₀/b for u=0,…,k, D₀=lcm(a,b). At p|f the first pure axial exponent is 2f+1, strictly less than the earlier nonaxial b+1; no optimal degree for extraspecial loops is claimed. For (3,13) one gets a=6, b=18 and an extraspecial loop of order 3^7·13. For (5,31) a=b=30, already two nonisotopic extraspecial-core loops of the same order at k=1.

## Mark, top group and original stabilizer

Write N=A5^(2P), H0=H∩N. The outside involutions with first P-top p form one N-conjugacy class of size 60^27, represented by `τ(x,x^-1)` with top(x)=p. Thus the actual mark is K_D={(x,x^-1):top(x)∈D} for some D⊆P containing 1. Conjugation by a top pair (a,b) in F acts on its label as `x↦b^-1 x a`. This action is transitive: for labels x,y choose a with `ab(a)=(ab(y)−ab(x))/2` in P_ab, and put `b=x a y^-1`. Then ab(a)+ab(b)=0 and b^-1 x a=y. Division by two is valid because P_ab has odd order. Actual G-invariance of the outside mark therefore gives D=P. Hence K is the full anti-diagonal of S.

The socle quotient is

`F=G/N={(p,q)∈P² : ab(p)+ab(q)=0}`,

of order 81. If the image of H were proper in the 3-group F, it would lie in a proper normal maximal subgroup of F. Its preimage L in G would be a nontrivial proper normal subgroup containing H. The transversal K would project onto all of G/L, giving equality in the normal-quotient test, contrary to simplicity. Hence HN=G, H maps onto F and `|H0|=60^27/27`. Every nontrivial normal subgroup of G meets N, since C_G(N)=1. Its intersection with N is a product of simple factors invariant under the transitive induced P-action on each row, hence contains one whole socle row N1 or N2. Consequently N1,N2 are the minimal normal subgroups. The canonical folder has core-free H.

The full-strip argument applies to original H0. If both rows have full projections, choose nonidentity involutions on the independent full strips; the unique A5 involution class makes every paired coordinate match, giving H0∩K²≠1. If only one row is full, its F-invariant strip partition has at most 27/2 strips: a singleton would, by regular transitivity, make every factor in that row a singleton and put the whole G-normal row inside H. The residual on the proper row then has order 60^(27−m)/27, divisible by both 3 and 5, although all its proper projections are conjugate under F and no proper A5 subgroup contains both. Thus both rows are proper. Up to global row orientation, the first row has projections C5 or D10, the second C3,S3 or A4.

If the second projection is C3, the available 2-part is too small. If it is S3, a Sylow-2 subgroup embeds in C2^27×C2^27 with dimension 54; its intersection with the matched-support diagonal is nontrivial, violating H0∩K²=1. Hence the second projections are A4. Let Q≤F3^P be the image under A4^P→(A4/V4)^P. It has dimension 24 and its coordinate functionals λ_x are nonzero and permuted transitively by P. If any two are proportional, every proportionality block has size at least 3, so dim Q≤9, a contradiction. The V4_x are pairwise nonisomorphic simple F2[Q]-modules. Since H0′ lies in C5^P×V4^P and projects onto every V4_x, `O2(H0′)=V4^P` by semisimplicity. This exhausts |H0|_2=2^54, so all first projections are C5. The full 5-part gives C5^P≤H0. Thus

`H0=C5^P × preimage(Q)`,  `dim(F3^P/Q)=3`.                         (1)

Even τ-compatible coordinate conjugations standardize each (C5,A4) pair: D10=N_A5(C5) is transitive on the five A4 point stabilizers. The first-side D10 reflection signs of H form a cocycle of F into F2^P under the first P-coordinate. Its kernel in F has order 3 and maps trivially to this 2-module, so it factors through P. Every cocycle of the regular permutation module F2^P is a coboundary: with r(p)(x) the coordinate sign, b(x)=r(x)(x) satisfies `r(p)(x)=b(x)+b(p^-1x)`. Simultaneous coordinatewise even reflections in D10∩A4 gauge all signs away, preserving τ. Thereafter Q is an actual translation-invariant subspace and H is the graph-preimage of a cocycle

`η:F→M=F3^P/Q`,

where the linear action is through the second P-coordinate. Write R for this P-representation and ρ(p,q)=R(q) for the F-representation. The quotient M is a cyclic F3[P]-module of dimension three. These deductions use H0 itself and do not infer any false equality between a projected intersection and an intersection of projections.

## Actual top-collision criterion

For f=(p,q)∈F, an H element over f can lie in K² only if q is conjugate in P to p^-1: K² consists exactly of pairs with second S-coordinate conjugate to the inverse of the first. Call such f *relevant*. For relevant f, there exists an actual H element over f in K² if and only if

`η(f)∈im(1−R(q))`.                                                  (2)

For sufficiency, choose first-side C5^P factors to kill its wreath cycle products. Condition (2) lets one choose a Q representative making the second A4/V4 cycle sums vanish; the independent V4^P in H0 then kills the second-side A4 cycle products. The two S elements are conjugate inverses. Conversely, equality of wreath cycle-product conjugacy classes forces all products to be identity because C5-side products have orders 1 or 5 whereas A4-side products have orders 1,2 or 3. Their second quotient cycle sums then vanish, giving (2). At top zero the same disjoint-order argument excludes any nonidentity collision. Hence the actual folder condition is precisely that (2) fail for every nonidentity relevant f.

## All cyclic modules and cocycles

Let `a=(g,g^-1)`, `b=(t,t^-1)`, `c=(z,1)` in F. With `[x,y]=xyx^-1y^-1`, direct calculation gives

`a^9=b^3=c^3=1`,  `c central`,  `[a,b]=c²a^-3`.                    (3)

Every f has a unique normal form `a^i b^j c^k`, 0≤i<9 and 0≤j,k<3. The first top is g^(i+3k)t^j and the second is g^(-i)t^(-j). Noncentral elements of P have all three central translates as conjugates, so every f whose first top is noncentral is relevant. If its first top is central, relevance is equivalent to k=0.

A unipotent 3×3 matrix over F3 has order dividing 3, so the linear action of P on M kills P′=<z>=<g³>. Put A=η(a), B=η(b), C=η(c). The relevant central element `a³=(z,z^-1)` acts trivially but must have nonzero displacement. Since `η(a³)=(ρ(a)−1)²A`, the matrix `ρ(a)=1+N` has one Jordan block of length three and A is cyclic. Set `e1=N²A,e2=NA,e3=A`. The centralizer of ρ(a) consists of polynomials in N, so write `ρ(b)=1+uN+vN²`. Centrality of c gives C∈ker N. The cocycle relation for (3) is

`(1−ρ(b))A + (ρ(a)−1)B = 2C−N²A`.

Its e2 coefficient forces the e3 coefficient of B to equal u. The relation b³=1 then gives `0=(ρ(b)−1)²B=u³e1`; hence u=0. Write `C=θ e1`. Solving the remaining relation yields the normal form

`ρ(a)=1+N`,  `ρ(b)=1+vN²`,  `A=e3`,
`B=δe2+βe1`,  `C=θ e1`,  `δ=v+2θ−1`,                         (4)

with v,θ,β∈F3. The displayed normal forms do satisfy all relators, hence give actual cocycles, not just necessary equations.

For i nonzero modulo 3, η(a^i b^j c^k) has nonzero e3 coefficient, while the image of `1−ρ(f)` is contained in <e1,e2>. For i=0 modulo 3 and j≠0, its e2 coefficient is jδ while the image is contained in <e1>. For i=j=0 modulo 3, relevance forces k=0, and `η(a^(3m))=m e1` is nonzero for m=1,2. Thus δ≠0 excludes every nontrivial relevant collision. If δ=0, choose j≠0 and multiply by a suitable central power a³ to make the displacement zero; the resulting top remains noncentral and hence relevant. Therefore (2) holds for a nontrivial relevant element exactly when δ=0. The six permissible (v,θ) are the ones listed in the theorem, with β arbitrary before isomorphism.

## Actual folder isomorphisms

A loop isomorphism conjugates the left translations and H. The canonical τ sends each generating left translation to its inverse, so the conjugation intertwines τ by uniqueness on the generating set, using the C0007 interface. The C5 and A4 H0 coordinate projections distinguish the two minimal normal socle rows: an isomorphism between standardized oriented data cannot swap them. Commutation with τ then gives the same permutation f and local automorphism on corresponding coordinates. Since the projection of F onto each row is P, f normalizes the regular P-action and lies in its holomorph, generated by common right translations and Aut(P). These permutations are themselves actual automorphisms of G commuting with τ.

After factoring out f, each local map α_x lies in the common normalizer `N_S5(C5)∩N_S5(A4)=C4`. For every p∈P choose an H element with first top p and first base u∈C5^P. Its transformed first base at x is `α_x u_x α_(p^-1 x)^-1`. Since α_x normalizes C5, membership in C5 forces `α_x α_(p^-1 x)^-1∈C5∩C4=1`. Regular transitivity therefore makes α_x uniform. Finally an automorphism fixing N pointwise fixes G, since its discrepancy on each g centralizes N and C_G(N)=1. This proves completeness of the common-coordinate and uniform-C4 description.

For clarity, the abstract basis change giving (4) must also normalize the actual coordinate kernel and cocycle. In those coordinates the original quotient map sends δ_1 to a cyclic generator w=u(N)e3, with u a unit of F3[N]/N³. Let `π0(δ_x)=R(x)e3`. Equivariance gives π_w=uπ0, so both maps have exactly the same kernel. In the standard π0 coordinates the original cocycle is u^-1 times (4). This describes the original subgroup; it does not yet assert an isomorphism.

Every such unit multiplier is realized by actual folder operations. Common right coordinate translations `x↦xg^r` commute with left P and induce R(g^r)=J^-r on the quotient, since R(P) is abelian. The top automorphism `g↦g^(1+3k), t↦t` preserves R and the quotient map π0; precomposing a cocycle by it sends a to a^(1+3k), fixes b,c, and adds kN²A to A while fixing B,C. This is multiplication of the whole cocycle by 1+kN² because N²B=N²C=0. Powers of J and 1+kN² give all nine constant-one units. The uniform common C4 induces −1 on A4/V4, providing the other scalar. Thus all eighteen units are realized, and the original cocycle is isomorphic to (4) in the fixed coordinate kernel. The top automorphism `t↦tz^j, g↦g` sends b to b a^(3j), changing β by arbitrary j while preserving (v,θ), so β is inessential.

Conversely (v,θ) are intrinsic under the actual ambient normalizer. Every automorphism of P has form `g↦g^i t^ℓ z^m`, `t↦t z^j`, `z↦z^i`, with i≠0 mod 3: comparing `[g,t]=z=g³` forces the coefficient of t in the image of t to be one. Since R(t^-1)−1=vN² and `(R((g^i t^ℓ z^m)^-1)−1)²=i²N²=N²`, v is unchanged. In the center of F, d=(z,z^-1)=a³ and c=(z,1) act trivially; η(d)≠0 and η(c)=θη(d). A common automorphism of P scales c and d by the same i, and coboundaries vanish on them, so θ is unchanged. These facts, together with the Hol(P)/uniform-C4 normalizer above, separate all six pairs. In particular an abstract row-swap formula is not an extra equivalence between standardized orientations.

## Existence, normal quotients and properness

For any of the six (v,θ), define R by `R(g^-1)=1+N`, `R(t^-1)=1+vN²`, `R(z)=1`, and take η from (4) with β=0. These matrices satisfy the P relations. Choose `π0(δ_x)=R(x)e3`, whose image spans M, and put Q=ker π0. Inside `L=G∩[(C5^P⋊P)×(A4^P⋊P)]`, the subgroup H0 from (1) is normal and `L/H0≅M⋊F`. Define H as the preimage of the graph of η. Then H maps onto F and

`|H0|=60^27/27`, `|H|=3·60^27`, `|K|=27·60^27`, `|G|=81·60^54`.

The criterion (2) proves H∩K²=1, and |H||K|=|G|. Since Λ=τK is G-conjugacy invariant, so is K²; the collision and order conditions make K a transversal to H and every conjugate. K contains 1, is inverse-closed and sandwich-closed, and it generates G: the anti-diagonal generates the inverse graph in S_ab×S_ab and contains S′×S′ in its generated group, with S′=A5^P⋊P′. The preceding socle argument and the proper coordinate projections of H0 make H core-free, so this is the actual faithful canonical left multiplication group. Since every nontrivial normal subgroup meets the product of nonabelian simple socle factors, G has trivial solvable radical.

For any nontrivial proper normal L, if it misses N2 then `[L,N2]=1` and the right S-projection of L is trivial. K projects injectively to G/L, so `|KL/L|=|K|=[G:H]>[G:HL]`, since corefreeness gives L⊄H. The same argument applies if L misses N1. If L contains N, then HL=G because H maps onto F; K generates G, so its image in the nontrivial quotient has size >1. These are all cases and the exact normal-subloop criterion gives simplicity. The loop is nonassociative because its canonical group G has proper nontrivial normal socle rows. H is strictly inside the proper subgroup `G∩[(D10^P⋊P)×(A4^P⋊P)]`, making the canonical action imprimitive; the cited simple-Moufang primitivity result makes the loop proper.

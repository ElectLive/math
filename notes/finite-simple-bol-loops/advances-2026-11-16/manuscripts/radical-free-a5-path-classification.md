The proper subgroups of A5 lie in A4, D10, or S3; all involutions form one A5-class and its two classes of elements of order 5 each meet every C5 subgroup. These facts are listed in the ATLAS of Finite Group Representations, A5, version 2.0, last updated 13 December 2001, sections Maximal subgroups and Conjugacy classes: https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/alt/A5/. The last assertion follows from Sylow conjugacy and the normalizer D10. A4 has normal V4 with quotient C3, and S3 has Sylow 2 of order 2.

Let c be the number of forest components, l their number of leaves, and m their number of full strips. Since k=m+l-c, the leaf group R has order 60^(l-c). Every proper projection embeds in a maximal subgroup, and only a D10 projection can contain 5 while only A4 or S3 can contain 3. Taking 5- and 3-parts yields l≥2(l-c). Each component tree has at least two leaves, so l≥2c; both inequalities are equalities. Thus every component is a path with two leaves, and exactly c leaves are of D10 type and c of A4/S3 type. The Sylow 5-subgroup of R has order 5^c, hence is the entire product of the c leaf C5's and belongs to R.

A path cannot have D10 leaves at both ends. Choose nonidentity 5-elements at those two leaves and identities elsewhere, using the product Sylow 5-subgroup. Choose one element independently in each full strip on the path and identity in all other strips. The automorphisms defining the twisted strips permute the two order-5 conjugacy classes, and every leaf C5 meets both classes; propagate a choice of class along the path and choose the terminal leaf value appropriately. Thus the entries in every original τ-pair are conjugate inverses (order-5 classes are real, as the normalizer D10 inverts C5). The resulting nonidentity element lies in H∩K², contradiction. Hence each path has one D10 and one A4/S3 leaf.

Index the paths by i=1,...,c, with D10 coordinate first. Let t be the number of S3-type leaves. Divide R by its full normal C5^c. Write Rbar≤E×∏ B_i, where E=C2^c is the product of D10/C5, B_i=A4 or S3, and |Rbar|=2^(2c)3^c. For any element of Rbar whose selected coordinates at the D10 and B_i leaves are both identity or both involutions, independently multiply a lift by C5^c to make every identity D10/C5 coordinate the identity; the remaining D10 coordinates are involutions. Fill full strips on each path with identity for an inactive path and with involutions for an active path. Twisting automorphisms preserve the unique involution class, so every original τ-pair is conjugate inverse. A nonidentity such element violates H∩K²=1. Call it a support-matched element.

If t>0, take a Sylow 2-subgroup Q of Rbar, of order 2^(2c). It lies in a Sylow 2-subgroup of E×∏ B_i, which is elementary abelian. Regard Q as a 2c-dimensional vector space over F2. On its ith coordinates write x_i∈F2 for E; for B_i=A4 identify V4 with F2² and write y_i,z_i; for B_i=S3 write y_i∈F2. A support match is exactly x_i+N(y_i,z_i)=0 for A4, where N=y_i²+y_i z_i+z_i², and x_i+y_i=0 for S3. These c polynomial equations on 2c variables have total degree 2(c−t)+t<2c. The number of common zeros is even: modulo 2 it is the sum over all vectors of \(\prod_i(1+f_i)\). Its degree is less than the number of variables, so every monomial misses a variable and sums to zero over that variable. Since zero is one common zero, another exists. This gives a nonidentity support-matched element, contradiction.

Thus t=0. In E×A4^c, let U=E×V4^c. A Sylow 3-subgroup of Rbar has order 3^c and projects isomorphically to A4^c/V4^c=C3^c. Conjugating Rbar inside the ambient product, choose this Sylow subgroup as the standard P=C3^c, with each factor acting on its own V4 and trivially elsewhere. Then Rbar=(Rbar∩U)⋊P. As an F2 P-module, U is the direct sum of the trivial E and c pairwise nonisomorphic irreducible two-dimensional modules V_i. For W=Rbar∩U, each operator g_i−1, with g_i a generator of the ith C3, kills E and all V_j for j≠i and is invertible on V_i. Thus whenever a vector of W has a nonzero V_i component, (g_i−1)W contains a nonzero vector in V_i; its C3 orbit spans V_i, so V_i≤W. Subtracting all such components from each vector of W leaves an element of E. Therefore W=B⊕⊕_{i∈S}V_i for B≤E and S⊆{1,...,c}. Its dimension is 2c, so dim B+2|S|=2c. If S is proper, the restriction B→F2^{S^c} has nonzero kernel because dim B=2(c−|S|)>c−|S|. Choose 0≠x in that kernel and choose a nonzero involution in V_i precisely where x_i=1. This is a forbidden support-matched element. Consequently S is all indices and B=0. Hence Rbar=A4^c, and R=C5^c×A4^c on the leaf coordinates.

Therefore H is the direct product over path components of the path-strip factor and its two independent leaf groups. If c>1, the normal-quotient criterion applied to any one component has equality, not the strict inequality needed for simplicity. Thus c=1, the graph is a single path, and R=C5×A4. To identify the folder, first swap the two coordinates within individual τ-pairs as needed to place the strip vertices on the two sides of the tree bipartition; these swaps commute with τ. Then gauge its twisted path as follows. Apply the same automorphism to both coordinates of each τ-pair and independently reparameterize each full strip by an automorphism of T. Starting at one endpoint and proceeding along the tree, choose these automorphisms successively to make every strip incidence the identity. There is no cycle consistency condition. Thus the path is the standard one, with endpoint groups C5 and A4. The ordered pairs of endpoint groups form a single A5-conjugacy orbit: after fixing C5 by Sylow conjugacy, its normalizer D10 is transitive on the five A4 point stabilizers. This is the simultaneous-conjugacy step in the reviewed paired-simple manuscript at `4c93742a59ccdd786f23598f0374f674c6f75fff`. The reviewed within-path criterion therefore identifies the resulting loop uniquely, including reversal of the path. The reviewed construction provides the converse and nonassociativity for every k≥1. No conclusion about other simple T follows; this extension has the independent reviews below.

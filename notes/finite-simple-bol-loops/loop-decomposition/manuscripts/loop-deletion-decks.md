# Intrinsic deletion decks and central gluing obstructions

*Mira Cult*

## Scope and dependencies

Fix a prime p different from 3. This manuscript defines a deletion deck from
loop multiplication, proves its independence of the admissible choices, and
identifies its precise reconstruction problem. It then gives a central gluing
obstruction outside the graphical family and a different, broader hyperplane
deck with a reconstruction theorem for scalar alternating forms.

The starting source is PR #446, delivery
`7653409e6155cd87c7f91ba810b155f517c7f44b`, scientific revision
`9059767c0ef28809bbe17de8a71ba0a8b11d0485`. Its
[Bruck construction](../../graph-loops/manuscripts/graph-eisenstein-loops.md),
[binary construction](../../graph-loops/manuscripts/graph-cubic-loops.md),
[subloop theorem](../../graph-loops/manuscripts/graph-associative-subloops.md) and
[review record](../../graph-loops/evidence/graph-loops-review.md) are retained unchanged.
The deck below concerns the proper-Bruck prime-field family; it is not silently
extended to the characteristic-three or binary Moufang suspensions, where the
marker-hyperplane exceptions differ.

The external graph-rigidity input is He--Qiao [HQ20, Proposition 1.2], over any
field. The new extension-rigidity lemma used for recognition has its canonical
proof in [the extension manuscript](bruck-extension-decomposition.md#extension-rigidity).
No claim here solves graph reconstruction. The full associative-subloop census
obtained from an unaligned deck is proved separately in
[the census manuscript](loop-deck-subloop-census.md).

## 1. Intrinsic data in a Bruck extension

Write A(L) for the normal subloop generated by all associators, using
(xy)z=x(yz)[x,y,z] when these associators are central. Write Z(L) for the full
center; these are different objects. In the graph family A(L) is exactly the
edge-coordinate space. The quotient U=L/A(L) is an elementary abelian p-group,
and hence a canonical F_p-vector space. Its trilinear associator map is

```math
D((a,u),(b,v),(c,w))
 =aB(v,w)-bB(u,w)-2cB(u,v),
\qquad U=F_pm_0\oplus W.                                      \tag{1}
```

This formula holds for every alternating bilinear B:W×W→A, not only graphical
ones. We assume A is spanned by the image of B when writing L_p(B). The product
is the source law `(a,u,s)(b,v,t)=(a+b,u+v,s+t+(a+2b)B(u,v))`.

Suppose B is nonzero, and put R=rad B. The right-alternative locus is

```math
\mathcal R(L)=\{y\in U:D(x,y,y)=0\ (x\in U)\}
 =W\cup(F_pm_0\oplus R).                                      \tag{2}
```

Indeed D(x,y,y)=−3bB(u,v). A nonzero alternating map has radical of codimension
at least two: a nonzero scalar component of B has rank at least two. Therefore
dim R+1<dim W. A vector space contained in a union of two subspaces lies in one
of them, so W is the unique largest-dimensional linear subspace in (2).
This recovers W from multiplication without a vertex basis, including p=2,
single edges, stars and isolated vertices. The edgeless case is treated below.

An **admissible marker** is an element m of U satisfying

```math
m\notin W,\qquad D(w,m,m)=0\quad(w\in W).                       \tag{3}
```

In (1), such markers are exactly m=a_0m_0+r with a_0≠0 and r∈R.
The repeated-argument condition is essential, not a convenient normalization.
For a marker put

```math
B_m(u,v)=D(m,u,v)=a_0B(u,v)\qquad(u,v\in W).                    \tag{4}
```

Multiplying the output space A by a_0 identifies this map with B. Formula (1)
also holds in the coordinates U=F_pm⊕W with B replaced by B_m.

## 2. Recognition without a hidden graph

Here are finite, multiplication-defined recognition conditions. They can be
tested by exhaustive linear algebra; no efficiency claim is implicit.

For a nonassociative loop L require:

1. L is a finite left Bruck loop of exponent p; A=A(L) is central and elementary
   abelian; U=L/A is elementary abelian; and D:U³→A is well-defined and trilinear.
2. The right-alternative locus has a unique largest linear subspace W of
   codimension one. There exists m satisfying (3).
3. B_m is alternating, its image spans A, and for all quotient coordinates
   a,b,c∈F_p and u,v,w∈W,

```math
D(am+u,bm+v,cm+w)
 =aB_m(v,w)-bB_m(u,w)-2cB_m(u,v).                               \tag{5}
```

4. There is a basis (w_1,…,w_n) of W for which the list

```math
\bigl(B_m(w_i,w_j)\bigr)_{i<j,\ B_m(w_i,w_j)\ne0}               \tag{6}
```

is linearly independent in A. Since B_m is bilinear and full, this list then
automatically spans A. It is a basis, not merely a list of distinct vectors.

**Theorem 1 (recognition).** These conditions hold if and only if L is isomorphic
to L_p(Γ) for a finite simple graph Γ with at least one edge. In the associative
case, the recognized family consists of elementary abelian groups C_p^{n+1},
n≥0, interpreted as the edgeless graphs on n vertices. The trivial loop is not
an additional graph in this one-marker family.

**Proof.** Graph loops satisfy the conditions by the source calculation and
(2)–(4); the vertex basis satisfies (6). Conversely, define a simple graph on
the basis indices by ij∈E iff B_m(w_i,w_j)≠0. The independent nonzero values in
(6) identify its exterior edge map with B_m. Conditions (1) and (5), together
with the extension-rigidity lemma, identify L with the explicit L_p(B_m): two
central elementary-abelian Bruck extensions of exponent p with the same
associator are isomorphic over their fixed quotient and kernel. Thus L is the
claimed graph loop. The associative branch follows because a group with
automorphic inversion is abelian. ∎

This recognition is intrinsic, but the search for (6) is precisely a search for
a graphical presentation of the alternating map. It is not an efficient
intrinsic extraction of individual vertices. This distinction remains after
choice independence has been proved.

## 3. The intrinsic frame deck

For a recognized nonassociative L, an **admissible frame** consists of:

- an admissible marker m as in (3);
- a basis (w_1,…,w_n) of the intrinsic W satisfying (6);
- arbitrary lifts t,x_1,…,x_n in L of m,w_1,…,w_n respectively.

The entire tuple generates L. Indeed its image generates U, while its
associators `[t,x_i,x_j]` generate A by (6). This is also a direct finite
generation check if desired. Define the i-th card to be the generated subloop

```math
C_i=\langle t,x_1,\ldots,\widehat{x_i},\ldots,x_n\rangle.        \tag{7}
```

Do **not** adjoin all of A to every card. Only the associators generated by the
retained tuple belong automatically to the card. The proposed deck is the
multiset `{[C_i]:1≤i≤n}`, retaining multiplicities.

For an elementary abelian L=C_p^{n+1}, choose any basis (t,x_1,…,x_n) and use
(7); equivalently define the deck as n copies of C_p^n. For n=0 it is empty.
This branch does not pretend that the group has a distinguished hyperplane.

**Lemma 2 (exact generated card).** Let an admissible frame define the graph H
on its basis indices. Then C_i is isomorphic to L_p(H−i), for every choice of
lifts and every p≠3.

**Proof.** Work in the model L_p(B), whose existence is Theorem 1. Write
m=a_0m_0+r with r∈rad B, and let H_i=span{w_j:j≠i}. Set
A_i=span{B(w_j,w_k):j,k≠i}. The central coordinates of the chosen lifts extend
uniquely to a linear map ℓ:F_pm⊕H_i→A. In original coordinates define

```math
s(\tau,u)=(a_0\tau,r\tau+u,\ell(\tau,u)).                      \tag{8}
```

Since r is radical, multiplying two such section values changes the central
coordinate from s(τ+σ,u+v) by
`a_0(τ+2σ)B(u,v)`, which lies in A_i. Consequently `s(F_p⊕H_i)+A_i` is a
subloop containing the generators and having the displayed source law with
alternating map a_0B restricted to H_i.

Conversely the generators project onto F_pm⊕H_i, and their associators generate
A_i. Hence their generated subloop is exactly this set. In particular it has
order p^{n+dim A_i}. The restriction has independent edge coordinates for H−i,
so the card is L_p(H−i). Central lift changes only the linear section ℓ; it does
not introduce additional central dimensions. ∎

**Theorem 3 (choice-independent abstract deck).** All admissible frames of L_p(Γ)
give the same multiset

```math
\mathcal D_p(L_p(\Gamma))
 =\{[L_p(\Gamma-v)]:v\in V(\Gamma)\}.                           \tag{9}
```

**Proof.** For any frame, its graph H has alternating map equivalent to B_Γ,
by (4) and (6). He--Qiao Proposition 1.2 says H≅Γ. This proposition permits an
arbitrary common domain change and independent output change over **any field**;
the odd-prime restriction in the neighboring group theorem is irrelevant here.
Graph isomorphism preserves the multiset of vertex-deleted card types. Apply
Lemma 2 and the source graph-to-loop naturality. If Γ is edgeless, every card
is C_p^n by the separate group definition. ∎

Isolates cause no failure: they form rad B, marker shifts by that radical are
allowed in (3), and He--Qiao includes isolates. The single-edge and star cases
also need no new exception because (2) recovers W at every p≠3. This contrasts
with the marker ambiguity of the separate Moufang suspensions.

The deck is invariant under loop isomorphism: associators, the vector spaces,
conditions (3)–(6), lifts and generated subloops are all transported by such an
isomorphism. Equivalently, this is a functor from the loop isomorphism groupoid
to the discrete category whose objects are finite multisets of loop isomorphism
classes: each isomorphism maps to the identity of the common deck, so identities
and composition are respected. No deletion functor on arbitrary homomorphisms
is asserted.

## 4. Abstract cards are not a canonical embedded family

The central shears `(u,z)↦(u,z+ℓ(u))`, for linear ℓ:U→A, are automorphisms of
the explicit source loop. They move the lifted cards while preserving all
quotient and associator data. Thus lift independence is an isomorphism-type
statement, not equality of subloops.

There is a stronger obstruction. For an edgeless graph on n≥1 vertices the loop
is C_p^{n+1}; GL_{n+1}(p) acts transitively on its index-p subgroups, of which
there are `(p^{n+1}−1)/(p−1)>n`. No automorphism-invariant set of n distinct
embedded index-p cards exists. Even if a marker is supplied, all hyperplanes of
an n-dimensional vertex complement number `(p^n−1)/(p−1)`, not n in general.

For K_n with n≥2, every g∈GL(W) extends to a loop automorphism by Λ²g on the
edge space. It acts transitively on the hyperplanes of W. The n coordinate
hyperplanes underlying a frame's cards therefore cannot form an invariant set:
their orbit has `(p^n−1)/(p−1)>n` members. Nonassociativity does not recover a
canonical embedded vertex-deletion family.

### Why each admissibility condition is needed

For K_2, take the standard marker m_0 and basis e_1,e_2, with edge coordinate z.
The replacement marker m'=m_0+e_1 has the same nonzero marker-edge associator.
Nevertheless

```math
D(e_2,m',m')=3z\ne0\qquad(p\ne3).
```

The purported card generated by m' and e_2 therefore contains z and has order
p³. The correct card for a one-vertex edgeless graph has order p². Merely taking
a transverse marker, without (3), is false.

Independence in (6) is also necessary for the frame theorem. In the edge-valued
map of 2K_2, replace the standard basis by
`(e_1+e_3,e_2,e_3,e_4)`. Its nonzero pair values include z_12 and z_34 twice.
Deleting e_3 leaves a three-dimensional space with two independent edge values,
so that card has order p^6. The four standard coordinate cards all have order
p^5. Dropping independence therefore destroys choice independence even inside
the original graph family.

Finally, adjoining all of A to the cards is wrong: for K_2 it gives order p³
instead of p². The generation rule (7), rather than an unrestricted full
preimage of a quotient hyperplane, is required.

## 5. Simpler deletion alternatives

**All maximal proper subloops.** In L_p(B) with A=span im B, every maximal proper
subloop contains A. To prove this, adjoin the central A to a maximal S. Either
SA=S or SA=L. In the second case S projects onto U, so its associators generate
all of A, forcing A⊆S and S=L, a contradiction. Thus all maximal subloops are
the full inverse images of hyperplanes of U. They are normal, of index p, and
number `(p^{n+1}−1)/(p−1)`. A vertex card instead has index p^{1+deg(v)}.
These are different decks, even before inspecting isomorphism types.

**All split quotients or all proper retracts.** This collection already contains
a cyclic subgroup of order p: the scalar-coordinate map has the marker subgroup
as a section. For n≥3, no vertex card has order p. Counting all such objects also
introduces many distinct kernels/sections of the same quotient. Additional
selection conditions would be a new definition, not an automatic vertex deck.

**Kernels of the intended vertex retractions.** In a graph frame the kernel is
an elementary abelian group of order p^{1+deg(v)}: its coordinates are the omitted
vertex and its incident edges, with marker zero. Hence the multiset of abstract
kernel types is exactly the degree multiset. For C_6 and 2K_3 all six kernels
are C_p³, but the graphs, and therefore their graph loops, are nonisomorphic.
Kernel types alone discard the extension incidence information.

**All associator-defined hyperplanes of W.** This gives a legitimate broader
operation, defined in Section 9 below, with `(p^n−1)/(p−1)` cards. It does not
have the n-card graph deck or the same reconstruction problem.

## 6. Exact reconstruction equivalence and established controls

For n≥3 let G_{p,n} be the class of unmarked loops L_p(Γ) with Γ a finite simple
graph on n vertices, using the one-marker construction of PR #446. Competitors
in the following statement are restricted to this class, equivalently to the
recognition conditions in Section 2 with the indicated n.

**Theorem 4 (exact equivalence).** At any fixed prime p≠3, reconstruction from
the intrinsic multiset D_p on all G_{p,n}, n≥3, is equivalent to the ordinary
finite simple graph reconstruction conjecture.

**Proof.** Theorem 3 identifies each deck, including multiplicities. The source
unmarked recovery theorem applies to every card, even when it is edgeless, so
equality of loop-card multisets is equivalent to equality of graph-card
multisets. The same theorem identifies parent-loop isomorphism with parent-graph
isomorphism. Apply these two equivalences in either direction. ∎

At n=2 the edgeless graph and K_2 both have two one-vertex cards; their loop decks
are two copies of C_p² despite parent orders p³ and p⁴. This verifies the
essential lower-order exclusion. Neither an equality of ordinary group factors
nor a full finite simple Bol classification supplies the missing n≥3 assembly.

The usual reconstructible graph classes give controls, not new graph results:

- **Regular graphs.** Recover e from the sum of card edge counts divided by n−2,
  and recover the degrees as e−e(Γ−v). Regularity and the degree r are determined.
  In any card, the former neighbors of the omitted vertex have degree r−1 and
  all other vertices degree r. Adjoin a vertex joined exactly to the former.
- **Disconnected graphs.** The deck determines component count: if isolates
  exist, c(Γ)=min_v c(Γ−v)+1; otherwise c(Γ)=min_v c(Γ−v). Isolates are detected
  by the recovered degrees. The second equality uses a non-cut vertex in a
  component of order at least two. For a disconnected parent, every component
  has order less than n. Kelly double counting gives the multiplicity of every
  proper connected induced graph. Subtract contributions inside already recovered
  larger components, in decreasing order, to recover the entire component multiset.
- **Trees.** Connectedness and e=n−1 identify this family. Kelly's 1957 main
  theorem reconstructs trees of order at least three from their vertex cards.
  Applying Theorem 4 gives the loop consequence. Kelly's proof, not a new
  loop argument, pays this control case.

These assertions use no correspondence between deleting a graph vertex and
deleting an element of the loop. A card is a subloop generated by an intrinsic
admissible frame and also a specified split quotient once that frame is chosen.

## 7. A central gluing obstruction beyond graphical competitors

The information in the central output spaces cannot be ignored when extending
the competitor universe. Let W=F_p^4. Define

```math
B_0(e_1,e_2)=z_1,\quad B_0(e_3,e_4)=z_2,
\qquad A_0=F_pz_1\oplus F_pz_2,
```

with all other basis pairings zero. This is the edge map of 2K_2. Let
B_1 be the scalar alternating map obtained by identifying z_1=z_2=z, so
B_1 has rank four. There is a central quotient

```math
L_p(B_0)\longrightarrow L_p(B_1),\qquad
\ker=\langle z_1-z_2\rangle.                                   \tag{10}
```

For each standard coordinate deletion, the retained edge space is either F_pz_1
or F_pz_2. It intersects the kernel trivially. Thus (10) restricts to an
isomorphism on every one of the four cards. Both coordinate decks are four
copies of L_p(K_2⊔K_1), of order p^5. The parents have orders p^7 and p^6 and
are not isomorphic.

This is a failure of reconstruction in an enlarged alternating-map universe,
even with identical vertex-coordinate deletions and individual quotient maps
that are isomorphisms on the cards. It does not fix the parent-loop order and
does not provide a graph reconstruction counterexample. The scalar rank-four
map has no graphical frame: its one-dimensional output allows at most one
independent nonzero basis pairing, which would have rank at most two.

Cross-card intersections reveal extra data: after the quotient, the two formerly
independent edge lines coincide. An unaligned deck contains neither those
intersection embeddings nor the common central relation. Assuming them during
gluing assumes information absent from the problem.

In the graphical family, a fully aligned deck is enough: if the cards specify
their common vertex coordinates and compatible embeddings of their edge spaces,
every edge pair is present in at least one card when n≥3. Its value is therefore
known consistently, and the independent edge coordinates give the parent B.
The extension-rigidity lemma then gives the parent loop. This statement requires
the specified alignments; no method recovering them from D_p is proved here.

## 8. What a frame-based definition does and does not extend

Theorem 1 recognizes precisely the graphical suspension subclass. Arbitrary
Bol or Moufang loops need not have a central elementary abelian associator group,
an elementary abelian quotient, a trilinear D, the suspension pattern (5), or a
basis satisfying (6). None of these properties is presumed.

Even inside the much larger family L_p(B), condition (6) can fail, as (10)
shows. Simply deleting a basis element then fails to be choice independent in
general, as the nonadmissible 2K_2 basis demonstrates. The graph-frame deck is
intrinsic but remains a graphical-presentation construction. The next operation
removes that presentation search at the price of changing the deck.

## 9. A broader intrinsic hyperplane deck

For nonzero alternating B with A=span im B, recover W by (2). For each hyperplane
H≤W choose any basis of H, any admissible marker, and arbitrary lifts. Take the
subloop generated by those lifts and the marker. Lemma 2's section proof, which
does not need (6) until its final graph identification, shows its type is

```math
L_p(B_H),\qquad
B_H:H\times H\longrightarrow A_H=\langle B(H,H)\rangle.         \tag{11}
```

Define H_p(L) as the multiset of (11) over **all** hyperplanes H of W. It has
`(p^n−1)/(p−1)` cards. Its isomorphism types are independent of all marker,
basis and lift choices. Under a loop isomorphism the intrinsic W and its
hyperplanes are transported, so the multiset is intrinsic for this entire
alternating-map suspension family. The product law itself identifies the class;
conditions (1)–(5), without (6), also recognize it by extension rigidity.

For B=0 use the elementary abelian group branch: H_p(C_p^{n+1}) is
`(p^n−1)/(p−1)` copies of C_p^n, with no cards when n=0. This does not require
a canonical embedded W. The edge center in (11) is reduced to its actual image;
unused central dimensions are not silently retained.

For (10), H_p distinguishes the parents. For B_0, exactly 2(p+1) hyperplanes
cut just one of the two symplectic planes and give L_p(K_2⊔K_1). The remaining
`(p−1)(p+1)^2` give L_p(P_3): if a defining functional is nonzero on both planes,
choose one kernel vector in each plane and a vector joining their complementary
directions. Its two independent pairings form the P_3 edge map. For B_1 every
hyperplane has scalar rank two and gives L_p(K_2⊔K_1). At p=2 these are respectively
six cards of order 32 and nine of order 64, versus fifteen cards of order 32.

### Scalar-form reconstruction

Let S_{p,n} consist of the loops of scalar alternating forms on F_p^n, using
their image A_B as the central coordinate space: A_B=0 for the zero form and
A_B=F_p otherwise.
This class includes nongraphical nondegenerate forms of rank at least four.

**Theorem 5 (hyperplane reconstruction in the scalar family).** For p≠3 and
n≥3, H_p reconstructs every member of S_{p,n} among competitors in that family.
No markings or aligned card embeddings are needed.

**Proof.** The number of cards determines n. A scalar alternating form is
classified up to invertible change of basis and output scale by its even rank
2r: choose a pair with nonzero value, rescale to value one, split its orthogonal
complement and iterate. Put k=n−2r.

For r>0, a hyperplane containing rad B has restriction rank 2r−2. Those not
containing rad B have rank 2r, because their image in W/rad B is the full
symplectic quotient. Their exact counts are

```math
\#\{H:\mathop{\mathrm{rad}}\nolimitsB\subseteq H\}
 =\frac{p^{2r}-1}{p-1},\qquad
\#\{H:\mathop{\mathrm{rad}}\nolimitsB\nsubseteq H\}
 =\frac{p^n-p^{2r}}{p-1}.                                     \tag{12}
```

Each nonassociative card determines its restricted rank: its quotient gives
dim H=n−1, its center modulo its associator group gives the radical dimension.
An associative card has restricted rank zero. If all cards are associative,
r=0 for n≥3. If two ranks occur, their maximum is 2r. If a single positive rank
occurs, k=0 and that rank is 2r−2; hence 2r=n. This determines B's rank and
therefore the parent-loop isomorphism type. ∎

The restriction n≥3 is sharp for this convention: at n=2, the zero form and
the nondegenerate form have the same p+1 cards C_p², with different parent
orders. Theorem 5 is an algebraic reconstruction result for a different deck;
it is not a new class of graphs for the original reconstruction conjecture.
Its rank-classification ingredient is standard linear algebra, and no
literature-wide priority claim is made for this hyperplane-deck formulation.

## 10. Consequences and remaining decisive obligation

The frame deck gives an exact intrinsic reformulation of graph reconstruction.
The broad hyperplane deck gives a meaningful nongraphical extension and the
restricted reconstruction theorem above. The central quotient (10), arbitrary
marker example and nonadmissible basis example are explicit failures of broader
gluing or choice-independence assertions. The
[extension/decomposition theorem](bruck-extension-decomposition.md) and
[full group-subloop census](loop-deck-subloop-census.md) are additional structural
results; they do not recover missing card alignment.

For the graph-frame deck, the decisive unpaid theorem is a method to recover
compatible domain/output alignments of the alternating-map cards from their
unmarked multiset, or another uniqueness argument that avoids those alignments.
The He--Qiao theorem recognizes a fully assembled graph map; it does not perform
this gluing. Simple-factor classification does not determine the associator
extension data. No new unresolved graph class is advertised as solved.

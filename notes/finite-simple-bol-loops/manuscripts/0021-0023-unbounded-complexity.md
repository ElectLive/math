# 0021–0023. Unbounded complexity in multiplication groups of simple Bol loops

*Mira Cult*

## Introduction and results

Simplicity of a loop does not force simplicity, or even bounded solvable complexity, of its multiplication group. We give three constructions showing how far these notions can diverge for finite simple proper left Bol loops of odd order. Here **proper** means non-Moufang, and **G-loop** means a loop isomorphic to each of its isotopes.

Throughout, G denotes the **canonical left multiplication group**: the permutation group generated by the left translations of the loop. The statements do not identify G with the full multiplication group, which also includes right translations.

**Theorem 0021 (nilpotency class of the derived subgroup).** For every integer c≥2, there is a finite simple proper left Bol loop of odd order whose group G has nilpotent derived subgroup G′ of class exactly c and satisfies Z(G′)=Z(G).

**Theorem 0022 (derived length).** For every integer n≥4, there is a finite simple proper left Bol G-loop of odd order for which the derived length of G is exactly 1+⌈log₂n⌉.

**Theorem 0023 (Fitting height).** For every integer h≥0, there is a finite simple proper left Bol loop of odd order for which the Fitting height of G is exactly h+3.

These are different obstructions to a bounded-complexity classification. Nilpotency class measures the lower central series of G′; derived length measures the derived series of G; Fitting height measures the shortest normal series of G with nilpotent factors. The first two constructions share a central-product mechanism. The third uses iterated wreath products and an affine factorization. None is an exhaustion or an isomorphism classification of all odd-order simple Bol loops.

## Preliminaries

A faithful generating Bol folder (G,H,K) realizes the loop through a transversal K to the conjugates of H; core-freeness of H and generation by K identify G with its canonical left multiplication group. We use the [folder correspondence](../packet/proofs/C0001.md) and the [canonical inversion theorem](../packet/proofs/C0007.md), Theorem 1.1, with its trivial-left-nucleus hypothesis when applied to simple nonassociative quotients.

All our groups G have odd order and an involutory automorphism τ, and K is the full set of elements inverted by τ. The central simplicity tool is the following form of the [odd-folder criterion](../packet/manuscripts/odd-folder-radicals.md#candidate-simplicity-criterion-using-τ-stable-normal-subgroups): for a faithful generating folder with these properties, simplicity is equivalent to

    |H∩N| < |C_N(τ)|

for every nontrivial proper τ-stable normal subgroup N of G. The fixed-point ratio behind it is

    |KN/N| / [G:HN] = |C_N(τ)| / |H∩N|.

Its converse uses a maximal simple loop quotient and the induced canonical inversion automorphism. One must not assume that an arbitrary normal quotient of G automatically inherits a loop transversal, or that every loop-quotient kernel is τ-stable.

For Theorem 0022 we also use the [symmetric exact-factorization lemma](../packet/manuscripts/exact-factorization-criterion.md#symmetric-exact-factorizations-and-full-inverted-folders): in the odd-order setting, H∩τ(H)=1 together with |H|=|C_G(τ)| and |G|=|H|² gives the required full-inverted-set transversal and the G-loop property.

Theorem 0023 uses the [exact-factorization construction](../packet/proofs/C0002.md), Proposition 1.1. Its non-Moufang conclusion uses the accompanying fact that the canonical left multiplication action of a finite simple Moufang loop is primitive. That dependency is distinct from the explicit failed-Moufang-identity witnesses used in Theorems 0021 and 0022.

For the first two constructions, the finite Lazard correspondence applies because the Lie nilpotency class is smaller than the characteristic. We use its subgroup, ideal, and automorphism correspondences, as stated by d’Elbée–Müller–Ramsey–Siniora, [arXiv:2310.17595v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17595v3), §2.3, Facts 2.13–2.14.

## Proof of Theorem 0021: paired filiform algebras

Fix distinct odd primes p,q>c, let r=ord_q(p), s=ord_p(q), E=F_{p^r}, F=F_{q^s}, and choose λ∈E×, η∈F× of orders q,p respectively. The construction below has order

    p^{(c+1)r} q^{2rs+1}.                                (1)

There are two distinct primes larger than every fixed c, so this supplies every claimed class under the stated hypotheses. 

### Lie algebra, group and stabilizer data

Let L⁺ be the E-Lie algebra with basis x,y₁,…,y_c and nonzero basic brackets [x,y_j]=y_{j+1} for 1≤j<c. It is the semidirect product of Ex with an abelian space on which x shifts the chain, so Jacobi holds. Its class is c and center Ey_c. Take a second copy on x′,y₁′,…,y_c′ and form the central product identifying y_c=y_c′=z, with all mixed brackets zero. Denote the resulting algebra by L. It has E-dimension 2c+1, center Ez, and class c. Indeed a central vector has zero x,x′ coordinates by bracketing with y₁,y₁′, and then all y_j,y_j′ coefficients for j<c vanish by bracketing with x,x′. The bracket chain ending in z has length c.

Let τ_P exchange the copies and fix z. Let b generate A_q=C_q and act on the first copy by

    x↦λx,   y_j↦λ^{−(c−j)}y_j,

with inverse weights on the second copy. The bracket relations are preserved; z is fixed; τ_P inverts b. Every noncentral weight is nontrivial since q>c. Give x,y₁,x′,y₁′ degree one, y_j,y_j′ degree j and z degree c; this grading agrees with the lower central filtration from degree two onward.

Regard L as an F_p-Lie algebra. Since c<p, the BCH/Lazard correspondence gives an exponent-p group P with this underlying set, class c and center Ez. See [d’Elbée–Müller–Ramsey–Siniora, arXiv:2310.17595v3](../packet/REFERENCES.md#lazard-bch-interface), §2.3, Facts 2.13 and 2.14, including subgroups, ideals and automorphisms. Restriction of scalars leaves the bracket chains and center unchanged. Both τ_P and A_q act as group automorphisms. Define

    𝔰_P=Ex ⊕ ⊕_{j=1}^{c−1}Ey_j′,    S_P=exp(𝔰_P).

This subalgebra is abelian; it is A_q-invariant, disjoint from its τ-image and the center, and complementary to its τ-image modulo the center. A_q acts without nonzero fixed vectors on 𝔰_P and on L/Ez, even for every nonidentity power of b.

Put A_p=(E,+), identified with Z_P=Z(P), and choose an F_p-basis to write a∈A_p as (a₁,…,a_r). Let W=⊕_{j=1}^rF², with the nondegenerate alternating F_q-form β equal to the sum of the field traces of the standard forms on the F² planes. Let R(a) multiply the j-th plane by η^{a_j}. These commuting operators are β-self-adjoint, faithful as a combined A_p-action, and have no common nonzero fixed vector. A nonzero a need not move every plane.

Take the exponent-q extraspecial group Q with coordinates W⊕W⊕F_q and product

    (u,v,t)(u′,v′,t′)
      =(u+u′,v+v′,t+t′+½[β(u,v′)+β(v,u′)]).

Let τ_Q swap u,v and fix t, while a acts by (R(a),R(a)⁻¹,1). A_q acts trivially on Q and A_p trivially on P. Set

    A=A_p×A_q,   G=(P×Q)⋊A,
    τ=(τ_P,τ_Q) with inversion on A,
    S_Q={(u,0,0):u∈W},
    H=(S_P×S_Q)⋊{a z(a):a∈A},

where z:A→Z_P×Z_Q identifies A_p with E and b with the unit generator of Z_Q=F_q. All central labels are fixed by G and τ. The twisted torus is a subgroup, since it is a central twist of the abelian complement A and has the same orders.

The A-action on P×Q is faithful. Its commutator subgroup is P×Q. For P, the fixed-free action on L/Z_P gives full commutator image on P/P′ by coprime averaging; Z_P≤P′=Φ(P), so the Frattini generation argument applies. For Q, the common fixed space of A_p on Q/Z_Q is zero, and the same argument applies with Φ(Q)=Z_Q. Thus

    G′=P×Q,   class(G′)=c,   Z(G)=Z_P×Z_Q=Z(G′).        (2)

These Frattini equalities use exponent-p and exponent-q in this construction; they are not imported into the arbitrary-exponent class-two theorem.

### Full marked transversal and canonical group

Put K={g∈G:τ(g)=g⁻¹}, and Λ=τ̂^G=τ̂K in G⋊⟨τ̂⟩. Oddness gives this single outside-involution class. The swaps and fixed centers give

    |C_P(τ)|=p^{(c+1)r},    |C_Q(τ)|=q^{2rs+1},
    |H|=|C_G(τ)|=p^{(c+1)r}q^{2rs+1},   |G|=|H|².

Hence |K|=[G:H]. We prove H∩Λ²=1 by showing that no nonidentity h∈H is inverted by an outside involution. Every element of Λ² is inverted by one of its two involution factors.

Write h=(s_P,s_Q)a z(a), with labels a_p∈A_p and a_q∈A_q. If a_p≠0, inspect the image in P⋊A_q. If also a_q≠1, the action of a_q on abelian S_P is fixed-point-free, so conjugation within S_P removes s_P. The remaining element is z_P(a_p)a_q, whose nontrivial p-primary component is z_P(a_p). An outside involution fixes this central component pointwise and therefore cannot invert the element. If a_q=1 and s_P≠1, the lowest nonzero graded component of log s_P lies in 𝔰_P. An outside involution sends it into the A_q-scaled opposite graded subspace τ𝔰_P, disjoint from it. Inner conjugation does not change the lowest lower-central component, so inversion is impossible. If s_P=1, the nonzero central label itself cannot be inverted.

If a_p=0 but a_q≠1, inspect Q⋊A_p, where the image is s_Q z_Q(a_q) with zero A_p-label. A nonzero S_Q shadow cannot be inverted: inner conjugacy is trivial on Q/Z_Q, the torus preserves the two halves and τ interchanges them. If its shadow is zero, its nonzero fixed central label prevents inversion. Finally if both labels vanish, a nonzero S_P or S_Q component is excluded by the same graded or half-space argument. This proof never assumes that every nonzero A_p-element is fixed-point-free on all Q-planes.

Thus H∩Λ²=1. As Λ²=K² is G-conjugacy invariant, the same holds for every conjugate of H; the size equality makes K a full transversal. It is inverse- and sandwich-closed, so the Bol-folder correspondence gives a left Bol loop. A⊂K and ⟨K⟩ is normal in odd G because K is the set of τ-commutators. The commutator-generation argument above gives ⟨K⟩=G. A normal subgroup of G contained in H meets P×Q trivially: its Sylow intersections are normal in the respective p-groups and any nontrivial such subgroup meets the center, which H avoids. It then centralizes P×Q. The faithful action makes that centralizer Z_P×Z_Q, also disjoint from H. Hence the folder is faithful and G is the canonical left multiplication group. Formula (1) follows.

### Normal-subgroup defect and simplicity

Let N◁G be τ-stable, L_P=N∩P, L_Q=N∩Q, C_P=L_P∩Z_P and C_Q=L_Q∩Z_Q. Write T_p×T_q for the image of N in A, and J_p×J_q for that of H∩N. Subgroups decompose this way because p≠q. Fixed elements have trivial odd A-image, so C_N(τ)=C_{L_P}(τ)×C_{L_Q}(τ).

By the Lazard ideal correspondence, log L_P is an F_p-subspace and ideal. Coprime A_q averaging splits it into its central part C_P and a part V with no trivial A_q-weight; the averaging projection on L itself is exactly its projection onto Ez. It commutes with τ. Projection along V₋ injects 𝔰_P∩log L_P into V₊, because 𝔰_P and τ𝔰_P are disjoint modulo the center. Consequently

    |C_{L_P}(τ)|/|S_P∩L_P|≥p^{dim_Fp C_P}.              (3)

The analogous half-space argument in Q gives

    |C_{L_Q}(τ)|/|S_Q∩L_Q|≥q^{dim_Fq C_Q}.              (4)

Any nontrivial normal L_P or L_Q meets its respective center.

We next prove J_p≤C_P under A_p≅Z_P, and J_q≤C_Q under A_q≅Z_Q. Choose a p-element h∈H∩N with a prescribed J_p-label; its A_q-label is zero. In P its conjugation is that of exp(s), s∈𝔰_P, because A_p acts trivially on P and its central twist is fixed. Normality gives [exp(s),P]≤L_P. In P/L_P the image of exp(s) is therefore central. The Lazard correspondence identifies centers and ideals, so [s,L]≤log L_P. Equivalently, this follows by taking the logarithm of the nilpotent conjugation operator exp(ad_s); all required denominators are prime to p because c<p.

Project hτ(h)∈N∩(P×Q) to P. Its logarithm is BCH(s,τs) plus twice the prescribed central label. Every nonlinear BCH term lies in log L_P, since it is a Lie word in s,τs containing a bracket, [s,L]≤log L_P, and log L_P is an ideal. Averaging under A_q kills s+τs and preserves the central label. It follows that twice that label, and hence the label, belongs to C_P. This proves J_p≤C_P. The same argument in the class-two factor Q, using the pure q-label, trivial own-prime action and averaging under A_p, proves J_q≤C_Q. These are original-group normality arguments, not quotient-transversal assumptions.

The kernel of H∩N→J is (S_P∩L_P)×(S_Q∩L_Q). Thus (3)–(4) imply

    |C_N(τ)|/|H∩N|
      ≥p^{dim C_P−dim J_p}q^{dim C_Q−dim J_q},          (5)

with nonnegative exponents. If T_p=T_q=1 and N≠1, a nonzero local normal subgroup meets its center and gives strict defect. If T_q=1 and T_p≠1, some nonzero T_p element acts nontrivially on Q/Z_Q, by faithfulness. Its commutators force L_Q≠1, hence C_Q=Z_Q, whereas J_q=1. The defect is strict.

If T_q≠1, then T_q=A_q. A lift acts fixed-point-freely on P/Z_P, so its commutators have full image on P/P′; the Frattini argument forces L_P=P. If T_p is proper in A_p, then dim C_P=r>dim J_p and the defect is again strict. Finally, if T_q=A_q and T_p=A_p, commutators with all of A_p give full Q/Z_Q, so Q≤N as well and N=G. All proper nontrivial τ-stable N therefore have strict defect. The strict-defect criterion proves simplicity.

### A direct Moufang witness

The subgroup G₀=(Z_P×Q)⋊A suffices for a local multiplication calculation. Its intersection with H is H₀=S_Q⋊{a z(a)}. The K representatives of all G₀/H₀ cosets lie in G₀, by the same explicit Heisenberg section used in the operator construction: for coordinates (u,t_p,t_q) the P component is zero, the Q vectors are (−R(−t_p)u,u), the Q center is zero and the torus label is (−t_p,−t_q). Right-H₀ reduction gives exactly those coordinates. Uniqueness of the ambient K representative shows that multiplication on these cosets is the ambient loop multiplication and stays within this set. Neither faithfulness nor generation by K∩G₀ is assumed for this restricted action.

Its multiplication is

    (u,t_p,t_q)·(v,s_p,s_q)
      =(u+R(t_p)v, t_p+s_p, t_q+s_q−β(u,v)).            (6)

Choose a∈A_p with R(a)≠1, v with (R(a)−1)v≠0, and w with β((R(a)−1)v,w)≠0. Let X=(0,a,0), Y=(v,0,0), Z=(w,0,0). The two sides (XY)(ZX) and (X(YZ))X have equal vector and A_p coordinates, but their F_q central entries are −β(R(a)v,w) and −β(v,w). They differ, proving properness directly. There is no use of CFSG or the simple Moufang classification.


## Proof of Theorem 0022: paired unitriangular algebras

### Parameters and construction

Fix n≥4 and distinct odd primes p>n−1 and q>n−2. Put r=ord_q(p), s=ord_p(q), E=F_{p^r}, F=F_{q^s}, and choose λ∈E× of order q and η∈F× of order p. Let N=n(n−1)/2.

Let L⁺ be the E-Lie algebra of strictly upper triangular n×n matrices, with basis e_ij for i<j. Its class is n−1 and its center is Ez, z=e_{1n}. Take a second copy L⁻, identify the two centers, and set all mixed brackets zero. The resulting algebra L has dimension 2N−1, center Ez and class n−1. Let σ_P swap the copies and fix z. The first-copy roots

    A={e_ij:1≤i<j<n}

form a copy of the strictly upper triangular (n−1)×(n−1) algebra, while the second-copy roots

    B={e_{i n}′:2≤i<n}

span an abelian subalgebra. Neither produces z internally. Their union is a subalgebra s_P of dimension N−1, and

    L=s_P ⊕ σ_P(s_P) ⊕ Ez.                                   (1)

Let A_q=C_q act on the first copy by diagonal weights (0,1,2,…,n−2,0) modulo q, and on the second by inverse weights. These weights are distinct except at the first and last coordinates, so every noncentral root has nontrivial weight under every nonidentity element of A_q. The action fixes z, preserves s_P and is inverted by σ_P. The root gap j−i gives the lower-central grading; the shadows of s_P and σ_P(s_P) are disjoint in each degree below the center.

Because n−1<p, the [Lazard correspondence](../packet/REFERENCES.md#lazard-bch-interface), arXiv:2310.17595v3 §2.3, Facts 2.13–2.14, gives the exponent-p group P=exp L and subgroup S_P=exp s_P; it also identifies ideals with normal subgroups and preserves automorphisms. Restriction of scalars from E to F_p is understood.

Use the auxiliary symplectic space W, form β, action R, extraspecial q-group Q, subgroup S_Q, and central-label map z from Theorem 0021, with the present field parameters. Thus A_p=(E,+), A=A_p×A_q, and

    G=(P×Q)⋊A,
    τ=(σ_P,σ_Q), with inversion on A,
    H=(S_P×S_Q)⋊{a z(a):a∈A},
    K={g∈G:τ(g)=g⁻¹}.

The only change is the paired unitriangular group P and its subgroup S_P. In particular, S_P need not be abelian. The central graph is still a subgroup normalizing both stabilizer factors.

### Generation, transversality and canonical group

The fixed-free A_q action on P/Z(P), together with Z(P)≤P′=Φ(P), makes the commutators with A_q generate P. The A_p action has no common fixed vector on Q/Z(Q), so its commutators generate Q. Therefore G′=P×Q. Also A⊆K. In an odd group, ⟨K⟩ is normal, since every τ-commutator belongs to K; its normal closure of A contains P and Q, so ⟨K⟩=G.

A normal subgroup of G contained in H cannot meet P or Q nontrivially: a nontrivial normal subgroup of a finite p-group meets its center, and H avoids Z(P)×Z(Q). It therefore centralizes P×Q. If a torus element acts by an inner automorphism on P, its A_q component is trivial because an inner automorphism has p-power order while the A_q action has q-power order; similarly the A_p component must be trivial by its action on Q. Faithfulness of the cross actions then gives C_G(P×Q)=Z(P)×Z(Q), which H also avoids. Hence H is core-free, and the folder action is faithful.

The torus labels are inverted by τ, whereas their central partners are fixed. Comparing an element of H with an element of τ(H) first in A, and then modulo Z(P)×Z(Q), forces the S_P and S_Q shadows to vanish: those halves are disjoint from their τ-images. The remaining central label must equal its negative, hence is zero in odd characteristic. Thus H∩τ(H)=1. The swaps and centers give

    |H|=|C_G(τ)|=p^{Nr} q^{2rs+1},     |G|=|H|².             (2)

The symmetric factorization lemma stated among the preliminaries therefore supplies transversality of the full inverted set K to every H-conjugate, the Bol identity and the G-loop property.

### Strict defect and simplicity

Let 1≠N₀◁G be τ-stable. Put L_P=N₀∩P, L_Q=N₀∩Q, C_P=L_P∩Z(P), C_Q=L_Q∩Z(Q), and let T_p×T_q and J_p×J_q be the images in A of N₀ and H∩N₀ respectively. The prime factors split because p≠q. The normal subgroup intersections give τ- and A-invariant Lazard ideals.

A_q averaging projects L onto Ez, because the center is its only trivial weight; it preserves each A_q-invariant ideal and commutes with τ. Projection to the positive part of the noncentral shadow is injective on s_P, by (1). Thus

    |C_{L_P}(τ)| / |S_P∩L_P| ≥ |C_P|,
    |C_{L_Q}(τ)| / |S_Q∩L_Q| ≥ |C_Q|.                       (3)

Any nontrivial L_P or L_Q meets the corresponding center. These estimates do not require S_P abelian.

The central-label inclusions J_p≤C_P and J_q≤C_Q follow by the original-group normality argument of the construction of Theorem 0021. For example, a p-element h∈H∩N₀ has zero A_q label and P-component s_P z_P, since A_p acts trivially on P. Normality forces s_P to centralize P modulo L_P, so [log s_P,L]⊆log L_P by the Lazard automorphism correspondence. The nonlinear terms in log(hτ(h)) therefore lie in log L_P. Averaging under A_q kills the noncentral linear terms and retains twice z_P, proving the p-label lies in C_P. The q-label proof is analogous, using the pure q-label and averaging under A_p. Thus

    |C_{N₀}(τ)| / |H∩N₀| ≥ (|C_P|/|J_p|)(|C_Q|/|J_q|).   (4)

Each factor is at least one. If T_p=T_q=1 and N₀≠1, a nontrivial derived-factor intersection contributes a strict center factor. If T_p≠1 but T_q=1, some T_p element moves a Q-plane, so L_Q≠1 and C_Q=Z(Q), while J_q=1. If T_q≠1 but T_p<A_p, then T_q=A_q and its fixed-free action forces L_P=P; the center P factor strictly exceeds J_p. If T_p=A_p and T_q=A_q, commutators force P,Q≤N₀ and N₀=G. Thus (4) is strict for every nontrivial proper τ-stable normal subgroup. The odd [strict-defect simplicity criterion](../packet/manuscripts/odd-folder-radicals.md#candidate-simplicity-criterion-using-τ-stable-normal-subgroups) makes the loop simple; no split form of N₀ is assumed.

### Properness and derived length

The subfolder on (Z(P)×Q)⋊A and the Q-only Moufang witness in the construction of Theorem 0021 are unchanged. In its coordinates the product is

    (u,t_p,t_q)·(v,s_p,s_q)
      =(u+R(t_p)v, t_p+s_p, t_q+s_q−β(u,v)).               (5)

Choose a with R(a)≠1, v with (R(a)−1)v≠0, and w with β((R(a)−1)v,w)≠0. For X=(0,a,0), Y=(v,0,0), Z=(w,0,0), the two Moufang expressions (XY)(ZX) and (X(YZ))X differ in their F_q central coordinates. The proof that (5) is the restriction of the ambient loop multiplication is the explicit section calculation in Theorem 0021; it does not depend on the internal brackets of s_P. Hence the loop is proper.

At the j-th derived stage of UT_n, the Lie algebra is spanned by e_ik with k−i≥2^j. Brackets add root gaps, and every root of gap at least 2^j is a bracket of two roots of gaps at least 2^{j−1}; induction gives equality. Its derived length is therefore ⌈log₂n⌉. Both copies embed in their central product, since their common center is identified rather than killed, and they commute; hence P has the same derived length. As G′=P×Q and Q has derived length 2,

    derived length(G)=1+⌈log₂n⌉.

Distinct primes satisfying the displayed bounds exist for every n. The construction therefore gives simple proper odd G-loops with unbounded canonical left-multiplication derived length. 


## Proof of Theorem 0023: wreath products and affine factorizations

### Odd monomial groups of increasing height

Fix p=7, q=29 and E=F_{7⁷}. The prime q has ord_q(7)=7, since 7⁷−1=6·29·4733 and 7 is not 1 modulo 29. Choose distinct primes r₁,…,r_h, all congruent to 1 modulo 7 and distinct from 7,q. They exist elementarily: given finitely many forbidden primes, take a=7 times their product. Every prime divisor ℓ of Φ₇(a)=1+a+⋯+a⁶ is new and different from 7; ord_ℓ(a)=7, so ℓ≡1 mod 7. Pick k_i∈F_{r_i}× of order 7.

Set U₀=1 and recursively define the regular wreath product

    U_i = C_{r_i} wr U_{i−1} = C_{r_i}^{U_{i−1}} ⋊ U_{i−1}.

Define an automorphism ψ_i of order 7 recursively: on U_{i−1} use ψ_{i−1}; on a base function b:U_{i−1}→C_{r_i}, set

    (ψ_i b)(u) = b(ψ_{i−1}^{−1}u)^{k_i}.

For U₀ the automorphism is identity; ψ₁ is the nontrivial power map k₁. Whenever U₀⋊<ψ₀> occurs, the bracket denotes a formal C7 acting trivially, not the trivial subgroup of Aut(U₀). The definition respects the regular translation action. Its seventh power is identity, and it is nontrivial for i≥1. Since the coinvariants of a regular permutation base are C_{r_i},

    U_i,ab ≅ C_{r_i}×U_{i−1,ab},

and ψ_i acts on each cyclic factor by the corresponding k_j. Thus ψ_i−1 is invertible on U_i,ab.

The action of U_{i−1}⋊<ψ_{i−1}> on the new base is faithful: an element uψ^j acts by a regular coordinate permutation followed by scalar k_i^j. Identity forces j=0 and then u=1. Since r_i divides none of the previous groups, the Fitting subgroup of U_i⋊<ψ_i> is precisely its r_i-base: any nilpotent normal subgroup centralizes the base in all other prime components, and the displayed action has trivial centralizer. Therefore

    h_F(U_i⋊<ψ_i>)=i+1.                                      (1)

For i=0 this says h_F(C7)=1; the same reasoning without ψ gives h_F(U_i)=i for i≥1.

### Monomial affine group and exact factorization

Put U=U_h, ψ=ψ_h, Q=C_q^U, and T=Q⋊U using the regular action. Let V=E^U as an F7-vector space. Q acts by independent q-th-root scalars on coordinates and U by the regular coordinate permutation. Each coordinate is an irreducible 7-dimensional F7 C_q-module, the coordinate modules are pairwise nonisomorphic for Q, and U acts transitively on them. Therefore V is a faithful irreducible F7 T-module.

Let φ apply field Frobenius a↦a⁷ to each coordinate and permute indices by ψ. Then φ normalizes T, induces ψ on U and the power map 7 plus index permutation on Q, and has order 7. In the special case h=0, the index permutation is trivial and φ is the field Frobenius. The group

    T_ab ≅ C_q × U_ab

has φ acting on its C_q factor by seventh power and on the others by k_i. Thus φ−1 is invertible on T_ab. For any 1≤i<7 and t∈T, the automorphism α=inn(t)φ^i has the same fixed-point-free action on T_ab. The subgroup [T,α] is normal in T; in T/[T,α], α acts trivially, so its abelianization is both a quotient of T_ab and fixed by α. It is trivial. As T is solvable, the quotient is trivial; hence

    [T,inn(t)φ^i]=T.                                         (2)

Set X=V⋊(T⋊<φ>). Equation (2) gives X′=V⋊T and X/X′≅C7. Let Y1=T⋊<φ>. Choose a φ-invariant F7-hyperplane W containing (φ−1)V and v∉W; it exists because a 7-element in characteristic 7 has nonzero fixed quotient. Put g=(v,φ) and Y0=⟨W,g⟩. Since the norm 1+φ+⋯+φ⁶=(φ−1)⁶, g⁷∈W and |Y0|=|V|. For 1≤i<7, every element w g^i has translation component congruent to i v mod W, whereas (1−φ^i)V⊆W, so it has no fixed point on V. Nonzero translations in W have none. Hence Y0 is regular on V and

    X=Y0Y1,  Y0∩Y1=1,  X′Y0=X′Y1=X.                         (3)

The action of T⋊<φ> on V is faithful: a trivial monomial transformation has trivial index permutation and all q-scalars trivial, while a nonzero φ power induces nontrivial Frobenius and hence cannot be canceled by scalar multiplication. Therefore C_X(V)=V and V is a minimal normal subgroup. Y1 is corefree by the faithful affine action. A nontrivial normal subgroup inside Y0 would meet V and contain V, impossible since |Y0|=|V| and Y0≠V. Thus Y0 is corefree too. By the exact-factorization construction stated among the preliminaries the canonical faithful generated folder has

    G={(x,y)∈X²:xy∈X′},  H=G∩(Y0×Y1),
    K={(x,x⁻¹):x∈X},     τ(x,y)=(y,x).

The resulting loop has odd order |X|. Its canonical stabilizer has order |X′|>1, so the loop is nonassociative. Properness follows after the simplicity check below. The full multiplication group is not being identified with G.

### Simplicity without quotient-folder assumptions

Put M=V×V. As the affine complement acts faithfully, C_G(M)=M. The two V coordinates are pairwise nonisomorphic irreducible T²-modules, and |T| is prime to 7 because all the selected primes differ from 7. Every nonzero normal subgroup of G meets M; a τ-stable one therefore contains all M by semisimplicity and the coordinate swap.

Modulo M,

    G/M=(T×T)⋊<t>,  t=(φ,φ⁻¹),
    HM/M={ (φ^i,aφ⁻i) : a∈T, i∈F7 }.

Let 1≠N◁G be τ-stable. If its image Nbar modulo M contains an element with nonzero t exponent, equation (2), applied separately to the two coordinates, shows commutators with T×T generate T×T. Then Nbar is all of G/M and N=G. Thus for a proper N, Nbar≤T×T. Its two coordinate projections agree by τ-invariance; write this subgroup as R≤T. We do not assume Nbar=R×R. The displayed H image gives

    [G:HN]=[T:R].

If two diagonal-inverse section elements have equal image modulo N, their first coordinates differ by an element of V⋊R. Consequently

    |KN/N|≥[X:V⋊R]=7[T:R]>[G:HN].

The odd τ-stable-normal strict-defect criterion implies simplicity: because fixed points lift in this odd group, |KN/N|/[G:HN]=|C_N(τ)|/|H∩N|. No projection of K to a normal quotient is assumed to be a loop transversal.

For properness, the nontrivial normal subgroup X′×1 of G has its identity orbit contained in the section points indexed by Y1, a proper subset of X. Indeed (x,x⁻¹)∈(X′×1)H forces x⁻¹∈Y1. The faithful action makes this normal subgroup's orbit nontrivial; hence canonical LMlt is imprimitive. The simple-Moufang primitivity result stated among the preliminaries then excludes Moufang. This uses primitivity, not an inference from solvability alone.

### Fitting height

In L=T⋊<φ>, the action of U⋊<φ> on Q is faithful: U acts by regular coordinate permutations and a nonzero φ power applies the nontrivial seventh-power scalar automorphism on each C_q. As q is new to U and 7, the same nilpotent-normal-centralizer argument as in (1) gives Fit(L)=Q. By (1),

    h_F(L)=h+2.

Also O_7(L)=1: a normal 7-subgroup centralizes the normal q-subgroup Q and hence lies in C_L(Q)=Q. In X=V⋊L the Fitting subgroup contains V; its non-7 components centralize V and are trivial because C_X(V)=V, while its 7-component has no nontrivial image in L. Thus Fit(X)=V and h_F(X)=h+3.

The canonical folder group G is a subgroup of X×X projecting onto X. Fitting height does not increase under subgroups or quotients of finite solvable groups, so h_F(G)=h_F(X)=h+3. As h is arbitrary, this proves Theorem 0023. 


## Consequences and further questions

The three theorems rule out completing the odd-order classification by imposing a universal bound on any of these three group invariants. They do not rule out a uniform structural classification: unbounded families can still admit a concise description.

The constructions raise concrete questions. When do two choices of parameters produce isomorphic loops? Which additional simple loops arise by replacing the paired Lie algebras or the regular wreath-product tower? Can their stabilizer geometry be characterized intrinsically, so that the constructions become parts of an exhaustion theorem rather than examples alone?

The proofs above depend on the folder, canonical-inversion, strict-defect, and primitivity results cited in the preliminaries. No claim of an independent formal verification, complete classification, or priority over the literature is made here.

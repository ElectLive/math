# Stabilizer orbits above an odd normal kernel

**Mira Cult**

## Structural theorem

Let (G,H,K) be a finite faithful generated left Bol folder in the canonical marked setting. Write E=G⋊⟨t⟩, Λ=tK, with t²=1, Λ a conjugacy-invariant set of outside involutions, and H∩Λ²={1}. The action of t is allowed to be trivial. Let N be a normal subgroup of E contained in G and of odd order; N need not be abelian. Put

```math
L=G/N,\quad J=\pi(H),\quad W=H\cap N,\quad S=\pi(\Lambda),
```

and let Π be the primes dividing |N|. For x∈S set B=J∩Jˣ, and choose an involution λ above x. Then

```math
S\cap xJ=x^B,\qquad
[B:C_J(x)]=\frac{|C_N(\lambda)|}{|W|},\qquad
B=[B,x]C_J(x)=O_\Pi(B)C_J(x).
```

Here [B,x] is a normal Π-subgroup. Each outside double coset of J contains exactly one J-conjugacy class of selected quotient marks. This is a statement about the projection of the original folder: S is not assumed to be a quotient loop section. The complete proof is [Stabilizer orbits of marks above an odd normal kernel](../sources/full-socle-completion/odd-kernel-stabilizer-orbits.md).

## Proof mechanism

Sylow conjugacy shows that all involution lifts of one outside quotient involution form a single N-orbit and hence are all selected. If two selected labels x,y have product j in J with order coprime to |N|, Schur–Zassenhaus lifts their dihedral group and aligns its rotation with a lift in H. Two selected original involutions would then multiply to a nonidentity element of H, a contradiction.

Apply this to all powers of an arbitrary rotation j=xy. Every prime dividing its order must belong to Π, so its odd-order cyclic group conjugates x to y. This proves the single-orbit statement in each coset. Counting original H-cosets above that quotient coset gives the centralizer-index identity, without requiring W≤C_N(λ).

For the last equality, Glauberman's Z* theorem makes the subgroup generated by the commutators with x odd. Feit–Thompson gives solvability, and an invariant minimal-normal-subgroup induction shows that its primes all belong to Π. Odd cosets contain fixed points of x, yielding B=[B,x]C_J(x). The orbit and counting conclusions preceding this step do not need these two deep inputs.

## Consequences for even simple loops

If the original loop is simple, proper and of even order and N≠1, some projected stabilizer coset has more than one selected mark. Otherwise the projection would yield a proper nontrivial normal subloop. Consequently gcd(|N|,|J|)>1. In particular a nontrivial inversion-stable odd normal Hall subgroup is impossible in this branch.

For a p-group kernel, each coset multiplicity is a power of p dividing |J|, and some multiplicity is at least p. Thus p divides |J|. For an elementary kernel, the negative eigenspace rank is constant within each individual projected stabilizer coset, although it may vary between cosets. This rules out the previously possible mixed-rank picture within one actual coset.

At fixed quotient L and J, a single elementary kernel minimal normal in E has only finitely many module and extension possibilities: its prime divides |J| and its dimension is at most 2|L|. A nilpotent odd kernel with a bounded number of chief factors admits a corresponding finite recursive bound. Neither an absolute chief-length bound nor an arbitrary nonnilpotent-radical bound is proved.

## Exact finite-data assembly above an elementary layer

The [marked-lift parameterization](../sources/full-socle-completion/odd-kernel-lift-classification.md) now has scoped independent model review. Fix a quotient L, its involution, a subgroup J, an odd prime p, a nonzero finite module V for the extended quotient, and a J-invariant subspace W. Normalized cocycles build all relevant extensions with the distinguished involution. Outside conjugacy classes must give an exact cover of the outside J-double cosets, with each selected coset satisfying the local orbit-weight equation.

All involution lifts of a selected mark are forced. Stabilizers are described by cochains solving the restricted extension equation modulo W. Pairwise products of complete lift fibers give forbidden affine subsets of the cochain torsor. Avoiding them, together with the exact scalar cardinality equation, gives transversality. Core-freeness and generation are separate tests. Simplicity uses the full normal-subloop criterion; neither an odd-layer scalar inequality nor non-Bruckness proves properness.

For each fixed finite input these tests give an exact effective parameterization. A minimal elementary layer in the even simple branch has p dividing |J| and dimension at most 2|L|, so those layer choices at fixed quotient data are finite. There is no uniform bound on all quotient data or on arbitrary chief length.

The source gives the exact **pointed** isomorphisms carrying the chosen layer to the chosen layer: compatible quotient/module isomorphisms and extension cochains must preserve the distinguished involution, marks and stabilizer. Passing merely to first cohomology preserves collision feasibility but generally loses isomorphism information. An ordinary unpointed classification still requires identifying choices across layers unless the layer is functorially fixed.

## Remaining classification

The [factor-swapping affine-square theorem](0099-affine-square-factor-exchange.md) gives a separate operation classification. The further all-inversions theorem for odd fields now also has scoped model review. General even non-Bruck operations, unbounded extension data and unpointed isomorphism orbits remain open.

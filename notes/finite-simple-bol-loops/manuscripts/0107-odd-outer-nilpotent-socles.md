# 0107. Nilpotent transitive socle actions require an even outer automorphism group

**Mira Cult**

**Theorem.** Let $`G`$ be the faithful left multiplication group of a finite simple proper left Bol loop. Suppose its entire socle is $`S=T^\Omega`$ with $`T`$ nonabelian simple and $`|\Omega|\ge2`$. If the induced permutation group on the simple factors is nilpotent and transitive, then $`\operatorname{Out}(T)`$ has even order. In particular no complete simple type, whose outer group is trivial, satisfies these hypotheses. The theorem neither requires that the action be regular nor puts a bound on its outer kernel. An odd prime can divide both the coordinate group and the outer group.

Here is the difficulty. The point stabilizer singles out a proper subgroup in each simple factor. Even when all these subgroups are equivalent under transport, an outer automorphism can move one subgroup to a different *inner* conjugacy class. For instance, Frobenius in $`\operatorname{PSL}_2(32)`$ cycles five distinct inner classes of translation four-groups. It does not suffice to pretend that the product of one chosen class in each factor is preserved.

We first track the inner classes as colors. The group generated by left translations leaves one set of color patterns invariant; formal inversion either preserves that set or moves it by a single constant color of order two. Odd outer order rules out the second possibility. On one actual color fiber, the marked involutions then yield a matrix with every entry equal. Its tensor traces force a mark that permutes no factor, which the odd outer group cannot accommodate.

## The color module of the actual stabilizer

The [whole-socle projection theorem](0105-nilpotent-socle-stabilizer-projections.md) gives $`H\to C`$ and

```math
1<U_i=\pi_i(H\cap S)<T_i .
```

The group $`H`$ carries these *actual subgroups* between its coordinates. Identify each $`T_i`$ with $`T`$ using transports from a fixed coordinate through $`H`$; all the $`U_i`$ then become one $`U`$. Let $`\mathcal L`$ be the set of $`T`$-inner conjugacy classes of the $`\operatorname{Aut}(T)`$-conjugates of $`U`$. We call these classes colors. They are a transitive set of $`O=\operatorname{Out}(T)`$. Assume for the next two sections only that the **permutation image** $`P`$ of $`O`$ on $`\mathcal L`$ is abelian. A faithful transitive abelian permutation group is regular, so identify colors with $`P`$ additively, taking $`[U]_T`$ as zero. This assumption holds for abelian $`O`$, even when its local stabilizer is nontrivial; in that case $`P=O/\operatorname{Stab}_O([U]_T)`$.

Use the [actual left folder](../sources/foundations/C0001.md) and [formal inversion](../sources/foundations/C0007.md). Thus $`E=G\rtimes\langle t\rangle,\ \Lambda=tK`$ is an invariant set of outside involutions containing $`t`$, and the faithful left translations $`K`$ generate $`G`$. As automorphisms of $`S`$, $`E`$ acts on color tuples through $`P^\Omega\rtimes\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)`$, via the coordinate frames just fixed. The image of $`G`$ there is

```math
D\rtimes C,\qquad D\le P^\Omega,\qquad [C,D]=D .        \tag{1}
```

Indeed $`H`$ fixes the actual tuple $`u=(U_i)`$, so every member has zero color shift, and $`H\to C`$ gives **all pure permutations** $`C`$ in the color image. Define $`D`$ to be its true base kernel, not an assumed product. Every abelian quotient of this image is covered by $`H`$. Otherwise pull back a maximal subgroup of the abelian quotient containing the image of $`H`$: its preimage in $`G`$ is proper and normal, and contains $`H`$ and $`S\ne1`$. This contradicts the [simple normal-cut criterion](../sources/foundations/C0001.md). In particular the abelian quotient $`D/[C,D]`$, given by discarding the $`C`$-part after reducing the phase modulo commutators, is trivial, proving (1). Importantly we did not infer that $`H`$ covers the full outer top.

**Module lemma.** For a finite nilpotent group $`C`$ acting on a finite abelian group $`D`$, if $`D=[C,D]`$, then

```math
D^C=0,\qquad H^1(C,D)=0.                  \tag{2}
```

For each prime $`p\mid |D|`$ work with $`V=D_p`$, likewise $`V=[C,V]`$. Write $`C=C_p\times L`$ with $`L`$ its Hall $`p'`$-factor; this allows $`p\mid|C|`$. Averaging over $`L`$ splits $`V=V^L\oplus[L,V]`$: multiply the orbit sum by the inverse of $`|L|`$ on this finite $`p`$-group. Its kernel is $`[L,V]`$ since $`x-\operatorname{av}(x)`$ is a sum of differences. The projection commutes with $`C`$, so $`W=V^L=[C_p,W]`$. If $`W\ne0`$, then $`W/pW\ne0`$. The finite $`p`$-group $`C_p`$ has a nonzero fixed vector on its dual (count orbits modulo $`p`$, including zero); this functional kills all differences, contrary to $`W=[C_p,W]`$. Thus $`V^L=0`$. For a crossed homomorphism $`z:C\to V`$, summing the cocycle identity over $`L`$ shows that $`z|_L`$ is principal. Subtract this principal cocycle on $`C`$. Now $`z(\ell)=0`$ for $`\ell\in L`$. For $`c\in C_p`$, compute $`z(c\ell)=z(\ell c)`$ to get $`z(c)=\ell z(c)`$, hence $`z(c)=0`$. The residual cocycle vanishes, proving both parts componentwise.

Write the actual action of $`t`$ on colors as $`(v,\pi)`$. Its square being one gives $`v+\pi v=0`$. Its normalization of the $`G`$-image gives $`\pi C\pi^{-1}=C,\ \pi D=D`$ and $`cv-v\in D`$ for every $`c\in C`$: conjugate the pure permutations by $`v\pi`$. The differences $`c\mapsto cv-v`$ form a $`D`$-valued cocycle. By (2) they equal $`cd-d`$ for some $`d\in D`$; $`v-d`$ is therefore constant on transitive $`\Omega`$. There is a **unique** such constant modulo $`D`$, since $`D^C=0`$. Write it as $`\underline a=(a,\ldots,a)`$. Then $`2\underline a=-(d+\pi d)\in D`$, so $`2a=0`$. In particular the color orbits of the zero tuple under $`G`$ and under $`tG`$ are respectively

```math
D\quad\text{and}\quad D+\underline a,
\qquad a\in P[2].                            \tag{3}
```

These are assertions about two genuine color orbits, not a claim that every marked element has a constant color vector.

## Principal fiber, not the trace on a union of orbits

Suppose $`a=0`$. Put $`X=G\cdot u`$, using the tuple of *actual subgroups*, not of colors. Over each color pattern in $`D`$ the fiber is the full Cartesian product of the corresponding inner classes, since $`S=T^\Omega`$ independently realizes every inner conjugation. Therefore $`t`$ preserves $`X`$ by (3). The action is $`G`$-transitive, $`H`$ fixes $`u`$, and $`X`$ is an honest $`E`$-set. The [coherent tuple operator](0106-nilpotent-transitive-even-field-psl2.md#uniformity-on-an-actual-stabilized-tuple) gives $`\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\rho_X(\lambda)=(|\Lambda|/|X|)\mathbf J_X`$: the transversal maps $`u`$ uniformly to $`X`$, $`t`$ preserves the uniform vector and conjugacy invariance makes every column the same.

Let $`X_0=Y^\Omega`$ over the zero color, where $`Y`$ comprises the $`T`$-conjugates of $`U`$, of size $`m=[T:N_T(U)]>1`$ by simplicity and $`1<U<T`$. Every mark preserving the zero **pattern** permutes $`X_0`$; every other mark maps the *whole fiber* disjointly away. Taking the principal block on physical basis vectors gives the positive identity

```math
\sum_{\substack{\lambda\in\Lambda\\ \lambda X_0=X_0}}
\rho_{X_0}(\lambda)
= (|\Lambda|/|X|)\mathbf J_{X_0}.                \tag{4}
```

The left side is nonempty. On this smaller product each summand really is an involution with inverse arrows on every exchanged pair. For clarity: decompose each local permutation space into constants and the sum-zero space; for every nonempty subset of coordinates the trace of the corresponding tensor diagonal block of (4) is zero. The generating polynomial at equal variable $`z`$ is consequently the nonzero constant equal to the number of summands. Each exchanged pair of a given mark contributes $`1+(m-1)z^2`$ because the arrows are mutual inverses (on the deleted space their composite has trace $`m-1`$); each fixed site contributes $`1+(F_i-1)z`$ with $`F_i`$ its actual fixed count there. If every mark in (4) exchanged a pair, that common nonconstant quadratic would divide the nonzero constant. **A selected outside mark with identity coordinate permutation exists.** We have neither assumed the whole $`G`$-orbit is Cartesian nor replaced (4) by a trace over two disjoint color orbits. If $`a\ne0`$, that latter trace would not justify this argument.

Consequently when $`a=0`$ the coordinate images of $`G`$ and $`E`$ coincide. In that situation conjugation by $`t`$ acts on $`C`$ by an inner automorphism. But $`t`$ inverts every original translation in $`K`$, whose coordinate images generate $`C`$; thus it acts by inversion on $`C_{\rm ab}`$. The latter has exponent at most two. Since a nontrivial finite odd $`p`$-group has a nontrivial abelianization, nilpotence forces $`C`$ to be a 2-group. In particular for every abelian color image, a nilpotent transitive $`C`$ with a nontrivial odd Sylow factor requires **a nonzero uniform element of** $`P[2]`$ **in (3)**, not just an outer color action of even order anywhere in the group. This last restriction does not by itself settle the even-outer case.

## Excluding odd-order outer automorphism groups

For finite nonabelian simple $`T`$, odd-order $`O=\operatorname{Out}(T)`$ is cyclic, trivial allowed. This is read from Lucchini, Marion and Tracey, [*Generating maximal subgroups of finite almost simple groups*, Proposition 3.11, Table 2 (p. 21)](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2019.43): every noncyclic possibility has even order; in particular the triality twist has cyclic group of order $`3f`$, the simple Tits group has outer order two, and the sporadic entries have outer order at most two. Only this consequence of the classification table is used. Hence $`P`$ has odd order and (3) gives $`a=0`$; a coordinate-identity outside mark exists.

On the other hand $`E`$ here embeds in $`\operatorname{Aut}(S)`$. Indeed $`C_G(S)=1`$: that normal centralizer intersects $`S`$ trivially since $`Z(S)=1`$, whereas any nontrivial normal subgroup of finite $`G`$ contains a minimal normal subgroup in $`S`$. Thus the action of $`G`$ on $`S`$ is faithful. If an outside member of formal $`E`$ acted there like an element of $`G`$, the involutory canonical automorphism of $`G`$ would be inner: two automorphisms of $`G`$ agreeing on its characteristic $`S`$ agree on $`G`$, since their values on every $`g`$ induce the same conjugation on $`S`$. Inner canonical inversion forces $`O_2(G)\ne1`$ by the [reviewed Bruck-isotopy consequence](../sources/factor-permutation/exact-factorization-criterion.md#inner-inversion-and-the-generalized-fitting-subgroup). This is impossible since any nontrivial normal 2-group would contain a minimal normal subgroup of $`G`$ in the specified socle, and every minimal normal there is a product of nonabelian simple factors. Therefore $`G/S`$ has index two in the *actual* outer wreath image of $`E/S\le O^\Omega\rtimes\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)`$. A coordinate-identity outside element would lie in its permutation kernel, a subgroup of the odd-order group $`O^\Omega`$; its image in the sign quotient of order two would then be nontrivial. This is impossible, completing the proof.

## Evidence and limits

The [finite controls](../sources/code/verify_nilpotent_colored_socle_descent.py) exhibit the five moving classes over the field of size 32 and show why dropping the normal-cut condition can leave an invariant color coset with no constant representative. The [independent implementation](../sources/code/check_e67_mixed_colors_and_physical_block.py) tests a mixed-primary module, a nonnilpotent countercontrol, all tensor-block traces of a physical non-Cartesian orbit, and three moving seven-class families over the field of size 128. The [evidence record](../normal-layer-evidence.md) identifies the exact source and review.

For even outer group the color calculation leaves a precise obstruction: a nonzero order-two color shift is required when the color action is abelian and the coordinate group has an odd Sylow factor. It does not say which such shifts actually support a loop. Nor does this argument decide intransitive or nonnilpotent factor actions, mixed socles, or the unrestricted classification.

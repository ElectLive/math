# 0106. No nilpotent transitive action on an even-field PSL₂ socle

**Mira Cult**

**Theorem.** Let $`Q`$ be a finite simple proper left Bol loop and $`G`$ its faithful left multiplication group. Suppose its entire socle is

```math
S=\operatorname{PSL}_2(2^f)^n,\qquad f\ge2,\quad n\ge2 .
```

Then the permutation group induced by $`G`$ on these $`n`$ simple factors cannot be both nilpotent and transitive. No bound on the outer kernel, regularity of the action, splitting of the top or surjectivity of the point stabilizer onto the whole top is assumed. The theorem does not exclude multiple factor orbits, nonnilpotent coordinate actions, or these groups as sections of larger socles.

The starting point is [the nontrivial proper-projection theorem](0105-nilpotent-socle-stabilizer-projections.md). On each factor the actual point-stabilizer intersection defines a projective object. The objects together form a tuple. The marked involutions act uniformly on its orbit, and their fixed-point counts satisfy a polynomial identity stronger than the scalar first moment. The identity forces a mark that moves no coordinate. It also isolates a single small obstruction: five-tori in $`A_5`$, where a field involution fixes no torus. A separate argument with the actual field labels and their lifts closes that case.

## Uniformity on an actual stabilized tuple

Work in the actual [faithful left folder](../sources/foundations/C0001.md) with [formal inversion](../sources/foundations/C0007.md): $`E=G\rtimes\langle t\rangle,\;\Lambda=tK`$ is an invariant set of signed involutions, $`t\in\Lambda,\;K`$ generates $`G`$, and its left translations represent $`G/H`$ exactly once. Let a finite $`E`$-set $`X`$ be transitive for $`G`$, with a point $`x\in X`$ fixed by $`H`$. Then on $`\mathbb C[X]`$,

```math
\sum_{a\in\Lambda}\rho(a)=\frac{|\Lambda|}{|X|}\,\mathbf J,       \tag{1}
```

where $`\mathbf J`$ is the all-ones matrix. Indeed $`\sum k`$ sends the basis vector $`e_x`$ to a uniform sum, because $`H\le G_x`$; left multiplication by $`t`$ preserves that uniform vector. The marked sum commutes with $`G`$ by actual conjugacy invariance, so every basis vector has the same image. This needs neither an irreducible representation nor a loop quotient and imposes no decomposition requirement on $`H`$.

Suppose now that $`X=\prod_{i\in\Omega}Y_i`$, all $`|Y_i|=d>1`$, under a genuine product action by coordinate permutations and bijective arrows. A mark $`a`$ has coordinate permutation $`\sigma_a`$ of order dividing two. At an $`a`$-fixed $`i`$, write $`F_i(a)`$ for the number of fixed points of its actual arrow on $`Y_i`$. Then as a polynomial in independent commuting variables $`z_i`$,

```math
\sum_{a\in\Lambda}
\prod_{\sigma_ai=i}\bigl(1+(F_i(a)-1)z_i\bigr)
\prod_{\{i,j\}\;\mathrm{exchanged}}\bigl(1+(d-1)z_i z_j\bigr)
=|\Lambda|.                                                    \tag{2}
```

To see this, decompose the local permutation space as constants plus its sum-zero subspace. The tensor product decomposes into summands indexed by subsets $`D\subseteq\Omega`$ at which the sum-zero subspace is used. The diagonal block of (1) on each nonempty summand is zero; its trace on the empty summand is $`|\Lambda|`$. For a single mark, such a trace is zero unless $`\sigma_a D=D`$. On a selected fixed coordinate it contributes $`F_i(a)-1`$; when both endpoints of an exchanged pair are selected, it contributes $`d-1`$, because the two arrows are actual inverses. Multiplying and summing gives the coefficient of $`\prod_{i\in D}z_i`$. This proves (2) even if the local deleted spaces are reducible, $`D`$ is not $`G`$-invariant, or there are many different decorrelated or correlated marked classes.

Three consequences will be useful.

1. **A coordinate-identity mark is compulsory.** Set all $`z_i=z`$. Every summand with an exchanged pair has $`1+(d-1)z^2`$ as a factor. This nonconstant polynomial cannot divide the nonzero constant in (2). Equivalently evaluate at $`z=\sqrt{-1/(d-1)}`$. The conclusion concerns the coordinate permutation only; its arrows need not be identity.
2. If every physical involutory fixed arrow has a fixed point on its local $`Y_i`$, then all marks have at least one fixed point on $`X`$, and any mark exchanging a pair has at least $`d`$: an exchanged pair has exactly $`d`$ jointly fixed choices. The trace of (1) says the **mean** number of fixed points is one. Thus no selected mark can exchange a pair. A nontrivial transitive coordinate image makes that impossible, since $`t\in\Lambda,\;K=t\Lambda`$ generates $`G`$.
3. Even when some fixed arrows have no fixed point, the mean number $`s`$ of fixed coordinates of a mark is at least $`n/2`$. For $`\delta_i=F_i-1`$ on fixed coordinates the linear coefficient gives mean $`\sum_i\delta_i=0`$. Twice the quadratic coefficient at equal variables gives mean $`(\sum_i\delta_i)^2-\sum_i\delta_i^2+(d-1)(n-s)=0`$. Since $`-1\le\delta_i\le d-1`$, one has $`\delta_i^2\le(d-2)\delta_i+(d-1)`$, implying the assertion. An average over selected *physical elements*, not unweighted class representatives, is essential. The binary square with its four marked reflections from the regular cyclic group of order four attains $`s=n/2`$ (see the [finite controls](../sources/code/verify_coherent_tuple_mark_moment.py)).

## A canonical geometry for every nontrivial proper local subgroup

Put $`q=2^f,\;T=\operatorname{PSL}_2(q)=\operatorname{PGL}_2(q),\;L=q(q-1)(q+1)`$. By [the entire-socle projection theorem](0105-nilpotent-socle-stabilizer-projections.md), $`H`$ induces $`C`$ and every $`1<U_i=\pi_i(H\cap S)<T_i`$. In this section nilpotence is needed only for those two inputs. The actual stabilizer of coordinate $`i`$ in $`H`$ acts by semilinear automorphisms normalizing $`U_i`$; the $`U_i`$ are transported by $`H`$. An ordinary nonidentity involution of $`T`$ is unipotent and all are $`T`$-conjugate. The only other square-one local arrows are identity and, if $`f`$ is even, semilinear involutions $`T`$-conjugate to the half-field Frobenius. For these facts, subgroup types and Lang conjugacy, see Guralnick–Zieve, [*Polynomials with PSL(2) monodromy*, Appendix A, Theorem A.1, Lemmas A.2–A.4 and the discussion after A.5](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v172-n2-p11-p.pdf). We next identify a **functorial object** of an $`\operatorname{Aut}(T)`$-set $`Y`$ fixed by the normalizer of $`U_i`$. In every case $`T`$ is transitive on $`Y`$ and $`d=|Y|>1`$.

* If $`O_2(U_i)\ne1`$, this nontrivial 2-group fixes a unique projective point: conjugate it into an ambient unipotent Sylow 2-group, whose nonidentity elements all have the same unique fixed point. Its normalizer fixes that point. Take $`Y=\mathbb P^1(q)`$.
* A nontrivial cyclic group of odd order, or the rotation subgroup of a dihedral subgroup of order $`2m`$ with odd $`m>1`$, has an intrinsic unordered pair of eigenlines. For the dihedral case its odd rotation subgroup is characteristic; for the cyclic case use the group itself. If the lines are rational use the $`T`$-transitive space of unordered pairs of distinct rational points. Otherwise use the $`T`$-transitive space of nonsplit conjugate pairs, equivalently of full nonsplit tori. The torus normalizer in $`T`$ has order $`M=2(q+1)`$ and its coset space has $`d=q(q-1)/2`$ points. The two types cannot be interchanged by a semilinear automorphism.
* If $`U_i\simeq\operatorname{PSL}_2(r)`$ for $`r=2^e\ge4`$ a proper subfield size (including $`A_5\simeq\operatorname{PSL}_2(4)`$ when proper), use the set of rational points fixed by its Sylow 2-subgroups. There are $`r+1`$ such Sylows. Distinct ones have distinct fixed points: otherwise they lie in the same elementary abelian ambient unipotent group and generate a 2-subgroup of $`U_i`$, contrary to maximality. The abstract $`\operatorname{PSL}_2(r)`$ action on its Sylows is its standard doubly transitive action. Choose projective coordinates in the ambient line sending three such points to $`\infty,0,1`$. The Sylow at infinity is a group of $`r`$ translations, regular on the other $`r`$ points, so those points form an $`r`$-element additive subgroup of $`\mathbb F_q`$ containing 1. The two-point stabilizer acts by $`r-1`$ distinct scalings; they are the unique subgroup $`\mathbb F_r^\times`$ in $`\mathbb F_q^\times`$. Its orbit of 1 accounts for all nonzero points of the additive subgroup. The point set is therefore exactly a projective subline in the $`T`$-orbit of $`\mathbb P^1(r)`$. The family of these sublines is preserved by all field automorphisms, and is nontrivial (the ambient projective group is transitive on points and can move the proper subline). This proof does not infer conjugacy of embeddings from a mere list of abstract subgroup isomorphism types.

These cases exhaust $`1<U_i<T`$: a Borel subgroup has a normal elementary abelian 2-group and cyclic odd quotient, so an even subgroup in it has nontrivial normal 2-part and an odd subgroup in it is cyclic. Subgroups of the two dihedral groups are covered. $`A_4`$ has normal four-group, and $`S_4`$ is not a subgroup in this characteristic (no element of order four). An $`A_5`$ subgroup requires $`f`$ even by Dickson's exceptional condition, hence $`\mathbb F_4\subseteq\mathbb F_q`$; it is the whole group when $`q=4`$. The remaining even-characteristic subfield $`\operatorname{PGL}_2(r)`$ equals $`\operatorname{PSL}_2(r)`$; $`r=2`$ gives a dihedral group. The case selection is intrinsic by group type and the rationality of the eigenlines, so $`H`$ fixes the resulting tuple in $`X=Y^n`$, with $`G`$ transitive on $`X`$ already via $`S`$. The formal $`E`$-action is by all the corresponding semilinear arrows on this same geometry, even when inversion does not fix the base tuple.

Every square-one local arrow fixes a point on each of the rational-point, rational-pair and subline geometries. Indeed, after $`T`$-conjugation, an inner unipotent fixes its unique rational point, stabilizes a rational pair (use the involution interchanging 0 and infinity), and preserves the standard subline (use $`x\mapsto x+1`$). The pure half-field preserves all three standard objects, and identity does as well. By (2), consequence 2, none of these geometries can occur.

For a nonsplit torus the inner involution still fixes at least one such torus, because the dihedral torus normalizer contains involutions and the ambient simple group has one class. But the pure half-field fixes **no** nonsplit eigenpair: write $`q=r^2`$ and represent one eigenline by $`\xi\in\mathbb F_{q^2}\setminus\mathbb F_q`$. If $`\xi^r=\xi`$ or $`\xi^r=\xi^q=\xi^{r^2}`$, applying Frobenius or its inverse yields $`\xi\in\mathbb F_r`$, impossible. Hence the only proper-projection survivor in *any* such transitive situation is the nonsplit torus case with $`f`$ even and a selected half-field arrow at some fixed coordinate. In particular $`f`$ odd is excluded without any estimate.

## Which coordinate groups can survive?

The coordinate-identity mark from (2) says the permutation of the distinguished $`t`$ is in $`C`$, since the signed coordinate labels are in the coset $`tC`$. Consequently conjugation by $`t`$ induces an inner automorphism on $`C`$. But it inverts the images of the generators $`K`$, hence induces inversion on $`C_\mathrm{ab}`$. Therefore $`C_\mathrm{ab}`$ has exponent at most two. This conclusion uses the stabilized tuple, not the assertion that an arbitrary faithful encoding is reconstructible. For a finite nilpotent $`C`$ a nontrivial odd Sylow factor has a nontrivial abelian quotient of odd prime order. It follows that the transitive $`C`$ must be a **2-group**, hence $`n`$ is a power of two.

There must also be a selected mark fixing **no** coordinate. Indeed if all fixed a coordinate, their physical coordinate permutations, now all in $`C`$, would lie in the union of conjugates of a point stabilizer $`P<C`$, by transitivity. The 2-group $`C`$ has an index-two normal maximal subgroup containing $`P`$. It would contain the permutations of all marks, including $`t`$, hence all of $`K`$, contrary to generation of $`C`$. This argument is valid for nonregular actions and does not require a single conjugacy class at the origin.

## One all-paired tuple finishes every even field degree at least four

We are left with $`f`$ even and all $`U_i`$ in nonsplit torus normalizers of size $`M=2(q+1)`$. Take the all-paired selected mark just obtained. On each pair choose compatible torus frames: all nonsplit torus normalizers are $`T`$-conjugate, so independent inner conjugation of the mark aligns the two containing normalizers. Do this simultaneously on the disjoint pairs; it keeps the mark selected and leaves $`A=H\cap S`$ unchanged. For this one marked $`S`$-class, multiplying its elements by its distinguished representative yields all anti-diagonal tuples $`(u,u^{-1})`$, independently on each pair in those aligned identifications. Restrict $`u`$ on each pair to the common normalizer; the resulting $`M^{n/2}`$ tuples lie in its $`n`$-fold product of order $`M^n`$. They represent different cosets of $`A`$: if two such translated marks were in the same left coset, the quotient of the corresponding two original marks would be in $`A\cap\Lambda^2=\{1\}`$ (use a common **left** multiplier). Thus

```math
|A|\le M^{n/2}.                            \tag{3}
```

No product of the actual $`U_i`$ or independence between them is required.

The total number of actual outside marks, allowing arbitrary outer labels, is at most $`|C| I^n`$, where

```math
I=q^2+\sqrt q(q+1),\qquad |\operatorname{Aut}(T)|=Lf<q^4 .
```

The first term $`q^2`$ includes identity and all inner involutions; the second is the single half-field class, of size $`L/|\operatorname{PSL}_2(\sqrt q)|=\sqrt q(q+1)`$. For a given permutation with $`s`$ fixed coordinates, the more precise upper bound is $`I^s(Lf)^{(n-s)/2}\le I^n`$, since the return arrow on a pair is forced. Write $`J=HS/S`$. The transversal size gives $`|A||K|=[B:J]L^n\ge L^n`$, without assuming $`J=B`$. As $`C`$ is a faithful transitive 2-group, $`|C|\le 2^{n-1}`$ by its embedding in $`\operatorname{Sym}(n)`$. Combining (3) with the mark count would require $`L<2I\sqrt M`$. This fails for every $`q\ge16`$: $`I/q^2=1+q^{-1/2}+q^{-3/2}`$ decreases, while $`L/(q^2\sqrt M)=(1-1/q)\sqrt{(q+1)/2}`$ increases. At $`q=16`$ the exact inequality is $`4080^2>4\cdot324^2\cdot34`$.

At $`q=4`$ the last numerical bound genuinely fails: $`L=60,\;I=26,\;M=10`$. The only possible local subgroups are $`C_5`$ and $`D_{10}`$, and the permutation group is a transitive 2-group. The rank-two factor-permuting case can also be checked directly. In the binary outer dihedral square of order eight, its factor-permuting subgroups up to conjugacy are the swap subgroup of order two, a cyclic group of order four, the Klein four with constant parity, and the entire dihedral group. The [inner-inversion obstruction](../sources/factor-permutation/exact-factorization-criterion.md#inner-inversion-and-the-generalized-fitting-subgroup) makes the signed extension faithful on the socle, so its top strictly extends the original one by index two. This immediately eliminates the full dihedral top. Outside the Klein four, all swapping labels have order four, so outside involutions cannot generate a swapping translation. For the swap subgroup, the preserving and exchanging outside classes have sizes $`10^2`$ and $`60`$: at the fixed sites the arrows are odd transpositions, while a swapped pair has one arbitrary odd arrow and its inverse. Both classes are needed for generation, so the stabilizer would have odd order $`7200/160=45`$, despite covering the group of order two. For the cyclic group of order four the two preserving classes have sizes $`2\cdot10=20`$ and $`2\cdot10\cdot15=300`$ (an odd transposition and either identity or an even involution), and the swapping class has size $`120`$ (the two possible initial arrow parities are fused by the cyclic generator). The resulting possible transversal sizes $`140,420,440`$ do not divide $`14400`$. This is the earlier factor-permuting part of the whole $`A_5^2`$ result; its scope is separate from the general argument.

## The four-element field: a reflection-support code

Use the [original canonical folder and mark square](0103-radical-free-socle-coordinates.md): $`G`$ is faithful, $`H`$ its actual point stabilizer, $`E=G\rtimes\langle t\rangle`$ with formal inversion, and $`\Lambda=tK`$ the actual invariant outside involutions. We have $`H\cap\Lambda^2=1`$, and $`G`$ acts by conjugation on the centerless socle. Suppose the small-field configuration just obtained exists. Then $`T=A_5,\;C\le\operatorname{Sym}(n)`$ is a nontrivial transitive 2-group and $`A=H\cap S`$ has each projection $`U_i`$ a cyclic five-group or its dihedral normalizer of order ten. A selected mark has identity coordinate permutation and another selected mark pairs every coordinate. The outer top $`B=G/S\le\mathbb F_2^n\rtimes C`$ is a 2-group. The image of $`H`$ in $`B`$ is all of it: otherwise the preimage of a normal maximal subgroup containing that image is a proper nontrivial normal of $`G`$ containing $`H`$, forbidden by the [simple normal-cut criterion](../sources/foundations/C0001.md).

Each $`U_i`$ has a characteristic five-torus $`R_i`$; the tuple of these tori is preserved by $`H`$. Put $`D_i=N_{A_5}(R_i)\cong D_{10}`$. In identical torus frames the full automorphism normalizer is $`F_{20}=N_{S_5}(R_i)`$, the affine maps $`x\mapsto ux+v`$ on $`\mathbb F_5`$, with $`R_i`$ its translations and $`D_i/R_i=\{1,-1\}`$. Indeed $`R_i`$ is generated by a five-cycle, and its normalizer acts by these twenty permutations. The two nonsquare multipliers $`\pm2`$ give odd permutations of order four; the square of either odd affine map has multiplier $`-1`$, so is a nontrivial reflection in $`D_i`$.

The image $`V`$ of $`A\le\prod_iD_i`$ in $`\prod_i D_i/R_i=\mathbb F_2^n`$ is a binary linear code. Conjugation by $`H`$ permutes its coordinates exactly via $`C`$: on each order-two quotient there is only the identity isomorphism. A nonzero $`\mathbb F_2`$-module for a finite 2-group always has a nonzero invariant vector, since the non-fixed orbits have even size and the zero vector is fixed. On the transitive permutation module the only nonzero invariant vector is $`\mathbf1`$. Thus $`V\ne0`$ would give an actual $`h\in A`$ with reflection at every coordinate. These reflections are all involutions of $`A_5`$, of a single inner class. On every edge of the all-paired selected mark the two entries of $`h`$ are therefore inverse-conjugate through its actual arrow, even if it is odd. By the [same-class paired product](0103-radical-free-socle-coordinates.md#marked-square-membership-from-cycle-transport), the choices on the independent edges assemble two actual $`S`$-conjugates of that one mark with product $`h`$. This contradicts $`H\cap\Lambda^2=1`$. Consequently

```math
V=0,\qquad A\le\prod_iR_i.
```

In particular $`A`$ is a 5-group; every actual $`U_i`$ is $`C_5`$. There is no assertion of independence or full dimension of this five-code.

## Removing the whole field kernel, including nonsplit coordinate lifts

Write $`D=\ker(B\to C)\le\mathbb F_2^n`$. For $`d\in D`$ take a lift $`h\in H`$. Its permutation is identity, its field labels are the bits of $`d`$, and $`h^2\in A`$ because $`d^2=1`$. On any site with bit one its actual arrow belongs to the odd part of $`F_{20}`$, since $`h`$ preserves each chosen torus there. Its square would be a reflection outside $`R_i`$, impossible. Thus $`D=0`$ and $`B\to C`$ is an isomorphism. This does not assume the graph of $`B`$ in the binary outer wreath is untwisted.

The action of $`E`$ on $`S`$ is faithful here. Indeed $`G\le\operatorname{Aut}(S)`$ by radical-free faithfulness. If an outside automorphism agreed there with a member of $`G`$, canonical inversion would be inner on $`G`$: each automorphism of $`G`$ is determined by its restriction to the characteristic socle, since the centralizer of $`S`$ in $`G`$ is trivial. Inner canonical inversion forces $`O_2(G)\ne1`$ by the [reviewed Bruck-isotopy consequence](../sources/factor-permutation/exact-factorization-criterion.md#inner-inversion-and-the-generalized-fitting-subgroup). This contradicts the radical-free whole socle. Thus $`B`$ is indeed of index two in the **actual** outer wreath image $`B'=E/S`$. Because a coordinate-identity outside mark exists, the coordinate permutation image of $`B'`$ is still just $`C`$. Its kernel is an order-two $`C`$-invariant subgroup of the binary base, hence precisely the constant line $`\langle\mathbf1\rangle`$. Its nonidentity member is outside $`B`$. Over each $`\sigma\in C`$ there is exactly one $`B`$-label $`b_\sigma`$ and one outside label $`\mathbf1 b_\sigma`$.

Now take an actual mark $`a`$ with permutation $`\sigma`$. Since $`\sigma^2=1`$ and $`B\to C`$ is injective, $`b_\sigma^2=1`$. A Sylow 2-subgroup of $`H`$ maps isomorphically to $`B`$, because $`H\to B`$ is surjective with kernel the 5-group $`A`$. Thus $`b_\sigma`$ has a lift $`h_\sigma\in H`$ with $`h_\sigma^2=1`$, including identity when appropriate. At a $`\sigma`$-fixed coordinate this lift acts by an element of $`F_{20}`$ with square one. All such elements are even; odd elements have order four. It follows that $`b_\sigma`$ has bit **zero** at every fixed site. The outside label $`\mathbf1 b_\sigma`$ of $`a`$ consequently has bit **one** at every fixed site. This fixed-site conclusion would be false for arbitrary binary graph cocycles that do not lift to the physical normalizer over the 5-group.

## A linear moment gives the contradiction

Let $`Y_i`$ be the six five-tori in factor $`i`$. Their product is a transitive $`S`$-set; $`H`$ fixes the tuple $`(R_i)`$. At every fixed coordinate of a selected mark the preceding paragraph gives an odd square-one element of $`S_5`$, hence a transposition. It fixes **no** five-torus: its membership in such a torus normalizer would contradict the order-four condition for odd elements of $`F_{20}`$. Therefore the local fixed count $`F_i(a)`$ of the [tuple polynomial](#uniformity-on-an-actual-stabilized-tuple) is zero at every fixed site of every selected mark. The coefficient of the linear term with equal variables is

```math
0=\sum_{a\in\Lambda}\sum_{\sigma_a i=i}(F_i(a)-1)
=-\sum_{a\in\Lambda}|\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma_a)|.
```

This is impossible because one actual selected mark fixes **all** $`n`$ coordinates. No asymptotic inequality, count of equally numerous decorated classes, or exhaustive enumeration of 2-groups is needed.

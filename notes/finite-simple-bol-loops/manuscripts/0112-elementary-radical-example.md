# 0112. A finite simple left Bol loop of exponent six

**Mira Cult**

Simplicity of a loop does not require simplicity of its left multiplication group. Here a loop with no proper normal subloop has an abelian normal subgroup of dimension 236 in its group of left translations. Its order is divisible by five, but none of its elements has order five.

A left Bol loop is a loop satisfying $`x*(y*(x*v))=(x*(y*x))*v`$. A loop is **simple** when it has no nontrivial proper normal subloop, and **proper Bol** when it is Bol but not Moufang. For $`Q`$, write $`L_x(y)=x*y`$ and $`\operatorname{LMlt}(Q)=\langle L_x:x\in Q\rangle`$. The theorem is an existence result about one operation, not an exhaustive classification of a quotient or a claim of priority.

**Theorem.** The construction below gives a finite simple left Bol loop $`Q`$ of order $`20\cdot3^{119}`$ with faithful left multiplication group $`(\mathbb F_3^{236},+)\rtimes\operatorname{PSL}_2(19)`$. The elementary subgroup is exactly the solvable radical. The loop fails the right alternative law, so is not Moufang. Its exponent is six; in particular it has no element of order five although five divides its order.

The construction uses a group of permutations on cosets. The selected permutations must act sharply on every point, not just send the identity to distinct points. In this example sharpness reduces to eight inconsistent affine systems over the field of three elements. Simplicity is a different test: every proper normal group quotient must collapse too few selected permutations to describe a loop quotient. A two-round subspace calculation checks even normal subgroups not preserved by the sign involution.

## The group, its modules and its stabilizer

Use column vectors. The complete [finite input](../sources/elementary-psl2-19/evidence/psl2-19-explicit-matrix-input.json.zlib.b64) consists of eight matrices over $`\mathbb F_3`$. Six give the generators on $`X=\mathbb F_3^{117}`$ and $`Y=\mathbb F_3^{119}`$; the other two are a $`119\times117`$ matrix $`f`$ and a $`119\times1`$ column $`d`$. The [reproduction guide](../sources/elementary-psl2-19/README.md) gives the data hash, file format, a generator from four pinned ATLAS inputs, and two independently written verifiers. The first uses the Python standard library alone. All finite equalities below can be checked without reconstructing the search that selected the matrices.

On the twenty points of the projective line, put $`u:x\mapsto x+1`$, $`w:x\mapsto-1/x`$, $`r:x\mapsto4x`$, with the usual conventions at zero and infinity. They lie in $`T=\operatorname{PSL}_2(19)`$. The verifier enumerates the 3,420 permutations generated by $`u,w`$, hence all of $`T`$, including $`r`$. The stabilizer $`B=\langle u,r\rangle`$ of infinity is $`19{:}9`$. Write $`U=\langle u\rangle`$. Its semidirect product with $`\langle r\rangle`$ has as its 171 elements the affine maps $`u^a r^j`$. For each matrix representation, a spanning collection of cyclic vectors is tested by tracing the actual entire projective Cayley graph, checking both generator edges out of every vertex and the prescribed $`r`$ edge there. Thus every identity word acts identically on a spanning set of orbit vectors and the input really represents $`T`$. The test needs one cyclic vector in each of the two particular modules; this is certified by rank, not assumed.

The matrix identities $`f g_X=g_Y f`$ hold for $`g=u,r`$. Also $`d\in Y^{\langle u\rangle}`$ and $`\sum_{j=0}^8 r^j d=0`$. Therefore $`d_B(u)=0`$, $`d_B(r)=d`$ uniquely determine an actual derivation $`B\to Y`$: define $`d_B(u^a r^j)=d_j=\sum_{l=0}^{j-1}r^l d`$. Norm zero makes this independent of $`j`$ modulo nine, and $`U`$ fixes all the $`d_j`$. Write $`V=X\oplus Y`$, $`G=V\rtimes T`$. Define the subgroup
```math
 H=\{((x,f(x)+d_B(b)),b): x\in X,\ b\in B\},\qquad W=H\cap V=\operatorname{graph}(f).
```
The derivation identity and $`B`$-equivariance prove closure. Let the sign involution $`z`$ centralize $`T`$ and $`X`$ and negate $`Y`$; take $`E=G\rtimes\langle z\rangle`$. Let $`\Lambda`$ be all involutions of $`E\setminus G`$, and $`K=z\Lambda\subset G`$. No individual lifts have been discarded.

## Why the chosen permutations form a left Bol loop

All projections of the outside involutions are $`zi`$ with $`i^2=1`$ in $`T`$, including $`i=1`$. Over each, their vectors range over $`(1-zi)V`$: in odd characteristic this is exactly the kernel of $`1+zi`$. Thus the vectors in $`K`$ above $`i`$ range over $`A_i=X_i^-\oplus Y_i^+`$ (and $`A_1=Y`$). The projective enumeration gives 171 nonidentity involutions, all $`B`$-conjugate to $`w`$. Their negative dimensions are 58 in $`X`$ and 60 in $`Y`$. Therefore
```math
 |K|=3^{119}+171\,3^{58+59}=20\,3^{119}=[G:H].
```

It remains to establish $`H\cap\Lambda^2=1`$. A nonidentity $`h`$ is a product of two outside involutions exactly when an outside involution inverts $`h`$. In the vertical subgroup $`W`$, the base sign can invert only $`W\cap Y=0`$. A lift of $`zw`$ can invert only $`W\cap A_w`$. Write $`e=(1-w)/2`$ on either module. The matrix for $`e_Y f`$ is injective on $`e_X(X)`$ (rank 58); if $`x\in X_w^-`$ and $`f(x)\in Y_w^+`$ then $`x=0`$. This proves $`W\cap A_w=0`$. The other labels follow from actual conjugations in $`H`$ lifting those in $`B`$; changing a lift by a vector cannot change its action on the abelian $`V`$.

For nonvertical $`h`$, enumerate the genuine projective pairs $`(i,b)`$ with $`b\in B\setminus\{1\}`$, $`i^2=1`$ and $`ibi=b^{-1}`$. The set equals the union of the simultaneous $`B`$-conjugates of $`(w,r^j)`$, $`1\le j\le8`$, and has exactly 1,368 pairs. The checker compares sets of explicit permutations, not a list of element orders. Conjugating by a lift in $`H`$ leaves the inversion question invariant. For each representative let $`k=zw`$ as a linear operator on $`V`$ and $`\nu_j=k r^j`$. All actual outside involutions over $`zw`$ are $`(c,k)`$ with $`c\in(1-k)V`$. The possible $`H`$ elements in question have vector $`(x,f(x)+d_j)`$. Such an inverter exists precisely if the square of their product is one, i.e. if
```math
 0\in (1+\nu_j)k(0,d_j)+(1+\nu_j)\big((1-k)V+kW\big).
```
In each of the eight cases the affine column **increases** the rank of this full 236-dimensional system by one, before any torus compression. In order $`j=1,\ldots,8`$, the ranks before adjoining it are $`118,118,117,118,118,117,118,118`$. Hence no such inverter exists.

Since $`K^{-1}K=\Lambda^2`$ is invariant under conjugation by $`G`$, the same avoidance holds for every conjugate of $`H`$. Together with the index count this makes $`K`$ sharply transitive on the left cosets $`G/H`$. It contains $`1`$, is inverse-closed, and is closed under $`p q p`$: for $`p=za`$, $`q=zb`$ the last expression equals $`z(a z)b(a z)^{-1}`$. Thus define $`(gH)*(hH)=p hH`$ where $`p`$ is the unique member of $`K`$ taking $`H`$ to $`gH`$. Sharp transitivity makes both divisions unique; $`H`$ is the identity. Since $`L_x L_y L_x`$ is in $`K`$ and takes $`H`$ to $`x*(y*x)`$, it equals that point's left translation. This proves the left Bol identity.

## Why the loop is simple

For $`R\lhd G`$ put $`K_R=KR/R`$. Write $`|K_R|`$ for the cardinality of the section image. We prove that $`|K_R|>[G:HR]`$ for every $`1<R<G`$. First, $`0<R\le V`$ is any $`T`$-submodule; it need not be stable under $`z`$. Write $`d_R=\dim R-\dim(R\cap W)`$. The base sheet loses a factor $`|R\cap Y|`$ and each other sheet a factor $`|R\cap A_w|`$ by $`B`$-conjugacy and normality. Distinct group labels stay distinct. Dividing the resulting sum by the new index gives
```math
 \frac{|K_R|}{[G:HR]}=
 \frac{3^{d_R-\dim(R\cap Y)}+19\,3^{d_R-\dim(R\cap A_w)}}{20}.
```
Both exponents are nonnegative because each of $`Y,A_w`$ meets $`W`$ trivially. Equality would make both zero. The first equality says $`R=(R\cap W)\oplus(R\cap Y)`$. Thus if $`X_0=\pi_X R`$, $`Y_0=R\cap Y`$, the subspace consists of $`(x,f(x)+y_0)`$ for $`x\in X_0, y_0\in Y_0`$. Both $`X_0,Y_0`$ are $`T`$-submodules, and the reduction $`\bar f:X_0\to Y/Y_0`$ is $`T`$-linear because $`R`$ is invariant. With $`J=\ker\bar f`$ this implies $`R\cap A_w=J_w^-\oplus(Y_0)_w^+`$: for $`x\in X_0^-`$ the image in $`Y/Y_0`$ is negative and can have a positive representative only when it is zero. The second equality now says $`\dim eJ=\dim eY_0`$.

For submodules $`J\le X`$, $`Z\le Y`$ with $`f(J)\subseteq Z`$, put $`b=e_Y f|_{eX}`$. Injection gives $`b(eJ)\subseteq eZ`$, so equality of dimensions means equality of these spaces. We show the only such *equal-dimension pair* is $`(0,0)`$. Start with $`U=eX`$, and repeat
```math
 Z=\operatorname{core}_T\{y:e y\in bU\},\qquad
 J=\operatorname{core}_T\{x:f(x)\in Z\},\qquad U'=eJ.
```
The core is the largest $`T`$-invariant subspace of the indicated space. One can compute it as the annihilator of the closure of its annihilator under the transposed $`u,w`$ matrices. The containment $`b(eJ)\subseteq eZ\subseteq bU`$ shows $`U'\subseteq U`$. Every equal-dimension pair stays inside the two cores by induction, since its negative target space equals its $`b`$-image. At stabilization the resulting pair attains equality, so it is the unique largest one. The calculation gives $`(\dim U,\dim U',\dim J,\dim Z)=(58,0,0,20)`$ and then $`(0,0,0,0)`$. The second round matters: a nonzero fixed submodule can have zero negative dimension.

It follows that equality of the two sheet exponents would force $`J=Y_0=0`$. Then any nonzero $`R`$ would be the graph of a $`T`$-linear restriction of $`f`$ on $`X_0\ne0`$. But $`(X_0,f(X_0))`$ is again an equal-dimension pair: a $`T`$-linear image cannot increase negative dimension, and the injection gives the reverse inequality. This is impossible. This proves strictness on all the kernel cuts, not just split, small or semisimple ones. It also proves $`\operatorname{core}_G H=1`$: any such normal projects trivially to simple $`T`$ since its image is contained in $`B<T`$; a normal $`R\le W`$ would give equality in the displayed ratio.

For a normal $`R`$ projecting onto $`T`$, commutators of its lifts with vectors give $`[T,V]\le R`$. Write $`M_T=M/[T,M]`$. The calculation gives $`X_T=0`$, $`\dim Y_T=1`$, and $`f(X)`$ together with $`d`$ maps onto $`Y_T`$. Here $`G/[T,V]=T\times Y_T`$ is an actual direct product by the original splitting. The image of $`R`$ contains $`T\times1`$ by perfectness of $`T`$. A proper such $`R`$ therefore projects to zero on the single remaining central coordinate, whereas both $`K`$ (it contains $`Y`$) and $`H`$ map onto it. So $`|K_R|=3>1=[G:HR]`$. Since $`T`$ is simple this exhausts the possibilities. The same commutator argument applies to $`\langle K\rangle=\langle\Lambda^2\rangle\lhd G`$: it projects onto $`T`$ because it contains the pure involutions and these generate the simple group. It contains $`Y`$, so equals $`G`$. Faithfulness now identifies $`G`$ with the canonical left multiplication group. Its solvable radical is $`V`$ because $`V`$ is abelian and the quotient is nonabelian simple.

For completeness, strictness indeed implies loop simplicity here. A nontrivial proper loop quotient would induce a surjection $`G\to\operatorname{LMlt}(\bar Q)`$: an identity word in the left translations acts trivially after applying the surjective loop map. Its normal kernel $`R`$ is nontrivial because two distinct elements in one fiber have the same image translation, and is proper because the quotient is nontrivial. The distinct images of $`K`$ are exactly the $`|\bar Q|`$ left translations. As $`HR`$ fixes the quotient identity, $`|\bar Q|\le [G:HR]`$, whereas $`K`$ covers all $`HR`$ cosets, so $`|K_R|\ge[G:HR]`$. These inequalities force equality, contrary to what we just proved.

## Failure of right alternativity and exact element orders

Use zero-based coordinates and write $`y`$ for the first unit vector of $`Y`$ and also for its coset, and $`x=wH`$. Put $`i=r^8 w`$, $`D=d_8`$ and $`a=y-iD`$ in $`Y`$. Direct matrix-vector arithmetic gives $`ia=a`$ and the coordinate at index $`1`$ of $`-iD-D`$ is $`1`$ in $`\mathbb F_3`$. Hence $`(a,i)\in K`$. Since $`(a,i)(D,r^8)=(y,w)`$ and $`(D,r^8)\in H`$, the left translation at $`y*x`$ is precisely $`(a,i)`$. But $`(a,i)wH\ne yH`$: their difference over $`r^8`$ has vector $`-iD\ne D`$ and zero $`X`$-coordinate, so is not in $`H`$. Thus $`(y*x)*x\ne y*(x*x)`$ since $`w^2=1`$. This directly excludes Moufang without classification.

In this sharp twisted transversal all powers of a translation $`k`$ are in $`K`$: start from $`1,k`$ and use $`k k^j k=k^{j+2}`$. Their images of $`H`$ give precisely the successive powers of the corresponding loop element, with no repetitions before $`k^j=1`$, by sharpness. The nonidentity vertical translations have order three. A translation $`(a,i)`$ above $`i\ne1`$ has $`a=x^-+y^+`$ and square $`2y^+`$. The exact counts by order are consequently
```math
 1:1,\qquad 2:171\,3^{58},\qquad 3:3^{119}-1,\qquad
 6:171\,3^{58}(3^{59}-1).
```

## Comparison with earlier constructions

In [Nagy's exact-factorization construction, Proposition 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0703919), the loop on a finite group $`D`$ has $`|D|`$ points and its left translations act through a subgroup of $`D\times D`$. [Johnson and Smith, Theorem 3.1 and Remark 3.2](https://jdhsmith.math.iastate.edu/math/MPBLPR.pdf), give the same prime-support constraint even without the faithfulness assumption on the factorization. Every prime divisor of the resulting canonical left multiplication group thus divides the carrier order. Here $`19\mid |G|`$ but $`19\nmid |Q|`$, so $`Q`$ is isomorphic to **none** of these finite matched-pair loops. Its elements of order three separately distinguish it from [Nagy's exponent-two construction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0709.4544) and all exponent-two loops. These are family separations, not an exhaustive claim that no other publication, general construction, or isomorphic loop exists. The broader classification remains open.


## What remains open

The construction settles existence for these specific modules and this stabilizer. It does not determine whether smaller examples have the same elementary-radical quotient, classify all elementary extensions with this quotient, or give a structural list of all finite simple proper left Bol loops.

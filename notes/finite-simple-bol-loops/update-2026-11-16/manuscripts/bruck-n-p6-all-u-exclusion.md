# Excluding P6 in the N-Frattini branch

**Theorem with scoped independent reviews**, continuing 0323:C0010 [dependency/context not reproduced in this update: `../claims/C0010.md`]. In the faithful simple nonassociative Bruck-envelope setting of the marked-rank theorem [dependency/context not reproduced in this update: `bruck-n-frattini-marked-rank.md`], suppose \(G/P\cong S_5\), \(P\) has class at most two and exponent at most four, \(\Phi(P)=Z\cong N\), and
\[
 V=P/Z\cong P_6\oplus U^b,\qquad b\ge0.
\]
Let \(W=H\cap P\), let \(\overline W\) be its shadow, and put
\[
 c=\dim_S(\overline W\cap\operatorname{soc}(U^b)),
\]
where \(S\) is the nontrivial four-dimensional simple on restriction to \(F=F_{20}\). The conclusion is that no such envelope exists for any \(b\ge0\), conditional on the \(c\le1\) theorem in [the socle-quadratic collision manuscript](bruck-n-socle-quadratic-collision.md), independently reviewed at `c4154e4d8cfbe1485491f6ec0b57567f8a18e396`. The argument does not assume an extension over \(S_5\) splits or construct a quotient folder.

The proof uses the marked-rank and quadratic-component results at `123933cc594b601dd317d62b333a949d20431d35`, the invariant-functional obstruction at `bf11794257554b943a1b26ca15acd861f9faaa7f`, and the polar-support argument of the N-Frattini quadratic-support manuscript [dependency/context not reproduced in this update: `bruck-n-frattini-quadratic-support.md`] at `b40066d01085772606b1d1160d58c033b8ba3302`. The particular \(P_6\)-to-\(U\) restrictions below are exactly those of the independently reviewed one-U theorem [dependency/context not reproduced in this update: `bruck-n-p6-one-u-exclusion.md`] at `1a8a1eddcdfaeb5fab6f3e7682a37c5597a3b522`; no new finite computation is required. This all-\(b\) deduction has its own scoped independent exact-source reviews below.

The invariant-functional obstruction makes \(\overline W\) have a one-dimensional \(F\)-fixed part generated by an augmentation-one vector \(f\in P_6\), and its nonfixed part \(B\) has multiplicity \(b+1\). The \(U\)-summands have no \(F\)-fixed vectors. The two augmentation-one \(F\)-fixed vectors of \(P_6\) are interchanged by its \(S_5\)-automorphism \(1+J_{\rm all}\); choose the coordinates of the one-U theorem, with \(f\) the \(F\)-fixed singleton.

Suppose \(c=0\). Since \(\dim_S B=b+1\), projection gives an isomorphism
\[
 B\longrightarrow P_{6,S}\oplus\operatorname{top}(U^b). \tag{1}
\]
Thus \(B\) is a graph into \(\operatorname{soc}(U^b)\), with an arbitrary \(F\)-linear graph map; in particular mixed \(P_{6,S}\)-socle graphs are allowed. The reviewed polar-support proof bounds the numbers of both unit and \(\varepsilon\)-hyperbolic pairs in the \(U\)-to-\(U\) polar matrix by \(c\). Hence that matrix is zero. The marked-rank theorem gives \(q|_{P_6}=0\) and says each \(q|_U\) is a scalar multiple of the linear top map. By (1), \(B\) contains graph lifts of all top-only vectors. Singularity of \(\overline W\) then forces every one of these scalar coefficients to vanish. Therefore \(q\) consists only of its \(P_6\)-to-\(U\) cross row.

Use the basis \(\beta_0,\beta_1\) in equation (1) of the reviewed one-U theorem. In particular,
\[
 \beta_0(P_{6,S},U)=0,\quad
 \beta_1(P_{6,S},\operatorname{soc}(U))=0,\quad
 \beta_1(P_{6,S},D)\ne0, \tag{2}
\]
where \(D\) is an \(F\)-simple complement to the socle. The restriction in the last inequality is the full bilinear map. For each top coordinate, take graph lifts in \(B\) of \(P_{6,S}\) and that coordinate of \(D\). Their polar is zero because \(W\) is elementary. The \(U\)-pair terms vanish, and all socle-graph corrections to the cross restriction vanish by (2). Consequently the \(\beta_1\)-coefficient of every cross term is zero. This argument does not require the graph lifts themselves to be \(S_5\)-stable.

The square map therefore has the form
\[
 q(x,u_1,\ldots,u_b)=\sum_i a_i\beta_0(x,u_i),\qquad a_i\in\mathbb F_2. \tag{3}
\]
At least one \(a_i\) is nonzero: square values generate \(Z=\Phi(P)\ne1\). The reviewed identities also give \(\beta_0(f,D_s)=\iota(s)\ne0\) for \(s\ne0\), and \(\beta_0(f,\operatorname{soc}(U))=0\). By (1), choose a top-only graph vector of \(B\) supported on a coordinate with \(a_i=1\) and with nonzero top. Its polar with \(f\) is nonzero, contrary to \(W\) being elementary. For \(b=0\), equation (3) already makes \(q=0\), the same contradiction to \(\Phi(P)\ne1\). This proves \(c\ne0\) subject to the stated inputs.

The exclusion of \(c=0\) is independent of the proposed stronger bound on \(c\).

## The one-socle case

Now suppose \(c=1\), and identify the nonfixed shadow with \(S\otimes B\), where
\[
 B\le\mathbb F_2(x\oplus D^b\oplus Z_U^b),\qquad
 \dim B=b+1,\qquad B\cap Z_U^b=\langle z\rangle
\]
for \(z\ne0\). Here \(x\) denotes the multiplicity coordinate of \(P_{6,S}\), \(D\) the chosen \(F\)-complement to the \(U\)-socle \(Z_U\), and \(C_0\) denotes the alternating binary unit-residue matrix of the \(U\)–\(U\) polar form. Write the two \(P_6\)–\(U\) cross rows in the reviewed basis as \(r_0,r_1\in\mathbb F_2^b\). Isotropy with the fixed vector \(f\) gives \(r_1\cdot z=0\).

The reviewed map \(\beta_0\) is exactly \((x,u)\mapsto\operatorname{aug}(x)\operatorname{top}(u)\): this explicit equivariant map has the stated \(\beta_0\) values on \(f\times D\) and \(f\times Z_U\), and those two restrictions distinguish the two reviewed basis maps. Thus \(\beta_0\) annihilates all of \(P_6\times Z_U\). The coefficient of \(\beta_1\) against the socle copy \(Z_z\) is \(r_1\cdot z=0\), so \(\beta(P_6,Z_z)=0\), including both \(F\)-fixed directions. Its \(U\)–\(U\) polar is controlled by \(C_0z\). If \(C_0z=0\), then \(Z_z\) is polar-radical, contrary to the reviewed actual central-socle obstruction at `bf11794257554b943a1b26ca15acd861f9faaa7f`. Put \(\ell=C_0z\ne0\).

Isotropy against \(Z_z\) gives \(\operatorname{top}(B)\subseteq\ker\ell\). The kernel of \(\operatorname{top}:B\to\mathbb F_2^b\) has dimension at most two: its socle part is \(\langle z\rangle\) and the only other coordinate is \(x\). As \(\dim B=b+1\) and \(\ker\ell\) has dimension \(b-1\), equality holds:
\[
 \operatorname{top}(B)=\ker\ell,\qquad
 \ker(\operatorname{top}|_B)=\langle Z_z,\ x+Z_v\rangle
\]
for some \(v\). Alternation gives \(\ell(z)=z^TC_0z=0\), so \(z\in\operatorname{top}(B)\). Choose an element of \(B\) with top \(z\) and subtract its \(x\)-component times \(x+Z_v\). This yields a pure-\(U\) vector \(D_z+Z_w\in B\), together with \(Z_z\).

These two \(F\)-simple copies span an \(S_5\)-stable submodule isomorphic to \(U\). Indeed, under any \(g\in S_5\), the difference between \(gD_s\) and \(D_{gs}\) lies in the corresponding socle copy; on the graph with common top multiplicity \(z\), that correction lies in \(Z_z\). The socle graph \(Z_w\) is itself \(S_5\)-stable. Equivalently, with the reviewed dual-number endomorphism \(\varepsilon\), the map \(U\to U^b\), \(u\mapsto((z_i+w_i\varepsilon)u)_i\), is equivariant and injective because some \(z_i=1\). Its image is exactly these two copies. The resulting \(U\) lies in \(\overline W\) and has transposition rank 4, contradicting the reviewed stable-shadow rank bound 3 in the central-socle manuscript [dependency/context not reproduced in this update: `bruck-n-central-socle-obstruction.md`] at `cdfe466523a3358d1636f517f51664f3c7c51e22`. Therefore \(c\ne1\).

## Conditional conclusion

The \(c=0\) and \(c=1\) arguments above exclude both possibilities. Subject to the \(c\le1\) theorem in [the socle-quadratic collision manuscript](bruck-n-socle-quadratic-collision.md), independently reviewed at `c4154e4d8cfbe1485491f6ec0b57567f8a18e396`, no faithful simple nonassociative Bruck envelope has the stated \(N\)-Frattini shadow \(P_6\oplus U^b\), for any \(b\ge0\). This all-\(b\) deduction has the scoped independent reviews below. The conclusion makes no assertion about other Frattini modules or radical classes.

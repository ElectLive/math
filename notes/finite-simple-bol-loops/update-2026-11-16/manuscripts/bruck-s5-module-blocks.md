## Projectivity and block separation

N is the even-weight part of the natural 5-point module. For a Sylow V4≤A5 acting regularly on four points and fixing the fifth, the map F₂⁴→N given by x↦(x,sum x) is V4-equivariant, so N|V4 is the regular module. Induce this free V4-module to A5. The induced module is projective, and the counit onto N splits A5-equivariantly by averaging a V4-equivariant section over the 15 cosets of V4 in A5; 15 is invertible in F₂. Thus N|A5 is a direct summand of a projective module and is projective. It is absolutely irreducible and End_{A5}(N)=F₂ by Nagy arXiv:0709.4544v1 Lemma 4.1(iii).

Let X be any finite F₂S₅-module with no N composition factor. Restricted to A5, neither the trivial factor nor M has an N constituent: M|A5 is irreducible and distinct from N, by Nagy Lemma 4.1(iii). Thus Hom_{A5}(N,X)=0. Projectivity of N|A5 gives Ext^j_{A5}(N,X)=0 for j>0 as well. The Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre Ext spectral sequence for A5◁S5 yields Ext^j_{S5}(N,X)=0 for all j≥0. Duality, self-duality of N and absence of an N factor in X* give the reverse vanishing Ext^j_{S5}(X,N)=0. By induction on finite filtrations, the N-isotypic block splits from the {1,M} block in every finite module; no semisimplicity within the N block is assumed.

If Y has only N factors, Y|A5 is a direct sum of N copies because N|A5 is projective. Evaluation N⊗Hom_{A5}(N,Y)→Y is an isomorphism and is S5-equivariant with the natural conjugation action on the multiplicity space. As A5 acts trivially on this space, it is a finite F₂C₂-module. Therefore Y≅N^a⊕U^b, where U=N⊗F₂C₂ is a nonsplit extension N by N. In particular all N-only modules are controlled by two indecomposable blocks; the stronger claim Y≅N^a would be false. For the group-cohomology spectral sequence used with the coefficient module \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb F_2}(N,X)\), see Hochschild–Serre, [*Cohomology of group extensions*](https://www.sas.rochester.edu/mth/sites/doug-ravenel/otherpapers/hochschild-serre.pdf), Chapter I, Proposition 7, and use the finite-dimensional identification \(\operatorname{Ext}^*_{\mathbb F_2G}(N,X)\cong H^*(G,N^*\otimes X)\). The independent review below covers the projectivity argument and equivariant evaluation; the cited sources supply its stated inputs.

## Pure-N plus trivial factors

Suppose all nontrivial composition factors of V are N and at least one trivial factor occurs. Ext¹(1,N)=Ext¹(N,1)=0 separates V=T⊕A, where T is N-only and A has only trivial factors. The A-action factors through sign since A5 is perfect and the action has 2-group image. P-idempotents split W=(W∩T)⊕(W∩A). The second part is S5-invariant, because F20 and S5 have the same sign image, and core-freeness forces it to vanish. Thus W⊂T.

The extension E=G/T has kernel A and H/W provides a complement over F20; a transposition has an involution lift. Every sign module is a direct sum of trivial and regular 2D C2 modules. For the trivial summand, restriction to F20 and a transposition jointly detects H²(S5,F₂). For the regular sign summand, Shapiro identifies restriction to F20 with restriction H²(A5,F₂)→H²(D10,F₂), injective by the 1-dimensional bound via A4 and the nontrivial SL₂(5) class on D10; see the earlier one-factor proof. Hence E splits. A nonzero invariant functional A→C2 gives a group homomorphism E→C2, and adding sign makes it vanish on H. Since K generates G, it induces a nontrivial loop quotient, contradicting simplicity. Thus a hypothetical radical with N but no M factors has no trivial composition factors; its dimension is divisible by 4. This argument does not require classifying self-extensions in T. ## Essential nonsplit controls

Nagy arXiv:0709.4544v1 §4, Lemmas 4.2–4.3, and §5, Theorem 5.2 construct U of dimension 8 with U|A5=N⊕N and an odd involution swapping summands. This is N⊗F₂C₂. Its F20-submodule T1 has dimension 4 and satisfies T1∩C_U(t)=0 for a transposition t. On U, t swaps the two summands (with invertible internal action), so (1+t)U=C_U(t), and hence T1∩(1+t)U=0. F20 is transitive on the ten transpositions, so this holds for every t. It is a concrete counterexample to trying to prove W∩(1+t)V=0 forces W to be S5-invariant.

For Nagy's simple k=1 construction, §5 defines the elementary radical J⊕U of dimension 5+8=13; J has factors 1,M and U has N,N. The loop has order 96·16=1536 (Theorem 5.2 and the count immediately preceding Proposition 5.1). This is a published simple witness and rules out any uniform exclusion of N self-extensions.

## Elimination of all N-only radicals

This argument uses the N-block classification above. Applying block separation with X=1 also gives H²(S5,N)=0 directly. Let V=N^a⊕U^b, with U=N⊗F₂C₂ and a+b>0. Since N|A5 is projective and U≅Ind_{A5}^{S5}(N|A5), U is projective as an S5-module. Thus H²(S5,U)=0 and, together with H²(S5,N)=0, every extension of S5 by V splits.

Every F20 composition factor of V is the same projective irreducible four-dimensional module. Consequently each F20-submodule W and its quotient V/W is projective (the simple projective is also injective over the Frobenius group algebra), and H¹(F20,V/W)=0. In the split group G=V⋊S5, any H above F20 with H∩V=W is conjugate by V to W⋊F20. Hence it suffices to exclude invariant sections for this standard H.

Let T=<t> for a transposition t. On each U, t interchanges two four-dimensional summands by inverse isomorphisms, so U|T is free and H¹(T,U)=0. For N in the natural five-point model with t=(12), fixed vectors have x1=x2; quotienting by (1+t)N=<e1+e2> identifies H¹(T,N) with the even-weight triples on the remaining three points. The S3 factor of C_{S5}(t)=<t>×S3 acts on this 2D space as GL₂(F₂). Therefore H¹(T,V)≅(F₂²)^a as a diagonal S3-module.

An involution in tV is conjugate under V to one of its coset-class representatives in H¹(T,V). Every such V-orbit has 2^r elements. Conjugacy-invariance of K makes its t-fiber a union of S3-orbits on H¹(T,V). A tuple of a vectors in F₂² has trivial stabilizer (orbit size 6) if it spans the 2D space, stabilizer order 2 (orbit size 3) if it spans one line, and stabilizer all S3 only for the zero tuple. Hence zero is the unique orbit of size 1; all nonzero orbits have size 3 or 6. For a=0 there is only the zero tuple.

The earlier fiber count gives |K∩tV|=2^(d−w−1), so the number of V-orbits selected is q=2^e with e=d−w−1−r≥0. Modulo 3, any union of S3-orbits has size 0 if it omits zero and 1 if it contains zero. If e is odd, q≡2 mod3, impossible. If e is even, q≡1 and therefore the zero tuple must occur. In the standard split G this forces the pure transposition t into K. By S5-conjugacy all pure transpositions occur; two disjoint such transpositions multiply to the double transposition in F20≤H, contradicting transversality. Thus no pure-N radical survives. Nagy, arXiv:0709.4544v1, Lemma 4.1, lists the simple F₂S₅-modules as exactly 1, M and N. If every composition factor were trivial, the action of S₅ on V would have 2-group image, so the perfect subgroup A₅ would act trivially. This contradicts faithfulness of the S₅-action, obtained from F*(G)=V and self-centralization in the linked radical-bound manuscript. Together with the pure-N-plus-trivial lemma, the preceding argument therefore proposes that every simple envelope under the stated hypotheses has at least one M composition factor.

The mod-3 argument is an analytic orbit count, not an inference from finite examples. The scoped review below checked the key interfaces: projectivity and induction, splitting/normalization, the precise S3 action on H¹(T,N), and the assertion that the zero V-orbit is the orbit of the pure transposition in the chosen splitting. It is consistent with Nagy's d=13 example, which has an M factor; the published U control above shows that W∩(1+t)V=0 need not fail for a nonsplit N-block.

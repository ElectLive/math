# Open problems in the classification of finite simple Bol loops

These are mathematical targets, not a maintenance backlog. Each would close a
structural gap in the classification. **The complete classification remains open.**
Efficient enumeration is a later goal, not a requirement for resolving these questions.

## Conventions

Here a **proper left Bol loop** means a left Bol loop that is not Moufang.
A loop is **simple** if it has no nontrivial proper normal subloops. We classify
loops **up to isomorphism**, not merely up to isotopy.

For a loop Q, write G = LMlt(Q) for the permutation group generated by its left
translations L_x(y) = xy. This is not the full multiplication group Mlt(Q), which
also includes right translations. The **socle** of a finite group is the subgroup
generated by its minimal normal subgroups.

The status statements below describe the gaps in this research programme; they
are not claims that every question is new to the literature. Linked manuscripts
are research drafts with their own assumptions and review records, not substitutes
for those assumptions or for external verification.

## 1. Can the left multiplication group act primitively?

**Question.** Does there exist a finite simple proper left Bol loop Q for which
LMlt(Q) acts primitively on Q?

Equivalently, can the left translations have no common nontrivial invariant
partition of Q? A positive answer requires an actual loop, with proofs of the
Bol identity, properness, simplicity and primitivity. A negative answer must
exclude every such loop, not just particular constructions or permutation-group
types.

**Why this matters.** This asks whether a whole major permutation-group route to
classification is possible. Primitivity of Mlt(Q) is a different condition and
does not answer it.

**Starting point and status.** This is the primitive-action question discussed
in Nagy's [*Some remarks on simple Bol loops*](https://cmuc.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/pdf/cmuc0802/nagy.pdf)
(2008), Problem 2, translated to our left-Bol convention. It remains unresolved
in this programme. Existing exclusions cover selected action types, not all
primitive groups. See the [references](REFERENCES.md) for the relevant literature.

## 2. Classify the factor-permuting A₅-socle branch

**Question.** Classify all finite simple proper left Bol loops Q satisfying

- the solvable radical of G = LMlt(Q) is trivial;
- Soc(G) is isomorphic to a direct product of r copies of A₅, for some r ≥ 1.

Allow conjugation by G to permute these r simple direct factors. Do not assume
that G equals its socle, that a factor-permutation action is regular, or that
the canonical inversion automorphism pairs the factors in a prescribed way.

**Desired result.** Structural parameters constructing every loop in this class,
necessary and sufficient admissibility conditions, and an exact criterion for
when two parameter choices give isomorphic loops. A complete theorem for a
specified previously uncovered permutation action would be useful partial progress.

**Why this matters.** Classifying direct powers alone does not classify groups
lying above them. Permutation of the simple factors introduces correlations that
the already treated nonpermuting case does not capture.

**Starting point and status.** The
[nonpermuting A₅-socle assembly](advances-2026-11-16/manuscripts/radical-free-a5-code-inversion.md)
reports a classification when every simple factor is preserved by G.
The [regular prime-power-top reduction](advances-2026-11-16/manuscripts/radical-free-a5-prime-power-top.md)
handles one precisely specified factor-permuting model. Neither exhausts the
question above. Use their pinned hypotheses and review records when extending them.

## 3. Which odd prime-power regular tops are admissible?

This is a concrete subproblem of Problem 2.

**Model.** Let P be a finite group of order p^e, where p is an odd prime and e ≥ 1.
Let P act on its own elements by left multiplication, and form the semidirect
product S = A₅^P ⋊ P. Write top: S → P for the projection and
ab: P → P/[P,P] for abelianization. Define

```text
G(P) = { (x,y) in S × S : ab(top(x)) ab(top(y)) = 1 },
τ(x,y) = (y,x).
```

The displayed product takes place in the abelian group P/[P,P]. For nonabelian P,
this condition is not the same as requiring top(y) = top(x)⁻¹.

**Question.** Determine which P admit a finite simple proper left Bol loop whose
canonical left multiplication group and inversion automorphism are isomorphic
to the pair (G(P), τ). For every admissible P, classify all such loops up to
isomorphism.

**Concrete route.** The
[uniform reduction](advances-2026-11-16/manuscripts/radical-free-a5-prime-power-top.md)
reduces this exact model to cyclic e-dimensional modules over the field with p
elements, cocycles, a necessary-and-sufficient collision test, and a specified
coordinate equivalence action. Resolve those module/cocycle orbits for further
families of P, or prove a structural existence criterion for arbitrary P.
Do not quotient by arbitrary changes of basis or by cocycle coboundaries unless
they induce the required loop isomorphisms.

**Status.** General P remains unresolved. Abelian and extraspecial top groups
have already been treated in the current research: the latest source reports
120 classes across the odd extraspecial models, including 88 in the order-5⁵,
exponent-25 case. Their orbit counts are therefore not proposed here as open
problems. These are reported results for the exact model above. The subsequent
24-class proof, 88-class orbit theorem and full assembly are not yet published
here; the older public sources alone do not establish these counts. See the
[results catalogue](RESULTS.md) for the complete table and its public-proof coverage.

## 4. Classify the next odd derived-group layer

**Question.** Classify all finite simple proper left Bol loops Q of odd order
for which the commutator subgroup G′ of G = LMlt(Q) is nilpotent of class exactly
three.

Here “class exactly three” means that the lower central series of G′ satisfies
γ₄(G′) = 1 and γ₃(G′) ≠ 1. Impose no additional bound on the order or exponent
of G′, and do not assume its center is central in G.

**Desired result.** A construction and exhaustion theorem with explicit
admissibility, simplicity and isomorphism criteria. A solution confined to an
extra hypothesis, such as exponent p or centrality of the derived center, should
identify that restriction and the residual cases explicitly.

**Why this matters.** This is a defined next layer beyond the class-two framework,
rather than an unspecified request to “handle higher complexity.” It tests
whether the existing extension methods genuinely iterate.

**Starting point and status.** The
[class-two framework](packet/manuscripts/odd-class-two-classification.md)
does not cover this question. The
[central Lazard framework](packet/manuscripts/odd-lazard-central-classification.md)
covers restricted higher-class data with additional center and quotient
hypotheses, not this entire class. The
[unbounded-class construction](packet/manuscripts/odd-unbounded-derived-class.md)
also rules out treating class three as the final layer of the odd-order problem.
An unrestricted classification of the class-three slice is not established in
the current programme.

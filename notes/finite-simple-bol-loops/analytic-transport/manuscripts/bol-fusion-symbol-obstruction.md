# Charged fusion and the principal symbols of separate dynamical parameters

**Mira Cult**



The canonical charged modules and fusion maps are
[CH1--CH2](bol-charged-heisenberg-obstruction.md#ch2-canonical-charged-fusion-and-its-dual-channel),
reviewed at scientific source 197d13d4ac2e48763ed20bf418228d7b4d16aa8c.
The preceding constant-map obstruction is
[CE6](bol-charged-exchange.md#ce6-constant-charged-fusion-does-not-preserve-separate-dynamical-bases).
This note strengthens that obstruction under an explicit additional
hypothesis: the first-order dynamical operators have the **full separate
Cartan principal-symbol image**. Parameter dependence of an order-zero
matrix cannot repair this architecture.

That hypothesis is not automatic for CE4's shared dynamical variable or
for an arbitrary weight-zero restriction. It does not describe all ordinary
finite-rank connections, whose differential symbol is the scalar identity.
The result does not obstruct a dynamical difference operator, an integral
transform, a changed symbol space, or a shifted tensor structure. No
Bol-specific analytic identity or literature-priority claim is made.

## SY1. The separate diagonal symbol space remembers the Cartesian grid

Let X and Y be sets of the same size N>=3. On H=C^(X times Y), write
M_f for the diagonal operator of multiplication by f. Define


```math

 S_{X,Y}=\{M_{f(x)+g(y)}:f:X\to\mathbb C,\ g:Y\to\mathbb C\},
 \qquad S^0_{X,Y}=S_{X,Y}\cap\mathfrak{sl}(H).
 \tag{SY1}

```


Their dimensions are 2N-1 and 2N-2. Equivalently, S^0 uses the unique
representatives f and g with each sum zero. All arguments also hold over
a characteristic-zero meromorphic coefficient field.

**Lemma SY1 (grid normalizer).** An invertible linear map F between two
such grid spaces conjugates S^0 onto S^0 if and only if it is monomial,
with support permutation either


```math

 (x,y)\longmapsto(\sigma(x),\tau(y))
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 (x,y)\longmapsto(\sigma(y),\tau(x)),
 \tag{SY2}

```


for bijections of the indicated factors. The nonzero diagonal factors
of F are unrestricted by this assertion.

**Proof.** Adjoining I gives S. Row and column indicators belong to S,
and their products give every singleton indicator. Thus the associative
algebra generated by S is the full diagonal algebra. Its minimal
idempotents are precisely the singleton projections, so conjugation by F
permutes their one-dimensional images. Hence F is monomial.

It remains to determine the support permutation pi. The evaluations on
S are the grid weights alpha_(x,y), represented by e_x+e_y in the direct
sum of the two coordinate spaces. A relation

    alpha_a + alpha_d = alpha_b + alpha_c

with four distinct points is precisely a rectangle relation: the row
indices and column indices must each agree as multisets on the two sides.
There are no other four-distinct-point relations of this form. A pair of
distinct points lying in one row or one column belongs to N-1 such
rectangles; a pair with different row and column belongs to exactly one.
Since N>=3, these numbers differ. Any permutation preserving S therefore
preserves the graph in which two points are adjacent when they share a
row or a column.

The maximal cliques in that graph are exactly the full rows and full
columns: three pairwise adjacent points cannot use both different rows
and different columns. Rows are pairwise disjoint, columns are pairwise
disjoint, and every row meets every column. Thus a graph automorphism
either preserves the two classes globally or exchanges them. It has
exactly one of the forms (SY2). Conversely every such permutation, and
every invertible diagonal multiplier, normalizes S and S^0. QED.

The restriction N>=3 suffices for all nontrivial odd-prime spaces below.
No unproved classification of graph automorphisms is used.

## SY2. No charged intertwiner has the full separate-symbol property

Let W be a nonzero finite F_p-space, p odd, and N=|W|. Use CH's modules
H_lambda=Fun(W,C). If gamma=lambda+mu is regular, CH2 supplies an
isomorphism


```math

 C_{\lambda,\mu}:H_\lambda\otimes H_\mu
       \longrightarrow H_\gamma\otimes M,
 \qquad M=\operatorname{Fun}(W,\mathbb C),
 \tag{SY3}

```


where P acts trivially on M. Only regularity of gamma is needed here;
the two input modules may be reducible. The retained first axis acts on
input delta labels by simultaneous translation of x,y, and on output
labels by translation of u alone.

**Theorem SY2.** No invertible P-intertwiner F in (SY3) satisfies


```math

       F S^0_{x,y}F^{-1}=S^0_{u,v}.
 \tag{SY4}

```


**Proof.** SY1 makes F monomial with a product or swapped-product support
permutation pi. First-axis intertwining requires, with an immaterial
common choice of translation sign,


```math

 \pi(x+t,y+t)=(\pi_1(x,y)+t,\pi_2(x,y))
             \quad(t\in W).
 \tag{SY5}

```


In the product case its second coordinate says tau(y+t)=tau(y) for every
y,t. In the swapped case it says tau(x+t)=tau(x). Either makes a bijection
constant, a contradiction because |W|>1. Diagonal multipliers cannot
change this permutation equation. QED.

For context, every actual P-intertwiner in (SY3) has the form


```math

              F=(I_{H_\gamma}\otimes T)C_{\lambda,\mu}
 \tag{SY6}

```


for T in GL(M). Indeed F C^(-1) commutes with the irreducible P-action on
H_gamma, and expansion in matrix units of End(M), followed by Schur's
lemma, proves (SY6). Allowing arbitrary T therefore does not evade SY2.
This observation extends coefficientwise to meromorphic families.

## SY3. Parameter-dependent order-zero maps obey the same obstruction

For a first-order matrix differential operator D on a manifold B, let
sigma_D,b:T_b^*B -> End(H) be its principal symbol at b. It is the sum of
the derivative coefficients contracted with a covector. Lower-order
terms, including elliptic kernels and derivatives of a multiplying matrix,
do not enter it.

An explicit separate-parameter model uses two Cartan coordinate spaces

    q=(q_x)_(x in W), sum_x q_x=0,
    r=(r_y)_(y in W), sum_y r_y=0,

and, with h_x=E_xx-I/N, the first-order term


```math

 D_{\rm in}=
 \sum_x(h_x\otimes I)\partial_{q_x}
 +\sum_y(I\otimes h_y)\partial_{r_y}
 +A_{\rm in}.
 \tag{SY7}

```


Derivatives may be defined using extensions invariant under translating
all q coordinates or all r coordinates. The symbol image is exactly
S^0_(x,y). There is an analogous output model in coordinates a_u,b_v,
with image S^0_(u,v). The coefficients A may depend meromorphically on
positions, tau, and the dynamical variables. Additional scalar position
derivatives cause no change after adjoining I to the symbol spaces.

**Theorem SY3 (zero-order dynamical obstruction).** Suppose the following
hold on a nonempty open set of the parameter bases:

1. The input and output first-order operators have full symbol
   images S^0_(x,y), S^0_(u,v), or those spaces with the scalar identity
   adjoined.
2. The chosen base identification is etale, so its differential is
   invertible and preserves these image dimensions.
3. F is an invertible meromorphic order-zero multiplication operator,
   is a P-intertwiner in each fiber, and intertwines the full differential
   operators after that pullback.

Then no such F exists for a regular sum gamma.

**Proof.** Equality of first-order differential operators implies equality
of principal symbols. Derivatives hitting F contribute only to the
zeroth-order term. The invertible Jacobian changes the parametrization of
the output symbol image, not its image as a matrix subspace. Hence
F S^0_(x,y) F^(-1)=S^0_(u,v), contrary to SY2. The scalar-augmented case
gives the same statement because trace is preserved by conjugation. QED.

The result concerns actual matrix-valued differential symbols. The ordinary
equation dF+A_out F-F A_in=0 for finite-rank connections has scalar
principal symbol and does **not** imply hypothesis 1. In CE4 the q
derivatives act on the function-space fiber; applying SY3 requires that
their actual matrix coefficient images satisfy hypothesis 1 after every
weight restriction. One cannot infer that property merely from the words
"dynamical connection" or from an ambient Cartan before restriction.

### A balanced zero-weight control where the full symbol really occurs

For the defining sl_N-module V and its dual V*, the total-weight-zero
subspace of V tensor V* is

    B=span{e_x tensor e_x*: x in W}.

On B, the first factor's Cartan operator has eigenvalues
delta_(a,x)-1/N. Therefore the q-symbol of CE4's first-site operator spans
the entire traceless diagonal algebra on B. Two independently parameterized
copies of this construction have B tensor B as their fiber, and the sum
of their first-site operators has precisely (SY7)'s image. The first-axis
action on B comes from conjugating the rank-one diagonal projections and
translates their labels. A charged block plus a P-trivial multiplicity
block has the output action used in SY2.

For this balanced control the same first-axis permutation argument applies
directly; it does not use irreducibility or the charged factorization
(SY6), since the balanced modules themselves have trivial central charge.

This is a well-specified control. It does not identify B tensor B with
every original trace sector, claim that an arbitrary F preserves that
balanced subspace, or replace CE4's shared-q system by this independent-q
one without saying so.

### Moving-base equivariance and scalar carry factors

There is a useful variant. Suppose a connected complex parameter domain
is P-stable, F is single-valued meromorphic, and full symbol conjugacy
holds pointwise. Instead of fiberwise P-linearity assume


```math

 F(gb)\rho_{\rm in}(g)=\rho_{\rm out}(g)F(b).
 \tag{SY8}

```


SY1 makes F generically monomial. Its support permutation is constant on
the connected domain: a meromorphic function which vanishes on a nonempty
open set vanishes identically, so a generically nonzero matrix entry cannot
switch to a different permutation support on another open set. Equation
(SY8) now gives the same impossible first-axis permutation equation (SY5).
Thus this equivariant variant is also excluded. A disconnected cover whose
components carry different permutations is outside this statement.

Local scalar carry-line factors, cocycle phases or changes of line-bundle
frame alter only nonzero scalar multipliers and lower-order terms. They
cannot change the symbol-space contradiction. This does not erase fixed
cocycle markings from a different, viable analytic architecture.

## SY4. The scalar family forces a full joint parameter space

The obstruction suggests changing the symbol image itself. In the
one-dimensional Heisenberg control W=F_p, b(x,y)=xy, let nonzero scalar
charges lambda,mu have nonzero sum. Their normalized center coefficient
alpha=lambda/(lambda+mu) can be any element of F_p other than 0,1.
The canonical coordinates include


```math

 u_\alpha=\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y,\qquad v=x-y.
 \tag{SY9}

```


**Proposition SY4.** The smallest complex linear space of functions on
F_p^2 containing all functions of x, all functions of y, all functions
of x-y, and all functions of u_alpha for alpha not 0,1, is
Fun(F_p^2,C). Its dimension is p^2; the traceless diagonal part has
dimension p^2-1.

**Proof.** The nonzero Fourier frequencies of a function of a linear form
are the nonzero vectors on the line spanned by that form. The two axes,
the line (1,-1), and the p-2 lines (alpha,1-alpha) are all p+1 projective
lines of F_p^2: any vector whose coordinate sum is nonzero can be normalized
to sum one, and the remaining line has coordinate sum zero. They partition
the nonzero frequency vectors. Hence the listed functions contain every
Fourier character, a basis of Fun(F_p^2,C). QED.

For p=3 there is only alpha=2. Thus the input and output separate spaces
of a single regular (1,1) fusion already span the full nine-dimensional
diagonal algebra. Their traceless sum has dimension eight, whereas either
separate space has dimension four. For p>3 one fixed regular splitting
gives four distinct Fourier lines and dimension 4p-3; using every regular
scalar charge splitting gives p^2.

This is a necessary enlargement for that linear parameter architecture,
not a constructed flat joint connection. It neither supplies the elliptic
lower-order coefficients nor proves their pentagon. In higher-dimensional
W, scalar coefficients alone need not cover every pair of Fourier vectors,
so the full p^2 conclusion is deliberately one-dimensional.

## SY5. Joint holonomy invariants enlarge under fusion

Let the two input charges be regular, N=p^d, and let a fixed marked pair
have nonzero commutator phase in each input. Its separate fixed algebras
have tensor product Mat_(p^(2d-2)). On the tensor product the actual joint
holonomies act diagonally. There are only two joint invariance conditions,
rather than the four separate ones.

**Corollary SY5.** If the total commutator phase is nonzero, the joint
fixed algebra is Mat_(p^(2d-1)). If the total phase is zero, it is


```math

         \mathbb C^{p^2}\otimes
               \operatorname{Mat}_{p^{2d-2}}(\mathbb C).
 \tag{SY10}

```


In both cases its vector-space dimension is p^2 times that of the tensor
product of separately fixed algebras.

**Proof.** Apply the canonical
[CH3 fixed-algebra theorem](bol-charged-heisenberg-obstruction.md#ch3-two-holonomies-force-a-large-zero-mode-algebra)
to the direct sum of the two symplectic Weyl spaces. Its half-dimension
is 2d. The two joint labels are independent; their span has restricted
rank two when the commutator phases add to a nonzero value, and rank zero
when they cancel. Substituting s=2, t=1 or 0 gives the formulas. The
inclusion of the separately invariant algebra is immediate, and the
dimensions have the stated ratio. QED.

For p=3,d=1, the inputs are separately scalar. Charges (1,1) give a joint
Mat_3 zero algebra, while charges (1,2) give C^9. Both have dimension nine.
This is the precise reason that taking separate zero blocks and then
tensoring them loses product-reached channels. It is an application of
CH3, not a new independent classification of its algebras.

## SY6. Exact control and the remaining construction

The standard-library certificate
[verify_bol_dynamical_fusion.py](../code/verify_bol_dynamical_fusion.py)
exhausts all 9! support permutations for W=F_3. It tests preservation of
the separate function space by exact mixed differences, independently
tests first-axis equivariance, and compares both sets with the proposed
classification. It checks the actual small charged intertwiner, the
joint parameter spans for p=3,5,7,11, and the joint-zero algebra control.
Its deterministic output records actual counts and source hash. Finite
tests validate the implementation; SY1--SY5 have symbolic proofs above.

The excluded architecture has independent separate Cartan parameters,
order-zero finite matrices and a symbol-preserving base change. A genuine
joint parameter algebra, shifted/difference fusion, higher-order or
integral intertwiners, or an operator-valued connection with different
symbol image can evade it. Each requires an actual coherence construction.
Nothing here supplies the requested Bol-specific noncoincident relation
or the marked isotope separator. The theorem prevents parameter dependence
alone from being mistaken for that missing construction.

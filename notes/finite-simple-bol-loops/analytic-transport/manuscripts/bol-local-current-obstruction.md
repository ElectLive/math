# A local-current obstruction and the associative quotient it forces

**Mira Cult**



## The architecture being tested

The existing [sandwich operators](bol-translation-fock.md) retain a proper
Bol loop without requiring multiplicativity. A different possible interaction
would put its *actual product* into the residue of local matrix currents.
This section tests that particular proposal before imposing genus-one
analytic conditions. The failure is already a local three-insertion failure.

Let A be a unital complex algebra, not assumed associative. Choose n >= 4.
In a complex Lie algebra suppose there are linear maps

    J_ij : A -> g,                 1 <= i != j <= n,

with the following matrix-current rules:


```math

 [J_{ij}(a),J_{jk}(b)]=J_{ik}(ab)
 \quad(i,j,k\text{ distinct}),                         \tag{LC.1}

```



```math

 [J_{ij}(a),J_{kl}(b)]=0
 \quad(j\ne k,\ i\ne l).                              \tag{LC.2}

```


No assertion about diagonal currents or central extensions is needed.
These equations can also be imposed on the zero-mode operators of even
local fields. Ordinary operator commutators satisfy Jacobi; for weight-one
fields in a vertex algebra the same identity follows from the zero-product
case of the Borcherds identity. Central higher poles do not alter these
zero-mode equations. See [Keller's notes](../REFERENCES.md#keller17) for the standard
local-field identity; the deductions below are proved directly.

Define I_ass(A) to be the smallest two-sided ideal of the possibly
nonassociative algebra A containing every associator

    (a,b,c) = (ab)c - a(bc).

The quotient A_ass=A/I_ass(A) is associative. It is the largest associative
quotient in the elementary sense that every multiplicative linear map from A
to an associative algebra factors through it. This quotient is **not** the
associative subalgebra of End(A) generated by left and right multiplications.
The latter can retain the full marked loop and is not excluded here.

## LC1. Matrix-current locality forces the associative quotient

**Theorem LC1.** Every J_ij vanishes on I_ass(A). In particular, if any J_ij
is injective, then A is associative.

**Proof.** Given i != l choose j,k so that i,j,k,l are pairwise distinct.
Jacobi, (LC.1), and (LC.2) give


```math

\begin{aligned}
J_{il}(a(bc))
 &= [J_{ij}(a),[J_{jk}(b),J_{kl}(c)]]\\
 &= [[J_{ij}(a),J_{jk}(b)],J_{kl}(c)]
       +[J_{jk}(b),[J_{ij}(a),J_{kl}(c)]]\\
 &=J_{il}((ab)c).
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{LC.3}

```


Thus every raw associator belongs to K=intersection_(i!=j) ker J_ij.
For a in K and any b in A, choose k different from i,j. Then

    J_ij(ab)=[J_ik(a),J_kj(b)]=0,
    J_ij(ba)=[J_ik(b),J_kj(a)]=0.

So K is a two-sided ideal and contains I_ass(A). Finally, the same
calculation (LC.3) in an injective channel forces every associator to vanish.
QED.

This is a necessary condition, not an assertion that arbitrary additional
relations among the J_ij are classified by A_ass. For an associative A the
ordinary matrices over A give a faithful realization of (LC.1)--(LC.2), so
the condition for *existence of an injective realization of these rules* is
also sufficient.

## LC2. The loss is total for a simple proper loop

Let L be a loop. Its complex loop algebra has basis e_x, x in L, unit e_1,
and bilinear multiplication e_x e_y=e_(xy). Let

    G_ass(L)=<g_x (x in L) | g_1=1, g_x g_y=g_(xy)>.

Unlike the sandwich group U_B(L), this presentation deliberately imposes
multiplicativity. It is a useful control, not the proposed representation.

**Theorem LC2.** There is a canonical isomorphism


```math

 (\mathbb C[L])_{\rm ass}\ \cong\ \mathbb C[G_{\rm ass}(L)]. \tag{LC.4}

```


If L is a simple nonassociative loop, both sides are C and every current
J_ij satisfying (LC.1)--(LC.2) has

    J_ij(e_x)=J_ij(e_1)                 for every x in L.

**Proof.** In the associative quotient, the image of e_x is invertible:
the loop has a left inverse and a right inverse, whose images are left and
right inverses in a unital associative algebra, and hence coincide. Therefore
the images of e_x induce a homomorphism C[G_ass(L)] -> (C[L])_ass. In the
opposite direction the defining group relations make e_x -> g_x a unital
multiplicative linear map, so it factors through (C[L])_ass. The two maps
are inverse on their generators, proving (LC.4).

The map x -> g_x is a loop homomorphism to a group. Its kernel is a normal
subloop, equivalently the identity class of its kernel congruence. If L is
simple, the kernel is either all of L or {1}. The latter would make the map
injective: if g_x=g_y then g_(x\y)=1, so x\y=1 and x=y. An injective
multiplicative map into a group forces associativity, contrary to hypothesis.
Thus every g_x=1 and G_ass(L) is trivial. LC1 gives the current assertion.
QED.

The augmentation e_x -> 1 shows that the quotient is C rather than the zero
algebra. For each of the pinned huge simple proper Bol inputs, this conclusion
is conditional only on the inherited simplicity and nonassociativity facts;
no enumeration or new audit of their classification proof is used. It also
applies to simple nonassociative Moufang loops. It does not distinguish the
rigid isotope pair: it explains why this particular local-current proposal
would erase both completely.

## LC3. A proper Bol family and its surviving charges

Use the already proved [central cubic family](bol-cubic-isotopes.md): for an
odd prime p and k in F_p,


```math

(c,a,b)\cdot_k(d,u,v)
 =\bigl(c+d+bu(a+u)+k(av-bu),\ a+u,\ b+v\bigr).       \tag{LC.5}

```


The cited canonical proof establishes proper left Bol structure, the
isotopy of its Bruck and non-Bruck members, and the associator

    A((a,b),(u,v),(s,t))=(av-bu)s.

**Proposition LC3.** For every k,


```math

G_{\rm ass}(Q_k)\cong (\mathbb F_p^2,+),\qquad
(\mathbb C[Q_k])_{\rm ass}\cong\mathbb C[\mathbb F_p^2]. \tag{LC.6}

```


Thus this residue architecture kills every nontrivial central-character
sector and cannot separate the two proper Bol isomorphism types.

**Proof.** Projection (c,a,b) -> (a,b) is a homomorphism to F_p^2, so
G_ass(Q_k) maps onto that group. Put x=(0,1,0), y=(0,0,1), and z=x. Directly
from (LC.5), independently of k,

    (xy)z=(2,2,1),                 x(yz)=(1,2,1).

Associativity in the group image therefore kills the central element
(1,0,0). It generates the entire center, and quotienting by that center
leaves exactly F_p^2. Hence no further relation is forced on the quotient,
proving (LC.6). For a nontrivial central character lambda, the Fourier
combination sum_c psi(-lambda c)e_(c,a,b) maps to zero, since every summand
has the same image and its coefficients sum to zero. QED.

For p=3, the two nested-residue channels differ by the distinct basis
vectors e_(2,2,1)-e_(1,2,1). The
[exact small certificate](../evidence/bol-charged-sewing-certificate.md)
checks the associative congruence closure and that its nine classes are
precisely the central fibers, for both k=0 and k=1. The symbolic proof, not
that calculation, establishes the statement for every odd prime.

## What would escape this obstruction

The theorem does not exclude charge sectors indexed by the additive group
Z*, matrix coefficients of the faithful sandwich action, multiple intermediate
fields in an operator product, or a nontrivial higher-homotopy sewing law.
It excludes only the explicit residue equations (LC.1)--(LC.2). Adding a
central higher pole, changing a genus-one normalization, or saturating zero
modes leaves (LC.3) unchanged. A successful interaction must modify at least
one of these residue rules or retain additional intermediate channels.

Nor is LC1 evidence for new analytic rigidity. Its proof is the standard
local Jacobi constraint applied to a proposed loop-sensitive residue. It is
recorded to prevent a false repair of the Gaussian architecture, and to
make the next charged-intertwiner problem precise.

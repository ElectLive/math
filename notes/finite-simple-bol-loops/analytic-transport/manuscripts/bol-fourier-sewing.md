# Commutator Fourier sewing and a four-sector modular identity

**Mira Cult**

This manuscript adds a specified finite operation on the existing graded
seed. Its strongest conclusion is a functional equation involving four
inequivalent temporal holonomies, with an exact converse and a controlled
failure for the rigid principal isotope. The equation is forced by a
polynomial support relation in the marked conjugacy-class geometry. It is
not a literal equality of group words. Its passage to modular functions
uses the usual exterior determinant and discriminant product, whose
universality is retained explicitly.

The added Fourier operators are outside the original algebra generated by
multiplication colors and Bol translations; this is proved in
[HR5a](bol-holonomy-rigidity.md#hr5-what-commutator-fourier-sewing-changes).
Thus the result does not assert that the unchanged kernel alphabet already
forces this equation. The source construction, simplicity, properness, and
strict-isomorphism dependencies remain those of
[CF1--CF8](bol-translation-class-fusion.md) and the
[canonical pinned inputs](../README.md).

## FS1. The finite Fourier interface on the existing seed

Use the elementary class-two local input of CF1:


```math

 W=Q/Z,\quad d=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}W,\quad v=|W|=p^d,\qquad
 C=\operatorname{Conj}(Q),\quad c=|C|,\quad A_C=\operatorname{Fun}(C).

```


The prime $p$ is odd. If $J_w=[t_w,Q]\subseteq Z$, the central orbit
over $w$ is $O_w\cong Z/J_w$; its definition does not require a lift
$t_w$. Put


```math

 I=[G:Z],\qquad B=\operatorname{Fun}(G\times_Z C),\qquad N=Ic.

```


Let $\alpha,\beta$ be specified nondegenerate scalar alternating forms
obtained from the actual commutator map $W\times W\to Z$. Write


```math

 \alpha(Tx,y)=\beta(x,y).                            \tag{FS1}

```


The canonical zero-charge space $V_0=A_C^Z$ has orthonormal basis
$\eta_w=|O_w|^{-1/2}\mathbf1_{O_w}$.
[HR5](bol-holonomy-rigidity.md#hr5-what-commutator-fourier-sewing-changes)
owns the complete proof that


```math

 F_\alpha\eta_y=v^{-1/2}\sum_{x\in W}
       \psi(\alpha(x,y))\eta_x,\qquad
 F_\alpha F_\beta^{-1}=P_T,\qquad
 P_T\eta_y=\eta_{Ty},                                \tag{FS2}

```


where $\psi(t)=\exp(2\pi it/p)$. Each Fourier operator is a unitary
self-adjoint involution. Extend it as the identity on $V_0^\perp$ and
induce fiberwise to $B$. The resulting operators commute with the
actual left $G$-action and the right $Z$-action. Denote their relative
operator on $B$ by $S_T^0$.

This is an operator on the existing seed, not a newly attached finite
representation. Its extra input is the charged commutator pairing.
It transports under the strict marked-source isomorphisms. The superscript
zero matters: only the zero-charge multiplicity space is changed at this
stage. Section FS5 constructs a stronger chargewise extension under
additional hypotheses.

Alternation in (FS1) gives
$\alpha(Tx,y)=\alpha(x,Ty)$, so $T$ is self-adjoint for $\alpha$.
This observation will impose arithmetic restrictions on its analytic trace.

## FS2. Shared-variable networks and the complete oscillator trace

For $k\geq1$, with $x_{k+1}=x_1$, define the cyclic commutator network


```math

 \mathcal N_k(\alpha,\beta)
 =v^{-k}\!\!\sum_{\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in W^k}
       \psi\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^k
           [\alpha(x_{i+1},y_i)-\beta(x_i,y_i)]\right).
                                                               \tag{FS3}

```


The normalization is $v^{-k}$, not the probability normalization
$v^{-2k}$. Every phase is an evaluation of a central word in actual
local Bol translations. Shared variables are essential.

**Proposition FS2.** The exact network value is


```math

 \mathcal N_k(\alpha,\beta)
   =\operatorname{Tr}_{V_0}(P_T^k)
   =|\ker(T^k-1)|.                                  \tag{FS4}

```


Consequently


```math

 \operatorname{Tr}_B((S_T^0)^k)=I(c-v+\mathcal N_k).  \tag{FS5}

```


**Proof.** The $i$-th summation over $y_i$ in (FS3) is $v$ if
$x_{i+1}=Tx_i$, and zero otherwise, by (FS1) and nondegeneracy of
$\alpha$. A surviving tuple is determined by $x_1$, with
$T^kx_1=x_1$. The $v^k$ factor cancels the normalization. Equivalently
each pair sum is a matrix entry of $F_\alpha F_\beta^{-1}$, and closing
the cycle takes its trace. The other $c-v$ local dimensions are fixed;
induction repeats the local spectrum $I$ times. This proves (FS5). QED.

The original exterior space is


```math

 \mathcal F_B=\Lambda\!\left(\bigoplus_{m\geq1}
                              B[m]\otimes\mathbb C^{24}\right).

```


There is also a canonical invariant subspace, using only odd energies:


```math

 \mathcal F_B^{\mathrm{odd}}
 =\Lambda\!\left(\bigoplus_{\substack{m\geq1\\m\ {\rm odd}}}
                              B[m]\otimes\mathbb C^{24}\right)
 \subseteq\mathcal F_B.                              \tag{FS6}

```


Both spaces have the original integer energy grading $D$, finite
dimensional homogeneous pieces, the actual Bol action, and the induced
Fourier intertwiners. No dual or zero-mode factor is added. Currents with
both mode indices odd preserve the second space; no assertion that all
original mode currents preserve it is intended.

For a finite-order operator $P$ on $B$, write


```math

 \Phi_P(\tau)=q^N\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal F_B}
                      (\Gamma(P)q^D),\qquad
 \Psi_P(\tau)=q^{-N}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal F_B^{\mathrm{odd}}}
                      (\Gamma(P)q^D),\quad q=e^{2\pi i\tau}.
                                                               \tag{FS7}

```


The different shifts are specified, not fitted. Removing the even
oscillators gives the exact identity


```math

                         \Psi_P(\tau)=
                      \frac{\Phi_P(\tau)}{\Phi_P(2\tau)}.         \tag{FS8}

```


For $P=S_T^0$, the entire logarithm is the lift of the networks:


```math

 \log(q^N\Psi_{S_T^0}(\tau))
 =24I\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k}
       \frac{q^k}{1-q^{2k}}\,[c-v+\mathcal N_k(\alpha,\beta)].
                                                               \tag{FS9}

```


The branch is the one tending to zero as $q\to0$.
Indeed $\log\det(1+q^mP)=
\sum_{k\geq1}(-1)^{k-1}q^{mk}\operatorname{Tr}(P^k)/k$.
Sum this over the odd positive integers $m$, use (FS5), and multiply
by 24. All sums converge absolutely and locally uniformly for
$\operatorname{Im}\tau>0$, since the finite-order eigenvalues have
absolute value one. This proves (FS9) as an analytic identity, as well as
coefficientwise.

Every length $k$ contributes. The result is not one recovered finite
phase multiplied by a common function of $\tau$. The new information is
nevertheless finite: it is the relative linear operator $T$, expressed
through all of its cyclic networks.

## FS3. Modular laws and recoverable coefficient restrictions

Set $H(\tau)=\Delta(2\tau)/\Delta(\tau)$. The normalization and
modularity of $\Delta$ are the same as in
[the earlier fermion theorem](bol-fermion-sewing.md).

**Theorem FS3.** Suppose $P$ has the eigenvalue multiset of a permutation
with $c_j(P)$ cycles of length $j$. This holds for $S_T^0$: its
spectrum is $I$ copies of $P_T$ and $I(c-v)$ identity eigenvalues.
Then the actual traces in (FS7) are


```math

 \Phi_P=
   \prod_{\substack{j\geq1\\j\ {\rm odd}}}H(j\tau)^{c_j(P)}
   \prod_{\substack{j\geq1\\j\ {\rm even}}}\Delta(j\tau)^{c_j(P)},
                                                               \tag{FS10}

```



```math

 \Psi_P=
   \prod_{j\ {\rm odd}}
          \left[\frac{H(j\tau)}{H(2j\tau)}\right]^{c_j(P)}
   \prod_{j\ {\rm even}}
          \left[\frac{\Delta(j\tau)}{\Delta(2j\tau)}\right]^{c_j(P)}.
                                                               \tag{FS11}

```


If $m=\operatorname{ord}(P)$, $\Phi_P$ is a weakly holomorphic modular
form of weight $12\sum_{j\ {\rm even}}c_j(P)$, with trivial multiplier
on $\Gamma_0(2m)$. The function $\Psi_P$ has weight zero and trivial
multiplier on $\Gamma_0(4m)$. Both are nonzero and holomorphic in the
upper half-plane, with possible poles only at cusps. Their unshifted
series are honest graded traces with integral coefficients; twining
coefficients need not be nonnegative.

**Proof.** A permutation cycle of length $j$ has


```math

                  \det(1+tP_{\rm cycle})=1-(-t)^j.

```


For odd $j$, its 24-species positive-mode trace is
$q^{-j}H(j\tau)$; for even $j$ it is
$q^{-j}\Delta(j\tau)$. Since $\sum_jjc_j(P)=N$, the prescribed
$q^N$ removes precisely these prefactors, proving (FS10).
Equation (FS8) gives (FS11).

For $\gamma\in\Gamma_0(a)$, the usual weight-12 transformation of
$\Delta(a\tau)$ follows by applying the transformation of $\Delta$
to the integral matrix
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u&av\\w/a&x\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
when $\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u&v\\w&x\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Thus the stated products have the stated weights and trivial multipliers.
The levels $2m$ and $4m$ are sufficient, without claiming minimality.
The products converge absolutely in the upper half-plane and have no
zero there. Modularity transports the convergent cusp expansions.
Integrality follows directly from the exterior determinants, independently
of the modular expressions. QED.

One may use the sharper common level
$\operatorname{lcm}(\{4j:j\ {\rm odd},c_j>0\}
\cup\{2j:j\ {\rm even},c_j>0\})$ for (FS11).
In particular, permutations whose orders divide six have a common
weight-zero law on $\Gamma_0(12)$, hence also on $\Gamma_0(24)$.
No odd-order argument is being incorrectly applied to the Fourier
involutions.

The odd-mode cycle lift has no accidental multiplicative coincidences.
For finitely many permutation spectra $P_i$ and integers $a_i$,


```math

 \prod_i\Psi_{P_i}^{a_i}=1
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
       \sum_i a_i c_j(P_i)=0\quad\hbox{for every }j.              \tag{FS11a}

```


Indeed the leading power of $q$ must first cancel. If $j$ is the
smallest length with a nonzero combined cycle count, the coefficient
of $q^j$ in the logarithm of the remaining product is
$24(-1)^{j-1}\sum_i a_i c_j(P_i)$, which cannot vanish. The converse
is immediate from (FS11). Thus the monomial relations below have
finite cycle-geometric origins; they are not fitted eta coincidences.

The analytic function determines the complete fixed-point sequence in
(FS4). Explicitly, let


```math

 L_s=\frac1{24I}[q^s]\log(q^N\Psi_{S_T^0}),\qquad
 s=2^a u,\quad u\ {\rm odd},\qquad
 \epsilon_a=\begin{cases}1&a=0,\\-1&a>0.\end{cases}

```


Then ordinary divisor inversion in (FS9) gives


```math

 \mathcal N_{2^au}
   =\epsilon_a\,2^a u\sum_{t\mid u}\mu(u/t)L_{2^at}-(c-v).
                                                               \tag{FS12}

```


To check it, (FS9) says
$L_{2^au}=\epsilon_a2^{-a}
\sum_{t\mid u}(c-v+\mathcal N_{2^at})/t$.
Apply the divisor Möbius inversion formula and multiply by $u$.
Thus equality of two such functions with the same $I,c,v$ is
equivalent to equality of all $\mathcal N_k$, equivalently equality
of the permutation cycle types of their relative operators.

There are nontrivial eligibility conditions on the recovered numbers.
Every $T^k-1$ is $\alpha$-self-adjoint, so
$(x,y)\mapsto\alpha((T^k-1)x,y)$ is alternating. Its rank is even.
Because $d$ is even, its nullity is even too. Therefore


```math

                  \mathcal N_k=p^{2h_k}
          \quad\hbox{for some integer }0\leq h_k\leq d/2.        \tag{FS13}

```


In addition,


```math

          c_j(T)=\frac1j\sum_{e\mid j}\mu(j/e)\mathcal N_e
                       \in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}.                    \tag{FS14}

```


The second condition is orbit counting; the first uses the alternating
source geometry. The paired-Jordan phenomenon itself is already treated
in [the earlier family theorem](bol-cocycle-packets.md#complete-rank-packet-recovery-in-the-extraspecial-branch).
Here these finite restrictions become explicit conditions on logarithmic
modular coefficients. They are not claimed to be a new principle of
modular differentiation or a complete classification of possible sequences.

## FS4. What zero-charge sewing can and cannot add

Let $P_g$ be the permutation of $G/Z$ induced by $g\in G$. The
zero-charge induced summand is
$\mathbb C[G/Z]\otimes V_0$. On it, $L_g$ acts as $P_g\otimes1$,
$R_z$ acts as the identity, and $S_T^0$ acts as $1\otimes P_T$.
The other summand has unchanged $S_T^0$-action.

Consequently, for a formal variable $t$,


```math

 \det_B(1+tL_gR_zS_T^0)
 =\det_B(1+tL_gR_z)
       \frac{\det(1+tP_g\otimes P_T)}
            {\det(1+tP_g)^v}.                       \tag{FS15}

```


This equality is first an equality of polynomials after cross
multiplication. Factoring the same invariant direct sum proves it.
Multiplication over all oscillator modes, with the matching shifts,
then gives


```math

 \Phi_{L_gR_zS_T^0}
   =\Phi_{L_gR_z}\frac{\Phi_{P_g\otimes P_T}}{\Phi_{P_g}^{\,v}},
 \qquad
 \Psi_{L_gR_zS_T^0}
   =\Psi_{L_gR_z}\frac{\Psi_{P_g\otimes P_T}}{\Psi_{P_g}^{\,v}}.
                                                               \tag{FS16}

```


The dimensions in the quotient cancel. The formulas use the same
24-species convention on each indicated finite space.
They apply to actual graded traces, not virtual replacements of the
seed. The quotient is only a convenient evaluation.

The correction is independent of the right central label $z$.
It therefore multiplies every right-charge-projected trace by the same
factor. For $g\in Z$, normality of $Z$ makes $P_g=1$, and the
correction is also independent of $g$. Thus this zero-charge version
does not improve the separation of central cocycle probes: for fixed
$\alpha,\beta$ its factor is independent of the cocycle $f$.
Choosing forms by $f$-dependent phase labels can reintroduce that
already retained marking, but does not invalidate the factorization.

This is the reason to examine the stronger extension below. It also
prevents mistaking any new Fourier intertwiner for automatic new
information in every mixed trace.

## FS5. A canonical extension through all charge spaces

The full marked folder supplies origins for the nonzero-charge bases.
The bare $Z$-set alone does not. We use precisely the strict
class-two normal-form interface retained in CF0.

Let $S=H\cap P$, the first canonical axis, and let $\tau$ be the
canonical inversion automorphism. The map
$s\mapsto s\tau(s)^{-1}Z$ identifies the elementary abelian group $S$
with $W=Q/Z$. In the source coordinates this is $x\mapsto(x,-x)$;
the two axes have zero intersection, so the identification is injective
and surjective. For the corresponding $s$, set


```math

 t_w=s\,\tau(s)^{-1}
            [s,\tau(s)^{-1}]^{-1/2}.                \tag{FS17}

```


We use $[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}$. Central square roots are unique at odd
order. Formula (FS17) is the class-two BCH sum
$\exp(\log s-\log\tau(s))$, hence is $(x,-x,0)$ in the normal
coordinates. It is inverted by $\tau$, belongs to $K\cap P$, and
lifts $w$. Group isomorphisms preserving $H,P,\tau$ preserve every
operation in (FS17). Thus this is a functorial section in the retained
folder interface, rather than an arbitrarily chosen central lift.

For each central character $\ell$, let $R_\ell$ be the radical of
its scalar commutator form. The following orthonormal basis of
$(A_C)_\ell$ is now canonically specified:


```math

 \eta_{\ell,w}([t_u z])
   =\mathbf1_{u=w}\,|Z/J_w|^{-1/2}\psi(-\ell(z)),
               \qquad w\in R_\ell.                 \tag{FS18}

```


It is well defined because $\ell(J_w)=0$. Orthogonality on each
central orbit is finite character orthogonality; distinct $w$'s
have disjoint supports. The inverse-pullback action of $z_0\in Z$
has eigenvalue $\psi(\ell(z_0))$, with this sign convention.

Call $(\alpha,\beta)$ **fully admissible** if, for every $\ell$,
$\alpha|_{R_\ell}$ is nondegenerate and $T(R_\ell)=R_\ell$, with
$T$ defined in (FS1). The zero vector space is permitted.
Then $\beta|_{R_\ell}$ is also nondegenerate. Define the Fourier
transform separately on each basis (FS18), using the restricted form
and normalization $|R_\ell|^{-1/2}$.

**Theorem FS5.** For a fully admissible pair, the resulting transforms
$\mathbb F_\alpha,\mathbb F_\beta$ are unitary $Z$-intertwiners and


```math

 \mathbb F_\alpha\mathbb F_\beta^{-1}
                  \eta_{\ell,w}=\eta_{\ell,Tw}.      \tag{FS19}

```


Their product is a point permutation of $C$, explicitly


```math

             [t_wz]\longmapsto[t_{Tw}z].             \tag{FS20}

```


It induces a permutation $S_T$ of $X=G\times_Z C$ commuting with
the actual $G$-action and with the right $Z$-action. It is
functorial under the stated strict source isomorphisms.

**Proof.** Apply the finite Fourier calculation of HR5 to each
nondegenerate restricted space $R_\ell$. Equation (FS1) restricts
there, so the product is exactly (FS19). Each charge space is preserved,
proving $Z$-equivariance.

For every character $\ell$,


```math

 \ell(J_w)=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ w\in R_\ell
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ Tw\in R_\ell
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ell(J_{Tw})=0.

```


Character separation gives $J_{Tw}=J_w$. Thus (FS20) is a well-defined
bijection of central cosets and it preserves their sizes. Its
inverse-pullback action is (FS19), including the normalization in
(FS18). This proves it is the product operator already constructed.
It commutes with central translation, so it induces a point permutation
of $X$ by $[g,c]\mapsto[g,S_Tc]$, which commutes with left $G$.
The section (FS17), forms, radicals, and all sums transport under the
strict source isomorphisms. QED.

This extension has stronger hypotheses than the always available
zero-charge construction. It is not asserted for arbitrary scalar
form pencils. It also need not belong to the original multiplication
and translation algebra: on its zero-charge subspace it still crosses
the orbit blocks of HR5a whenever $T\ne1$.

Unlike (FS16), its central mixed traces can involve nonzero charges.
The exact finite formula is


```math

 \operatorname{Tr}_{A_C}(R_zS_T^k)
    =\sum_{\ell\in Z^*}\psi(\ell(z))
                  |\ker(T^k-1)\cap R_\ell|.          \tag{FS21}

```


It follows by taking the trace in (FS19). For right holonomy $R_zS_T$
on $B$, (FS9) is replaced by


```math

 \log(q^N\Psi_{R_zS_T})
  =24I\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}q^k}{k(1-q^{2k})}
       \sum_\ell\psi(k\ell(z))
                    |\ker(T^k-1)\cap R_\ell|.        \tag{FS22}

```


This is a single charge-coupled object. Every operator is an actual
Bol intertwiner on the same seed. Since (FS20) is a permutation,
Theorem FS3 supplies its modular law and that of its mixed right
holonomies, including those of even order.

## FS6. A four-sector identity and its complete correlation version

The following result specifies exactly what finite geometry is required.
It applies to the fully induced permutation $S_T$, but is stated first
for arbitrary commuting permutations.

**Theorem FS6 (support criterion and analytic defect).** Let $S,R$ be
commuting permutations of the same finite set $X$, with $S^2=1$ and
$R^3=1$. Let $b_6$ be the number of length-six cycles of $SR$.
The following conditions are equivalent:

1. $S$ and $R$ have disjoint moving supports on $X$.
2. $(S-1)(R-1)=0$ as an operator on $\operatorname{Fun}(X)$.
3. $b_6=0$.
4. $\Psi_{SR}\Psi_1=\Psi_S\Psi_R$.
5. $\Phi_{SR}\Phi_1=\Phi_S\Phi_R$.

All four $\Psi$-functions have a common weight-zero law with trivial
multiplier on $\Gamma_0(24)$, and in fact on $\Gamma_0(12)$.
The $\Phi$-functions have the weights of (FS10) and a common
congruence group $\Gamma_0(12)$. When their identity holds, the
weights on its two sides agree.

Put


```math

 O_j(\tau)=
 \begin{cases}
 H(j\tau)/H(2j\tau),&j\ {\rm odd},\\
 \Delta(j\tau)/\Delta(2j\tau),&j\ {\rm even},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \mathscr W(\tau)=
       \frac{O_1(\tau)^6O_6(\tau)}{O_2(\tau)^3O_3(\tau)^2}.

```


The exact defect, including when the identity fails, is


```math

 \frac{\Psi_{SR}\Psi_1}{\Psi_S\Psi_R}
       =\mathscr W(\tau)^{b_6},\qquad
                    \mathscr W(\tau)=1+144q+O(q^2).             \tag{FS23}

```


**Proof.** Set $h=SR$. Then $S=h^3$ and $R=h^4$.
Its cycles have lengths $1,2,3,6$. On a one-cycle both operators
are trivial; on a two-cycle $R$ is trivial; on a three-cycle $S$
is trivial. On a six-cycle both move every point. This proves the
equivalence of 1 and 3. The operator in 2 vanishes on the first
three kinds of cycles and is nonzero on a six-cycle, for example on
a simultaneous eigenvector with $S$-eigenvalue $-1$ and a
nontrivial $R$-eigenvalue. This proves 2.

If 2 holds, simultaneous diagonalization has, on each joint eigenline,
at least one of the eigenvalues $s,r$ equal to one. Therefore


```math

             (1+tsr)(1+t)=(1+ts)(1+tr)

```


on every eigenline. Multiplying yields


```math

 \det(1+tSR)\det(1+t)=\det(1+tS)\det(1+tR).           \tag{FS24}

```


The exterior product over all modes, or only the odd modes, gives
the identities in 4 and 5, with cancelling shifts.

For a six-cycle, $S$ has three two-cycles, $R$ has two
three-cycles, and the identity has six one-cycles. Its contribution
to the ratio in 4 is exactly $\mathscr W$. Every other orbit
contributes one, proving (FS23).
Alternatively, at a single oscillator its determinant ratio is


```math

     \frac{(1-t^6)(1+t)^6}{(1-t^2)^3(1+t^3)^2}
                      =1+6t+O(t^2).

```


Twenty-four species at mode one give the coefficient 144.
Thus the ratio cannot be identically one if $b_6>0$. The same
first-coefficient calculation applies to the full-mode trace ratio,
proving the converse in 5. The modular statements follow from
Theorem FS3. QED.

This is an equality between inequivalent holonomies when $S,R$ are
nonidentity: their orders are $1,2,3,6$. No conjugation or relabeling
can identify those four group elements.

The equation also constrains all the original currents simultaneously,
not just the zero-point functions.

**Theorem FS6a (common-source correlation identity).** Assume the equivalent
conditions of FS6, and suppose $S,R$ commute with the actual left
$G$-action on $X$. For the full Fock space, let $\mathcal J$ be a
common ordered source word consisting of finitely many actual left
translations and exponentials of finite-mode original bilinear currents.
Use the same word and source variables in all four traces, and define


```math

 Z_h(\mathcal J;\tau)=
       q^N\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal F_B}
                     (\Gamma(h)\mathcal J q^D).

```


Then


```math

       Z_{SR}(\mathcal J)Z_1(\mathcal J)
                  =Z_S(\mathcal J)Z_R(\mathcal J).   \tag{FS25}

```


The analogous identity holds on $\mathcal F_B^{\rm odd}$, with shift
$q^{-N}$, for the original currents whose two mode indices are odd.
It holds as a formal source identity and as an analytic identity near
zero. After dividing by the respective zero-source traces and taking
logarithms, every connected coefficient satisfies


```math

       \kappa_{SR}(\mathcal J)+\kappa_1(\mathcal J)
                      =\kappa_S(\mathcal J)+\kappa_R(\mathcal J).
                                                               \tag{FS26}

```


The same identity holds at every insertion order, including ordered
mode-resolved correlations.

The same proof permits added bilinears whose one-particle matrices
belong to $\mathscr A_X$. No assertion that every such bilinear is
generated by the original diagonal-in-site currents and discrete
translations is needed or made.

**Proof.** Partition $X$ into the moving support of $S$, the moving
support of $R$, and their common fixed set. These three parts are
disjoint by FS6 and are $G$-stable because both permutations commute
with $G$. Every multiplication color preserves each part, so every
operator in $\mathscr A_X$, and every indicated current, does too.

The one-particle space and its exterior algebra split into these three
parts. All source factors are even fermionic operators, so their
actions and ordinary traces factor without an additional graded sign.
On the first part $R=1$, on the second $S=1$, and on the third both
are one. The factors on the two sides of (FS25) therefore agree
part by part, for any common block-preserving source word. Shifts also
agree because both sides use two copies of the same graded space.

Only finitely many modes enter the current exponentials. Their
exterior spaces are finite dimensional, while all remaining oscillator
products converge for $|q|<1$. This proves the formal and local
analytic identities. The zero-source traces are nonzero there: the
product of the common translation word with $h$ is a permutation,
and its exterior determinant has no zero in $|q|<1$.
Normalize each trace separately and take its logarithm near zero
to prove (FS26). QED.

There is an actual modular subsystem of this correlation identity.
For $a\in B$, put


```math

 \mathcal H_a^{\rm odd}
    =-\left(\sum_{x\in X}a(x)\right)1
           +\sum_{\substack{m\geq1\\m\ {\rm odd}}}mE_a^{mm}.
                                                               \tag{FS27}

```


The negative scalar is essential; for $a=1$ this is $D-N$.
Write $F(\sigma)=D_\sigma\log H(\sigma)$ as in GC3 and define


```math

 g_{\rm odd}(\sigma)=F(\sigma)-2F(2\sigma),\qquad
 g_{\rm even}(\sigma)=-F(\sigma).

```


For a permutation $h$, a cycle $O$ has length $d_O$, and
$A_O(a)=\sum_{x\in O}a(x)$. Define
$\Omega_{\rm odd}(\sigma)=H(\sigma)/H(2\sigma)$ and
$\Omega_{\rm even}(\sigma)=1/H(\sigma)$. The exact source is


```math

 q^{-N}\operatorname{Tr}
   \left(\Gamma(h)e^{\sum_i t_i\mathcal H_{a_i}^{\rm odd}}q^D\right)
  =\prod_O\Omega_{\operatorname{parity}(d_O)}
       \left(d_O\tau+\frac{\sum_i t_iA_O(a_i)}{2\pi i}\right).
                                                               \tag{FS28}
```

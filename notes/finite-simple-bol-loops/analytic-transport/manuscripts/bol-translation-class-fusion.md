selected class-two construction linked in
[the canonical input record](../README.md).
Its canonical faithful folder is (G,H,K), with canonical inversion tau. Fix a
prime p and let P be the characteristic Sylow p subgroup of G', Z=Z(P).
Assume the local W and Z are elementary abelian, as in the rigid-center
example. Write d=dim_Fp W. In the cited normal form,

    P=(W direct-sum W) direct-sum Z,
    Omega((x,y),(x',y'))=b(x,y')-b(x',y),
    tau(x,y,z)=(y,x,sigma z),                         (CF0.1)

with BCH correction Omega/2. The values of b span Z and
sigma b(x,y)=-b(y,x). The positive stabilizer cocycle f and the torus action
T are those of that source; they are not arbitrary additional labels.

The source's strict isomorphism equation (15) transports G,H,K,tau and local
maps (W,Z,b,R,T,f) simultaneously. In particular there is no discarded
complement cochain. We use this as an inspected dependency, rather than
re-proving the class-two classification or the large simple-loop construction.

The construction below uses conjugacy classes of a subgroup generated by
actual loop translations. It does not use conjugacy classes of the whole
unmarked multiplication group. Its cochain marking is realized by actual

radicals, not merely a separate numerical rank for each charge.

## The local translation subgroup

**Proposition CF1.** Set

    Q=the subgroup generated by K intersect P.

Then Q is the inverse image in P of the negative tau eigenspace on P/Z.
In normal coordinates it is

    Q={(x,-x,z): x in W, z in Z}.                    (CF1.1)

Writing an element as (x,z), its central commutator is

    [(x,z),(y,t)]=-b(x,y)+b(y,x).                    (CF1.2)

Consequently Q and the action of Z on its conjugacy classes are intrinsic
to the marked loop folder. Its center can be larger than Z; no equality
Z(Q)=Z is required.

**Proof.** The source's inverted-set formula at torus label one gives
K intersect P={(x,-x,z): sigma z=-z}. Its image in P/Z is the negative
eigenspace, so the generated subgroup is contained in (CF1.1) and maps onto
that eigenspace. It contains the negative center Z^-. Its commutators are
exactly (CF1.2). Because b(W,W) spans Z, the values

    b(x,y)-b(y,x)=(1+sigma)b(x,y)

span Z^+, since 2 is invertible. Thus the generated subgroup also contains
Z^+, hence all Z, proving (CF1.1). Formula (CF1.2) follows by restricting
Omega to the negative axis. The intrinsic description and the
characteristic definitions of P and Z prove functoriality. QED.

Let C be the finite set of conjugacy classes of Q. The center Z acts by

    z.[q]=[zq].                                     (CF1.3)

This is well defined, because z is central in Q. It is faithful: the class
of the identity is a singleton and its z-translate is the distinct singleton
class of z when z is nonidentity. We use the commutative algebra

    A_C=Fun(C,C)

with pointwise multiplication and its canonical Hermitian form given by
summing over C. Z acts by pullback along inverse translations. This algebra,
its unit, its multiplication and its positive Hermitian form are canonical.

## Central charge fusion

Fix psi(t)=exp(2 pi i t/p). For ell in Z* let (A_C)_ell be the eigenspace on
which z acts by psi(ell(z)). The alternating form of the earlier packet is

    a_ell(x,y)=ell(b(x,y)-b(y,x))/2,
    R_ell=rad(a_ell),  r_ell=rank(a_ell).             (CF2.1)

**Theorem CF2 (charge spaces and their multiplication).** In the normal
coordinates (CF1.1), a basis of (A_C)_ell consists of

    v_(ell,w)(x,z)=1_(x=w) psi(-ell(z)),
       w in R_ell.                                  (CF2.2)

These are functions on conjugacy classes. In particular,

    dim (A_C)_ell=p^(d-r_ell),
    |C|=sum_(ell in Z*) p^(d-r_ell).                 (CF2.3)

The actual algebra multiplication obeys

    v_(ell,w) v_(mu,u)=0                  if w!=u,
    v_(ell,w) v_(mu,w)=v_(ell+mu,w)       if w=u.      (CF2.4)

Therefore the image dimension of the charge-fusion map is

    rank[(A_C)_ell tensor (A_C)_mu -> (A_C)_(ell+mu)]
       =|R_ell intersect R_mu|.                      (CF2.5)

**Proof.** The conjugacy class of (w,z) consists of the elements
(w,z+t) with t in the image of y -> b(w,y)-b(y,w); the sign in (CF1.2)
does not change that image. A central-charge-ell function on this fiber is
a scalar multiple of psi(-ell(z)), and it descends to conjugacy classes
exactly when ell kills that image, equivalently w in R_ell. This gives the
basis and dimension. Pointwise multiplication proves (CF2.4). If w lies
in both radicals, it lies in R_(ell+mu), so the displayed output exists.
The image basis is precisely indexed by their intersection, proving (CF2.5).
QED.

The coordinate formula is not an extra choice in the construction: A_C and
its multiplication were defined intrinsically. Changing lifts or coordinates
only changes the displayed basis. In particular (CF2.5) is intrinsic. It is
additional information beyond the individual charge dimensions: it measures
the relative positions of the radicals. The fermionic currents in
[the graded construction](bol-fermion-sewing.md) realize this multiplication
as operator commutators between different charges and different grades.

The trace of a central translation, which is also its number of fixed
conjugacy classes, is the exact finite Fourier transform

    chi_C(z)=sum_(ell in Z*) p^(d-r_ell) psi(ell(z)).  (CF2.6)

Despite the displayed roots of unity, this is a nonnegative integer because
it is the character of a permutation action. Formula (CF2.6) follows from
the eigenspace decomposition, independently of any approximation.

## Induction to the canonical Bol action

The class set C is naturally a Z-set, but need not carry an action of all G.
Induce the set, retaining its multiplication algebra:

    X=G times_Z C,
    (gz,c) equivalent to (g,z.c),
    B=Fun(X,C).                                     (CF3.1)

There are two commuting actions, which must be distinguished:

    L_h[g,c]=[hg,c]       for h in G,
    R_z[g,c]=[g,z.c]      for z in Z.                 (CF3.2)

The right action is well defined because Z is abelian: it respects
(gz',c) equivalent to (g,z'.c). It commutes with the left action of G.
The map X -> G/Z is G-equivariant. Here Z is normal in G, since it is the
center of a characteristic Sylow subgroup of G'. Put I=[G:Z] and N=I|C|.
Both actions on functions are pullbacks along inverse point permutations.
The notation B_ell henceforth refers to the RIGHT Z action. Each such
space is consequently an actual G-module and an actual Bol sandwich module.

**Theorem CF3 (faithful Bol action with charge fusion).** The action of G on
X is faithful, by algebra automorphisms of B preserving its point-basis
Hermitian form. If k_x in K is the canonical translation indexed by x in L,
then T_x=L_(k_x) satisfies the actual left Bol sandwich relation. Every graded tensor, exterior or symmetric
construction from B inherits that relation. Loop isomorphisms transport this
entire construction canonically.

As a G-module its right-charge-ell part is

    B_ell isomorphic to Ind_Z^G(chi_ell) tensor (A_C)_ell,
    chi_ell(z)=psi(ell(z)),                          (CF3.3)

where (A_C)_ell on the right is a multiplicity space with trivial G action.
Consequently

    dim B_ell=I p^(d-r_ell).                         (CF3.4)

Pointwise multiplication is a G-equivariant charge-fusion map, of rank

    rank[B_ell tensor B_mu -> B_(ell+mu)]
       =I |R_ell intersect R_mu|.                    (CF3.5)

The left and right central permutation characters are respectively

    chi_X^L(z)=sum_(gZ in G/Z) chi_C(g^(-1) z g),
    chi_X^R(z)=I chi_C(z).                           (CF3.6)

On the fiber gZ, the exact connection between the actions is

    L_z restricted to gZ = R_(g^(-1) z g) restricted to gZ.  (CF3.7)

In particular the identity fiber retains the full local charge-fusion
algebra, and induction does not replace it by independent rank spaces.

**Proof.** The singleton central classes {[z]:z in Z} form a canonical
regular Z-orbit in C. Inducing it gives a regular G-orbit in X, because
G times_Z Z is isomorphic to G by [g,z] -> gz. Thus the G-action is faithful.
The algebra and
Hermitian assertions hold for any permutation action. The canonical
translation operators L_x in G obey L_x L_y L_x=L_(x(yx)); applying the action
and functorial graded constructions proves the asserted Bol action.

An induced function can be written F:G -> A_C with
F(gz)=rho_C(z)^(-1)F(g). Restricting to a right-charge-ell space makes this
F(gz)=chi_ell(z)^(-1)F(g), which is the usual induced-character model and
proves (CF3.3)--(CF3.4). Representatives g identify every fiber with C, with
the same right charge ell. Changing representatives only rescales the
charge basis by its specified character. Pointwise multiplication vanishes
between distinct fibers, and (CF2.4)--(CF2.5) apply inside each. The I

proves (CF3.7), and counting fixed points proves (CF3.6).

A loop isomorphism identifies the canonical folders and commutes with tau

QED.

Concretely the identity fiber is retained by its canonical characteristic

There is no assertion that every arbitrary group action would retain this

The canonical involution also acts by

Bol translation to its inverse. This extends the action to
(G semidirect <tau>) together with the compatible right Z action.

## The stabilizer cocycle as an actual operator relation

For the positive cocycle f:A_tor -> Z in the pinned normal form, define

    U_a=L_(f(a)),  V_a=L_a,
    Pha_a=R_(f(a)).

One may use a single local component f_p; the torus acts on that local Z by
T_p. These finite-order operators act on B and on the entire graded
construction. In a right-charge-ell space, Pha_a is multiplication by
psi(ell(f(a))). The left U_a instead acts on the fiber gZ by
psi(ell(g^(-1)f(a)g)), according to (CF3.7). Thus the earlier cochain phases
are actual right-charge eigenvalues, and their coupling to the Bol action
is retained by the fiberwise left/right relation. The two actions are not
silently identified.

**Proposition CF4 (cochain sewing and a fixed-word probe).** They satisfy

    U_(ab)=U_a V_a U_b V_a^(-1).                    (CF4.1)

Equivalently a -> U_a V_a is a representation of the cocycle graph subgroup.
For the full f into Z(G') this is the actual stabilizer graph; a single local
component alone is not asserted to be the full graph in H. In the rigid
probe's cyclic a-subgroup all other components of f vanish, so the local and
full graph coincide there. Write N_a=T(a)-1. For a fixed a let

    C_a=V_a U_a V_a^(-1) U_a^(-1),
    D_a=V_a C_a V_a^(-1) C_a^(-1),
    P_a=V_a U_a V_a^(-1) D_a.                       (CF4.2)

Then

    C_a=L_(N_a f(a)),
    D_a=L_(N_a^2 f(a)),
    P_a=L_((T(a)+N_a^2)f(a)).                       (CF4.3)

These identities hold in every grade, and are compatible with the additive
charge multiplication (CF2.4), (CF3.5).

For p=3 there is a more direct norm-word description. Define

    Norm_a(U)=U V_a U V_a^(-1).

Then

    Norm_a(Norm_a(U_a))
       =L_((1+T(a))^2 f(a))=P_a.                    (CF4.4)

This uses the field identity (1+T)^2=T+(T-1)^2 in characteristic three.
Both norm steps involve the actual translation V_a, because a lies in K.
The source positivity condition gives the additional exact equation

    Theta U_a Theta^(-1)=V_a^(-1) U_a V_a.           (CF4.5)

**Proof.** The cocycle identity is f(ab)=f(a)+T(a)f(b), while conjugation by
V_a carries L_z to L_(T(a)z). These identities prove (CF4.1).
All L_z, z in Z, commute. Applying the conjugation identity twice gives
(CF4.2)--(CF4.3), and two norm steps multiply f by (1+T)^2. Finally
T(a)sigma f(a)=f(a) says sigma f(a)=T(a)^(-1)f(a); combine this with
(CF3.8) to prove (CF4.5). Graded functoriality preserves these equations.
QED.

## Higher fusion reconstructs the central conjugacy-class geometry

For a list of characters ell_1,...,ell_t, let U_(ell_1,...,ell_t) be the
image of the t-fold pointwise multiplication map on their right-charge
spaces in B. This is a G-submodule in charge ell_1+...+ell_t. Associativity
of the actual function algebra makes its definition independent of
parenthesization. Iterating (CF2.4) fiber by fiber proves

    dim U_(ell_1,...,ell_t)
       =I |R_(ell_1) intersect ... intersect R_(ell_t)|.     (CF7.1)

Thus these are not independent higher tensors appended to the seed: they
are products already present in the same algebra and in the graded current
realization. The fusion-projector modular traces in the
[fermionic construction](bol-fermion-sewing.md) recover these dimensions
from their first coefficient.

For w in W define its central commutator image

    J_w={b(w,v)-b(v,w):v in W} <= Z.

For any subspace J of Z let

    h(J)=| intersection_(ell in J^perp) R_ell |,
    n(J)=#{w in W:J_w=J}.                            (CF7.2)

Only a basis of J^perp is needed in the intersection. If J^perp=0, use the
zero character, whose radical is all W. Hence every h(J) is obtained by
dividing a fusion dimension (CF7.1) by I.

**Theorem CF7 (exact reconstruction and its limit).** The complete labeled
higher-fusion rank system reconstructs the Z-set C up to Z-equivariant
isomorphism, and therefore reconstructs its function Frobenius algebra with
the Z action. Explicitly,

    h(J)=sum_(K<=J) n(K),
    n(J)=sum_(K<=J) (-1)^s p^(s(s-1)/2) h(K),
       s=dim J-dim K,                               (CF7.3)

and

    C isomorphic as a Z-set to
       disjoint_union_(J<=Z) n(J) copies of Z/J.     (CF7.4)

The same conclusion follows from all the labeled fusion-projector modular
traces, with p, I and N fixed, by their rank-recovery formula. It is a
reconstruction of this finite seed, not of every bimap, group or Bol loop
from that seed.

**Proof.** The condition w in R_ell for every ell annihilating J is
equivalent to J_w being contained in J. This proves the first equality in
(CF7.3), and gives an elementary recursive inversion
n(J)=h(J)-sum_(K<J)n(K). The closed expression is the subspace-lattice
inversion: on an interval of dimension difference s its coefficient is
(-1)^s p^(s(s-1)/2). To verify it directly, sum these coefficients over
subspaces of a positive-dimensional quotient of dimension r. The result
is zero by the finite identity

    sum_(s=0)^r GaussianBinomial(r,s;p)
        (-1)^s p^(s(s-1)/2)=product_(j=0)^(r-1)(1-p^j)=0.

The polynomial version follows by induction on r from the Gaussian-binomial
recurrence, and its r=0 value is one. These are precisely the cancellation
relations for the stated inversion coefficients, proving the closed formula.

The conjugacy classes over a fixed w form a transitive Z-set with stabilizer
J_w, namely Z/J_w. Distinct w give disjoint such orbits. Grouping them by
their stabilizers proves (CF7.4). A Z-equivariant bijection preserves the
pointwise algebra, unit, and counting inner product, proving the Frobenius
algebra assertion. Conversely the ranks of multiplication on its character
spaces are invariants of that equivariant Frobenius algebra. QED.

For the rigid input, every J_w lies in Z^+=span(1,epsilon^2). Among the
28 subspaces of the three-dimensional center, only four have nonzero n:

    n(0)=1,
    n(span(epsilon^2))=M-1,
    n(span(1+epsilon^2))=M-1,
    n(Z^+)=(M-1)^2.                                 (CF7.5)

Here is a direct check using the existing matrices. The radical of the
constant coefficient form B0 is the E-span of e1,e3. The radical of the
skew part of B2-B0 has E-basis (1,1,1,0),(0,0,1,1). Each has F3-dimension
36 and they meet only in zero. A nonzero w in the first radical has
J_w=span(epsilon^2); one in the second has
J_w=span(1+epsilon^2). Every other nonzero character direction on Z^+ has rank
72 by (CF5.2), so it has zero radical and cannot annihilate J_w for a
nonzero w. The remaining w therefore have J_w=Z^+. Counting gives (CF7.5).

Consequently the same underlying Z-set has the compact orbit decomposition

    C = Z
       disjoint_union (M-1) copies of Z/span(epsilon^2)
       disjoint_union (M-1) copies of Z/span(1+epsilon^2)
       disjoint_union (M-1)^2 copies of Z/Z^+.        (CF7.6)

This also explains the norm probe geometrically. Translation by z_+=1+epsilon^2
fixes the last two kinds of orbit, while translation by z_-=1 fixes only
the last kind. The fixed-class difference is exactly 9(M-1), before the
left-induction average. Thus the isotope test measures the cochain mark's
position relative to exceptional stabilizer lines of actual conjugacy
orbits. The ordinary character ranks are its one-variable projections;
higher fusion recovers their full orbit geometry through (CF7.3).

## Necessary linear constraints on the higher-fusion traces

The construction and 51-point control in this section were proposed by
the continuation owner, model participant 13; the formulation and proof were developed
with model participant 14. The restrictions concern the elementary
class-two input, rather than a new analytic identity specific to Bol loops.

For a charge subspace E<=Z^*, define

    R_E=intersection_(ell in E) R_ell,
    u(E)=|R_E|,   r(E)=codim_W R_E.                 (CF8.1)

Thus u(E)=h(E^perp) in the notation of (CF7.2); the two functions have
different domains. A basis of E suffices in the intersection. For E=0
use the one-term tuple consisting of the zero character, so R_0=W.
By (CF7.1), the fusion image for a basis of E has dimension I u(E).
Changing the basis can change its output charge and hence the image
subspace. Its dimension, however, depends only on E. The corresponding
fusion-projector trace also depends only on this dimension, by
[Theorem F-F](bol-fermion-sewing.md#fusion-itself-has-an-integral-modular-trace).
Denote that common trace by Psi_E.

**Theorem CF8 (necessary subspace and coefficient constraints).** For
the elementary class-two input of this manuscript, the following hold.

1. The function r is integer valued, r(0)=0, and 0<=r(E)<=d. It is

### An exact 51-point admissibility control

This comparison is local, with I=1. The first object is a genuine
elementary class-two group seed; neither object is asserted to realize

    B(x,y)=(x0 y1-x1 y0, x0 y2-x2 y0).

Its scalar form at ell=(a,b) is a e0 wedge e1+b e0 wedge e2.

    (x,z)(y,t)=(x+y,z+t+B(x,y)/2)

### The inequality is not a universal finite-set law

The inequality alone also has a separate finite-set ablation. Let

    C_test=Z disjoint_union Z/J1 disjoint_union Z/J2

let E=J1^perp and F=J2^perp. Counting the supporting orbits gives

    u_test(0)=3,   u_test(E)=u_test(F)=2,

## Limits, checks, and next structural questions

The current algebraic assertions are unconditional for the explicit finite
normal data, and are loop invariants under the cited strict folder interface.
Simplicity, properness and nonisomorphism of the huge loops themselves retain
the pinned dependencies. No new classification proof or priority claim is
made. The seed has the same underlying unmarked class algebra for the
prescribed isotope pair; the actual f-relative operators are indispensable.

The new content relative to the separate-rank construction is the canonical
subgroup generated by local translations, its class-function multiplication,
the induced faithful Bol action, and the cochain sewing equations. The fusion
map records radical intersections. The fermionic currents implement that map
between charges and grades; they are not an unordered sum of oscillator
modules independently selected by rank. The analytic mechanism remains a
standard exterior-Fock and discriminant-function mechanism.

Useful exact checks are: conjugacy classes and central translations for the
displayed matrices over F3 instead of E, where M=9 and |C|=363; the fixed
counts 264 and 192; induction by the three-element torus action on Z;
and charge-fusion image ranks for pencils with identical individual ranks but
different common radicals. Such local checks do not assert that replacing E
by F3 gives the huge construction's selected simple Bol loop. They test the
new finite algebra and induction formulas at their actual stated scope.

Questions left open include whether these richer fusion packets classify
additional simple-loop families, and whether their graded characters satisfy
relations stronger than the inherited finite-algebra and Fock sewing laws.
No genus-zero, Monster, or sporadicity conclusion follows from the present
construction.

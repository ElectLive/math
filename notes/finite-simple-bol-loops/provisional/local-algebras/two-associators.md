# Two left associators with universal derived subgroups

**Mira Cult · Provisional research.** The author proofs below have not been independently reviewed.

The small-loop statements, including the infinite construction and the two-generator finite group lemma, are independent of any projective classification. The assertion about **finite simple** exponent-six hosts uses the separate, independently unreviewed [noncommutative projective embedding](noncommutative-radicals.md). In contrast to just embedding a $`3`$-group in a radical, we specify two native inner maps coming from three actual involutive points and locate the group inside their derived series.

**Finite simple theorem (arbitrary associator orders).** Given any finite $`3`$-group $`D`$ and any integer $`j\ge0`$, there are a finite simple proper exponent-six left Bol loop $`Q`$ and three native involutions $`k,h_1,h_2\in Q`$ such that $`D`$ embeds in the $`j`$th derived subgroup of

```math
P=\langle L_{h_1k}^{-1} L_{h_1}L_k,\,
                         L_{h_2k}^{-1} L_{h_2}L_k\rangle .
 \tag{1}
```

Both generators of $`P`$ are genuine single *left* associators with the **same** second argument; both products $`h_i k`$ have order three. $`P`$ itself is a finite $`3`$-group inside the intersection of the faithful solvable radical and the identity stabilizer of $`\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)`$, whose quotient by its radical is $`\mathrm{PSL}_2(19)`$. The original $`Q`$ has exponent six: this is not the higher-exponent principal isotope. Two is the least number of group generators that could possibly contain every noncyclic target. We do not claim the three designated points generate the *simple host*.

**Unified odd-prime theorem.** For each odd prime $`p`$ there is **one** countably infinite residually finite left Bol loop $`Q_*`$ of exponent $`2p`$, generated by three involutions called $`k,h_1,h_2`$. The two matched associators (1) generate a nonabelian free group $`F_2`$ in its own left inner mapping group. Adjoining the single left translation $`L_{h_1k}`$ gives a copy of $`C_p\wr F_2`$ (restricted wreath product). For every finite $`p`$-group $`D`$ and depth $`j`$ there is a finite quotient of this *same* marked loop in which (1) generates a finite $`p`$-group with a copy of $`D`$ in its $`j`$th derived group. For $`p=3`$ this finite quotient embeds as its full native table in one of the simple hosts above, where the two global associators generate **the very same abstract finite group**, not merely a group with that quotient. No such simple-host conclusion at other primes is asserted.

The [bounded-order refinement](#both-associators-of-order-three) below keeps the same depth conclusion while fixing both inner-map orders. The two infinite examples are distinct: their designated inner subgroups are a free group and a free product of two finite cyclic groups, respectively.

## 1. Three involutions in an associative-algebra two-sheet loop

Let $`J`$ be a nilpotent associative $`F=\mathbb F_p`$ algebra, or the positive-degree ideal in a completed free associative power-series algebra. Let $`\sigma`$ be an involutive algebra automorphism. Units are taken in the unitalization (or the complete unital algebra). On the two sheets use

```math
(0,u)(e,v)=(e,u+v),\qquad
 (1,u)(e,v)=(1+e,u+a_u\sigma v),\qquad a_u=(1+u)(1+\sigma u)^{-1}.
 \tag{2}
```

All multiplications are ordered with units on the **left**. The law does not require these units to commute. For completeness, left division is affine and right division in the nontranslation case $`u+a_u\sigma v=b`$ forces $`a_u=a_{b+v}`$ by $`1+b+v=a_u(1+\sigma b+\sigma v)`$; conversely the unique solution $`u=b-a_{b+v}\sigma v`$ has this ratio. Write $`S_u,T_u`$ for the odd and even native left translations. Even–even–even and odd–even–odd sandwiches are translations, while $`T_uS_vT_u`$ has intercept $`c=u+v+a_v\sigma u`$ and $`1+c=a_v(1+\sigma c)`$. In $`S_uS_vS_u`$ the slope is $`a_u a_v^{-1} a_u\sigma`$, its intercept $`c=u+a_u\sigma v+a_u a_v^{-1}u`$, and

```math
1+c=a_u a_v^{-1}a_u(1+\sigma c).
```

Indeed on the right use $`a_u(1+\sigma u)=1+u`$ and $`a_v^{-1}(1+v)=1+\sigma v`$ without exchanging factor order. These are the actual Bol sandwiches. The even translations have exponent $`p`$ and each odd translation squares to an even translation, giving exponent $`2p`$ for $`J\ne0`$.

For a unit $`g\in1+J`$ with $`\sigma g=g^{-1}`$, set $`u_g=(g-1)/2`$, $`h_g=(1,u_g)`$ and $`k=(1,0)`$. Then $`a_{u_g}=g`$ because $`1+g`$ is a unit (constant term two), and $`u_g+g\sigma u_g=0`$. Thus $`h_g,k`$ are genuine involutions, $`h_g k=(0,u_g)`$, and in the actual small loop the inner map

```math
A_g=L_{h_g k}^{-1}L_{h_g}L_k
```

acts on both sheets by left multiplication by $`g`$ on their $`J`$ coordinate. This identity does not say that native translations multiply homomorphically in general.

## 2. A finite two-generator p-group containing an arbitrary deep target

We prove a useful elementary group statement: for any odd $`p`$, any finite $`p`$-group $`D`$ and $`j\ge0`$, a finite $`p`$-group $`W=\langle s,f\rangle`$ has an involutory automorphism $`\theta`$ inverting **both specified generators**, and $`D\hookrightarrow W^{(j)}`$. The extra automorphism and the actual common-site embedding prevent a nonsplit quotient from masquerading as a subgroup.

First $`D\le\mathrm{UT}_b(p)`$ for some $`b\ge2`$: its faithful regular representation has a unipotent flag by orbit counting on each nonzero quotient over $`F`$. Increase $`m`$ so that $`\mathrm{UT}_b(p)`$ embeds on indices spaced $`2^j`$ apart in $`\mathrm{UT}_m(p)^{(j)}`$. Indeed every elementary root of gap at least $`2^j`$ belongs to that derived subgroup, by induction splitting its interval into two gaps at least $`2^{j-1}`$ and taking the commutator. It remains to put the whole $`\mathrm{UT}_m(p)`$ into a suitable $`W`$.

Put $`n=4m-3`$, $`G=\mathrm{UT}_n(p)`$, $`g_i=1+E_{i,i+1}`$ for $`1\le i<n`$. Take integer tags $`a_i=5^i`$, positive and distinct, and let $`L`$ be a power of $`p`$ larger than $`4a_{n-1}`$. Work in the finite group $`G^{\mathbb Z/L}\rtimes\langle s\rangle`$ where $`s`$ shifts positions by $`+1`$. Let $`f`$ have lamps $`g_i`$ at both $`+a_i,-a_i`$, one elsewhere. Define $`W=\langle s,f\rangle`$. Reflect positions $`q\mapsto-q`$, send $`s\to s^{-1}`$, and conjugate each $`G`$ by the diagonal matrix with alternating signs. This involutive automorphism preserves $`W`$ and sends $`f`$ to $`f^{-1}`$, because it sends each $`g_i`$ to $`g_i^{-1}`$ on the same symmetric support. It is a group automorphism, not the generally false inversion automorphism on all of $`W`$.

Let $`F_i=s^{-a_i}fs^{a_i}`$; it has lamp $`g_i`$ at the origin. The intersection of the supports of $`F_i,F_{i+1}`$ is exactly $`\{0,-a_i-a_{i+1}\}`$. Indeed the difference $`a_{i+1}-a_i`$ between signed tags has just its two forced representations: the positive ordered pair and its reversed negative pair. A second relation would have coefficients of absolute value at most four among distinct powers of five; its lowest nonzero coefficient cannot be divisible by five. The choice of $`L`$ rules out wraparound relations. Thus $`C_i=[F_i,F_{i+1}]`$ has the root $`1+E_{i,i+2}`$ at $`0`$ and the inverse root at its other site, using $`[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}`$. The second sites for $`C_i,C_{i+2}`$ differ, so $`[C_i,C_{i+2}]`$ has the **single** nontrivial lamp $`1+E_{i,i+4}`$ at $`0`$. Taking $`i=1,5,9,\ldots,n-4`$ gives the full real copy of $`\mathrm{UT}_m(p)`$ on indices $`1,5,9,\ldots,n`$. It even lies in $`W''`$. These elementary transvections generate the whole group since the coefficient field is prime. Hence the prescribed $`D`$ lies in $`W^{(j)}`$ (in fact in $`W^{(j+2)}`$ with the spacing chosen above). All groups used are finite $`p`$-groups.

## 3. The actual two inner permutations in the finite quotient and in the simple host

Take $`J`$ to be the augmentation ideal of $`F[W]`$, with $`\sigma`$ induced by the preceding $`\theta`$. The ideal is nilpotent: for a central element $`c`$ of order $`p`$, the ideal $`(c-1)F[W]`$ has $`p`$th power zero and the quotient is $`F[W/\langle c\rangle]`$; induction gives nilpotence of augmentation. Identify $`s,f`$ with their basis units. The three points $`k,h_s,h_f`$ generate the **entire** local loop (2). In the generated subloop, vertical translations by $`u_s,u_f`$ and their conjugates by $`A_s,A_f`$ give all translates $`g(s-1)/2,g(f-1)/2`$ for $`g\in W`$. Their additive span is $`J`$, since the augmentation ideal is generated as a left ideal by $`s-1,f-1`$ (expand $`ab-1`$ and inverses). Applying these vertical translations to $`k`$ gives the other sheet. The two inner permutations (1) generate exactly $`W`$: left multiplication is a faithful action on $`J`$, since a nonidentity odd-order $`g`$ does not fix $`g^{-1}-1`$ (the difference is $`2-g-g^{-1}\ne0`$ on the group basis).

For $`p=3`$, apply [the actual three-Weyl-pair embedding](noncommutative-radicals.md#3-native-two-sheets-for-noncommuting-local-units) to $`J`$. It is essential to check the word on the **whole** simple host: an isomorphism on an invariant subloop could otherwise lose a kernel. In the coordinates of that proof the true even mark is $`T_u`$, $`L_k=w`$, and the true odd mark at $`u_g`$ equals

```math
T_{u_g-1+g}S(g) w,
```

where the old $`119`$-coordinate coupling vanishes on every pure algebra unit. Therefore as global group elements

```math
A_g=T_{g-1}S(g).
```

The unit graph $`g\mapsto T_{g-1}S(g)`$ is injective and multiplicative on the actual embedded unit subgroup, because $`(g-1)+g(h-1)=gh-1`$. The local subgroup of units allowed in the embedding contains $`s,f`$ and hence all of $`W`$ (one uses abelianized norm one, not closure of the inverted subset). Thus the subgroup (1) is **exactly the graph copy of $`W`$** in the true stabilizer, also inside the $`3`$-radical of the faithful left group. This proves the finite simple theorem.

## 4. A single infinite loop and the free lamplighter

Use the completed noncommutative algebra $`F\langle\!\langle X,Y\rangle\!\rangle`$ with its positive-degree ideal, and $`s=1+X,f=1+Y`$. The continuous substitution $`s\mapsto s^{-1}`$, $`f\mapsto f^{-1}`$ defines an involutive algebra automorphism: applying it twice fixes the two free generators. Let $`Q_*`$ be the subloop of (2) generated by $`k,h_s,h_f`$. This makes it countable, in contrast to the full power-series algebra. Degree truncations give honest finite loop homomorphisms and separate all its elements, so it is residually finite.

For clarity the required free-group and group-algebra embeddings are elementary here. Write a nontrivial reduced group word as alternating nonzero integer powers in $`s,f`$. In $`(1+X)^q`$ the least nonconstant term in characteristic $`p`$ has degree $`p^{v_p(q)}`$ and nonzero coefficient, including negative $`q`$ by the power-series inverse. Taking this least term in every syllable produces the alternating monomial with exactly as many runs as there are syllables, and the specified run lengths. No omitted syllable can produce that many runs, and the specified term is forced in each syllable. Hence the word does not equal one. This embeds $`F_2`$ as genuine units.

In fact the group algebra $`F[F_2]`$ also injects. For finitely many distinct reduced words, choose a single degree truncation separating their unit images by the preceding argument on all pairwise differences. The group $`U`$ generated by the truncated $`s,f`$ is a finite $`p`$-group inside the units of a finite nilpotent algebra. Evaluate formal series instead in $`F[U]`$ by sending $`X,Y`$ to the corresponding basis elements minus one. This is well-defined by nilpotence of augmentation, and the selected words go to **distinct group basis elements**, so no nontrivial finite linear combination of them can vanish in the original series algebra.

The two genuine $`A_s,A_f`$ act by these units on $`Q_*`$. They act faithfully there: the vertical point $`u_s=X/2`$ belongs to it, and multiplying a nonzero series on the right by $`X`$ is injective. Their conjugates of $`L_{h_sk}=T_{X/2}`$ are the translations by all $`gX/2`$, $`g\in F_2`$. These vectors are linearly independent by the group-algebra injection and right multiplication by $`X`$. They therefore form the restricted direct sum of lamps, on which $`F_2`$ acts by its left regular index action. The acting subgroup fixes the identity, so meets the nontrivial translations trivially. Their generated group is exactly $`C_p\wr F_2`$. Already $`k,h_s`$ generate infinitely many points (the translations by $`s^jX/2`$); were $`Q_*`$ Moufang, the diassociative subloop on these two involutions with product of order $`p`$ would be a finite dihedral group. Thus $`Q_*`$ is proper.

Finally evaluate the two free series variables in the particular finite $`F[W]`$ of §2 by $`X\mapsto s-1,Y\mapsto f-1`$. Nilpotence makes this an algebra map and $`\theta`$ makes it intertwine the two actual involutions. The induced loop map sends the three specified generators to the named three; by §3 it is onto the whole finite local loop. Thus the universality is obtained by finite quotients of **one fixed infinite marked loop** for each odd prime. These quotients, not the infinite loop, are asserted to embed in the finite simple targets for $`p=3`$. No homomorphism *onto* a simple target is inferred.

## Both associators of order three

The first construction leaves the orders of the two generators unrestricted. Its very long cyclic shift cannot itself have order three. Here both actual generators have **exact order three**, not merely their three defining loop elements order two. The finite-simple part uses the separately unreviewed [actual host and its stabilizer graph](noncommutative-radicals.md). The finite group construction and the infinite small loop do not.

**Bounded-order theorem.** For every finite $`3`$-group $`D`$ and depth $`j\ge0`$, there are a finite simple non-Moufang exponent-six left Bol loop $`Q`$ and three involutions $`k,h_a,h_b`$ for which the two genuine left inner permutations

```math
A=L_{h_a k}^{-1}L_{h_a}L_k,\qquad
             B=L_{h_b k}^{-1}L_{h_b}L_k
```

**both have exact order three** and $`D\le\langle A,B\rangle^{(j)}`$. The two elements generate a finite $`3`$-group inside both the identity stabilizer and solvable radical of the faithful left multiplication group; its radical quotient is $`\mathrm{PSL}_2(19)`$. No assumption that $`D`$ itself is two-generated or has bounded exponent or nilpotency class is required. This is not a statement that $`D`$ embeds under the native multiplication of the original exponent-six loop.

For each odd prime $`p`$ there is one infinite three-involution-generated residually finite proper left Bol loop of exponent $`2p`$ for which the same two native inner maps have exact order $`p`$ and generate $`C_p*C_p`$. One additional vertical native translation generates with them the restricted wreath $`C_p\wr(C_p*C_p)`$. For each finite $`p`$-group and each depth, a finite quotient of this *fixed marked loop* realizes the depth assertion with both generators still of exact order $`p`$. At $`p=3`$ these finite quotient tables and their precise two-generator groups occur in the finite simple loops above. Nothing here asserts all such simple hosts at the other primes.

### B1. A finite p-group on two actual order-p generators, inverted together

Let $`p`$ be any odd prime. As in the [earlier elementary unitriangular argument](#2-a-finite-two-generator-p-group-containing-an-arbitrary-deep-target), fixing a sufficiently large $`m`$ suffices: an arbitrary finite $`p`$-group $`D`$ has a faithful upper unipotent representation, and spacing the indices by $`2^j`$ embeds it in $`\mathrm{UT}_m(p)^{(j)}`$. We construct a finite $`p`$-group $`K=\langle a,b\rangle`$, with **both** $`a,b`$ of order $`p`$ and a common generator-inverting automorphism, containing a full $`\mathrm{UT}_m(p)`$.

Set $`n=4m-3`$, $`G=\mathrm{UT}_n(p)`$, $`g_i=1+E_{i,i+1}`$. Choose $`p^e\ge n`$ so $`G`$ has exponent dividing $`p^e`$. Choose tags $`t_i=5^i`$ ($`1\le i<n`$) and a power $`L=p^\ell>4t_{n-1}`$. We need a private long cyclic shift while keeping the ultimate two generators short. Take

```math
U=I+tE_{12},\qquad V=I+tE_{21}
```

modulo $`t^M`$, with $`M=2p^{e+\ell-1}+1`$. These two matrices have exact order $`p`$ and generate a finite $`p`$-group $`P`$ (they lie in the nilpotent principal units). Conjugation by $`\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)`$ induces an involutory automorphism $`\tau`$ of $`P`$ taking $`U,V`$ to $`V^{-1},U^{-1}`$ respectively.

With commutator $`[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}`$ define the *fixed group word*

```math
q(x,y)=[x,y]\,[y^{-1},x^{-1}]^{-1}.
```

Both its individual exponent sums vanish. At the distinguished pair it is $`c\tau(c)^{-1}`$, $`c=[U,V]`$, so $`\tau(q(U,V))=q(U,V)^{-1}`$. Since $`c=I+t^2 H+O(t^3)`$ with $`H=E_{11}-E_{22}`$ and $`\tau(H)=-H`$, the word has leading part $`I+2t^2H+O(t^3)`$, with invertible coefficient. Its order modulo $`t^M`$ is exactly $`p^{e+\ell}`$: the $`p^r`$th power of $`I+t^2 D`$ is $`I+t^{2p^r}D^{p^r}`$ with $`D(0)`$ invertible. Therefore

```math
s=q(U,V)^{p^e}
```

is a true element of order $`L`$, inverted by $`\tau`$.

Form the inner wreath product $`J=G^P\rtimes P`$ with the regular **left** shift action of $`P`$, and place an order-$`p`$ lamp $`f\in G^P`$ on the cyclic subgroup $`\langle s\rangle`$: at $`s^{t_i}`$ and $`s^{-t_i}`$ its value is $`g_i`$, elsewhere one. Extend $`\tau`$ to $`J`$ by acting on sites by $`\tau`$ and conjugating every $`G`$ value by the alternating-sign diagonal. This sends $`f\mapsto f^{-1}`$ and still swaps/inverts $`U,V`$.

Finally form the outer wreath $`J^{\mathbb Z/p}\rtimes\langle a\rangle`$ with its unit cyclic shift $`a`$. Let the outer base element $`b`$ have value $`f`$ at $`0`$, $`U`$ at $`+1`$, $`V`$ at $`-1`$, and identity at any other site. These three sites are distinct even when $`p=3`$. Thus $`a,b`$ have exact order $`p`$. Reflect outer positions, invert the shift, and apply the extended $`\tau`$ to the inner $`J`$ values. This group automorphism sends **both** $`a,b`$ to their inverses: the $`+1,-1`$ entries are exchanged while the central $`f`$ entry is inverted in place. Hence it restricts to the required generator-inverting automorphism of the finite $`p`$-group $`K=\langle a,b\rangle`$.

### B2. Extract a literal one-site long shift and then the whole target group

Put $`b_+=a^{-1}ba`$, $`b_-=aba^{-1}`$. Their entries at outer site zero are $`U,V`$. The outer supports of their nonidentity projections to inner $`P`$ are $`\{0,-2\}`$ and $`\{0,2\}`$. Their only common site is zero because $`p`$ is odd and does not divide four. Even for $`p=3`$ the other sites are different; they are not extra copies of the desired shift.

The element $`q(b_+,b_-)`$ is in the outer base. At an outside site its projection to $`P`$ is trivial: at most one of the two original letters has a nontrivial $`P`$ value there, and the word has zero exponent sums. Thus every outside component belongs to $`G^P`$, of exponent at most $`p^e`$, even though its actual lamps need not vanish. It follows that $`q(b_+,b_-)^{p^e}`$ is the *pure* $`s`$ at outer site zero, identity on every other outer site. At this step taking the power is essential to the argument; knowing only the projected top would not establish a copy of a group.

Conjugate $`b`$ by the powers $`s^{-t_i}`$ of this extracted pure shift. At zero we get $`F_i=s^{-t_i}f s^{t_i}`$; everywhere else these conjugates all have equal entries. Thus their commutators are already trivial at all the other outer sites. At the zero outer site the [true two-occurrence calculation](#2-a-finite-two-generator-p-group-containing-an-arbitrary-deep-target) takes place wholly on the inner cyclic subgroup $`\langle s\rangle`$: $`[F_i,F_{i+1}]`$ has $`1+E_{i,i+2}`$ at the identity and its inverse only at $`s^{-t_i-t_{i+1}}`$. The two forced support intersections are the only ones by the least nonzero base-five digit argument, and there is no wraparound since $`L>4t_{n-1}`$. Taking the commutator with $`[F_{i+2},F_{i+3}]`$ leaves $`1+E_{i,i+4}`$ at a **single physical inner and outer site**. At $`i=1,5,9,\ldots,n-4`$ these generate the full $`\mathrm{UT}_m(p)`$ there. This proves actual inclusion in $`K''`$, not a quotient or a coincidence of correlated projections. The spaced copy gives $`D\le K^{(j)}`$ as required.

### B3. Matched Bol maps and one bounded-order universal source

On the augmentation ideal $`I`$ of $`F[K]`$ use the algebra automorphism induced by the preceding joint inversion (only the two specified generators, not every word, are individually inverted). The two-sheet law and the actual points $`k,h_g=(1,(g-1)/2)`$ for $`g=a,b`$ are as in [the finite graph calculation](#3-the-actual-two-inner-permutations-in-the-finite-quotient-and-in-the-simple-host). The two matched associators act by left multiplication by the group-basis units $`a,b`$ on $`I`$, which is faithful. The three points generate the entire local loop since their translates span the augmentation ideal. In characteristic three the genuine projective global words are exactly $`T_{a-1}S(a)`$ and $`T_{b-1}S(b)`$ in the stabilizer, giving an injective copy of their group. In particular both have **exactly** the original order $`p=3`$ in the entire simple loop, not just on the small invariant orbit.

For the infinite common source use the complete augmented associative algebra on $`X,Y`$ over $`F`$ with $`X^p=Y^p=0`$ (complete by word length). Its polynomial subalgebra identifies with the group algebra $`F[H]`$ for $`H=C_p*C_p`$, via $`a=1+X`$, $`b=1+Y`$: each map respects the two presentations, and composing them fixes the algebra generators. The polynomial algebra injects into its completion by the word-length grading; its monomial basis is the words with no run of $`p`$ identical letters. Continuous substitution $`a\mapsto a^{-1}, b\mapsto b^{-1}`$ is an involutive algebra automorphism. Apply the [two-sheet Bol law](#1-three-involutions-in-an-associative-algebra-two-sheet-loop) to its positive ideal and take only the subloop generated by $`k,h_a,h_b`$. Degree truncations prove residual finiteness. These are honest native loop quotients of the designated subloop onto its finite images.

Left multiplication by $`a,b`$ represents $`H`$ faithfully on this subloop. Indeed its vertical points contain the left group translates of $`a-1,b-1`$ (multiply $`h_a k,h_bk`$ by the invertible scalar two using addition), and hence their entire span, including $`v=ab-1=(a-1)+a(b-1)`$. Right multiplication by $`ab-1`$ is injective on $`F[H]`$: a finitely supported vector invariant under right shift by the infinite-order $`ab`$ must be zero. Thus the translates $`g v`$, $`g\in H`$, are linearly independent, as physical vertical vectors. Their translations give the regular elementary lamp group. With the two actual inner maps they give exactly $`C_p\wr H`$, and the inner maps themselves freely generate the free product of their two order-$`p`$ cyclic subgroups.

This finitely generated loop really is infinite and proper. Already the translations by $`a^i(a-1)`$ span the augmentation ideal of $`F[\langle a\rangle]`$, of dimension $`p-1`$, in the subloop generated by $`k,h_a`$. Hence it contains at least $`2p^{p-1}>2p`$ points, while two involutions of product order $`p`$ in any diassociative loop generate a dihedral group of order $`2p`$. Finally evaluating $`X,Y`$ at the basis elements $`a-1,b-1`$ of each $`F[K]`$ is compatible with $`X^p=Y^p=0`$; it extends continuously because finite $`p`$-group augmentation is nilpotent. This gives an onto homomorphism of the one fixed marked loop onto the whole finite local loop, not merely a group-word approximation. All native loop exponents are $`2p`$ by the two-sheet power calculation.

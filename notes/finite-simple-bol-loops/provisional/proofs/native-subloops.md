# Size-optimal universal associative subloops in simple proper left Bol loops

**Mira Cult · Provisional research.** Author proof, not independently reviewed.

The [projective class-two parameters](../../projective-radicals/proofs/multiplicity.md) and [bounded-size finite group host](../../projective-radicals/proofs/universal-group-host.md) have separate independent reviews. Those reviews cover the genuine finite loop constructions and group embeddings; **they do not assert that the hosted groups are native subloops**. The [countable radical construction](countable-radical.md) and this promotion to native associative subloops of principal isotopes have not received independent mathematical review. In particular these host isotopes have an element of order nine; they are not the exponent-six precursors.

**Theorem A.** One countable locally finite simple non-Moufang left Bol loop contains, as **associative subloops**, isomorphic copies of every countable group of nilpotency class at most two and exponent dividing three. It is the increasing union of actual finite simple proper left Bol subloops, all with canonical left-group quotient $`\mathrm{PSL}_2(19)`$ over a solvable radical (the same quotient for the union). No input-dependent change of isotope is made.

For every $`k\ge1`$ there is a **single finite** simple proper left Bol loop $`Q(k)`$ of order $`20\cdot3^{119+38s}`$, $`s=\max\{k,\binom k2\}`$, containing all such groups of order at most $`3^k`$ as associative subloops. These finite bounds need not use the same choices as the countable tower. The optimal logarithmic cost for a hosting **loop** is $`\Theta(k^2)`$ even if the competing finite loops need not be simple, Bol or proper. Neither a prescribed whole nucleus nor an exponent-six claim for these host isotopes is intended.

There is global information beyond the inner group tables. In each hosted $`F`$, a pair $`g,h`$ associates on the left with **every** element of the ambient simple loop if and only if $`g,h`$ commute in $`F`$. The group generated by the true permutations $`L_{gh}^{-1}L_gL_h`$ for $`g,h\in F`$ is isomorphic to $`F'`$. It fixes $`F`$ pointwise, but is not globally trivial when $`F`$ is nonabelian.

**Theorem B.** A single finite simple proper left Bol loop of order $`20\cdot3^{119+38n}`$ realizes every graph on at most $`n`$ vertices as an induced subgraph of the **native commuting graph** on elements of order exactly three. In fact its commuting relation on an explicit $`3^{2n}-1`$-element subset is precisely ternary symplectic orthogonality on nonzero vectors. Each totally isotropic subspace gives an actual elementary abelian subloop, not just a clique. There is one countable locally finite simple proper left Bol loop whose ordinary commuting graph on order-three elements contains every countable graph. This is a theorem about ordinary multiplication, not equivalence of graph deletion decks or a solution of reconstruction. The exponential order of growth for the finite graph host is optimal up to constants in its logarithm even for arbitrary finite binary operations.

## 1. A coupled passive sign equation which costs at most two dimensions

Use the original true signed construction: $`G=P\rtimes T`$, $`T=\mathrm{PSL}_2(19)`$, a native left action on $`G/H`$ with mark set $`K`$ and involutory Weyl element $`w:x\mapsto-1/x`$. We use $`w`$ also for its pure lift and module action. Over $`\mathbb F_3`$ write $`e=(1-w)/2`$. The additive space of $`P`$ has plus/minus wings $`X,Y`$, and original vertical stabilizer $`W=\{(l,f(l)):l\in X\}`$. Both wings include the original spaces $`X_0,Y_0`$ of dimensions 117,119 and a sequence of paired copies of the $`19`$-dimensional augmentation module $`M`$ on the twenty projective sites. Passive copies are central in $`P`$ and their minus parts receive all brackets. On every active pair the stabilizer graph has equal plus and minus coordinates. The left group law in $`P`$ is $`g h=g+h+[g,h]/2`$. Brackets of active opposite wings use the projected pointwise product $`\beta`$ and arbitrary symmetric coefficient matrices $`C_j`$ as in (3) of the source. The selected old translations over $`w`$ are precisely $`(v,w)`$ with

```math
v=(x,y),\qquad wx=-x,\quad wy=y.                  \tag{1}
```

The pure $`(0,w)`$ is an actual old left translation.

Choose

```math
a=e_0-e_\infty,\qquad b=e_0+e_\infty-e_1-e_{18}\quad\text{in }M .
```

Then $`wa=-a`$, $`wb=b`$, and $`\beta(a,b)=a`$: coordinatewise multiplication already has zero sum. Write $`X_L,Y_L`$ for the old base and all passive slots available at a given time, and $`f_L:X_L\to Y_L`$ for the restriction. Always choose shear images in $`Y_L`$. After each passive addition we require the copy of $`a`$ *by itself* in that passive minus position to lie in $`e f_L(eX_L)`$. We keep actual preimages, supported in $`X_L`$, for later use.

Before each addition the injection $`e f_L:eX_L\to eY_L`$ has cokernel of dimension exactly two: this is the separately checked base difference, unchanged because each new passive pair adds the very same $`eM`$ on both sides with identity diagonal. For a newly appended passive input $`v`$, the new graph is $`(f_L(l)+A(v),v)`$ with $`A:M\to Y_L`$ a $`B`$-map for $`B=19:9`$. Impose the linear condition

```math
e A(a)\ \in\ e f_L(eX_L).                      \tag{2}
```

This costs at most **two** equations on $`A`$. Choose $`l\in eX_L`$ with $`e f_L(l)=-eA(a)`$. Then $`(l,a)`$ is an actual preimage of the single new passive $`a`$. Old preimages extend by zero forever.

For the finite construction take $`s`$ passive pairs first. At step $`i`$ the space of allowed $`B`$-maps preserving the fixed torus detector has dimension at least $`7+3i`$; the full bad space $`D_f=f\mathrm{Hom}_T(M,X)+\mathrm{Hom}_T(M,Y)`$ has dimension at most $`3+2i`$, by §1 at the finite multiplicity proof. Even after (2) there remains an allowed choice outside that space. The $`s`$ active additions are then chosen simultaneously independently modulo the stated forbidden space, using the unchanged detector margin $`4+s`$. For the countable construction append consecutively passive, passive, active and repeat, **never** using an earlier active output for a shear. If $`t`$ passive and $`u`$ active pairs already exist, the detector-preserving maps into $`Y_L`$ have dimension at least $`7+3t`$ and the forbidden space for the actual previous graph has dimension at most $`3+2(t+u)`$. As $`t\ge2u`$, the margin remains at least two after (2). The chosen shears exist at every step. The single-step zero-defect induction at the countable-tower proof applies in chronological order; it does not require infinitely many independent shears assigned at once. All transversality, genuine conjugated-sign, nonlinear normal-cut, faithfulness and finite simplicity inputs remain those of the finite multiplicity proof §§1–3: the new shears satisfy **all** its conditions. The countable argument uses active/passive counts as detailed at the countable-tower proof, not just a union of unrelated folders.

## 2. Exact native translations at a new fixed identity

In the old simple proper loop $`(Q,\cdot)`$ let $`c=wH`$ and put

```math
x\star y=x\cdot(c\cdot y).
```

This loop isotope has identity $`c`$ since $`L_c=w`$ is an involution. Its left translation set is $`K w`$, acting on the *same* cosets, and its full generated left group is still $`G`$ ($`w=L_1^\star`$ and $`K=(K w)w`$). This set is closed under sandwiches: $`k w h w k\in K`$ by two old Bol sandwiches. Evaluating at $`c`$ identifies the sandwich as the required new translation; this proves the left Bol law. Simplicity is also preserved here directly. The two multiplication laws with parameters determine each other through $`x\cdot y=x\star(1\star y)`$. Their division operations likewise determine each other by solving these same equations: e.g. $`x\backslash_{\cdot}y=1\backslash_{\star}(x\backslash_{\star}y)`$ and $`y/_{\cdot}x=y/_{\star}(1\star x)`$. Thus they have the same loop congruences. If the isotope were Moufang then the old loop would be Moufang by isotopy invariance of that property, not by the stronger and unnecessary assertion that they are isomorphic ([J. I. Hall, *Central automorphisms of Latin square designs and loops*, Theorem 3.14(a)](https://www.math.md/files/qrs/v15-n1/v15-n1-%28pp19-46%29.pdf)). Indeed they are **not** isomorphic: for $`r:x\mapsto4x`$, the new translation associated to the old pure mark $`rw`$ is the pure element $`r`$ of order nine. Its powers are again new marks by sandwich closure, and distinct marks take $`c`$ to distinct points, so the new loop has an element of order nine while the old loop has exponent six.

In $`P`$ form the group $`U`$ with active plus components $`a\xi_i`$, active minus components $`b\eta_i`$, and central passive minus components $`a z_j`$; all other coordinates zero. The scalar coordinates give exactly

```math
(v,z)(v',z')=(v+v',z+z'+\tfrac12\Omega(v,v')),\qquad
 \Omega_j((\xi,\eta),(\xi',\eta'))=\xi^t C_j\eta'-{\xi'}^t C_j\eta .
                                                                    \tag{3}
```

This group acts **regularly on its own orbit** $`O=Uc`$ (not necessarily transitively on the whole loop). Its stabilizer in $`P`$ at $`c`$ is $`w W w^{-1}`$. On active coordinates this group again has equal wings, so a member of $`U`$ in it has $`a\xi_i=b\eta_i`$ and hence both zero, as the two lines have opposite $`w`$-sign. The remaining pure central-minus element has zero $`X`$ component and cannot lie in the stabilizer except at zero. In particular all points of (3), **including all central phases**, are distinct in the actual loop.

For each vector $`z`$ of passive scalars write $`A(z)=\sum_j z_j a^{(j)}\in eY_L`$. Choose the additive map $`l(z)\in eX_L`$ using the preimages from (2), so that $`e f(l(z))=A(z)`$. Put

```math
h(z)=(w l(z),\ w f(l(z)))\in w W w^{-1}.
```

It lies in $`Z(P)`$ since no active slot occurs and the bracket uses active slots only. Thus $`h(z)`$ fixes **every** point of $`Uc`$, not only $`c`$. It is additive and injective in $`z`$, since $`e f(l(z))=A(z)`$. For $`g=(v,z)\in U`$, $`g h(z)`$ has negative $`X`$ part under $`w`$ and positive $`Y`$ part: its only unwanted negative $`Y`$ component was $`A(z)`$, and $`e w f(l(z))=-A(z)`$. No BCH term is produced by $`h(z)`$. By (1) it follows that $`g h(z)w\in K`$, and this mark sends the old unit to $`gc`$, since $`h(z)`$ fixes $`c`$. Hence, as an equality of **actual permutations of the complete ambient point set**,

```math
L_{gc}^{\star}=g h(z).                           \tag{4}
```

Consequently $`(gc)\star(g'c)=(gg')c`$. This proves that $`O`$ is a genuine associative subloop with all products and divisions transported from $`U`$. It would be wrong simply to identify $`g`$ with the ambient translation at $`gc`$: for $`z\ne0`$, it is not that translation.

There is a measurable distinction on the entire ambient loop. Multiplying (4) and comparing with (3) gives

```math
(L_{(gg')c}^{\star})^{-1} L_{gc}^{\star} L_{g'c}^{\star}
                   = h(-\tfrac12\Omega(v,v')).                       \tag{5}
```

The native action of $`G`$ is faithful; (5) is identity precisely for commuting $`g,g'`$. It always fixes the entire group subloop pointwise. For any subgroup $`F\le U`$, its commutator subgroup is spanned by its bracket values, so (5) generates exactly a copy of $`F'`$ in the true stabilizer. Nontriviality here guarantees a failure of the corresponding associativity equation at some external point, even though the embedded subgroup's own multiplication is associative.

## 3. Universal finite and countable groups, with the host-loop lower bound

For finite order bound $`3^k`$, name active slots $`(i,j)`$ for positive $`i+j\le k`$, using the available $`s`$. For each $`(i,1)`$ put a diagonal $`1`$ at that slot in $`C_1`$; for $`j>1`$ put symmetric $`1`$ entries in $`C_j`$ between $`(i,1)`$ and $`(i,j)`$. Use zero elsewhere (an unused harmless diagonal may be used for $`k=1`$). Name $`k`$ passive outputs. These arrays are chosen **before** any group is specified.

For a target $`F`$, let $`Z=Z(F)`$, $`V=F/Z`$ and write its commutator in ordered bases as $`\omega:V^2\to Z`$. One may use additive ternary coordinates $`V\oplus Z`$ with product equal to addition plus $`\omega/2`$: define the additive sum on $`F`$ by $`x+y=xy[x,y]^{-1/2}`$, which is commutative and associative by class two, then split its additive center. Send $`z_j`$ to central output $`j`$ of scalar size 1. Send a lift of $`v_n`$ to $`\xi_{(n,1)}=1`$ together with $`\eta_{(i,j)}=\omega_j(v_i,v_n)`$ for $`i<n`$. Other entries vanish. The $`V`$ and central coordinates separately give injectivity. In (3) for $`i<n`$ only the $`(i,1),(i,j)`$ coefficient contributes, yielding precisely $`\omega_j(v_i,v_n)`$; the reversed term vanishes because the earlier vector only uses earlier minus rows. Thus the native group subloop $`O`$ contains $`F`$ isomorphically. If $`F`$ is abelian only the passive targets are needed.

For the countable tower enumerate **all** $`(i,j)`$ by increasing $`i+j`$ with ties ordered by $`i`$, using two passive slots for each successive active one. Add each symmetric entry only with its new active index, so no old-old bracket ever changes. Keep (2) and its original preimages unchanged. The genuine signed coset embeddings of the old loops are the zero-coordinate inclusions from the countable-tower proof, preserve $`c`$, and hence preserve the displayed isotope multiplication as well. The old shears, native group products, and $`h(z)`$ from any previous stage all remain unchanged. Each stage with $`s`$ active pairs has order $`20\cdot3^{119+57s}`$. The direct union is a countable locally finite loop: multiplication and divisions agree on stages. A nontrivial congruence restricts nontrivially at a finite stage and is thus universal there and on every later simple stage. The resulting left group is the faithful direct union of the original left groups, so its radical quotient is still $`T`$. Non-Moufangness persists on the embedded finite stages.

For countable $`F`$ take finite or countably indexed bases and the same formula. It involves only finitely many $`i<n`$ per new basis vector; for each such $`i`$ the corresponding central vector is individually finitely supported. Hence images genuinely belong to this direct union; a full infinite commutator column placed on an earlier generator would not in general do so.

For the lower bound put $`t=\lfloor k/3\rfloor\ge2`$. On a $`2t`$-dimensional ternary space take $`t`$ independent alternating forms including one fixed nonsingular form. Their group with $`t`$ central coordinates has order $`3^{3t}`$, center equal to derived of dimension $`t`$, and is generated by $`2t`$ lifts. Writing $`b=\binom{2t}{2}`$, the $`t-1`$ choices give $`\prod_{j=1}^{t-1}(3^b-3^j)`$ tuples. Isomorphic groups must have their commutators transported by linear maps of the true quotient and derived subgroup, giving fewer than $`3^{5t^2}`$ tuples per isotype. There are therefore at least $`3^{2t^3-8t^2+1}`$ isotypes. Any hosting **loop** $`D`$ has at most $`|D|^{2t}`$ possible $`2t`$-generated subloops, since a tuple generates just one subloop (including divisions), and generators in an embedded group generate the very same subloop there. Thus

```math
\log_3 |D|\ge t^2-4t+\tfrac1{2t}.
```

The upper and lower orders match for *loop* size. Existence alone without cost or prescribed ambient quotient would miss the point: the older odd right-nucleus realization already hosts any specified finite odd group, and finite products then give a finite menu without these bounds. In the literature, [G. P. Nagy, *Some remarks on simple Bol loops*, Lemma 3.3](https://cmuc.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/pdf/cmuc0802/nagy.pdf) already identifies real group subloops from exact-factorization factors. Our use of a nontrivial pointwise central stabilizer correction on a fixed projective quotient is different. There is no claim of exhaustive historical priority.

## 4. A native graph universality bound at only exponential cost

For the graph host use $`n`$ passive and $`n`$ active pairs with $`C_1=I_n`$ and the other coefficient matrices zero. Equations (3)–(4) give a genuine Heisenberg group subloop on the $`2n`$ active scalar coordinates and first central scalar (other passive scalars may be omitted). On the points of central scalar zero its ordinary commutation is exactly $`\langle(\xi,\eta),(\xi',\eta')\rangle=\xi\cdot\eta'-\xi'\cdot\eta=0`$. Nonzero points have order three as native elements. A totally isotropic vector subspace has $`\Omega=0`$ throughout, so its zero-phase points with identity form a native elementary abelian subloop. Conversely a subloop wholly in that zero-phase slice must be an additive totally isotropic subspace, since its products would otherwise leave the slice.

Given any graph on $`m\le n`$ enumerated vertices, assign to vertex $`j`$ the vector $`\xi_j=e_j`$ and $`\eta_j=\sum_{i<j,\,ij\ \mathrm{not\ an\ edge}}e_i`$. The $`\xi`$ coordinates ensure the vectors are distinct and nonzero. For $`i<j`$ their symplectic pairing is $`0`$ for an edge and $`1`$ otherwise. Thus no additional edges appear and no graph labels are required in the native commutation test. The same causal construction with columns $`(i,1)`$ in the countable triangular tower, ignoring all other active coordinates, realizes every countable graph. Finally, if one finite binary algebra of size $`N`$ realizes every graph on $`n`$ sites as an induced commuting graph, at most $`N^n`$ ordered tuples must witness $`2^{\binom n2}`$ labelled graphs (relabel each embedding), so $`\log_2 N\ge(n-1)/2`$. This lower concerns the size of the whole ambient algebra, not just the symplectic slice. No claim that a commuting graph alone determines its parent is made.

# Provisional Bol-loop research

**Mira Cult**

These are author arguments and finite tests, **not independently reviewed mathematical results**. They are separate from the [reviewed results](../CLAIMS.md). In particular, extending a construction from its left multiplication group to an actual subloop is a new theorem, not a change of terminology.

| Question | What is proposed | Where to start |
|---|---|---|
| Does one simple proper Bol loop contain all countable class-at-most-two groups of exponent three as actual subloops? | A countable locally finite host and bounded-size finite hosts, with a quadratic logarithmic cost in the finite group-size bound. It also embeds any finite graph as a commuting pattern among elements of order three. | [Native subloop argument](proofs/native-subloops.md), [countable group-level precursor](proofs/countable-radical.md) |
| How large can the radical class, or the permutation order of one associator, be? | Both can be unbounded over the **same simple quotient**. One construction keeps the radical exponent nine; a different construction gives a single genuine associator of order any power of three while the *loop* exponent stays six. | [Radical class](proofs/radical-class.md), [one associator](proofs/associator-order.md) |
| Can a fixed projective quotient coexist with arbitrarily complicated radicals and actual inner maps? | The noncommutative construction places every finite 3-group in a faithful solvable radical over PSL₂(19). A second construction forces both designated left associators to have order three, with the target arbitrarily deep in their derived series. | [Local algebras, radicals and inner maps](local-algebras/README.md) |
| What are the quotients of the infinite affine Burnside example? | Every proper quotient is finite. In a related finite class, an operator with no prime-field eigenvalues would give a complete classification up to isomorphism and isotopy, in every dimension. | [Quotients, operator parameters and true generator rank](proofs/affine-quotients.md) |

The earlier [projective directions and finite checks](projective-and-affine.md) above are backed by complete author arguments and replayable programs.

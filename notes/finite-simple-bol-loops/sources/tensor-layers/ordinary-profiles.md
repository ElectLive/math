# Ordinary tensor profiles: actual sheets and rank restrictions

**Mira Cult**

This supplies the canonical and rank inputs for the [ordinary tensor theorem](../../manuscripts/0108-ordinary-tensor-idealizers.md). Use its notation; all fields here have odd characteristic $`p`$. Put $`S=\mathrm{SL}_d(q)`$, and call a mark selected only if it comes from the original set $`\Lambda`$. The action of $`Y`$ on the prime-field vector space $`V`$ is faithful and irreducible. For irreducibility, subtract the identity from left and right elementary transvections acting on a nonzero invariant additive subgroup: a nonzero entry produces a nonzero scalar matrix unit, and arbitrary field scalar multiples and all positions follow by further transvections and changes of basis. Faithfulness is exactly the reciprocal scalar kernel already divided out.

## Why a common complement exists

$`V`$ is the unique minimal normal subgroup of $`G`$: a disjoint minimal normal subgroup would centralize $`V`$, while faithfulness gives $`C_G(V)=V`$. Hence the canonical inversion $`\tau`$ preserves $`V`$. The [inner-inversion criterion](../factor-permutation/exact-factorization-criterion.md#inner-inversion-and-the-generalized-fitting-subgroup) rules out an inner $`\tau`$: it would imply $`F^*(G)=O_2(G)`$, incompatible with the nonzero normal odd group $`V`$. If its restriction to $`V`$ belonged to $`Y`$, removing conjugation by a lift would fix both $`V`$ and $`Y`$ pointwise. Such automorphisms embed by pointwise difference into the additive group of derivations $`Z^1(Y,V)`$, a $`p`$-group even for a nonsplit extension. That is impossible because the outer class of $`\tau`$ has order two. Thus the physical outside coset in the linear action is disjoint from $`Y`$, and the action of the formal quotient $`Y\rtimes\langle\bar t\rangle`$ on $`V`$ is faithful. Only **selected** involution classes in the outside coset contribute.

In even dimension the scalar $`-I`$ on $`V`$ belongs to $`Y`$ and is fixed-point-free. Its central subgroup of order two gives a $`\tau`$-stable complement by the [odd-kernel block theorem](../../manuscripts/0101-odd-kernel-block-action.md). In odd dimension write a physical outside involution as $`P\otimes Q`$; equality of pure tensors up to reciprocal scalars gives $`P^2=cI,\ Q^2=c^{-1}I`$. Because $`d`$ is odd, determinants show $`c`$ is a square. Normalize both factors to involutions, then choose their signs individually to have determinant one. Thus the original restriction of $`\tau`$ has the form $`-g`$ for an involution or identity $`g\in Y`$, the positive choice having already been excluded. Composing $`\tau`$ with conjugation by an inverse lift of $`g`$ fixes $`Y`$ and negates $`V`$. Consequently the extension class in $`H^2(Y,V)`$ is equal to its negative. This cohomology group is of odd order, so the extension splits. Complements to $`V`$ in any split abelian extension are the graphs of the derivations relative to one complement; their number is odd. The involution $`\tau`$ fixes at least one complement.

Use this common complement. Over each selected quotient mark $`a`$ the vector-zero involution is genuinely selected, because every involutory lift is conjugate under $`V`$ in the preimage of $`\langle a\rangle`$. Its other lifts have precisely the vectors in the negative eigenspace. The subgroup $`J`$ preserves $`W`$, and $`\langle\bar t\,\bar\Lambda\rangle=Y`$. When all sheets have size $`q^l`$, their common-coset multiplicity is $`h=q^{d^2-l}/|W|>1`$. Indeed $`h=1`$ gives a transversal over $`HV`$. If $`HV<G`$, then $`H<HV`$ by core-freeness, and the [normal-subloop criterion](../foundations/C0001.md) contradicts simplicity. If $`HV=G`$, a single projected label cannot generate the nontrivial $`Y`$ by translating it by the marked $`\bar t`$.

## The spectral interval

For odd $`d`$ the preceding argument gives a stable complement and a negative coset. Its quotient involution operators are $`-A\otimes B`$ with $`A,B\in S`$ actual involutions. Indeed their preimages in $`S^2`$ initially square to reciprocal central scalars; the scalar group has odd order, so unique reciprocal scalar adjustment makes their squares one. Let $`r,s`$ be the **even** dimensions of their negative eigenspaces, from 0 to $`d-1`$. Each $`(r,s)`$ specifies one $`Y`$-class: a conjugating change of eigenbases can have determinant corrected in its eigenbasis centralizer. The scalar kernel produces no additional identifications between normalized pairs. Its size is

```math
q^{r(d-r)+s(d-s)}{d\brack r}_q{d\brack s}_q
```

by choosing an eigenspace and a complement in each coordinate. The negative vector sheet has dimension $`(d-r)(d-s)+rs`$: these are the *same*-sign blocks of the matrix tensor.

For each selected $`(r,s)`$ the conjugates of that vector sheet contain every matrix of rank at most $`d-|r-s|`$. In the two diagonal rectangular blocks the possible ranks are precisely from zero to the sum of their two minimum side lengths, equal to $`d-|r-s|`$. Rank-deficient matrices of a fixed rank form one $`S^2`$-orbit (adjust a conjugator determinant in left and right null directions). For $`r=s`$ the square blocks realize every nonzero determinant as well, so all matrices are covered. Thus with $`m=\min|r-s|`$ over selected profiles, $`W`$ has minimum nonzero rank at least $`d-m+1`$, in particular $`W=0`$ if $`m=0`$. Deleting the first $`d-m`$ rows is injective on $`W`$; hence $`|W|\le q^{dm}`$, including additive rather than linear codes.

For each individual selected profile one also has

```math
q^{(r-s)^2}\ \le |W|.                         \tag{1}
```

Indeed the [odd-kernel orbit theorem](../../manuscripts/0098-odd-kernel-stabilizer-orbits.md) gives a $`p`$-group in $`J`$, normalized by this outside label $`a`$, whose conjugacy orbit of $`a`$ has length $`|C_V(a)|/|W|=q^{d(r+s)-2rs}/|W|`$. Here $`p=\operatorname{char}\mathbf F_q`$: one may take $`O_p(J\cap J^a)`$ by the source equality $`J\cap J^a=O_p(J\cap J^a)C_J(a)`$. This is stronger than just an orbit in an arbitrary Sylow $`p`$-group. Lift it to the unique Sylow $`p`$-subgroup of its preimage under $`S^2\to Y`$ (central kernel prime to $`p`$; a normal central Hall subgroup has a unique complement). This subgroup is normalized by $`(A,B)`$. Each projected $`p`$-group has an $`A`$- or $`B`$-stable full flag with unipotent upper triangular action: take a nonzero common invariant vector, choose an eigenline of the normalizing involution on the common fixed space and induct on the quotient. Lift the resulting flag basis in eigenspaces, possible since $`p`$ is odd. The orbit of the normalized diagonal sign matrix inside the whole upper unitriangular group has size $`q^{r(d-r)}`$ in the first coordinate, $`q^{s(d-s)}`$ in the second: all strictly upper entries pairing opposite signs are the moving coordinates. The actual orbit cannot be longer than the product. Rearrangement is (1). In particular if any balanced profile $`r=s`$ occurs, all selected profiles are balanced and $`W=0`$.

## Why a balanced profile is impossible

For a mark $`a`$ of a noncentral balanced profile, the [odd-kernel stabilizer-orbit theorem](../../manuscripts/0098-odd-kernel-stabilizer-orbits.md) supplies a $`p`$-subgroup $`R`$ of $`J\cap J^a`$, $`p=\mathrm{char}(\mathbf F_q)`$, normalized by $`a`$, such that its conjugacy orbit is all the marks in the cell $`aJ`$ and has size $`q^{2r(d-r)}`$. As in the proof of the spectral interval, lift $`R`$ through the central prime-to-$`p`$ kernel into two full upper unitriangular groups $`U_1,U_2`$ in eigenflag bases of $`A,B`$. Its orbit is contained in the orbit of $`U_1\times U_2`$ of size **exactly the same** $`q^{2r(d-r)}`$. The two actual quotient orbits therefore coincide as sets; no assumption that $`R=U_1\times U_2`$ is being made. Pick an upper root $`x\in U_1`$ connecting opposite sign coordinates, so $`AxA=x^{-1}`$. Its pure conjugation of $`a`$ is in $`aJ`$, so the product with $`a`$ gives the pure nonidentity transvection $`(u,1)=(x^2,1)\in J`$.

In fact **every** vector lift over every pure transvection lies in the genuine marked square of this one balanced class. To see this, write $`u=1+\iota\phi,\ \phi(\iota)=0`$, take an arbitrary $`v\in V`$, and regard it as a matrix map $`L^*\to L`$ with image $`C`$. There exists a basis adapted to the flag $`\langle\iota\rangle\le\ker\phi\le L`$ and to $`C`$ simultaneously: take the intersection with each flag step, extend bases successively, and, when $`C\not\subseteq\ker\phi`$, use a vector of $`C`$ for the outside basis vector. A diagonal sign matrix $`A'`$ in this basis can have opposite signs on $`\iota`$ and that outside vector, and exactly $`r`$ minus signs overall. It preserves $`C`$ and reverses $`u`$. Choose $`B'`$ with $`(B')^\mathsf t`$ preserving $`\ker v`$, inducing on the quotient the involution corresponding to $`A'|_C`$ via $`v`$, and fill its complementary sign counts on $`\ker v`$ so it has exactly $`r`$ minus signs. This is possible since $`C`$ is $`A'`$-invariant. Thus $`A'v(B')^\mathsf t=v`$, i.e. $`v`$ is in the negative space of the selected $`-A'\otimes B'`$. Since $`u`$ has odd order, $`A'u`$ is conjugate to $`A'`$ by a power of $`u`$. The two vector-zero marks at $`-A'\otimes B'`$ and $`-(A'u)\otimes B'`$ multiply to $`(u,1)`$, and varying the first by $`v`$ gives exactly the lift $`(v,(u,1))`$. This applies in particular to the genuine $`H`$-lift, contradicting $`H\cap\Lambda^2=1`$. It removes both the Sylow assumption and the stabilizer-cocycle normalization from this obstruction.

For odd dimension the central balanced class alone cannot generate the quotient, and the spectral interval excludes all unbalanced classes if a balanced class occurs. In even dimension the scalar balanced endpoints belong to the implemented coset. The same collision for an intermediate balanced split profile is unaffected by the presence of a quadratic class.

## The extremal obstruction

Let $`m=\min|r-s|`$, $`M=\max|r-s|`$ over selected profiles, and $`\rho=d-m`$. Now $`m\ge2`$ and even, $`\rho`$ is odd, every nonzero matrix in $`W`$ has rank at least $`\rho+1`$, and $`q^{M^2}\le|W|\le q^{dm}`$. If $`\rho=1`$, only profiles $`(0,d-1),(d-1,0)`$ occur; both are required for generation. Their total class size is $`2q^{d-1}(q^d-1)/(q-1)`$, and the vector sheet of each has dimension $`d`$. Put $`h=q^{d(d-1)}/|W|>1`$ by the strict original cut. Writing $`D=d(d-1)/2`$, we get $`|J|_p=hq^{2D-d+1}`$. In each pure factor $`J`$ has a $`p`$-subgroup of order at least $`hq^{D-d+1}>q^{(d-1)(d-2)/2}`$, by projecting onto the opposite projective factor. Such a group contains a rank-one transvection: embed it into an upper unitriangular group and project to the size-$`(d-1)`$ quotient after the first basis vector. The nontrivial kernel consists of rank-at-most-one differences from the identity. Each pure transvection fixes $`W`$ pointwise since its difference on $`W`$ has rank at most one. The two opposite restrictions put $`W`$ in a $`(d-1)`$-by-$`(d-1)`$ block, minimum rank at least two. Deleting one row bounds $`|W|\le q^{(d-1)(d-2)}<q^{(d-1)^2}`$, contrary to the spectral lower bound. Hence $`\rho\ge3`$.

## A defining-prime valuation

Write $`a,b`$ for the dimensions of the image and row spans of $`W`$, $`A=\max(a,b)`$, $`a_0=\min(a,b)`$, $`D=d(d-1)/2`$. For pure $`p`$-subgroups the [idealizer argument](ordinary-idealizers.md) gives $`[Y:J]_p\ge q^{\rho(2A-\rho-1)/2}`$; deleting $`\rho`$ rows on the smaller support gives $`|W|\le q^{A(a_0-\rho)}`$. Set $`M=\max|r-s|`$ and $`m=\min|r-s|=d-\rho`$. With only split profiles, counting their actual sheets gives $`[Y:J]=|W|\,\mathcal S/q^{M^2}`$, where $`\mathcal S`$ sums $`q^{M^2-(r-s)^2}{d\brack r}_q{d\brack s}_q`$ over the selected presentations, divided by two if simultaneous signs identify presentations. Hence

```math
\frac{v_p(\mathcal S)}{v_p(q)}
 \ge M^2+\rho(2A-\rho-1)/2-\log_q|W|
 \ge \binom{\rho}{2}+m^2-(A-\rho)^2
 \ge\binom{\rho}{2}.
```

The factor two is prime to $`p`$. This valuation is of the **sum**, not a termwise divisibility statement.

# Proof of Claim `0323-finite-simple-non-moufang-bol-loops:C0008`

**Mira Cult**

See the dependency and review limits in [the canonical claim](../../claims/0008.md#proof).


Let $`Q`$ be a finite simple loop ($`|Q| > 2`$), so $`M := \mathop{\mathrm{Mlt}}\nolimits(Q) \le \mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits(Q)`$ is primitive on $`Q`$ (`C0001` Proposition 2.1). By the O'Nan–Scott Theorem in the 8-type subdivision of M. W. Liebeck, C. E. Praeger, and J. Saxl (*J. Austral. Math. Soc. Ser. A* **44**(3) (1988), 389–396), $`M`$ belongs to one of **`HA`, `AS`, `HS`, `SD`, `HC`, `CD`, `PA`, `TW`**.

### 1. Direct Elimination of Affine Type `HA` for All Primes $`p \ge 2`$

#### Theorem 1.1 (No Finite Simple Nonassociative Left Bol Loop Has `HA` Multiplication Group)
No finite simple nonassociative left Bol loop $`Q`$ has $`\mathop{\mathrm{Mlt}}\nolimits(Q)`$ of O'Nan–Scott Affine type (`HA`).

*Proof*: An affine primitive group has degree $`p^d`$ for some prime $`p`$ and $`d\geq 1`$, so $`|Q|=p^d`$. The prime-power nonexistence portion of [C0011](../../claims/0011.md), proved in its [proof §§1–2](C0011.md), excludes finite simple nonassociative left Bol loops of this order. $`\square`$

**Historical audit:** Nagy, *Some remarks on simple Bol loops*, Comment. Math. Univ. Carolin. **49** (2008), 259–270, §6, p. 268, states prime-power-order solvability ([published source](https://cmuc.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/pdf/cmuc0802/nagy.pdf)) without a proof or precise underlying theorem locator. The former Jordan-algebra induction was removed because the cited McCrimmon Theorems 1–2 do not establish its required strict derived-algebra inclusion. Neither is used as the present argument.

### 2. Orbital Displacement Elimination of `PA`, `CD`, `HC`, `TW`, and `SD` ($`k \ge 3`$)

#### Lemma 2.1 (Uniform Orbital Displacement and Hamming-1 Isolation)
1. **Uniform Orbital Displacement**: For any loop $`Q`$ on $`\Omega`$ ($`e = 1_Q`$) with $`\mathop{\mathrm{Mlt}}\nolimits(Q) \le P \le \mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits(\Omega)`$ and any $`x \in \Omega`$, $`R_y \in P`$ sends $`(e, x) \mapsto (y, L_x(y))`$ and $`L_y \in P`$ sends $`(e, x) \mapsto (y, R_x(y))`$. Hence $`(y, L_x(y)) \in \Gamma_P(e, x)`$ and $`(y, R_x(y)) \in \Gamma_P(e, x)`$ for **all** $`y \in \Omega`$.
2. **Hamming-1 Isolation**: For $`|\Delta| \ge 3`$ and $`m \ge 2`$, if $`g = (h_1, \dots, h_m)\sigma \in \mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits(\Delta) \wr S_m`$ (acting by $`(g \cdot \mathbf{y})_i = h_i(y_{\sigma^{-1}(i)})`$) satisfies $`d_H(\mathbf{y}, g \cdot \mathbf{y}) = 1`$ for all $`\mathbf{y} \in \Delta^m`$, then $`\sigma = \mathrm{id}_{S_m}`$ and all but one $`h_i`$ equal $`\mathrm{id}_\Delta`$.
   *Proof*: If $`\sigma \neq \mathrm{id}`$, pick $`\sigma(j) = i \neq j`$ (so $`\sigma^{-1}(j) \neq j`$). Fixing all $`y_r`$ ($`r \neq j`$) keeps $`y_i`$ and $`(g \cdot \mathbf{y})_j = h_j(y_{\sigma^{-1}(j)})`$ constant while varying $`y_j \in \Delta`$ sweeps $`(g \cdot \mathbf{y})_i = h_i(y_j)`$ over $`|\Delta| \ge 3`$ distinct values; at least $`2`$ values of $`y_j`$ give $`(g \cdot \mathbf{y})_i \neq y_i`$, and at least one of those $`2`$ also satisfies $`y_j \neq (g \cdot \mathbf{y})_j`$, yielding $`d_H(\mathbf{y}, g \cdot \mathbf{y}) \ge 2`$, contradiction. Thus $`\sigma = \mathrm{id}_{S_m}`$, and if $`h_i \neq \mathrm{id}, h_j \neq \mathrm{id}`$ for $`i \neq j`$, picking $`h_i(y_i) \neq y_i`$ and $`h_j(y_j) \neq y_j`$ gives $`d_H(\mathbf{y}, g \cdot \mathbf{y}) \ge 2`$, contradiction. $`\square`$

#### Theorem 2.2 (Elimination of `PA`, `CD`, `HC`, `TW`, and `SD` ($`k \ge 3`$) for All Finite Simple Loops)
Let $`Q`$ be **any** finite simple loop ($`|Q| > 2`$) with $`M = \mathop{\mathrm{Mlt}}\nolimits(Q)`$. Then $`M`$ cannot have O'Nan–Scott type `PA`, `CD`, `HC`, `TW`, or `SD` with $`k \ge 3`$ simple factors.

*Proof*:
- **`PA`, `CD`, `HC`**: Here $`\Omega = \Delta^m`$ with $`m \ge 2`$ and $`|\Delta| \ge 3`$ (`PA` has $`|\Delta| \ge 5`$, `CD` and `HC` have $`|\Delta| = |T|^\ell \ge 60`$; note $`\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits(2) \wr S_m`$ on $`\{0, 1\}^m`$ for $`m \ge 2`$ is never primitive as central antipodal shift $`\mathbf{y} \mapsto \mathbf{y} \oplus \mathbf{1}`$ preserves $`2^{m-1}`$ blocks of size $`2`$), and $`M \le \mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits(\Delta) \wr S_m`$ projects transitively to $`S_m`$. Let $`\mathbf{e} = (e_1, \dots, e_m) = 1_Q`$ and $`\mathbf{x}_{i, \delta} = (e_1, \dots, \delta, \dots, e_m)`$ ($`\delta \neq e_i`$), so $`d_H(\mathbf{e}, \mathbf{x}_{i, \delta}) = 1`$. Since $`\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits(\Delta) \wr S_m`$ preserves $`d_H`$, Lemma 2.1(1)–(2) forces $`R_{\mathbf{x}_{i, \delta}} = (\mathrm{id}, \dots, r_\delta^{(i)}, \dots, \mathrm{id})`$ with $`r_\delta^{(i)}`$ fixed-point-free. For any $`\mathbf{z} \in \Delta^m`$, write $`L_{\mathbf{z}} = (f_1, \dots, f_m)\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}`$; since $`L_{\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{e}) = \mathbf{z}`$, $`L_{\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{x}_{i, \delta})`$ differs from $`\mathbf{z}`$ solely at coordinate $`\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}(i)`$, whereas $`L_{\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{x}_{i, \delta}) = R_{\mathbf{x}_{i, \delta}}(\mathbf{z})`$ differs from $`\mathbf{z}`$ solely at coordinate $`i`$. Hence $`\sigma_{\mathbf{z}} = \mathrm{id}_{S_m}`$ ($`\forall \mathbf{z}`$), and symmetrically for $`R_{\mathbf{z}}`$, forcing $`M \le \mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits(\Delta)^m`$ (imprimitive for $`m \ge 2`$, contradiction).
- **`TW` (regular nonabelian socle $`N = T^k \trianglelefteq M`$, $`k \ge 2`$, $`|T| \ge 60`$)**: Identifying $`Q \equiv T^k`$ via $`N`$, $`L_x(y) = x \cdot_N \lambda_x(y)`$ and $`R_y(x) = y \cdot_N \rho_y(x)`$ with $`\lambda_x = (\alpha_{x, j})\sigma_x, \rho_y = (\beta_{y, j})\tau_y \in \mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(T) \wr S_k`$. Taking $`y = \mathbf{u}_{i, t} = (1, \dots, t, \dots, 1)`$ ($`t \neq 1`$), $`x \cdot_N \lambda_x(\mathbf{u}_{i, t})`$ differs from $`x`$ solely at coordinate $`\sigma_x(i)`$, so $`d_H(x, R_{\mathbf{u}_{i, t}}(x)) = 1`$ ($`\forall x \in T^k`$). By Lemma 2.1(2), $`R_{\mathbf{u}_{i, t}}(x)`$ differs from $`x`$ solely at one fixed coordinate $`i_0`$ (which at $`x = 1`$ equals $`i`$), forcing $`\sigma_x(i) = i`$ ($`\forall x, i`$) and similarly $`\tau_y = \mathrm{id}_{S_k}`$ ($`\forall y`$), so $`T_1 \trianglelefteq M`$ is intransitive, contradiction.
- **`SD` with $`k \ge 3`$ ($`\mathop{\mathrm{Soc}}\nolimits(M) = T^k`$ on $`T^k / D`$)**: Write $`D=\{(t,\ldots,t):t\in T\}`$, and represent points by right-diagonal classes $`[y_1:\cdots:y_k]`$. Let $`\mathbf y\sim_1\mathbf z`$ mean that, for some $`t\in T`$, $`z_i=y_i t`$ at exactly $`k-1`$ indices. The common $`t`$ and the exceptional index are unique when $`k\ge3`$. Elements of the normalizer act by
  $$g[\mathbf y]=[a_1\alpha(y_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}):\cdots:a_k\alpha(y_{\sigma^{-1}(k)})],$$
  where $`a_i\in T`$, $`\alpha\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(T)`$ and $`\sigma\in S_k`$; they preserve $`\sim_1`$ and carry the exceptional index $`i`$ to $`\sigma(i)`$.

  **Displacement lemma.** If $`g`$ sends every point to a $`\sim_1`$-neighbor, then its factor permutation is trivial and $`g\in T_i`$ for one factor $`T_i`$.

  *Proof.* Put $`f_i(\mathbf y)=y_i^{-1}a_i\alpha(y_{\sigma^{-1}(i)})`$. The hypothesis says that, for every $`\mathbf y`$, exactly $`k-1`$ of the $`f_i(\mathbf y)`$ are equal. Suppose $`\sigma(r)=q\ne r`$ and fix all $`y_j`$ for $`j\ne r`$, varying $`y_r=t`$. Then $`f_r=t^{-1}a`$ and $`f_q=b\alpha(t)`$ for fixed $`a,b\in T`$, and the other $`k-2`$ values are fixed. Both moving functions are bijections. If $`k\ge4`$ and the fixed values are all $`c`$, at least one moving value must equal $`c`$, allowing at most two values of $`t`$. If the fixed values include distinct $`c,d`$, a common value at $`k-1`$ indices can only be $`c`$ or $`d`$, and both moving values must equal it; again at most two $`t`$ are possible. Either conclusion contradicts the hypothesis.

  For $`k=3`$, let $`c`$ be the remaining fixed value. Every $`t\in T`$ must satisfy $`t^{-1}a=c`$, $`b\alpha(t)=c`$, or $`t^{-1}a=b\alpha(t)`$. The first two equations have one solution each. The third is $`t b\alpha(t)=a`$ and has at most $`3|T|/4`$ solutions. Indeed, if $`t_0`$ is a solution, set $`x=t t_0^{-1}`$; comparison with the equation for $`t_0`$ shows that $`x`$ is inverted by the fixed automorphism $`\theta=\mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(t_0 b)\circ\alpha`$ (with $`\mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(u)(v)=uvu^{-1}`$). To see the bound, let $`S=\{x:\theta(x)=x^{-1}\}`$. If $`|S|>3|T|/4`$, then for each $`x\in S`$ the set $`S\cap x^{-1}S`$ has size greater than $`|T|/2`$. For each $`y`$ in this intersection, $`x,y,xy\in S`$, and comparing $`\theta(xy)`$ with $`(xy)^{-1}`$ shows that $`x`$ and $`y`$ commute. Hence $`|C_T(x)|>|T|/2`$, so $`x\in Z(T)`$. Thus $`S\subseteq Z(T)`$, forcing $`T`$ abelian, a contradiction. As $`|T|\ge60`$, $`3|T|/4+2<|T|`$. Therefore $`\sigma=1`$ also when $`k=3`$.

  Now $`f_i(\mathbf y)=h_i(y_i)`$ with $`h_i(t)=t^{-1}a_i\alpha(t)`$. A nonconstant $`h_i`$ has image size at least $`5`$: if $`\varphi=\mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(a_i)\circ\alpha`$, then $`h_i(t)=t^{-1}\varphi(t)a_i`$, whose fibers are cosets of $`\mathop{\mathrm{Fix}}\nolimits(\varphi)`$; this is a proper subgroup when $`h_i`$ is nonconstant, and a nonabelian simple group has no proper subgroup of index below $`5`$ (its faithful coset action would embed it in $`S_m`$ for $`m<5`$). If two $`h_i`$ are nonconstant, fix any third coordinate, whose value is $`c`$. Independently choose the two nonconstant values different from $`c`$ and from each other; their images each have size at least $`5`$, so this is possible. The resulting tuple has at least three distinct values, which makes a common value at $`k-1`$ indices impossible for any $`k\ge3`$. Thus at most one $`h_i`$ is nonconstant.

  If $`h_i`$ is constant, evaluating at $`1`$ gives $`h_i(t)=a_i`$, hence $`\alpha(t)=a_i^{-1}t a_i`$ for all $`t`$. Since $`T`$ is centerless, all constant-coordinate $`a_i`$ are the same element $`a`$. If every coordinate is constant, the displayed permutation sends $`[y_i]`$ to $`[y_i a]`$, the identity on $`T^k/D`$. Otherwise, with exceptional coordinate $`j`$, right-diagonal normalization gives
  $$g[\mathbf y]=[y_1:\cdots:(a_j a^{-1})y_j:\cdots:y_k],$$
  so $`g\in T_j`$. This proves the lemma. $`\square`$

  For $`\mathbf x_{i,s}=[1:\cdots:s:\cdots:1]`$ with $`s\ne1`$, Lemma 2.1(1) gives $`\mathbf y\sim_1 R_{\mathbf x_{i,s}}(\mathbf y)`$ for every $`\mathbf y`$. The displacement lemma and the exceptional index at $`\mathbf y=1_Q`$ give $`R_{\mathbf x_{i,s}}\in T_i`$. For every $`\mathbf z`$, the equality $`L_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf x_{i,s})=R_{\mathbf x_{i,s}}(\mathbf z)`$ now shows that $`L_{\mathbf z}`$ maps the exceptional index $`i`$ to itself. This holds for all $`i`$, so all $`L_{\mathbf z}`$ have trivial factor permutation; the same argument interchanging left and right translations gives the same for all $`R_{\mathbf z}`$. Consequently $`M`$ has trivial factor permutation, contrary to the transitivity on the simple factors of its unique minimal normal subgroup $`T^k`$. $`\square`$

### 3. Analytical Elimination of `HS` and `SD_2` ($`k = 2`$) and Almost-Simple Conclusion

#### Theorem 3.1 (Analytical Elimination of `HS` and `SD_2` for All Nonabelian Finite Simple Groups $`T`$)
Let $`T`$ be any finite nonabelian simple group, and let $`W = N_{\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits(T)}(T_L \times T_R) = (T_L \times T_R) \cdot (\mathop{\mathrm{Out}}\nolimits(T) \times C_2)`$ act on $`T`$. If $`(Q, *)`$ is **any** loop on $`T`$ with identity $`1_Q = 1_T`$ and $`\mathop{\mathrm{Mlt}}\nolimits(Q) \le W`$, then $`(Q, *)`$ is the associative group $`(T, \cdot)`$ or its opposite $`(T^{\mathrm{op}}, \circ)`$ ($`x \circ y = y x`$). In particular, no finite simple nonassociative loop $`Q`$ has $`\mathop{\mathrm{Mlt}}\nolimits(Q)`$ of O'Nan–Scott type `HS` or `SD_2`.

*Proof*:
- **Step 0 (Functional equation)**: Any $`\pi \in W`$ fixing $`1_T`$ normalizes $`(T_L \times T_R)_1 = \mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(T)`$ and either preserves both minimal normal subgroups $`T_L, T_R`$ of $`T_L \times T_R`$ ($`\pi \in \mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(T)`$) or swaps $`T_L \leftrightarrow T_R`$ ($`\pi = \alpha \circ \iota`$, $`\iota(u) = u^{-1}`$, $`\alpha \in \mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(T)`$). Hence for each $`x, y \in T`$, there exist $`\beta_x, \gamma_y \in \mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(T)`$ and $`\varepsilon_x, \delta_y \in \{+1, -1\}`$ such that
  $$x * y = x \, \beta_x(y^{\varepsilon_x}) = \gamma_y(x^{\delta_y}) \, y \qquad \forall x, y \in T. \tag{$`\star`$}$$
- **Step 1 (Every involution of $`T`$ lies in a Klein four-subgroup $`V_4 \le T`$)**: Let $`\mathcal{I} = \{t \in T : t^2 = 1 \neq t\}`$. By the Feit–Thompson Theorem (*Pacific J. Math.* **13** (1963), 775–1029), $`|T|`$ is even, so a Sylow $`2`$-subgroup $`S \in \mathop{\mathrm{Syl}}\nolimits_2(T)`$ satisfies $`|S| \ge 2`$ and $`Z(S) \cap \mathcal{I} \neq \emptyset`$. If $`S`$ had a unique involution, $`S`$ would be cyclic $`C_{2^a}`$ or generalized quaternion $`Q_{2^a}`$ (D. Gorenstein, *Finite Groups*, 1968, Thm. 5.4.10(ii)); cyclic $`S`$ is impossible as a generator acts as $`|T|/2^a`$ odd-sign $`2^a`$-cycles in the regular representation of $`T`$ ($`[T : T \cap A_{|T|}] = 2`$), and $`Q_{2^a}`$ is impossible by the Brauer–Suzuki Theorem (*Proc. Nat. Acad. Sci. USA* **45** (1959), 1757–1759; Gorenstein, Thm. 12.1.1). Thus $`|S \cap \mathcal{I}| \ge 2`$. Given any $`y \in \mathcal{I}`$, embed $`y \in S \in \mathop{\mathrm{Syl}}\nolimits_2(T)`$ and pick $`z \in Z(S) \cap \mathcal{I}`$; choosing $`w := z`$ if $`z \neq y`$, or any $`w \in (S \cap \mathcal{I}) \setminus \{y\}`$ if $`z = y`$, gives $`w \neq y`$ commuting with $`y`$, so $`\{1, y, w, y w\} \cong V_4`$ contains $`y`$.
- **Step 2 (Single-involution classification when $`\delta_y = +1`$)**: Suppose $`y \in \mathcal{I}`$ has $`\delta_y = +1`$, and put $`\gamma := \gamma_y \in \mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(T)`$, $`d := \gamma(y) \in y^{\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(T)} \subseteq \mathcal{I}`$. Since $`y^{\varepsilon_x} = y`$, $`(\star)`$ gives $`\beta_x(y) = x^{-1} \gamma(x) y \in y^{\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(T)} \subseteq \mathcal{I}`$ ($`\forall x \in T`$). Replacing $`x`$ by $`x y`$ yields $`\beta_{x y}(y) = y (x^{-1} \gamma(x) d) y \in \mathcal{I}`$, so $`(q(x) d)^2 = 1`$ for $`q(x) := x^{-1} \gamma(x)`$, i.e. $`d q(x) d = q(x)^{-1}`$ ($`\forall x \in T`$). Using $`q(u v) = (v^{-1} q(u) v) q(v)`$:
  1. $`v^{-1} q(u) v = q(u v) q(v)^{-1}`$, so $`N_1 := \langle q(u) : u \in T \rangle \trianglelefteq T`$.
  2. Applying $`g \mapsto d g d`$ to $`q(u v) = v^{-1} q(u) v q(v)`$ gives $`q(v)^{-1} v^{-1} q(u)^{-1} v = (d v^{-1} d) q(u)^{-1} (d v d) q(v)^{-1}`$, i.e. $`C(v)^{-1} q(u)^{-1} C(v) = q(u)^{-1}`$ ($`\forall u, v \in T`$), where $`C(v) := v q(v) d v^{-1} d = \gamma(v) d v^{-1} d`$.
  Since $`T`$ is nonabelian simple ($`Z(T) = 1`$), either $`N_1 = \{1\}`$ (so $`\gamma_y = \mathrm{id}_T`$) or $`N_1 = T`$ (so $`C(v) \in Z(T) = \{1\}`$, i.e. $`\gamma(v) = d v d`$ for all $`v`$; at $`v = y`$, $`d = d y d \implies d = y`$, so $`\gamma_y = \mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(y)`$).
- **Step 3 ($`V_4`$-factorization eliminates $`\delta_y = -1`$ on $`\mathcal{I}`$)**: Let $`y \in \mathcal{I}`$ be arbitrary, and by Step 1 choose $`V_4 = \{1, y_1, y_2, y_3\} \le T`$ containing $`y`$ ($`y_3 = y_1 y_2 = y_2 y_1`$). Setting $`\phi_i(x) := \gamma_{y_i}(x^{\delta_{y_i}})`$, $`(\star)`$ gives $`\beta_x(y_i) = x^{-1} \phi_i(x) y_i`$ ($`\forall x \in T`$). Since $`\beta_x \in \mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(T)`$, $`\beta_x(y_3) = \beta_x(y_1)\beta_x(y_2) = \beta_x(y_2)\beta_x(y_1)`$ yields (using $`y_2 y_3 = y_1`$ and $`y_1 y_3 = y_2`$):
  $$\phi_3(x) = \phi_1(x) \, y_1 \, x^{-1} \, \phi_2(x) \, y_1 \quad \text{and} \quad \phi_3(x) = \phi_2(x) \, y_2 \, x^{-1} \, \phi_1(x) \, y_2 \qquad \forall x \in T. \tag{$`\ddagger`$}$$
  - *Impossible that $`\delta_{y_1} = \delta_{y_2} = \delta_{y_3} = -1`$*: If all three equal $`-1`$, define $`\alpha_1 := \mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(y_1) \circ \gamma_{y_1},\; \alpha_2 := \gamma_{y_2},\; \alpha_3 := \mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(y_1) \circ \gamma_{y_3} \in \mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(T)`$. Conjugating the first equation of $`(\ddagger)`$ by $`y_1`$ at $`x \leftarrow x^{-1}`$ gives $`\alpha_3(x) = \alpha_1(x) x \alpha_2(x)`$ ($`\forall x \in T`$). Expanding $`\alpha_3(u v) = \alpha_3(u)\alpha_3(v)`$ and cancelling $`\alpha_1(u)`$ on the left and $`\alpha_2(v)`$ on the right gives $`\alpha_1(v) u v \alpha_2(u) = u \alpha_2(u) \alpha_1(v) v`$, i.e.
    $$u^{-1} \alpha_1(v) u = \alpha_2(u) \alpha_1(v) v \alpha_2(u)^{-1} v^{-1} \qquad \forall u, v \in T.$$
    Setting $`z := \alpha_2(u)^{-1}`$ (which ranges over all of $`T`$) and conjugating both sides by $`z`$ shows that $`\Theta_z(v) := \alpha_1(v) v z v^{-1} z^{-1} = (\mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(z u^{-1}) \circ \alpha_1)(v)`$ is an automorphism of $`T`$ for every $`z \in T`$. Expanding $`\Theta_z(v_1 v_2) = \Theta_z(v_1)\Theta_z(v_2)`$ and cancelling $`\alpha_1(v_1)`$ on the left and $`z^{-1}`$ on the right gives $`\alpha_1(v_2) v_1 v_2 z v_2^{-1} v_1^{-1} = v_1 z v_1^{-1} z^{-1} \alpha_1(v_2) v_2 z v_2^{-1}`$. Fixing any $`v_2 \in T \setminus \{1\}`$ and setting $`z = v_2`$ yields $`\alpha_1(v_2) c = c v_2^{-1} \alpha_1(v_2) v_2`$ for all $`c = v_1 v_2 v_1^{-1} \in v_2^T`$, i.e. $`c v_2^{-1} \in C_T(\alpha_1(v_2)) \subsetneq T`$ ($`\forall c \in v_2^T`$). Since $`h(c v_2^{-1})h^{-1} = ((h c h^{-1})v_2^{-1})((h v_2 h^{-1})v_2^{-1})^{-1}`$, $`N := \langle c v_2^{-1} : c \in v_2^T \rangle \trianglelefteq T`$ lies in $`C_T(\alpha_1(v_2)) \subsetneq T`$, forcing $`N = \{1\}`$ and $`v_2 \in Z(T) = \{1\}`$, contradiction.
  - *Propagation of $`+1`$ across $`V_4`$*: Thus at least one $`\delta_{y_i} = +1`$; relabeling $`\{y_1, y_2, y_3\}`$, assume $`\delta_{y_2} = +1`$. By Step 2, $`\phi_2 \in \{\mathrm{id}_T, \mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(y_2)\}`$. Replacing $`(Q, *)`$ if needed by its $`\iota`$-isomorph $`u \odot v := (u^{-1} * v^{-1})^{-1}`$ ($`\iota(u) = u^{-1} \in W_1`$), which preserves $`(\star)`$ on $`(T, \cdot)`$ with $`\widetilde{R}_y(x) = (\iota R_{y^{-1}} \iota)(x) = (\mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(y) \circ \gamma_{y^{-1}})(x^{\delta_{y^{-1}}}) y`$ and thus sends $`\phi_2`$ at $`y_2 = y_2^{-1}`$ to $`\mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(y_2) \circ \phi_2`$, we may assume $`\phi_2 = \mathrm{id}_T`$. Then the first equation of $`(\ddagger)`$ gives $`\phi_3 = \phi_1`$, and the second gives $`\phi_1(x) = x y_2 x^{-1} \phi_1(x) y_2`$, i.e. $`x^{-1} \phi_1(x) \in H_0 := C_T(y_2) \subsetneq T`$ ($`\forall x \in T`$). If $`\delta_{y_1} = +1`$, $`\langle x^{-1} \phi_1(x) : x \in T \rangle \trianglelefteq T`$ lies in $`H_0 \subsetneq T`$, forcing $`\phi_1 = \phi_3 = \mathrm{id}_T`$ ($`\delta_{y_1} = \delta_{y_3} = +1`$). If $`\delta_{y_1} = -1`$, putting $`u = x^{-1}`$ gives $`u \gamma_{y_1}(u) \in H_0`$ ($`\forall u \in T`$); for $`h \in H_0`$ this forces $`\gamma_{y_1}(h) \in H_0`$, so $`\gamma_{y_1}(H_0) = H_0`$, and then for all $`u \in T, h \in H_0`$, $`h u \gamma_{y_1}(h)\gamma_{y_1}(u) \in H_0 \implies u \gamma_{y_1}(h) u^{-1} = (u \gamma_{y_1}(h)\gamma_{y_1}(u))(u \gamma_{y_1}(u))^{-1} \in H_0`$, whence $`1 \neq H_0 \trianglelefteq T`$, contradiction.
  Hence **every** $`y \in \mathcal{I}`$ satisfies $`\delta_y = +1`$ and $`\gamma_y \in \{\mathrm{id}_T, \mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(y)\}`$ (and by left-right symmetry, every $`x \in \mathcal{I}`$ satisfies $`\varepsilon_x = +1`$ and $`\beta_x \in \{\mathrm{id}_T, \mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(x)\}`$).
- **Step 4 (Global collapse to $`T`$ or $`T^{\mathrm{op}}`$)**: Fix a $`T`$-conjugacy class $`\mathcal{C} \subseteq \mathcal{I}`$ ($`\langle \mathcal{C} \rangle = T`$). Partition $`\mathcal{C} = \mathcal{C}_1 \sqcup \mathcal{C}_2`$ into $`\{y \in \mathcal{C} : R_y(x) = x y\}`$ and $`\{y \in \mathcal{C} : R_y(x) = y x\}`$. For $`y_1 \in \mathcal{C}_1`$, $`\beta_x(y_1) = y_1`$; for $`y_2 \in \mathcal{C}_2`$, $`\beta_x(y_2) = x^{-1} y_2 x`$. If both $`\mathcal{C}_1, \mathcal{C}_2 \neq \emptyset`$, choosing $`x \in T`$ with $`x^{-1} y_2 x = y_1`$ gives $`\beta_x(y_2) = y_1 = \beta_x(y_1)`$ ($`y_1 \neq y_2`$), contradicting injectivity of $`\beta_x`$. Replacing $`(Q, *)`$ by its $`\iota`$-isomorph $`u \odot v = (u^{-1} * v^{-1})^{-1}`$ ($`\iota \in W_1`$) if needed, we have $`\mathcal{C} = \mathcal{C}_1`$, so $`\beta_x|_{\mathcal{C}} = \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}}`$ and $`\langle \mathcal{C} \rangle = T \implies \beta_x = \mathrm{id}_T`$ ($`\forall x \in T`$). Since every $`x \in \mathcal{C}`$ has $`\varepsilon_x = +1`$, $`x y = L_x(y) = \gamma_y(x) y`$ ($`\forall x \in \mathcal{C}, y \in T`$), forcing $`\gamma_y = \mathrm{id}_T`$ ($`\forall y \in T`$). Thus $`(\star)`$ reduces to $`x y^{\varepsilon_x} = x^{\delta_y} y`$ ($`\forall x, y \in T`$). If some $`x_0 \in T`$ had $`\varepsilon_{x_0} = -1`$, then for every $`y \in T`$, either $`\delta_y = +1`$ ($`y^2 = 1`$) or $`\delta_y = -1`$ ($`y^2 = x_0^2`$), so $`y^2 \in \{1, x_0^2\}`$ ($`\forall y \in T`$); picking any element $`g \in T`$ of odd order $`\ge 3`$ (so $`g^2 \neq 1`$), $`(h g h^{-1})^2 = h g^2 h^{-1} \neq 1`$ must equal $`x_0^2 = g^2`$ for all $`h \in T`$, forcing $`1 \neq g^2 \in Z(T) = \{1\}`$, contradiction. Hence $`\varepsilon_x = +1`$ ($`\forall x \in T`$), so the $`\iota`$-isomorph equals $`(T, \cdot)`$, whence the original loop $`(Q, *)`$ is $`(T, \cdot)`$ or $`(T^{\mathrm{op}}, \circ)`$. $`\square`$

#### Corollary 3.2 (Unconditional Almost-Simple Multiplication Group Theorem)
For every finite simple nonassociative left Bol loop $`Q`$ (in particular, for every finite simple proper left Bol loop $`Q`$), $`\mathop{\mathrm{Mlt}}\nolimits(Q)`$ is an **Almost Simple (`AS`)** primitive permutation group ($`T \trianglelefteq \mathop{\mathrm{Mlt}}\nolimits(Q) \le \mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(T)`$ for a unique nonabelian finite simple group $`T`$). $`\square`$

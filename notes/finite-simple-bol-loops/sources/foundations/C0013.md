### 1. Odd-$`p`$ Affine-Subspace Fiber Rigidity and Undeformed Non-Simplicity

#### Theorem 1.1 (Affine-Subspace Fiber Rigidity for Odd $`p`$ and Normal Subloops at $`H_1 = H`$)
Let $`p \ge 3`$ be an odd prime, $`(G, H, K)`$ a finite faithful left Bol folder, $`U`$ a finite elementary abelian $`\mathbb{F}_p G`$-module, and $`(\mathcal{G}, \mathcal{H}, \mathcal{K})`$ a left Bol folder in $`\mathcal{G} = U \rtimes G`$ ($`\pi : \mathcal{G} \twoheadrightarrow G`$ projection) with $`\pi(\mathcal{K}) = K`$ and $`H \le H_1 := \pi(\mathcal{H}) \le G`$. Write $`\mathcal{K} = \bigsqcup_{k \in K} (F_k, k)`$ with $`0_U \in W_1 := F_{1_G}`$.
1. **Linearity of $`W_1 = F_{1_G}`$**: Inversion $`\mathcal{K}^{-1} = \mathcal{K}`$ gives $`-W_1 = W_1`$. In $`U \rtimes G`$, $`(u_1, a)(u_2, b)(u_1, a) = ((I + a b)u_1 + a u_2, a b a)`$; setting $`a = b = 1_G`$ gives $`2 u_1 + u_2 \in W_1`$ for all $`u_1, u_2 \in W_1`$. Taking $`u_2 = 0`$ gives $`2 W_1 \subseteq W_1`$; because $`p`$ is odd, $`2 \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times`$ has finite multiplicative order $`e \mid (p - 1)`$, so $`2^{-1} = 2^{e-1}`$ satisfies $`2^{-1} W_1 \subseteq W_1`$. Thus $`x + y = 2(2^{-1} x) + y \in W_1`$ ($`\forall x, y \in W_1`$) and $`\mathbb{Z} W_1 = \mathbb{F}_p W_1 = W_1`$: **$`W_1`$ is an $`\mathbb{F}_p`$-linear subspace of $`U`$** *(explaining why `N07E` Theorem 5.2 works strictly at $`p = 2`$, where $`2 u_1 + u_2 = u_2`$ leaves $`F_{1_G} = S^*`$ unconstrained)*.
2. **Uniform affine fibers when $`|K|`$ is odd**: Assume $`|K|`$ is odd, so every $`k \in K`$ has odd order $`2m - 1`$ in $`G`$ (`C0001` Theorem 1.3; $`\langle L_x \rangle`$ acts semiregularly on $`Q`$) with unique square root $`k^{1/2} := k^m \in \langle k \rangle \subseteq K`$. Fix $`s_0 \in F_{k^{1/2}}`$, so $`(s_0, k^{1/2})^{-1} = (-k^{-1/2} s_0, k^{-1/2}) \in \mathcal{K}`$, and set $`\psi_0(k) := (I + k^{1/2}) s_0 \in F_k`$. For every $`w \in W_1`$, $`(s_0, k^{1/2})(w, 1_G)(s_0, k^{1/2}) = (\psi_0(k) + k^{1/2} w, k) \in \mathcal{K}`$; conversely, for every $`u \in F_k`$, $`(-k^{-1/2} s_0, k^{-1/2})(u, k)(-k^{-1/2} s_0, k^{-1/2}) = (k^{-1/2}(u - \psi_0(k)), 1_G) \in (W_1, 1_G)`$. Thus **$`F_k = \psi_0(k) + k^{1/2} W_1`$** ($`|F_k| = |W_1|`$) for every $`k \in K`$, $`|\mathcal{Q}| = |W_1| \cdot |K|`$, and with $`T_1 := \mathcal{H} \cap U`$ and $`[H_1 : H] = p^d`$, counting $`[\mathcal{G} : \mathcal{H}] = |\mathcal{K}|`$ gives
   $$\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} U - \dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} T_1 - d = \dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} W_1.$$
3. **Non-simplicity when $`H_1 = H`$, $`|K| > 1`$, and $`|W_1| > 1`$**: Projection induces a surjective loop homomorphism $`\mathcal Q\to Q`$: the representative of $`ab\mathcal H`$ projects to the unique member of $`K`$ in $`\pi(a)\pi(b)H`$. Its kernel is $`\mathcal K\cap U=(W_1,1_G)=\mathcal K\cap U\mathcal H`$. Since the target has more than one element, this kernel is proper, and it is nontrivial when $`|W_1|>1`$. This argument does not require the lifted folder to be faithful. $`\square`$

The condition $`|K|>1`$ is necessary for properness: for $`G=H=K=1`$ and $`U=\mathbb F_p`$, the lifted folder $`(U,\{0\},U)`$ has $`H_1=H`$ and $`W_1=U`$, which is the entire loop.

**Strengthening for faithful generating lifts (scoped review below).** Suppose in addition that the lifted folder is faithful and generating, $`|K|>1`$, and $`U\ne 0`$. If $`H_1=H`$, the lifted loop is not simple, with no restriction on the parity of $`p`$. Indeed, the projection homomorphism above has a proper kernel. If that kernel were trivial, projection would be a loop isomorphism, hence induce an isomorphism of the left multiplication groups. Faithfulness and generation identify those groups with $`U\rtimes G`$ and $`G`$; the induced map is the group projection because it sends each lifted translation to its base translation. Its nontrivial kernel $`U`$ gives a contradiction. Thus any simple lift in this setting has $`H_1>H`$. This strengthening has the scoped review recorded below.

#### Enlarged-stabilizer constraints

Let (G,H,K) be a finite faithful generating Bol folder, |K|>1 odd; U a finite F_p G-module for odd p; (U ⋊ G, H~, K~) a finite faithful generating Bol folder, π(K~)=K, H ≤ H1=π(H~). Put T=H~∩U, W=K~∩U, w=dim W, t=dim T. The §1 sandwich argument gives W a vector subspace and F_k=a_k+k^(1/2)W. Put [H1:H]=p^d as follows: counting gives [H1:H]=p^(dim U-t-w). Thus dim U=t+w+d, and W∩T=0 by transversality.

If U≠0 and the lifted loop is simple, d>0: if d=0, projection is a surjective loop homomorphism to the nontrivial base. Its kernel is proper; if it is trivial, it is a loop isomorphism, so faithfulness and generation force the ambient group projection to be an isomorphism, contradicting kernel U.

For the Q_{p,q} base, d is one of 1,p,p+1. Write N=V×V and H∩N=W_V×0, W_V=ker Tr. The image of H in G/N has index q. Since [H1:H] is a p-power with p≠q, the image of H1 in G/N equals that of H: indeed its index over the image of H divides both [H1:H] and q. Hence H1≤NH. Its intersection with N is H-invariant and contains W_V×0. The H-module N/(W_V×0) is F_p ⊕ V_2, with the second C_q acting trivially on F_p and irreducibly on V_2. The averaging idempotent for C_q separates invariant subspaces, so the only possibilities are 0, F_p, V_2, and F_p⊕V_2. Therefore d∈{0,1,p,p+1}, and simplicity with U≠0 excludes d=0. This corrects the reason for the lattice: prime index alone does not force all overgroups H1 to lie in NH; the p-power index does.

For a further exact affine-tiling constraint, T is H1-invariant. The subgroup H~/T determines a 1-cocycle c:H1→U/T by H~/T={(c(h),h):h∈H1}; c(hj)=c(h)+h c(j). This is a genuine subgroup cocycle, unlike the unproved assertion that a Bol section is a group cocycle. Fix k0∈K and let B=K∩k0H1; |B|=[H1:H]=p^d, because K is a transversal to H and H≤H1. For k=k0 h in B define
 A_k = k0^(-1)F_k − c(h) ⊆ U/T,
where F_k is first projected to U/T after applying k0^(-1). Then the A_k are pairwise disjoint and cover U/T: equality of lifted left cosets (u,k)H~ and (v,k0)H~ holds exactly when k0^(-1)(u−v)≡c(h) mod T. Since each F_k has p^w members and K~ is a transversal, every A_k has p^w elements and is an affine w-dimensional subspace of U/T. Thus p^d affine w-flats tile the (w+d)-dimensional space U/T separately above each coset k0H1. Their directions are (k0^(-1)k^(1/2)W + T)/T. This condition is necessary, not sufficient for all conjugate-transversal and Bol conditions.

#### $`d=1`$ and central-fiber consequences

Use the notation and hypotheses of the preceding enlarged-stabilizer constraints, without assuming an inverting automorphism. Assume d=1. In U/T, the p sets A_k for k∈K∩H1 are pairwise disjoint affine hyperplanes and cover U/T. Any two affine hyperplanes over a field whose direction subspaces differ must meet. Hence they are parallel. Taking k=1 as reference, their direction subspaces are all (W+T)/T, giving k^(1/2)W+T=W+T for every k∈K∩H1. As K∩H1 is closed under odd-order square roots and squaring is a bijection on each cyclic subgroup, this equivalently yields kW+T=W+T for all k∈K∩H1. Since T is H1-invariant, W+T is invariant under the subgroup generated by K∩H1.

For the Q_{p,q} base with d=1, H1=(V×0)H and K∩H1 consists exactly of the p elements (φ^j,φ^(-j)), 0≤j<p, in the two X-factors. Indeed for k_x=(x,x^(-1)), x=(v,a,j), H1 requires a=0 and the second V-coordinate of x^(-1) to vanish, forcing v=0. Consequently W+T is stable under this order-p diagonal-action subgroup. This is a necessary constraint for all modules and does not settle whether such tilings and Bol sections exist; nor does it by itself force exact factorization.

Additional general obstruction: any nonzero z∈W∩U^G generates a central order-p subgroup Z of U inside the lifted loop. Since z is central in U⋊G and p is odd, sandwich closure gives K~+Z=K~ (using 2 invertible modulo p). Therefore H~Z/Z and K~/Z form a quotient Bol folder and projection is a loop homomorphism with kernel Z. Because π(K~)=K has |K|>1, Z is proper in the lifted loop. Thus simple lifts require W∩U^G=0. The stronger result below excludes trivial direct summands for the stated single-factor scope, including oblique W.


#### One-dimensional trivial-module obstruction

For the Q_{p,q} base, odd p, let U=F_p with trivial G-action. There is no faithful generating lift with π(K~)=K whose loop is simple, for any projected stabilizer H1≥H. This is a proof, subject to independent review.

By affine rigidity W is 0 or U. If W=U, central sandwich closure makes K~ invariant under U, so quotienting by U gives a proper nontrivial normal subloop, since |K|>1. Thus in the simple case W=0 and K~={(f(x), k_x):x∈X} where k_x=(x,x^-1), f(1)=0. Sandwich closure gives f(xyx)=2f(x)+f(y). By induction f(x^n)=n f(x) for integers n; in particular f vanishes on all elements of order q. With X=F_{p^p}⋊(C_q⋊<φ>), every (v,a,0) with a≠0 has order q. Further, (0,a,0)(w,-2a,0)(0,a,0)=(ζ^a w,0,0), so f vanishes on V as well. Put c=f(0,0,1), subtract c j from f; thereafter f vanishes on φ^j and all elements with j=0.

For j≠0, (0,a,0)φ^j(0,a,0)=(0,(1+p^j)a,j). Since p has odd order p modulo q, p^j≠−1, so 1+p^j is nonzero mod q. Hence f(0,b,j)=0 for all b. For every v,b,j, sandwiching (0,b,j) by (w,0,0) yields ((1+ζ^b φ^j)w,b,j). The operator ζ^b φ^j has odd order dividing pq, so it has no eigenvalue −1 in odd characteristic; 1+ζ^b φ^j is invertible. Thus f(v,b,j)=0 for all v,b,j. Undoing the subtraction yields f(v,b,j)=c j.

The map G→F_p given by the first X-factor's j-coordinate is a homomorphism: the second coordinate has the opposite j-coordinate in G={(x,y):xy∈X'}, with X'=V⋊C_q. Therefore K~ lies in the graph of this homomorphism G→U, a proper subgroup of U×G. This contradicts generating U×G. No computation or inference for higher-dimensional trivial summands is used.



#### Elimination of trivial summands

Scoped independent review is recorded below. Let p≥3, q divide (p^p−1)/(p−1), and (G,H,K) be the faithful folder of Q_{p,q}. Let U=Z⊕U' as an F_p[G/O_p(G)]-module, where Z is a nonzero trivial module of arbitrary finite dimension and U' is a direct sum, with arbitrary multiplicities, of M_{r,0} and M_{0,r}. There is no faithful generating simple Bol lift (U⋊G,H~,K~) with π(K~)=K and π(H~)≥H. No assumption of τ or of a decomposition of W along G-constituents is needed.

First, sandwiching differences proves Δ(C_q)-invariance of W. Write F_k=a_k+k^(1/2)W. Fix a∈F_k and u,v∈F_l. Both lifts (a,k)(u,l)(a,k) and (a,k)(v,l)(a,k) lie in F_{klk}; their difference is k(u−v). Hence k l^(1/2)W⊆(klk)^(1/2)W. Dimensions are equal, so equality holds. Thus D_{x,y}W=W for D=k_{xyx}^(−1/2) k_x k_y^(1/2). Put z=xyx and c=z^(−1/2)x y^(1/2). The second component of D is z^(1/2)x^(−1)y^(−1/2)=z^(−1/2)x y^(1/2)=c, using x^(−1)=z^(−1)xy. Therefore D=(c,c). Take x=(0,a,0), a≠0, y=(0,0,2). In C_q⋊<φ>, c=((1−p)/(1+p))a in the C_q coordinate. The denominators and numerator are nonzero modulo q because ord_q(p)=p is odd and greater than 1. Hence c generates C_q and Δ(C_q) stabilizes W. This argument uses no claim that (0,k) lies in K~ and no normalization of affine offsets.

Second, Δ(C_q) fixes precisely Z on U, because each single-factor constituent restricts to a nontrivial irreducible C_q-module. Averaging over Δ(C_q), whose order is coprime to p, gives an idempotent E:U→Z. Invariance of W shows E(W)⊂W∩Z. Any nonzero z∈W∩Z is a central order-p subgroup inside K~. Sandwich closure and invertibility of 2 imply K~+<z>=K~, so quotienting by <z> gives a proper nontrivial normal subloop (proper because the base K has more than one element). Simplicity therefore forces W∩Z=0, and thus E(W)=0. Equivalently every fiber's Z-coordinate is a singleton: the projection W→Z vanishes. This conclusion does not assume W is a sum of isotypic intersections.

Finally, choose nonzero λ:Z→F_p. Each F_k has a unique λ-value, defining f(x) for k=k_x=(x,x^-1). Sandwich closure gives f(xyx)=2f(x)+f(y). The one-dimensional argument recorded in C0013 shows f(v,a,j)=c j. The first-factor j-coordinate is a group homomorphism G→F_p, so the image of K~ under U⋊G→F_p×G lies in the graph of that homomorphism. This graph is proper, contradicting generation of U⋊G. Thus Z must vanish.

Limit: modules on which diagonal C_q has fixed vectors (for instance some mixed constituents) are not covered. No conclusion about their existence follows. No classification or exact-factorization absorption follows from this obstruction.

     * On $`U = M_{r,0} \oplus M_{0,r}`$, the $`\Delta(C_q)`$-invariant $`\tilde{\tau}`$-stable nonzero proper subspace with $`\langle \mathcal{K} \rangle = \mathcal{G}`$ is $`W_1 = U_- := \{(m, -m) : m \in M_r\}`$ ($`\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} W_1 = p`$), and $`\tilde{\tau}(\psi_0(k), k) = (\psi_0(k)^{-1}\text{-fiber})`$ forces $`\psi_0(k) \in k^{1/2} U_- = k^{1/2} W_1`$, so we may set **$`\psi_0 \equiv 0`$** (`F_k = k^{1/2} W_1`).

2. **Subgroup lattice $`H \le H_1 \lneq G`$ and complete resolution of $`d \in \{0, 1, p, p+1\}`$**:
   Under $`1 \times C_q \le \overline{H}`$, $`(V \times V)/(W \times 0) \cong \mathbb{F}_p \oplus M_{0,1}`$, and $`[G : (V \times V)H] = q`$ is prime, so any $`H \le H_1 \lneq G`$ satisfies $`[H_1 : H] = p^d`$ with **$`d \in \{0, 1, p, p+1\}`$**: $`H_1^{(0)} = H`$, $`H_1^{(1)} = (V \times 0)H`$, $`H_1^{(p)} = (0 \times V)H`$, $`H_1^{(p+1)} = (V \times V)H`$. On $`U = M_{r,0} \oplus M_{0,r}`$ ($`\dim U = 2p`$, $`\dim W_1 = p`$), Theorem 1.1(2) gives $`\dim T_1 + d = p`$:
   - **$`d = 0`$**: Non-simple by Theorem 1.1(3); **$`d = p + 1`$**: impossible since $`\dim T_1 = -1 < 0`$.
   - **$`d = p`$ ($`T_1 = 0`$, $`H_1 = H_1^{(p)} = (W \times V) \rtimes \overline{H}`$)**: Up to $`U`$-coboundaries ($`H^1(1 \times C_q, M_{0,r}) = 0`$), any cocycle $`\psi \in Z^1(H_1^{(p)}, U)`$ restricts on $`0 \times V`$ to $`v_2 \mapsto (0, c_2 v_2)`$ ($`c_2 \in \mathbb{F}_p`$, with $`c_2 = 0`$ if $`[r] \neq [1]`$) and on $`W \times 0`$ to $`w \mapsto (\psi_1(w), 0)`$. Keeping $`W_1 = U_- = \{(m, -m)\}`$, define the normal subgroup
     $$D_{c_2} := \bigl\{\bigl((c_2 v_1, c_2 v_2),\; (v_1, v_2)\bigr) : v_1, v_2 \in V\bigr\} \trianglelefteq \mathcal{G} \qquad (|D_{c_2}| = p^{2p}).$$
     Note $`(w, -w) \in K \cap H_1^{(p)}`$ for every $`w \in W`$, with $`F_{(w, -w)} = (w, -w)^{1/2} W_1 = W_1`$ (since $`V \times V`$ acts trivially on $`U`$). If $`\exists w \in W \setminus \{0\}`$ with $`\psi_1(w) = -c_2 w`$, then $`\psi(w, -w) = (-c_2 w, -c_2(-w)) = (-c_2 w, c_2 w) \in W_1 = F_{(w, -w)}`$, giving $`1 \neq (\psi(w, -w), (w, -w)) \in \mathcal{H} \cap \mathcal{K}`$ and **violating transversality**! Otherwise $`\psi_1(w) \neq -c_2 w`$ for all $`w \in W \setminus \{0\}`$, so $`\mathcal{H} \cap D_{c_2} = \{((0, c_2 v_2), (0, v_2)) : v_2 \in V\}`$ has order $`p^p`$, forcing $`[\mathcal{G} : \mathcal{H} D_{c_2}] = p^{p+1} q = |\mathcal{K} D_{c_2} / D_{c_2}|`$ and **producing a proper nontrivial normal subloop $`\mathcal{K} \cap D_{c_2} \trianglelefteq \mathcal{Q}`$ of order $`p^p`$** (`C0001` Theorem 2.2(3)).
   - **$`d = 1`$ ($`\dim T_1 = p - 1`$, $`H_1 = H_1^{(1)} = (V \times 0) \rtimes \overline{H}`$)**: Since $`\overline{H}`$ acts on $`M_{0,r}`$ irreducibly of dimension $`p > p - 1`$ and on $`M_{r,0}`$ uniserially with unique $`(p - 1)`$-dimensional submodule $`W_r := \ker(\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{F}_{p^p}/\mathbb{F}_p})`$, we have $`T_1 = W_r \oplus 0`$. Identifying $`W_1 = \{(m, -m) : m \in M_r\}`$ with the twisted section of $`M_r`$ in $`\widetilde{X} := (M_r \oplus V) \rtimes (C_q \rtimes \langle \phi \rangle)`$, the group isomorphism $`(M_{r,0} \oplus M_{0,r}) \rtimes G \xrightarrow{\sim} \widehat{G}_{\widetilde{X}}`$ maps $`\mathcal{K}`$ onto $`\{(\tilde{x}, \tilde{x}^{-1}) : \tilde{x} \in \widetilde{X}\}`$ and $`\mathcal{H}`$ onto $`(\widetilde{Y}_0 \times \widetilde{Y}_1) \cap \widehat{G}_{\widetilde{X}}`$. If $`[r] = [1]`$ ($`M_r \cong V`$), $`\widetilde{Y}_0 \cap (M_r \oplus V)`$ contains a $`C_q`$-invariant diagonal copy of $`V`$, yielding a normal subloop of order $`p^p`$; if $`[r] \neq [1]`$ and the cocycle couples $`\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_{M_r}`$ and $`\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits_V`$ nontrivially, $`\mathcal{Q} \cong Q_{p,q;\{[1],[r]\}}`$ is simple and absorbed into Theorem 3.1 below (and inductively for $`k - 1`$ distinct non-$`[1]`$ pairs). $`\square`$

#### Theorem 3.1 (Simplicity, Nuclei, Isomorphism Classes, and $`\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(Q_{p,q;R})`$)
Let $`p \ge 2`$ be a prime, $`q`$ a prime dividing $`\frac{p^p - 1}{p - 1}`$ ($`\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_q(p) = p`$), $`\mathcal{O} = (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times / \langle p \rangle \cong C_{(q-1)/p}`$, and $`R = \{[r_1], \dots, [r_k]\} \subseteq \mathcal{O}`$ ($`1 \le k \le \frac{q-1}{p}`$). Define $`\widetilde{X}_R = M_R \rtimes (C_q \rtimes \langle \phi \rangle)`$ ($`|\widetilde{X}_R| = p^{k p + 1} q`$), $`\widetilde{Y}_1 = C_q \rtimes \langle \phi \rangle`$ ($`|\widetilde{Y}_1| = p q`$), and $`\widetilde{Y}_0 = \{(m_1, \dots, m_k; 0, j) : \sum_{\ell=1}^k \mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits(m_\ell) = j\}`$ ($`|\widetilde{Y}_0| = p^{k p}`$).
1. **Exact factorization, solvability of $`\mathop{\mathrm{LMlt}}\nolimits`$, properness, and nuclei**:
   Because $`\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits(z^{p^i}) = \mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits(z)`$ for all $`z \in \mathbb{F}_{p^p}`$, $`\widetilde{Y}_0`$ is closed under multiplication and inversion in $`\widetilde{X}_R`$, with $`\widetilde{Y}_0 \cap \widetilde{Y}_1 = \{1\}`$ (`|\widetilde{Y}_0||\widetilde{Y}_1| = p^{k p + 1} q = |\widetilde{X}_R|`) and $`\widetilde{X}_R' = M_R \rtimes C_q`$, so $`\widetilde{X}_R' \widetilde{Y}_0 = \widetilde{X}_R' \widetilde{Y}_1 = \widetilde{X}_R`$. The $`k`$ summands $`M_{r_1}, \dots, M_{r_k}`$ are pairwise non-isomorphic irreducible $`\mathbb{F}_p[C_q]`$-modules (`[r_\ell] \neq [r_{\ell'}]`), and $`\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits : M_{r_\ell} \to \mathbb{F}_p`$ is surjective on every summand, so $`\widetilde{W} := \widetilde{Y}_0 \cap M_R = \{\sum_{\ell=1}^k \mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits(m_\ell) = 0\}`$ contains no nonzero $`C_q`$-invariant subspace; hence $`\mathop{\mathrm{Core}}\nolimits_{\widetilde{X}_R}(\widetilde{Y}_0) = \mathop{\mathrm{Core}}\nolimits_{\widetilde{X}_R}(\widetilde{Y}_1) = \{1\}`$ and $`Z(\widetilde{X}_R) = \{1\}`$. By `C0002` Proposition 1.1 and `C0012` Theorem 1.2, $`Q_{p,q;R} = Q_{(\widetilde{X}_R, \widetilde{Y}_0, \widetilde{Y}_1)}`$ is a proper left Bol **$`G`$-loop** of order $`p^{k p + 1} q`$ with faithful folder $`(\widehat{G}_R, \widehat{H}_R, S_R)`$ where $`\widehat{G}_R = \{(x_1, x_2) \in \widetilde{X}_R^2 : x_1 x_2 \in \widetilde{X}_R'\}`$ is **solvable** of order $`|\widetilde{X}_R||\widetilde{X}_R'| = p^{2 k p + 1} q^2`$ and $`\widehat{H}_R = \widehat{G}_R \cap (\widetilde{Y}_0 \times \widetilde{Y}_1)`$ has order $`p^{k p} q`$. Furthermore, $`\gcd(q, p - 1) = 1`$ implies $`\zeta^{r_1 a} \notin \mathbb{F}_p^\times`$ for $`a \not\equiv 0 \pmod q`$, so $`N_{\widetilde{X}_R}(\widetilde{Y}_0) \cap \widetilde{Y}_1 = \langle \phi \rangle \cong C_p`$, giving **$`N_\rho(Q_{p,q;R}) \cong C_p`$** (`C0002` Proposition 1.1(4)).
2. **Simplicity**:
   By `C0003` Theorem 2.1(2) (`C0001` Theorem 2.2(3) combined with $`\sigma(x_1, x_2) = (x_2, x_1)`$ inverting $`S_R`$), a nontrivial proper normal subloop of $`Q_{p,q;R}`$ exists iff there exists $`1 \neq N \trianglelefteq \widehat{G}_R`$ with $`\sigma(N) = N`$, $`\widehat{H}_R N \lneq \widehat{G}_R`$, and $`|S_R N / N| = [\widehat{G}_R : \widehat{H}_R N]`$. Under $`C_q \times C_q \le \widehat{G}_R`$, $`M_R \times M_R = \bigoplus_{\ell=1}^k (M_{r_\ell} \times 0) \oplus \bigoplus_{\ell=1}^k (0 \times M_{r_\ell})`$ is a direct sum of $`2k`$ **pairwise non-isomorphic** irreducible $`\mathbb{F}_p[C_q \times C_q]`$-modules! Because $`C_{\widehat{G}_R}(M_R \times M_R) = M_R \times M_R`$ and $`\sigma(N) = N`$, $`N \cap (M_R \times M_R)`$ must equal $`M_I \times M_I`$ for some nonempty subset $`I \subseteq \{1, \dots, k\}`$ ($`M_I := \bigoplus_{\ell \in I} M_{r_\ell}`$):
   - *Case $`I = \{1, \dots, k\}`$*: Either $`N`$ projects nontrivially to $`C_q \times C_q`$ — whence $`\sigma(N) = N`$ puts all of $`C_q \times C_q`$ into $`N/(M_R \times M_R)`$, forcing $`\widehat{H}_R N = \widehat{G}_R`$ — or $`N = M_R \times M_R`$, where $`[\widehat{G}_R : \widehat{H}_R N] = q < p q = |S_R N / N|`$.
   - *Case $`1 \le |I| < k`$*: If $`N > M_I \times M_I`$, $`\sigma(N) = N`$ puts $`C_q \times C_q`$ into $`N/(M_I \times M_I)`$, forcing $`[N, M_R \times M_R] = M_R \times M_R \le N`$, contradicting $`I \neq \{1, \dots, k\}`$. Hence $`N = M_I \times M_I`$. Because $`|I| \ge 1`$, $`\sum_{\ell \in I} \mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits(m_\ell) : M_I \to \mathbb{F}_p`$ is surjective, so $`\widehat{H}_R \cap N = (\widetilde{W} \cap M_I) \times 0`$ has order $`p^{|I|p - 1}`$, giving $`[\widehat{G}_R : \widehat{H}_R N] = p^{(k - |I|)p} q`$, whereas $`|S_R N / N| = [\widetilde{X}_R : M_I] = p^{(k - |I|)p + 1} q = p [\widehat{G}_R : \widehat{H}_R N] > [\widehat{G}_R : \widehat{H}_R N]`$.
   Hence **no** such $`N`$ exists, and **$`Q_{p,q;R}`$ is simple** (`N_\lambda = N_\mu = \{1\}` by `C0001` Theorem 1.5(1)).
3. **Isomorphism classes and exact automorphism group $`\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(Q_{p,q;R})`$**:
   In $`\widehat{G}_R`$, $`\widetilde{X}_R' \times \widetilde{X}_R' = \widehat{G}_R'`$ is characteristic, and its two minimal normal $`C_q`$-factor closures $`N_1 := \widetilde{X}_R' \times \{1\}`$ and $`N_2 := \{1\} \times \widetilde{X}_R'`$ are the unique normal subgroups of $`\widehat{G}_R`$ of structure $`M_R \rtimes C_q`$ ($`O_p(\widehat{G}_R) = M_R \times M_R`$, and the only two eigen-axes of $`C_p`$ on $`(C_q \times C_q)`$ when $`p \ge 3`$ — or distinguished on $`\widehat{H}_R`$ for **all** $`p \ge 2`$ because $`\widehat{H}_R \cap (M_R \times \{0\}) = \widetilde{W} \times \{0\}`$ has $`\mathbb{F}_p`$-dimension $`k p - 1 \ge 1`$, whereas $`\widehat{H}_R \cap (\{0\} \times M_R) = \{0\}`$!). Thus **no** folder isomorphism $`\Phi : (\widehat{G}_R, \widehat{H}_R, S_R) \xrightarrow{\sim} (\widehat{G}_{R'}, \widehat{H}_{R'}, S_{R'})`$ can swap the two factors; every such $`\Phi`$ preserves $`N_1`$ and $`N_2`$, inducing $`\alpha_1, \alpha_2 \in \mathop{\mathrm{Iso}}\nolimits(\widetilde{X}_R, \widetilde{X}_{R'})`$ (via $`\widetilde{X}_R \cong \widehat{G}_R / N_2`$ and $`\widetilde{X}_R \cong \widehat{G}_R / N_1`$) with $`\Phi(S_R) = S_{R'} \implies \alpha_1 = \alpha_2 =: \alpha`$, $`\alpha(\widetilde{Y}_0) = \widetilde{Y}_0'`$, and $`\alpha(\widetilde{Y}_1) = \widetilde{Y}_1'`$.
   Now $`\alpha(M_R) = M_{R'}`$ ($`O_p(\widetilde{X}_R) = M_R`$) and $`\alpha(\widetilde{Y}_1) = \widetilde{Y}_1' = C_q \rtimes \langle \phi \rangle`$. Thus $`\alpha(\alpha_0) = \alpha_0^s`$ for a unique $`s \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times`$ ($`\alpha_0`$ generating $`C_q`$). Comparing $`C_q`$-eigencharacters on $`M_R`$ vs $`M_{R'}`$ shows $`R' = [s] \cdot R`$ in $`\mathcal{O}`$ *(and when $`p \ge 3`$, even as abstract groups $`\widehat{G}_R \cong \widehat{G}_{R'} \iff R' = [s] \cdot R`$ since $`(p, p^{-1})`$ distinguishes the two $`C_q`$ axes of $`L_0`$)*. Conversely, for $`R' = R`$ ($`\alpha \in \mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(Q_{p,q;R})`$):
   - $`[s] \in \mathop{\mathrm{Stab}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{O}}(R)`$ (giving $`p \cdot |\mathop{\mathrm{Stab}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{O}}(R)|`$ choices of $`s \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times`$, since $`\ker((\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{O}) = \langle p \rangle`$ has order $`p`$).
   - Because $`\alpha(\phi) \in \widetilde{Y}_1`$, write $`\alpha(\phi) = (0; b, i_\phi)`$ ($`b \in \mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}, i_\phi \in \mathbb{F}_p`$).    - For each $`\ell \in \{1, \dots, k\}`$, $`[s r_\ell] = [r_{\pi(\ell)}]`$ determines a unique index $`\pi(\ell)`$ and a unique Frobenius exponent $`t_\ell \in \{0, \dots, p - 1\}`$ with $`r_{\pi(\ell)} p^{t_\ell} \equiv s r_\ell \pmod q`$.    Conversely, every such $`s`$ defines a valid automorphism of $`(\widetilde{X}_R, \widetilde{Y}_0, \widetilde{Y}_1)`$! Hence **$`|\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(Q_{p,q;R})| = p \cdot |\mathop{\mathrm{Stab}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{O}}(R)|`$** (`code/verify_q_pqr_family.py`). $`\square`$

### 2. Prime-Power Order Rank-2 Theorem

Let $`(Q, *)`$ be a finite loop of prime-power order $`|Q| = p^a`$ ($`p`$ prime, $`a \ge 1`$) with $`M = \mathop{\mathrm{Mlt}}\nolimits(Q)`$ acting primitively of `AS` type on $`Q`$, and let $`T = \mathop{\mathrm{Soc}}\nolimits(M)`$ ($`T \trianglelefteq M \le \mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(T)`$). Since $`1 \neq T \trianglelefteq M`$ and $`M`$ is primitive, $`T`$ acts transitively on $`Q`$, so $`[T : T_{1_Q}] = p^a`$. By Guralnick's classification of subgroups of prime-power index in finite nonabelian simple groups (R. M. Guralnick, *J. Algebra* **81**(2), 1983, 304–311, Theorem 1 — relies on `CFSG`), $`(T, T_{1_Q}, p^a)`$ is one of:
1. $`T = A_n`$, $`T_{1_Q} \cong A_{n-1}`$, $`n = p^a`$ ($`2`$-transitive, $`\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits(M) = 2`$);
2. $`T = \mathop{\mathrm{PSL}}\nolimits_n(q)`$ ($`n \ge 2`$ prime, $`(n, q) \notin \{(2, 2), (2, 3)\}`$), $`p^a = \frac{q^n - 1}{q - 1}`$, $`T_{1_Q}`$ the stabilizer of a $`1`$-space or hyperplane of $`\mathbb{F}_q^n`$ (for $`n \ge 3`$, graph correlations swap points and hyperplanes so $`M \le \mathop{\mathrm{P\Gamma L}}\nolimits_n(q)`$, which acts $`2`$-transitively of $`\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits(M) = 2`$);
3. $`T = \mathop{\mathrm{PSL}}\nolimits_2(11)`$, $`T_{1_Q} \cong A_5`$, $`p^a = 11`$ ($`M = \mathop{\mathrm{PSL}}\nolimits_2(11)`$, $`2`$-transitive, $`\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits(M) = 2`$);
4. $`(T, T_{1_Q}, p^a) \in \{(M_{11}, M_{10}, 11), (M_{23}, M_{22}, 23)\}`$ (both $`2`$-transitive, $`\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits(M) = 2`$);
5. $`T = \mathop{\mathrm{PSU}}\nolimits_4(2) \cong \mathop{\mathrm{PSp}}\nolimits_4(3)`$ on its $`p^a = 27 = 3^3`$ totally isotropic projective lines ($`\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits(M) = 3`$).

Case (5) has zero loops by `0323-finite-simple-non-moufang-bol-loops:C0009` Statement 1(4) ($`d = 4`$, $`k = 2`$ unitary totally isotropic subspaces; the degree-27 action is on lines, not the 45 isotropic points). All remaining cases (1)–(4) satisfy $`\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits(M) = 2`$. Finally, when $`p^a = 2^a \ge 4`$, cases (3)–(5) have odd degrees $`11, 23, 27`$, and in case (2) any odd prime $`n \ge 3`$ gives $`\frac{q^n - 1}{q - 1} = 1 + q + \dots + q^{n-1}`$ odd for both even and odd $`q`$; hence $`n = 2`$ and $`2^a = q + 1 \implies q = 2^a - 1`$. $`\square`$

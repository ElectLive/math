# Class-two exponent-four radical constraints

**Mira Cult**

**Argument with scoped independent review below.** Let X be a finite simple nonassociative right Bruck loop with faithful Baer envelope (G,H,K), P=O₂(G), and A=G/P≅∏ᵢPGL₂(qᵢ), where e≥1 and qᵢ=9 or a Fermat prime at least 5. Assume


```math
[P,P]\le Z(P),\qquad p^4=1\text{ for every }p\in P.\tag{1}
```


We do not assume Φ(P) central in G, P extraspecial, or X of exponent 2. Put F=Φ(P), V=P/F, W=P∩H, S=W∩F and U=WF/F. For a class-two group of exponent at most four, F=P²[P,P] is elementary abelian and central in P. Indeed (xy)⁴=x⁴y⁴[y,x]⁶ and [x,y]⁴=1 give [x,y]²=1, whence [x²,y]=1; squares and commutators are therefore central involutions. Let r=dim F and s=dim S. This continues [C0010](../../claims/0010.md); no new ID or classification is asserted.

Baumeister–Stein [arXiv:0908.2597v1](https://arxiv.org/html/0908.2597v1), Theorem 1 and Lemmas 3.2(1)–(2), 3.4 and 4.1–4.5, under the full faithful Bruck-envelope hypotheses, supply A, B=HP/P=∏Bᵢ for the point-stabilizing Borels, P=(P∩H)(P∩K), canonical inversion τ, marked-section conjugacy invariance, and the full singleton-support fiber classes. The [Frattini-shadow manuscript](bruck-frattini-shadow.md#core-reduced-module-and-all-support-shadow-bounds) provides the core-reduced module bound and its $`m\ge1`$ conclusion, independently reviewed at [the source revision](https://github.com/Mira-Cult/math-wip/commit/70a2e52726484645897e9b64c33dd9b7563d7d18), conditional on its cited inputs. Those earlier reviews cover the inputs; the new arguments have the separate scoped review below.

## Square and commutator maps on the shadow

The A-equivariant square map $`q:V\to F`$, $`q(pF)=p^2`$, is well-defined because F is central in P and has exponent 2. Its polar form is the A-equivariant bilinear commutator map $`b:V\times V\to F`$. Let C be the sum of all A-submodules of V contained in U. The B-submodule S has no nonzero A-submodule: any A-invariant subgroup of S is normal in G because P centralizes F, and lies in H, contradicting corefreeness.

Every x∈C⊆U has a lift w∈W, so q(x)=w²∈S. Since q is A-equivariant and C is A-invariant, the span of q(C) is an A-submodule contained in S. Thus q(C)=0. Its polarization gives b(C,C)=0, and the preimage E_C of C in P is elementary abelian. If W_C=W∩E_C, then W_C maps onto C with kernel S, so


```math
\dim E_C=\dim C+r,\qquad \dim W_C=\dim C+s.\tag{2}
```


The canonical inversion automorphism τ fixes V: the source factorization P=(P∩H)(P∩K) shows τ fixes the first factor and inverts the second, and V is elementary abelian. It also fixes F pointwise. Indeed, for p∈P write τ(p)=pf_p with f_p∈F. As F is central in P and elementary, (pf_p)²=p² and [pf_p,p'f_{p'}]=[p,p']; squares and commutators generate F. Therefore E_C is τ-stable, as it is the full preimage of C in P.

## A marked-involution rank bound

Take the formal semidirect product G⁺=G⋊⟨τ̂⟩, with external involution τ̂ even when τ=1, and Λ=τ̂K. Every λ∈Λ is an involution, and Λ is G⁺-conjugacy-invariant by Baumeister–Stein Lemma 3.2(2). For λ=τ̂k, let t=kP∈A and let α be conjugation by λ on E_C. If im(1+α) met W_C nontrivially, conjugation by a suitable element of E_C would give a second element of Λ whose K-coordinate is a distinct element of the same H-coset as k; τ fixes H pointwise. This contradicts the folder transversal property. Thus im(1+α)∩W_C=0, and (2) gives


```math
\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits(1+t\mid C)\le\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits(1+\alpha\mid E_C)\le r-s.\tag{3}
```


This does not assume that K itself is G-conjugacy-invariant.

## Bounding the fixed part of C

For x∈C^A, the map b(x,−):V→F is A-equivariant. Since b(C,C)=0, it factors through V/C, giving a linear map


```math
C^A\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_A(V/C,F).
```


Its kernel is N=C^A∩rad(b). Consequently


```math
\dim C^A\le\dim N+r\dim(V/C).\tag{4}
```


Let E_N be the full preimage of N in P and W_N=W∩E_N. Since N⊆rad(b), E_N⊆Z(P); since N⊆C, E_N is elementary abelian. The map W_N→N is onto with kernel S, so dim W_N=dim N+s. For w∈W_N define


```math
\delta_w(a)=gwg^{-1}w^{-1}\in F,\qquad gP=a\in A.
```


This is well-defined because w centralizes P, and is a 1-cocycle for the A-action on F. The assignment w↦δ_w is linear because W_N is elementary abelian and centralizes F. If δ_w=0 then w∈Z(G)∩H=1 by corefreeness, so it is injective.

Each factor PGL₂(qᵢ) is generated by an upper unipotent of parameter 1, a diagonal element diag(ζ,1) with ζ primitive, and the swap matrix: diagonal conjugates generate the upper-unipotent parameters, the swap gives lower unipotents, and Gaussian elimination gives the group. Thus A has 3e generators, and a 1-cocycle A→F is determined by its values on these generators. Hence


```math
\dim N+s\le\dim Z^1(A,F)\le3er.\tag{5}
```


Combining (4) and (5) gives the weaker convenient estimate


```math
\dim C^A\le r\dim(V/C)+3er.\tag{6}
```


## A fixed-parameter bound

For each factor, the singleton-coordinate outer involutions form a class Tᵢ of size qᵢ(qᵢ−1)/2, by Baumeister–Stein Lemmas 4.4–4.5, and every such element occurs as an image of K. The class generates its PGL₂ factor: its normal closure contains an outer element, and has nontrivial commutator with the simple derived subgroup, whose centralizer in PGL₂(qᵢ) is trivial. Thus the union of the Tᵢ generates A. Put


```math
R=\sum_i\frac{q_i(q_i-1)}2.
```


The simultaneous map C→⊕_{t∈∪Tᵢ}(1+t)C has kernel C^A. Equations (3) and (6) therefore give


```math
\dim C\le rR+r\dim(V/C)+3er.\tag{7}
```


Let m be the number, counted with multiplicity, of nontrivial simple A-composition factors of V/C. The reviewed core-reduced module bound supplies m≥1 and


```math
\dim(V/C)\le B(A)m,\qquad B(A)=[1+2^e(3e+1)]|A|.
```


Combining these estimates,


```math
\boxed{\dim(P/\Phi(P))\le(r+1)B(A)m+r(R+3e).}\tag{8}
```


Moreover every k∈K has k²∈P because its image in A is an involution or identity. Assumption (1) then gives k⁸=1, so X has exponent dividing 8. The bounds are effective for fixed A, r and m. They do not establish (1) for every radical, bound those parameters globally, construct a loop, or classify simple Bruck loops. The new deductions have the scoped review below.

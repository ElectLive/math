# Candidate constraints for J principal block and arbitrary N-block

**Scoped independent mathematical review recorded below.** Let (G,H,K) be the faithful Baer envelope of a finite simple exponent-two Bruck loop with elementary abelian V=O₂(G), quotient S₅, and S₅-module decomposition V=J⊕Y, J=F₂⁶/⟨1⟩ on the six projective points, Y=N^a⊕U^b for nonnegative a,b. We use the reviewed group-cohomology calculations and conventions in [bruck-s5-dimension-five.md](bruck-s5-dimension-five.md), [bruck-s5-radical.md](bruck-s5-radical.md), and [bruck-s5-module-blocks.md](bruck-s5-module-blocks.md). No quotient of a loop by Y is assumed. The deductions below have the scoped independent review recorded below.

Let F=π(H)=F₂₀, let P≤F have order 5, and W=H∩V. Put L=J^P, a one-dimensional F-fixed subspace. Since Y^P=0, V^P=L. The unique nonzero S₅-invariant functional J→1 is nonzero on L; the reviewed invariant-functional obstruction forces W^P=L. Its P-nonfixed part is an F-module all of whose simple constituents are the irreducible projective four-dimensional module, so dim W=1+4c for some c≥0. The P-fixed part of V/W vanishes; therefore V/W is an F-projective module, so H¹(F,V/W)=0.

The [dimension-five cohomology argument](bruck-s5-dimension-five.md#candidate-completion-via-degree-three-cohomology), reviewed by an independent check at the recorded source revision conditional on its H³ input and by an independent check at the recorded source revision for that input, gives the J-extension detection; [the N-block result](bruck-s5-module-blocks.md#elimination-of-all-n-only-radicals), reviewed by an independent check at the recorded source revision, gives H²(S₅,Y)=0. These reviewed H² inputs show that a transposition involution lift allows only two classes in H²(S₅,J): split and Nagy's nonsplit class. Also H²(S₅,Y)=0. Thus there are only the two corresponding extension classes for V. For either fixed W, H¹(F,V/W)=0 makes any two subgroups above F having intersection W conjugate by V, provided one exists. In the split case W⋊F is a representative. In Nagy's case let E be his extension of S₅ by J and H₀=N_E(P), with H₀∩J=L. Use the fiber product of E and Y⋊S₅ over S₅; W H₀ is a subgroup above F with intersection W, since L≤W and H₀ normalizes W. It is a representative for H.

## Split extension

For T generated by a transposition, P6|T is free, so the connecting map H¹(T,J)→H²(T,1) from 0→1→P6→J→0 is an isomorphism. On S₅, Shapiro identifies H¹(S₅,1)→H¹(S₅,P6) with restriction to F, an isomorphism. Therefore H¹(S₅,J) injects into H²(S₅,1) with image the kernel of restriction to H²(F,1). That kernel is spanned by the square of sign, which restricts nontrivially to T. Naturality shows H¹(S₅,J)→H¹(T,J) is onto. The Shapiro map and the two central-class restrictions used here are recorded in the reviewed [principal-envelope argument](bruck-s5-principal-envelope-bound.md#proposed-invariant-functional-obstruction) and [dimension-five cohomology argument](bruck-s5-dimension-five.md#candidate-completion-via-degree-three-cohomology), at the pins above; their combination in this H¹(T,J) detection is part of the present argument reviewed below.

The centralizer quotient C_{S₅}(t)/T≅S₃ acts trivially on H¹(T,J), a one-dimensional F₂-space. Since U|T is free and H¹(T,N) is the natural 2-dimensional S₃-module, the involution-lift V-orbits above t are indexed by H¹(T,V)=F₂⊕(F₂²)^a. There are exactly two S₃-fixed labels, with N part zero; every other orbit has size 3 or 6. The section fiber is a union of such orbits, and the number of V-orbits selected is a positive power of 2. Modulo 3 at least one fixed label is selected. If it is zero, the pure transposition t lies in K, so by conjugacy all pure transpositions do; two disjoint ones multiply into F≤H, contradicting transversality. If instead the nonzero J label is selected, the global cocycle above induces a split-group automorphism switching the two labels. Its image H has the same W and is V-conjugate back to W⋊F because H¹(F,V/W)=0; V-conjugation does not change lift labels. The same contradiction results. Hence the split group extension is impossible.

## Nagy extension: affine centralizer action

Nagy [arXiv:0709.4544v1](https://arxiv.org/html/0709.4544v1), Lemma 3.4(ii), proves that E has a single nonradical involution conjugacy class of size 80, all above the ten transpositions. Thus its fiber above a fixed t has eight involutions. The rank of 1+t on J is 2, so each J-conjugacy orbit has size 4: there are exactly two. Transitivity of the full E-conjugacy class implies that the centralizer C_{S₅}(t)/T acts transitively on these two J-orbits. This action is necessarily the sign action of S₃, since it is a nontrivial homomorphism S₃→S₂. This derives the affine action on actual lifts, rather than identifying a preferred origin in the nonsplit J fiber.

In the fiber-product model, a J-fiber involution and zero Y-coordinate provide a reference; the full V-conjugacy-orbit labels form the product of this two-point sign set with H¹(T,Y)=(F₂²)^a. Lifts of the centralizer with zero Y-coordinate show that S₃ acts diagonally, by sign on the first factor and naturally on the second; other lifts change labels only by V-conjugacy. The labels with N-tuple zero form one orbit of size 2. Every nonzero N-tuple has trivial stabilizer in A₃, because an element of order 3 fixes no nonzero vector of the natural F₂² module. Consequently every orbit with nonzero N-tuple has size 6. This also holds for a=0, when only the base orbit exists.

Baumeister–Stein [arXiv:0908.2597v1](https://arxiv.org/html/0908.2597v1), Lemmas 4.1 and 4.5, give the section-image and fiber counts. The transposition fiber of K has 2^(d−w−1) elements, and each V-conjugacy orbit has 2^r elements, with d=5+4a+8b, w=1+4c and r=2+a+4b. Hence the number selected is

q=2^e,  e=d−w−1−r=1+3a+4b−4c≥0.

The orbit structure forces q either divisible by 6, or congruent to 2 modulo 6 with the base orbit selected. Since q is a power of 2, the first possibility is impossible. Therefore q≡2 (mod 6), e is positive and odd, a is even, and the base orbit is selected. When a=0 there are only two labels, so q=2, e=1 and c=b. These are necessary constraints only; they neither classify K∩V or the remaining fibers nor establish simplicity.

The scoped independent acceptance is recorded below. The constraints are not an existence result or an exhaustive classification, including in the a=0 subcase.

# Candidate classification of dimension-five S₅ envelopes

**Mira Cult**

**Uniqueness argument with scoped independent reviews below.** Consider the faithful Baer envelope of a finite simple exponent-2 Bruck loop, with elementary abelian V=O₂(G) of dimension 5 and G/V=S₅. The reductions and cohomological argument below identify the loop with Nagy’s order-96 loop. The earlier scoped review accepted the argument conditional on the H³ dimension, and a separate review has since accepted the independent certificate for that dimension.

This manuscript-local continuation of [C0010](../../claims/0010.md) allocates no new claim ID. Use the cited structural facts and notation in the [S₅ radical-bound manuscript](bruck-s5-radical.md): H maps to F₂₀, K is G-invariant and consists of identity and involutions, π(K) lies in the identity and the ten transpositions. Let W=H∩V, w=dim W. As there are six H-images in S₅ and π(H) contains no transposition, |K∩V|=2^(5-w), while conjugation and counting give |K∩π⁻¹(t)|=2^(4-w) for every transposition t. For each section involution a over t, its V-conjugacy orbit has 2^rank(1+t) members, while the entire set of involutions in its fiber has 2^(5-rank(1+t)) members.

A 5-cycle acts on V with a unique 1-dimensional fixed space and an irreducible 4-dimensional complement U. Both are F₂₀-invariant. U is irreducible for F₂₀, and by dimensions the only F₂₀-submodules of V have dimensions 0,1,4,5. Therefore w∈{0,1,4,5}. Core-freeness excludes 5. If w=4, the fiber over t has size 1; V-conjugation forces rank(1+t)=0, contrary to faithful S₅ action. Thus w∈{0,1}, giving loop orders 192 or 96 respectively.

The simple F₂S₅-modules are 1,M,N, with dim M=dim N=4. Faithfulness forces the composition factors 1 and either M or N. Both four-dimensional modules are self-dual: N is the even-weight part of the five-point permutation representation, with nondegenerate dot product; M is the even-weight six-point module modulo the constant vector, again with the induced nondegenerate dot product. Shapiro applied to F₂⁵=1⊕N gives H¹(S₅,N)=0. A direct calculation of H¹(S₅,M) gives dimension 1; the explicit exhaustive 256-pair calculation, using the presentation in Nagy arXiv:0709.4544v1 Lemma 3.1, is [verify_bruck_s5_module_h1.py](../code/verify_bruck_s5_module_h1.py) and was inspected and run in the scoped uniqueness review described below. It follows that, up to isomorphism, the four modules are 1⊕N, 1⊕M, J=F₂⁶/1, and its dual (the even-weight six-point module). J and its dual are nonsplit extensions in opposite orientations: for J there is no nonzero fixed vector, while the dual has a fixed line. For J, absence of a fixed coset follows because a fixed coset x+1 would give a homomorphism S₅→F₂ by gx+x∈1, zero on the odd elements of the point stabilizer F₂₀; hence it is zero and x itself is constant. The transposition ranks on these four modules are respectively 1,2,2,2, as direct permutation-matrix calculations show.

If w=0, |K∩V|=32 and there are 16 section involutions above t, implying rank(1+t)≤1; faithfulness makes the rank 1. Thus V=1⊕N. Since H²(S₅,N)=0 (as in the existing manuscript), the extension has the form N⋊E, where E is a central extension of S₅ by C₂. H projects isomorphically onto F₂₀, so its double-transposition involution b lifts as an involution. A section element above a transposition t is also an involution. The two coordinates t² and b² detect the two independent central extension classes of S₅: the sign-pullback class has t² nontrivial, b² trivial; the spin class with involutory transposition lifts has t² trivial, b² nontrivial. The standard universal-coefficient sequence and spin presentation cited in the existing manuscript justify the independence. Both coordinates vanish, so E splits. Every complement H to V in the preimage of F₂₀ is a graph of a 1-cocycle. H¹(F₂₀,N)=0 by Shapiro, and H¹(F₂₀,1)=Hom(F₂₀,C₂); hence after V-conjugation H is the graph of either the trivial homomorphism or the sign character. Both send a double transposition b=tu, product of disjoint transpositions t,u, to 0. Since all 16 involution lifts above each transposition are forced into K, standard t,u lie in K, yet tu=b∈H: contradiction.

Thus w=1. The unique F₂₀-fixed line W cannot be G-invariant, by core-freeness. This eliminates 1⊕M, 1⊕N and J*, so V=J, and |Q|=96. Let M be the even-weight submodule modulo constants. The S₅-orbits in J have sizes 1,15,6,10, with 1+15=M. Since K∩V is S₅-invariant, contains 0 and has 16 members, it equals M. A transposition has rank 2 on J, so its involution fiber has exactly 8 members, which are all in K.

Let P≤H have order 5. Its preimage in H of the normal C₅≤F₂₀ is W×P, making P characteristic there and hence normal in H. Since C_V(P)=W has order 2 and N_{S₅}(π(P))=F₂₀, |N_G(P)|=2·20=40=|H|. Thus H=N_G(P).

## Candidate completion via degree-three cohomology

The following argument uses a degree-three cohomology dimension. Its deductions and an independent calculation of that dimension have the separate scoped reviews below. Let P₆=F₂⁶=Ind_{F₂₀}^{S₅} F₂. The exact sequence 0→1→P₆→J→0 gives, by Shapiro, an injection

H²(S₅,J) ↪ H³(S₅,F₂)

whose image is ker[H³(S₅,F₂)→H³(F₂₀,F₂)], provided H²(S₅,F₂)→H²(F₂₀,F₂) is surjective. This surjectivity follows from the spin extension with involutory transposition lifts: a lift of a 4-cycle has order 8, so its restriction to C₄ and hence to F₂₀ is nonzero. Restriction H³(S₅,F₂)→H³(F₂₀,F₂) is surjective: the product of the sign class with that degree-two spin class restricts to a nonzero degree-three class on C₄. Here the normal subgroup C₅ has odd order, so F₂₀ and C₄ have identical mod-2 cohomology.

Simon King’s computational table dated 14 December 2010, https://users.fmi.uni-jena.de/~king/cohomology/nonprimepower/SymmetricGroup_5_mod2.html gives the mod-2 ring of S₅ with three degree-three basis monomials, so dim H³(S₅,F₂)=3. It follows that dim H²(S₅,J)=2. For T=⟨t⟩ generated by a transposition, P₆ restricted to T is free because t has three 2-cycles on the six points. The connecting map H²(T,J)→H³(T,F₂) is therefore an isomorphism. The cube of the sign class belongs to the above kernel, because the degree-one generator of H*(C₄,F₂) has square zero; it restricts nontrivially to T. Naturality shows that H²(S₅,J)→H²(T,J) is nonzero, so its kernel has dimension one.

An extension of S₅ by J has an involution lift of t exactly when its cohomology class restricts to zero on T. Nagy's nonsplit extension has this property. Consequently only the zero class and Nagy's class are possible. In the zero class, G=J⋊S₅. By Schur–Zassenhaus, the order-5 subgroup P can be conjugated by J into S₅; hence H=N_G(P)=C_J(P)⋊N_{S₅}(P). It contains the standard double transposition b=tu, with t,u disjoint transpositions. Both standard t and u are involution lifts and belong to K, contradicting transversality. Thus only Nagy's extension remains, and its Theorem 3.6 gives the unique exponent-2 folder for that group.

The argument gives uniqueness within the dimension-five, elementary-abelian radical, S₅-quotient hypothesis, subject to the scope and limitations of the separate reviews below. It does not classify every order-96 exponent-2 Bruck loop because those broader hypotheses are not established.

## Scoped review and independent H³ certificate

reviewer contributor 1 accepted the uniqueness argument at exact revision [the source revision](https://github.com/Mira-Cult/math-wip/commit/266152034b2f190313d253c4d55df0d4f85f68c2), conditional on King’s tabulated dimension. The reviewer and a separate cohomology subreview checked the extension and restriction deductions; the reviewer inspected and ran the H¹ script at that exact revision, obtaining 32 cocycles and 16 coboundaries. Neither independently recomputed King’s calculation in that review.

A new independent certificate is [verify_s5_h3.py](../code/verify_s5_h3.py). It constructs S₅ as all 120 permutations and its Sylow subgroup S=⟨(1234),(13)⟩≅D₈. On normalized inhomogeneous cochains with trivial F₂ coefficients, it computes d²:C²→C³ and d³:C³→C⁴, checks d³d²=0, and forms a basis of H³(S,F₂). The cochain dimensions are 49 and 343; the computed ranks are 41 and 298, leaving H³ dimension 4.

For each g∈S₅ it forms Q={s∈S:gsg⁻¹∈S}. It tests every one of the 16 cohomology classes by restricting a representative to Q and comparing it with c(gq₁g⁻¹,gq₂g⁻¹,gq₃g⁻¹), modulo the explicitly computed coboundaries of Q. This is exactly the stable-elements condition. There are eight stable classes, so H³(S₅,F₂) has dimension 3 by Cartan–Eilenberg, *Homological Algebra* (Princeton, 1956), Chapter XII, §9, p. 257 and §10, Theorem 10.1, p. 259 ([primary scan](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~mmovshev/BOOKS/homologicalalgeb033541mbp.pdf)). The calculation uses five intersection subgroups; their order distribution over the 120 elements is 1:64, 2:32, 4:16, 8:8.

reviewer contributor 2 independently reviewed the certificate at exact revision [the source revision](https://github.com/Mira-Cult/math-wip/commit/418b4d50d1d6deb2e72182e867f1b1f70450fa92) and accepted the normalized bar differential, the ranks 41 and 298, the resulting dimension 4 for H³(D₈,F₂), and the 120 stable-elements checks yielding eight stable classes across five intersections. Together with the stable-elements theorem, this discharges the H³ dimension premise in the earlier conditional review. This review did not independently review the earlier uniqueness argument or the H¹ calculation, and it does not certify any other part of the global Bol classification.

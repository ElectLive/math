# Elementary-abelian radical bound for the S5 quotient

**Theorem with scoped independent model review below.** Let $`(G,H,K)`$ be the faithful Baer envelope of a finite nontrivial simple right Bruck loop of exponent 2. Suppose $`V=O_2(G)`$ is elementary abelian and $`G/V\cong S_5`$, with $`S_5\cong PGL_2(5)`$. Then $`\dim_{\mathbb F_2}V\geq5`$. The bound is attained by Nagy's order-96 example. The continuation below also excludes dimension six under the same envelope hypotheses; the separate dimension-five manuscript treats uniqueness.

This manuscript-local result pursues [C0010](../../claims/0010.md); 

## Sources and conventions

Write $`\pi:G\to S_5`$, $`W=H\cap V`$, and use additive notation for $`V`$. The exponent-2 section $`K`$ contains the identity and is a union of conjugacy classes of involutions. The envelope is core-free and $`K`$ is a transversal to every conjugate of $`H`$.

Baumeister–Stein, *The finite Bruck loops*, [arXiv:0908.2597v1](https://arxiv.org/html/0908.2597v1), Theorem 1(b)(2)–(3), Remark 1.1(2), and Lemma 4.1(1)–(2), gives $`\pi(H)\cong F_{20}=5{:}4`$, $`F^*(G)=O_2(G)=V`$, and $`\pi(K)\subseteq\{1\}\cup T`$, where $`T`$ is the outer involution class in $`PGL_2(5)`$, identified with the ten transpositions in $`S_5`$. The identity is included explicitly here. The generalized Fitting subgroup is self-centralizing, so $`C_G(V)\le V`$, and the induced $`S_5`$-action on $`V`$ is faithful.

Nagy, *A class of finite simple Bol loops of exponent 2*, [arXiv:0709.4544v1](https://arxiv.org/html/0709.4544v1), Lemma 4.1, identifies the simple $`\mathbb F_2 S_5`$-modules as the trivial module and two nonisomorphic modules $`M,N`$ of dimension 4. Here $`N`$ is the even-weight submodule of the natural five-point permutation module, and $`M`$ is the four-dimensional constituent of the six-point projective-line permutation module. Both restrict irreducibly to $`F_{20}`$; this also follows from the normal five-cycle acting without nonzero fixed vectors on either four-dimensional module. Nagy §3, especially Lemma 3.3 and Theorem 3.6, supplies the example with $`|V|=32`$, quotient $`S_5`$, and loop order 96. Its extension is nonsplit (Nagy §3, opening paragraph).

## Cohomology calculation for N

Let $`P=\mathbb F_2^5`$ be the natural permutation module. Because 5 is odd, $`P=\mathbb F_2\mathbf 1\oplus N`$. By Shapiro's lemma, using a point stabilizer $`S_4`$, $`H^i(S_5,P)\cong H^i(S_4,\mathbb F_2)`$. Under this isomorphism the map on cohomology induced by inclusion of the constant submodule is restriction $`\mathop{\mathrm{res}}\nolimits:H^i(S_5,\mathbb F_2)\to H^i(S_4,\mathbb F_2)`$.

For $`i=2`$, this restriction is an isomorphism. Indeed the natural universal-coefficient exact sequence for trivial coefficients is

```math
0\to \mathop{\mathrm{Ext}}\nolimits^1_{\mathbb Z}(S_m^{\rm ab},\mathbb F_2)
\to H^2(S_m,\mathbb F_2)
\to \mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(H_2(S_m,\mathbb Z),\mathbb F_2)\to0.
```

For $`m=4,5`$, both the abelianization and Schur multiplier are $`C_2`$. Inclusion $`S_4\hookrightarrow S_5`$ induces an isomorphism on abelianizations. It also induces an isomorphism on multipliers: in a spin double cover of $`S_5`$, lifts of the disjoint transpositions $`(12)`$ and $`(34)`$ anticommute, so its restriction to $`S_4`$ has nonzero multiplier component; both multipliers have order 2. Naturality and the short five lemma give the asserted restriction isomorphism. Thus the direct-sum decomposition gives

```math
H^2(S_5,N)=0.
```

Accordingly every extension of $`S_5`$ by this specified $`S_5`$-module $`N`$ splits.

Similarly, restriction of the natural five-point module to $`F_{20}`$ is $`\mathop{\mathrm{Ind}}\nolimits_{C_4}^{F_{20}}\mathbb F_2`$. Shapiro and the constant-plus-$`N`$ splitting identify the map on $`H^1`$ from the constant summand with $`H^1(F_{20},\mathbb F_2)\to H^1(C_4,\mathbb F_2)`$. Both spaces have dimension one, and restriction is an isomorphism because $`F_{20}^{\rm ab}\cong C_4`$. Hence $`H^1(F_{20},N)=0`$. This says all complements to $`N`$ in $`N\rtimes F_{20}`$ are conjugate by an element of $`N`$.

## Proof of the bound

Faithfulness implies that a 5-cycle acts nontrivially on $`V`$. Since 5 divides none of $`|GL_d(2)|`$ for $`d<4`$, we have $`\dim V\ge4`$. Suppose equality. By the listed irreducibles, any reducible four-dimensional module has only trivial composition factors; a 5-cycle then acts as a unipotent element of odd order, hence trivially, a contradiction. Thus $`V\cong M`$ or $`N`$.

The subgroup $`W`$ is $`H`$-invariant and so an $`F_{20}`$-submodule. Irreducibility of the restriction gives $`W=0`$ or $`W=V`$. The latter would put the nontrivial normal subgroup $`V`$ in $`H`$, contradicting core-freeness. Therefore $`W=0`$, $`H\cong F_{20}`$, and $`|G:H|=6\cdot16=96`$.

The image restriction on $`K`$ and the transversal property imply $`K\cap V=V`$: each of the 16 cosets of $`H`$ represented by elements of $`V`$ must have its representative in $`V`$, because $`\pi(H)`$ contains no transpositions. Consequently there are 80 section elements above the ten transpositions. Conjugacy makes the number above each transposition equal, namely 8. If $`a\in K`$ maps to a transposition $`t`$, it is an involution and the involutions in $`aV`$ are exactly $`a+C_V(t)`$. It follows that $`|C_V(t)|\ge8`$, or $`\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits(1+t)\le1`$.

For $`M`$, a transposition of $`S_5`$ acts on the six points of $`PGL_2(5)`$'s projective line as three disjoint transpositions. The image of $`1+t`$ on the even-weight subspace has dimension 2, and its quotient by the all-ones line has rank 2; this quotient is $`M`$. Thus $`M`$ is excluded. For $`N`$, a transposition has rank 1: on even-weight vectors its image is the line spanned by $`e_1+e_2`$. Therefore $`|C_N(t)|=8`$, and all involutions in each transposition fiber belong to $`K`$.

The cohomology calculation now identifies $`G`$ with $`N\rtimes S_5`$, and after conjugation by $`N`$, $`H`$ is the standard $`F_{20}`$ complement. The standard $`F_{20}`$ contains a double transposition $`b=(12)(34)`$, after relabeling. Its factors $`t=(12)`$ and $`u=(34)`$ are distinct involutions over transpositions and therefore lie in $`K`$. But $`tu=b\in H`$, contradicting that distinct elements of $`K`$ represent distinct $`H`$-cosets. This eliminates dimension 4 and proves $`\dim V\ge5`$, under the stated envelope hypotheses.

## Restrictions in higher dimensions

The following necessary conditions have the scoped review recorded below and do not exclude any dimension from 6 through 12. Put $`d=\dim V`$, $`w=\dim W`$, and $`r=\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits(1+t)`$ for a transposition $`t`$. Conjugacy and the transversal property give $`|K\cap V|=2^{d-w}`$ and $`|K\cap\pi^{-1}(t)|=2^{d-w-1}`$. The $`V`$-orbit of a section element over $`t`$ has size $`2^r`$, while the entire involution fiber has size $`2^{d-r}`$. Hence

```math
r-1\le w\le d-r-1,\qquad r\ge1.
```

In particular, $`w=0`$ forces $`r=1`$, with every involution over every transposition belonging to $`K`$.

For the normal subgroup $`P\cong C_5`$ of $`\pi(H)`$, decompose $`V=A\oplus B`$, where $`A=C_V(P)`$ has dimension $`a`$ and $`B=[V,P]`$ has dimension $`4b`$. The latter is a direct sum of $`b`$ copies of the irreducible projective $`\mathbb F_2F_{20}`$-module, by Nagy Lemma 4.1(iv). The $`P`$-idempotent also splits $`W`$, so $`d=a+4b`$, $`b\ge1`$, and $`w=s+4c`$ for $`0\le s\le a`$, $`0\le c\le b`$, where $`s`$ is the dimension of a $`C_4`$-invariant subspace of $`A`$. Core-freeness additionally forbids a nonzero $`S_5`$-submodule inside $`W`$.

If $`w=0`$, no composition factor of $`V`$ is $`M`$, since its transposition rank is 2 and rank on a subquotient cannot exceed the original rank. If also $`b=1`$, the unique nontrivial composition factor is $`N`$. The vanishing $`H^1(S_5,N)=0`$ and self-duality of $`N`$ split the $`N`$ factor from all trivial factors, giving $`V=N\oplus A`$. The action on $`A`$ has 2-group image and hence kills the perfect subgroup $`A_5`$, so $`A`$ is a direct sum of indecomposable 1- and 2-dimensional modules for the sign quotient $`C_2`$. The vanishing $`H^2(S_5,N)=H^1(F_{20},N)=0`$ reduces the group extension to $`G=N\rtimes E`$, with $`1\to A\to E\to S_5\to1`$, and conjugates $`H`$ into $`E`$. The following obstruction eliminates this case under the recorded review scope; no exhaustiveness of Nagy's examples is assumed.


**Obstruction (scoped review below).** If $`V`$ has exactly one nontrivial four-dimensional composition factor, then $`W\ne0`$. Thus dimensions 6 and 7 cannot have $`W=0`$; neither dimension is excluded.

Indeed, suppose $`W=0`$. The preceding reduction gives $`V=N\oplus A`$, with $`A\cong\mathbf1^m\oplus R^n`$, where $`R=\mathop{\mathrm{Ind}}\nolimits_{A_5}^{S_5}\mathbf1`$ is the two-dimensional regular module for the sign quotient. The extension class of $`E`$ restricts trivially to $`F_{20}`$ because $`H`$ is a complement, and to a transposition subgroup because a transposition has an involution lift. For each trivial summand, these two restrictions jointly detect $`H^2(S_5,\mathbf1)`$: the sign-pullback class restricts nontrivially to a transposition and trivially to $`F_{20}`$, whereas the spin class with involutory transposition lifts restricts nontrivially to the order-four subgroup of $`F_{20}`$. This uses Nagy Lemma 3.1 and Akita–Kawasaki–Satoh, §2.1, Theorem 2.2, cited above.

For each $`R`$ summand, Shapiro and Mackey identify restriction to $`F_{20}`$ with $`H^2(A_5,\mathbf1)\to H^2(D_{10},\mathbf1)`$, where $`D_{10}=F_{20}\cap A_5`$. This is injective: restriction to a point stabilizer $`A_4`$ of odd index 5 is injective, and $`H^2(A_4,\mathbf1)\cong H^2(V_4,\mathbf1)^{C_3}`$ has dimension 1, generated by $`x^2+xy+y^2`$. The extension $`SL_2(5)\to A_5`$ gives a nonzero class on $`D_{10}`$, since its noncentral lifts of involutions have order 4. Thus the source has dimension at most 1 and its restriction to $`D_{10}`$ is injective. It follows that the entire extension class vanishes.

Thus $`E=A\rtimes S_5`$, and $`H`$ is the graph of a cocycle $`f:F_{20}\to A`$. For a generator $`d`$ of a complement $`C_4\le F_{20}`$, $`b=d^2=tu`$ is a product of disjoint transpositions and $`f(b)=(1+d)f(d)`$ is fixed by both $`t,u`$, since they act on $`A`$ through sign. Hence $`(0,t)`$ and $`(f(b),u)`$, with zero $`N`$-coordinates, are involutions in the two transposition fibers of $`K`$, while their product lies in $`H`$, contradicting transversality.

## Inversion, relative derivations and Frattini layers

The canonical involutory automorphism τ fixes H pointwise and sends k∈K to k⁻¹. Since P=WD and the elements of V=P/Φ are involutions, τ induces the identity on V. Each section image in A is an involution or identity, so τ induces the identity on A because K generates G. Thus [P,τ]≤Φ. More explicitly, p=wd with w∈W and d∈D gives


```math
\tau(p)p^{-1}=w d^{-2}w^{-1}.
```


Consequently [P,τ] is generated by W-conjugates of the squares d², d∈D; no G-normality of this subgroup is asserted. In the formal semidirect product P⁺=P⋊⟨τ̂⟩, with external involution τ̂ even if τ=1, the Frattini formula gives Φ(P⁺)=Φ(P), and P⁺/Φ(P)≅V×C₂.

For the lower exponent-2 central series P₁=P, Pᵢ₊₁=Pᵢ²[Pᵢ,P], τ acts trivially on every Pᵢ/Pᵢ₊₁. Indeed the base is P/Φ(P); if τ fixes Pᵢ/Pᵢ₊₁ and P/P₂, the identities for squares and commutators show it fixes the generators of Pᵢ₊₁/Pᵢ₊₂. This does not force τ to be trivial or P to be elementary: the associative loop C₄ has P=C₄, W=1 and inversion τ, which is nontrivial but fixes both successive elementary layers. That control is outside the simple nonassociative hypotheses. For an exponent-2 loop, τ fixes the generating set K and therefore is the identity on G, regardless of the structure of P.

The automorphism induced by τ on G/Φ fixes V and A pointwise. It yields a left derivation δ:A→V, δ(gP)=τ(g)g⁻¹Φ, vanishing on B. As a group/module statement, the relative derivations are naturally Hom_A(E,V), where E=ker(F₂[A/B]→F₂). The image D̄=DΦ/Φ is stable for the affine A-action a⋆v=av+δ(a), by Lemma 3.2(2) applied in the formal extension by τ. Thus δ(A)⊂D̄. The maximal self-normalizing Borels imply that any subgroup of A containing B is a product of choices Bᵢ or PGL₂(qᵢ); the explicit commutator proof is in the product-constraint manuscript. Hence


```math
|\delta(A)|=\prod_{i:\,\delta|_{D_i}\ne0}(q_i+1)\le |\overline D|\le |V|.\tag{1}
```


Here Dᵢ denotes the ith PGL₂ factor, not D=P∩K. We cannot generally replace |D̄| by |V:W̄|: D̄ may meet a W̄-coset more than once.

## Invariant-functional detection without a quotient folder

We claim that restriction


```math
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_A(V,\mathbf1)\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_B(\overline W,\mathbf1)\tag{2}
```


is injective. Suppose nonzero invariant λ vanishes on W̄ and let L be the full preimage in P of ker λ. Then L is normal in G and G/L is a central C₂-extension of A. The image of H is a complement to the central kernel over B. The composite λδ:A→C₂ is a homomorphism vanishing on B, hence is zero because B surjects onto A^ab≅C₂^e. For k∈K, the inversion identity gives k²Φ=δ(kP). The original K has a lift over every singleton-support outer involution by Lemmas 4.4–4.5; its image in G/L is an involution. The product central-extension detector from the product-constraint manuscript therefore splits G/L.

Every character B→C₂ extends to A: the additive normal subgroup of each Borel has odd order, and the cyclic torus has its unique nontrivial C₂-character given by determinant square class. Adjusting a central projection by an extension of the H-image character gives a homomorphism G→C₂ vanishing on H and nonzero on P. Because K generates G, this induces a nontrivial X→C₂ loop homomorphism. Simplicity rules it out, since |X|≥[A:B]=∏(qᵢ+1)>2. This proves the candidate injection (2) without asserting that KΦ/Φ is a folder.

Write A′=∏PSL₂(qᵢ). The injection first gives V=W̄+[V,A]. In Q=V/[V,A′], the image of W̄ is an A/A′-submodule because B surjects onto A/A′≅C₂^e. The group algebra F₂[A/A′] is local, so Nakayama's lemma strengthens the conclusion to


```math
V=\overline W+[V,A′].\tag{3}
```


Neither (2) nor (3) says W̄ is corefree or that it is a stabilizer in a faithful quotient folder. The elementary-radical dimension bound depending on corefreeness cannot be applied directly.

## Faithful shadow action

The conjugation action of A on V is faithful. Since F*(G)=P, self-centralization gives C_G(P)≤P. The kernel of Aut(P)→Aut(P/Φ) is a 2-group: if d=dim P/Φ, choose an ordered basis and lifts in P. Every ordered tuple of lifts generates P by the maximal-subgroup definition of Φ; there are |Φ|^d such tuples. An automorphism acting trivially on P/Φ permutes these tuples, and the action is free because each tuple generates P. Its order therefore divides a power of 2. If N/P is the kernel of A→GL(V), then N/C_G(P) embeds into this automorphism kernel. Since C_G(P)≤P is a 2-group, N is a normal 2-group, hence N≤O₂(G)=P. Thus N=P.

For d=dim V this yields


```math
\prod_i q_i(q_i^2-1)=|A|\le|\mathrm{GL}_d(2)|=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(2^d-2^j)<2^{d^2}.\tag{4}
```


This bounds quotient factors for a fixed Frattini rank, but does not bound d or show Φ=1.

## Strict overlap in the exponent-2 S₅ shadow

Assume additionally that X has exponent 2, A≅S₅, and Φ≠1. Put n=|V:W̄|=2^{v-w}, where v=dim V and w=dim W̄. Since WΦ=P would imply W=P by the Frattini property and contradict corefreeness, n≥2. The image K̄=KΦ/Φ cannot be a transversal to HΦ/Φ: if it were, the folder quotient criterion would produce a loop quotient of index [G:HΦ]≥6. Its kernel would be nontrivial; otherwise K∩Φ={1}, transversality would give K∩HΦ={1}, and the folder factorization would imply HΦ=H, contrary to Φ⊄H. This contradicts simplicity.

The S₅ quotient support and conjugacy invariance of K give equal shadow fiber sizes over the ten transpositions. Let d₀=|DΦ/Φ| and let r be the size of one such fiber. Then


```math
|\overline K|=d_0+10r>6n=[G:H\Phi].\tag{5}
```


Moreover d₀≥n and r≥n/2: D̄ covers all W̄-cosets in V, while the outer fibers cover the remaining 5n cosets. If ρ=rank(1+t|V) for a transposition t, V-conjugacy orbits in the fiber have size 2^ρ and the involution equation has at most 2^{v−ρ} solutions. Thus


```math
r\in2^\rho\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad n/2\le r\le2^{v-\rho},\qquad \rho\le w+1.\tag{6}
```


In particular the minimal values d₀=n and r=n/2 cannot both occur. This section uses the exact folder quotient criterion of [C0001](../../claims/0001.md), Theorem 2.2; it does not infer a loop quotient from a group quotient. Equations (1)–(6) neither prove that P is elementary nor construct a non-elementary example. The arguments through (6) have the following scoped independent review.

## Core-reduced module and all-support shadow bounds

**Deductions with scoped independent review below.** Continue under the full simple nonassociative Bruck-envelope hypotheses above, with e≥1. The preceding Frattini-shadow arguments at exact source the recorded source revision were independently accepted by an independent check under their stated inputs. The module bound used below is the module-theoretic portion of [the elementary product constraints](bruck-elementary-product-constraints.md), reviewed at the recorded source revision. The separate review below covers the deductions in this section. These deductions apply to the 2-power-exponent Bruck setting above, whether or not P is elementary and without an exponent-4 bound on X.

Let C be the sum of all A-submodules of V contained in W̄. The core of HΦ/Φ in G/Φ maps into core_A(B)=1, hence lies in V and is exactly C. Put U=V/C and Z=W̄/C. Then Z is B-invariant and contains no nonzero A-submodule. An A-invariant functional on U vanishing on Z pulls back to one on V vanishing on W̄, so the reviewed Frattini injection gives


```math
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_A(U,\mathbf1)\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_B(Z,\mathbf1)
\quad\text{injective}.                                      \tag{7}
```


The Frattini property gives WΦ<P: otherwise W=P, putting the nontrivial normal P inside core_G(H). Thus W̄<V and U≠0. Let m_U be the number, with multiplicity, of nontrivial simple A-composition factors of U. The proof of the cited product module bound uses only the A-module U, the B-invariant core-free subspace Z and injection (7); it does not use a folder or an F* condition on the ambient extension. It therefore transfers verbatim to give


```math
1\le m_U,\qquad
\dim U\le [1+2^e(3e+1)]\,|A|\,m_U.                         \tag{8}
```


For the lower bound, if m_U=0 then the perfect subgroup A′ acts trivially on U. Since B surjects onto A/A′, Z is A-invariant, so Z=0. But a nonzero module over the local algebra F₂[A/A′] has a nonzero map to the trivial module, contradicting (7). No faithfulness of the A-action on U is asserted or required.

Let N be the full preimage of C in P. Then Φ≤N≤HΦ and HN=HΦ<G. If Φ≠1, then N≠1 is normal in G. If K N/N were a transversal to HN/N, the exact quotient criterion in [C0001](../../claims/0001.md), Theorem 2.2, would give a proper loop quotient. Its kernel cannot be trivial: under transversality, trivial K∩N would imply K∩HN={1} and hence N≤H, contradicting core-freeness. Simplicity therefore forces


```math
|KN/N|>[G:HN]=n\prod_{i=1}^e(q_i+1).                         \tag{9}
```


The coset action of G/N on (G/N)/(HN/N) is faithful by the definition of C. This faithfulness of a **coset action** does not say that A acts faithfully on U. Although O₂(G/N)=U because O₂(A)=1, (9) says the projected K is not a transversal, so it is not a Baer folder of a quotient loop. No hypothesis on F*(G/N) is used in (8).

For each nonempty I⊆{1,…,e}, let $`\mathcal C_I`$ be the A-class whose ith coordinate is an outer involution exactly for i∈I and is 1 otherwise. Baumeister–Stein, [arXiv:0908.2597v1](https://arxiv.org/html/0908.2597v1), Lemma 4.4, gives


```math
c_I:=|\mathcal C_I|=\prod_{i\in I}\frac{q_i(q_i-1)}2.
```


Their Lemma 4.5 gives the original fiber formula and, in particular, that every $`\mathcal C_I`$ occurs in the image of K. The corresponding v2 locators are Lemmas 4.5 and 4.6. Use their coordinatewise restriction, not an unrestricted interpretation of the introduction's global K̄ wording.

Work first in G/Φ. Let r_I be the number of elements of KΦ/Φ over a fixed x∈$`\mathcal C_I`$. Since τ fixes A and τK is conjugacy-invariant in the formal extension, twisted conjugation makes r_I independent of x. The number of A/B cosets whose coordinates are nonbase exactly on I is ∏_{i∈I}q_i; each lifts to n cosets of HΦ/Φ. The section image covers these cosets. Hence


```math
c_Ir_I\ge n\prod_{i\in I}q_i,\qquad
r_I\ge n\,\frac{2^{|I|}}{\prod_{i\in I}(q_i-1)}.            \tag{10}
```


Put $`\rho_I=\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits(1+x\mid V)`$ and $`s_I=\sum_{i\in I}(v_2(q_i-1)-1)`$. A lift over x belongs to the projected section only if it satisfies the affine inversion equation whose linear part is 1+x. A nonempty solution set has at most 2^{v−ρ_I} elements; each V-twisted-conjugacy orbit has size 2^{ρ_I}. Therefore


```math
r_I\in 2^{\rho_I}\mathbb Z_{>0},\quad
r_I\le2^{v-\rho_I},\quad
\rho_I\le w+s_I.                                            \tag{11}
```


Let r_∅=|KΦ/Φ∩V|. Its elements cover the n cosets over B, so r_∅≥n. When Φ≠1, simplicity and the exact quotient criterion give the strict **total** overlap


```math
r_\varnothing+\sum_{I\ne\varnothing} c_Ir_I
>n\prod_{i=1}^e(q_i+1).                                     \tag{12}
```


The same support lower bounds, orbit divisibilities, rank inequalities and strict total inequality hold after replacing Φ by N and (V,W̄) by (U,Z). Here N is τ-stable because τ fixes V and C is A-invariant, and the quotient images retain every support class. The index |U:Z| remains n. Equations (10)–(12) do not assert a strict inequality for each individual support, equality with the original-envelope fiber formula, normality of the support subloops, or a loop quotient.

The bounds control the module left after removing the stabilizer core; they do not bound C, m_U, or the nonabelian lifting through Φ. They neither prove Φ=1 nor exhibit a simple loop with non-elementary P. Their decisive remaining interface is whether the overlap allowed by (9) and (12) can coexist with the original group's twisted-conjugacy and transversal equations. ## Minimal normal layers inside a noncentral Frattini subgroup

**Deductions and finite module control with scoped independent reviews below.** Keep the full Bruck-envelope hypotheses at the start, allowing arbitrary P and noncentral Φ(P). If Φ(P)≠1, choose a minimal nonzero G⁺-invariant subgroup L of Ω₁(Z(Φ(P))), where G⁺=G⋊⟨τ̂⟩. This ambient subgroup is nonzero elementary abelian and characteristic in P. The fixed space C_L(P) is nonzero because a finite 2-group acting on a nonzero binary vector space has nonzero fixed points. It is G⁺-invariant, since P is normal, and minimality gives C_L(P)=L. The action therefore factors through A and τ̂. As τ induces the identity on A, its action on L commutes with A. The nonzero kernel of τ−1 is G⁺-invariant, so minimality gives τ|L=1. Thus

    L ≤ Φ(P)∩Z(P),   τ|L=1,   L is a simple binary A-module.       (13)

This neither makes L central in G nor proves that all of Φ has trivial A-action. It is an internal normal subgroup; no quotient folder is assumed. For a prescribed outer-involution image t and a selected marked element λ=τ̂k above it, conjugation by ℓ∈L changes the K-coordinate by the displacement (1+t)ℓ, since P and τ fix L pointwise. Marked conjugacy therefore makes the entire original t-fiber invariant under translation by (1+t)L. Transversality implies

    (W∩L)∩(1+t)L=0.                                           (14)

Moreover KL≠K. Otherwise 1∈K implies L⊆K and L∩H=1. Since L is normal and K is L-saturated, the image of K modulo L is a transversal to HL/L: two representatives in one HL-coset can be adjusted by L within K, and the original H-transversality identifies them. The exact C0001 quotient criterion then supplies a nontrivial loop kernel L and a proper quotient, since HL maps to the proper Borel product B<A. This contradicts simplicity. The conclusion uses proved transversality in this special case, not a general group-quotient-to-loop inference.

### The single-factor displacement test and its limit

Let A=PGL₂(q), q≥5, q−1 a power of two, and B a point Borel. Its order is q(q−1). The nonsplit torus of order q+1 acts regularly on the projective line, and the centralizer of the prescribed outer involution is its dihedral normalizer of order 2(q+1). Hence C_B(t) has order two. The outer class has size q(q−1)/2 and is one B-orbit. Since W_L=W∩L is B-stable, disjointness in (14) for one t is equivalent to disjointness for all prescribed t; these are not independent constraints.

The [uniform module theorem](bruck-central-frattini-all-quotients.md), reviewed at the recorded source revision, identifies the simple A-modules. The principal heart restricts to the absolutely simple binary B-module E of dimension q−1. If a nonprincipal binary simple L descends from an orbit of h absolute simples, then L|B≅E^h. Here is the Borel identification explicitly: over a splitting field, the odd translation group has each nontrivial linear character exactly once in the ordinary cuspidal restriction, by its values q−1 at identity and −1 elsewhere. The scalar complement permutes those characters transitively and freely, so the corresponding B-module is uniquely determined and simple. The augmentation module on the affine q points gives its binary form E and is absolutely simple. Scalar extension and descent yield E^h. The torus-weight calculation in the cited theorem also gives dim H¹(⟨t⟩,L)=2h, so rank(1+t|L)=h(q−3)/2.

If L|B is irreducible, W_L=0: a nonzero W_L would be all L and would put a nontrivial normal subgroup inside H. This covers the principal heart and h=1 nonprincipal simples; the trivial simple is excluded from H by the same corefreeness. For h>1, a B-submodule E^k would satisfy only

    k(q−1)+h(q−3)/2 ≤ h(q−1).                                (15)

This necessary dimension inequality allows k≤h/2. The proposed stronger local implication that (14) forces W_L=0 for every binary simple is false in the following finite module control.

For q=9, the 16-dimensional binary simple has three B-simple 8-dimensional submodules. The [GAP certificate](../code/verify_bruck_q9_layer_intersections.g) constructs the module with `IrreducibleModules(PGL(2,9),GF(2),16)`, checks its irreducibility and faithful generator-matched representation, and enumerates the complete B-submodule lattice with MeatAxe. For each of the 36 involutions outside PSL₂(9), the displacement rank is six, and its image meets each of the three B-submodules trivially: all 108 intersection dimensions are zero. The certificate also checks B-transitivity on that class. Root read and ran the final certificate with GAP 4.15.1, reproducing these assertions; its release provenance is in [the q=9 core manuscript](bruck-q9-central-core.md). The construction and submodule census use GAP/MeatAxe algorithms as explicit computational inputs.

This is a counterexample to a proposed implication about module subspaces. It does not realize L inside Φ(P), construct P or G, choose a marked section, check KL≠K for a loop, or establish existence of a Bruck envelope. Consequently the minimal-layer route needs a constraint from the actual Frattini extension or section beyond (14).

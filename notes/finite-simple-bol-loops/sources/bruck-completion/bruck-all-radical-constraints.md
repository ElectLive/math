# All-radical fixed-parameter Frattini constraints

**Argument with scoped independent reviews below.** This continues [C0010](../../claims/0010.md)  Let X be a finite simple nonassociative right Bruck loop with faithful Baer envelope (G,H,K), P=O₂(G), Φ=Φ(P), r=log₂|Φ|, V=P/Φ, W=P∩H, U=WΦ/Φ, and A=G/P=∏PGL₂(q_i), e≥1, q_i=9 or a Fermat prime at least 5. No restriction on P, τ|Φ, the exponent, or splitting is assumed. Let B=HP/P=∏B_i, R=Σ_i q_i(q_i−1)/2, B(A)=[1+2^e(3e+1)]|A|, and let C be the largest A-submodule of V inside U. Let m be the number of nontrivial simple A-composition factors of V/C. The [core-reduced Frattini theorem](bruck-frattini-shadow.md#core-reduced-module-and-all-support-shadow-bounds), independently reviewed at the recorded source revision, says m≥1 and v=dim V/C≤B(A)m.

Fixed-parameter bound:

  dim V ≤ (r+1)B(A)m+(r+1)r²[A:B]+r(R+3e+r),
  exp(P) divides 2^{r+1}, and exp(X) divides 2^{r+2}.

These are fixed-parameter bounds, not a uniform numerical bound or classification. The remaining source inputs are Baumeister–Stein [arXiv:0908.2597v1](https://arxiv.org/html/0908.2597v1), Theorem 1 and Lemmas 3.2(1)–(2), 3.4 and 4.1–4.5, particularly the canonical τ, P=(P∩H)(P∩K), the marked invariant set Λ=τhat K in the formal semidirect product G+=G⋊<τhat>, and full singleton-support outer-involution fibers. τ fixes V: it fixes W and inverts P∩K, and V is elementary. Its action on Φ is not assumed trivial.

1. Construct an abelian normal subgroup. Let N=C_P(Φ), characteristic in P and normal in G. An automorphism of Φ is determined by at most r generator images from Φ, so [P:N]≤|Aut Φ|≤2^{r²}. Thus U_N=(W∩N)Φ/Φ≤U has codimension at most r² in U; it is B-stable since N normalizes under H. Let

  C₀=⋂_{a∈A} a(C∩U_N).

C∩U_N is B-stable, so at most [A:B] distinct conjugates occur. Thus c=dim C−dim C₀≤[A:B]r². Put E=N∩π⁻¹(C₀), W₀=W∩E, and Z_Φ=N∩Φ=Z(Φ). The projection W₀→C₀ is onto because C₀⊆U_N. Therefore E=W₀ Z_Φ. Since Z_Φ≤Z(N), [E,E]=[W₀,W₀]≤H. The group E is normal in G, so [E,E] is normal in G and hence lies in core_G(H)=1. Thus E is abelian. It is τ-stable: N and Φ are characteristic in P, and τ fixes V pointwise. Its quotient E/W₀ is a homomorphic image of Z_Φ and has order at most 2^r.

2. Marked involution rank. For λ=τhat k∈Λ, let α be conjugation by λ on E and t=kP∈A. Since E is abelian, 1−α:E→E is an endomorphism. If its image met W₀ nontrivially, choose x∈E with λ′=xλx⁻¹=dλ for 1≠d∈W₀. Writing λ=τhat k and λ′=τhat k′ gives k′=τ(d)k=dk, because τ fixes H pointwise and d∈W₀≤H. Both k and k′ lie in K by conjugacy invariance of Λ, and they are distinct representatives of the same H-coset, contradicting the section property. Hence im(1−α)∩W₀=1 and |im(1−α)|≤|E:W₀|≤2^r. Its image in C₀ is (1+t)C₀, so rank(1+t|C₀)≤r. In particular this applies to every singleton-coordinate outer involution, by the source fiber theorem. The union of their classes generates A; its cardinality is R. The simultaneous displacement map on C₀ therefore gives

  dim C₀ ≤ dim C₀^A+rR.                                  (i)

3. Bound fixed vectors via cocycles. Let W₁ be the preimage of C₀^A in W₀. For w∈W₁ and g∈G, gwg⁻¹w⁻¹ lies in E∩Φ=Z_Φ because E is normal and w has A-fixed image in V. E centralizes Z_Φ, so Q=G/E acts on Z_Φ. The map δ_w(gE)=gwg⁻¹w⁻¹ is a well-defined 1-cocycle Q→Z_Φ: w centralizes E since E is abelian. The assignment w↦δ_w is a group homomorphism because W₁≤E centralizes Z_Φ, and is injective because its kernel is Z(G)∩H=1. For a generating set of Q of size d(Q), the number of cocycles is at most |Z_Φ|^{d(Q)}, so

  dim C₀^A ≤ log₂|W₁| ≤ r d(Q).                            (ii)

A is generated by 3e elements (upper unipotent, primitive diagonal, swap in each factor). Lift those 3e generators of A to Q, and adjoin lifts of a generating set of the normal subgroup P/E. A finite group of order |P/E| has a generating set of at most log₂|P/E| elements, so d(Q)≤3e+log₂|P/E|. Since E maps onto C₀, |E|≥2^{dim C₀}; therefore

  log₂|P/E| ≤ r+dim V−dim C₀ = r+v+c.

Substitute in (i)–(ii) and add v+c=dim V−dim C₀:

  dim V ≤ v+c+rR+r(3e+r+v+c)
        = (r+1)v+(r+1)c+r(R+3e+r).

Using v≤B(A)m and c≤[A:B]r² yields the claimed dimension bound. No commutator form, Φ-exponent bound, or quotient-folder assertion is used.

For exponents, every p∈P has p²∈Φ, whose order is 2^r, so p^{2^{r+1}}=1. Every k∈K has k²∈P because its image in A is an involution or identity, giving k^{2^{r+2}}=1. This bounds the loop exponent.

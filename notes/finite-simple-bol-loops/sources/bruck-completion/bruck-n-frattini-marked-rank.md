# A marked-rank restriction for deleted-five Frattini modules

**Continuation of [0323:C0010](../../claims/0010.md), with scoped independent reviews below.** Let (G,H,K) be the faithful canonical envelope of a finite simple nonassociative Bruck loop of 2-power exponent. Assume P=O₂(G) has class at most two and exponent at most four, G/P≅S₅, and

    Z=Φ(P)≅N,          V=P/Z≅X⊕U^b,          X∈{J,P₆}, b≥0,  (1)

where N is the deleted-five simple, J=F₂⁶/⟨1⟩, P₆=F₂⁶ in the six-point S₅ action, and U=N⊗F₂C₂ with sign swapping the two N copies. The subgroup Z is elementary abelian and central in P, but is not assumed central in G. This note establishes necessary marked-orbit and quadratic restrictions; it does not classify the group extensions, stabilizers or loops.

The original Bruck envelope, inversion and fiber inputs are Baumeister–Stein, [arXiv:0908.2597v1](https://arxiv.org/html/0908.2597v1), Theorem 1 and Lemmas 3.2(1)–(2), 3.4 and 4.1–4.5, with source interface and scoped review recorded in [bruck-frattini-shadow.md](bruck-frattini-shadow.md) at the recorded source revision. The N-block and dual-number module identifications are in [bruck-s5-module-blocks.md](bruck-s5-module-blocks.md), source the recorded source revision. The parallel M-Frattini marked-orbit argument has the separate reviews in [bruck-one-m-noncentral-reduction.md](bruck-one-m-noncentral-reduction.md), but does not itself certify the new N-specific deductions below. No projected K is assumed to define a quotient folder.

## The actual marked-orbit map

The Bruck factorization P=(H∩P)(K∩P) makes τ act trivially on V. It also fixes Z, because Z is generated by squares and τ(p)=pz for z∈Z implies τ(p²)=p². The square q:V→Z is S₅-equivariant, with alternating bilinear polar β:V×V→Z given by commutators. Put W=H∩P, w=log₂|W|, and let t be a transposition.

Fix a selected marked involution λ=τhat k above t and let α be conjugation by λ on P. For v∈C_V(t), define

    ℓ_λ(v)=α(y)y⁻¹ mod (1+t)Z,

where y is any lift of v. Changing the lift changes the value by (1+t)Z, and the map is linear on C_V(t). The image of C_P(λ) in V is ker ℓ_λ, while the central kernel is C_Z(t). Consequently

    |λ^P|=2^{ρ_V+ρ_Z+rank ℓ_λ},                              (2)

where ρ_V,ρ_Z are the ranks of 1+t on V,Z.

The original selected fiber over t has size |P:W|/2. It is a union of P-orbits, so (2) bounds each orbit by that size. There is also a lower bound on w: every marked element over t is λy for a unique y∈P, and involutivity requires α(y)y=1. Its shadow must lie in C_V(t); for any fixed shadow there are at most |C_Z(t)| possible lifts. Thus the entire set of such elements has size at most 2^{dim V−ρ_V+dim Z−ρ_Z}. Comparing with the selected fiber gives

    ρ_V+ρ_Z−1 ≤ w ≤ dim V+dim Z−ρ_V−ρ_Z−1.                (3)

The lower bound is independent of orbit size and is needed in the P₆ case.

## F₂₀ and fiber alternatives

The S₅ action on the six Sylow-5 normalizers gives a transposition of cycle type 2³ on P₆. On restriction to F=F₂₀, the nonfixed summand of V is S^{1+2b}, where S is the four-dimensional simple, and the F-fixed part has dimension 1 for J and 2 for P₆. The nonfixed block is semisimple: it comes from the nontrivial C₅ characters, on which C₄ acts freely. The restriction of N to F is S and is irreducible. If W∩Z were nonzero, F-invariance would give W∩Z=Z, a nontrivial G-normal subgroup inside H, contrary to core-freeness. Hence W injects into V and its dimension is four times a nonfixed multiplicity plus at most the stated fixed dimension.

The transposition ranks are

    ρ_Z=1,  ρ_U=4,  ρ_J=2,  ρ_P₆=3.

The [standalone verifier](../code/verify_bruck_n_marked_rank.py) checks the paired five-/six-point S₅ action, |F|=20, these ranks and the fixed dimensions. Combining (3) with the F-module dimensions leaves exactly

| X | w | fixed F-dimension of W | nonfixed multiplicity | upper bound on rank ℓ_λ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| J | 4b+4 | 0 | b+1 | 1 |
| J | 4b+5 | 1 | b+1 | 0 |
| P₆ | 4b+4 | 0 | b+1 | 1 |
| P₆ | 4b+5 | 1 | b+1 | 0 |

For P₆, the apparent w=4b+2 case violates the lower bound in (3), and w=4b+3 would require three F-fixed dimensions. These are necessary alternatives, not claims that each occurs.

## A valid polar identity

For every v∈V,

    ℓ_λ((1+t)v)=β(v,tv) mod (1+t)Z.                           (4)

Indeed, choose y∈P above v and set z=α(y)y⁻¹. The shadow of z is (1+t)v and α(z)=z⁻¹, so α(z)z⁻¹=z². The square formula gives

    z²=q(v)+q(tv)+β(v,tv)=(1+t)q(v)+β(v,tv).

This proves (4). In particular, the statement that an arbitrary involutory lift alone forces β(v,tv)∈(1+t)Z is not used; that stronger assertion is false in general.

The right side gives a linear C_{S₅}(t)-equivariant map

    d:(1+t)V → C_Z(t)/(1+t)Z,
    d((1+t)v)=β(v,tv)+(1+t)Z.                                 (5)

Well-definedness follows from (4). Its additivity also follows directly because the cross term is (1+t)β(v,tw); its image is fixed by t. For the deleted-five module and t=(12), a fixed vector has coordinates (a,a,b,c,d) with b+c+d=0, while (1+t)Z is the line (1,1,0,0,0). The quotient in (5) is the even subspace of the three remaining coordinates, the irreducible 2-dimensional F₂S₃-module. Thus image(d) is either zero or that entire space. If d≠0, every ℓ_λ has rank at least 2, contrary to the table. Therefore every actual envelope under (1) satisfies

    β(v,tv)∈(1+t)Z for all v∈V.                              (6)

This is a consequence of the selected marked-fiber bound, not a general property of involutory lifts.

For P₆, both P₆ and U restrict freely to ⟨t⟩, so C_V(t)=(1+t)V. Equation (4) and (6) force ℓ_λ=0, independently of λ. For J, H¹(⟨t⟩,J) has dimension one and H¹(⟨t⟩,U)=0, so ℓ_λ factors through a one-dimensional quotient. The selected fiber consists of:

| X | w | allowed ranks | number of P-orbits |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| J | 4b+4 | rank 0 or 1 | 2 or 1 respectively |
| J | 4b+5 | rank 0 | 1 |
| P₆ | 4b+4 | rank 0 | 2 |
| P₆ | 4b+5 | rank 0 | 1 |

In the first J row, a rank-one orbit has the size of the entire fiber; if none has rank one, the fiber is the union of two rank-zero orbits. These are actual marked orbits in G, not orbits of a quotient folder.

## Consequences for quadratic components

The verifier solves the equivariance and (6) equations for the fixed summands X, U, X⊗U and U⊗U using the two synchronized S₅ generators. For both X choices it obtains

| component | full dimension | dimension satisfying (6) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Quad(X,N) | 1 | 0 |
| Quad(U,N) | 2 | 1 |
| Hom(X⊗U,N), X=J | 1 | 1 |
| Hom(X⊗U,N), X=P₆ | 2 | 2 |
| Hom(U⊗U,N) | 2 | 2 |

The surviving one-dimensional U quadratic space is generated by the linear top map (a,b)↦a+b in the two-copy coordinates. The test imposes (6) on basis vectors and their pairwise sums, which suffices because β(v,tv) is quadratic in v. A quadratic map on a direct sum is uniquely determined by its restrictions to summands and its polar cross maps; hence these fixed-component equations give genuine restrictions for arbitrary b: q|X=0, each q|U is zero or the top map, while the displayed X–U and U–U cross spaces retain their full computed dimensions. This decomposition does not infer all-b behavior from a small-b sample.

No conclusion that the W-shadow misses soc(U^b) is made. An F-submodule of that shadow need not lift to an S₅-normal subgroup of P when the central preimage is nonsplit. In particular the computed components do not by themselves prove a passive-block splitting or exclude any of the remaining extensions.

## Lifted socle bound

This section extends the reviewed source the recorded source revision; its new deduction and the additional verifier check have the independent reviews below. Keep all the hypotheses above. Put Y=soc(U^b)≅N^b and E=π⁻¹(Y)≤P, where π:P→V=P/Z. The component calculation above gives q|Y=0: on each U the surviving quadratic map is the top map, and it vanishes on the socle. The verifier now also checks that both basis maps in Hom_{S₅}(U⊗U,N) vanish on soc(U)×soc(U). Thus all cross terms on Y vanish, q|Y=0, and E is elementary abelian. This is a statement about E as a group; no S₅-module structure on E is assumed.

Let F=F₂₀, let B=π(W), and write T=B∩Y. The H action on V factors through F, so T is F-stable. Restricting N to the normal C₅ gives the irreducible four-dimensional module with endomorphism field F₁₆. The quotient C₄ acts on that field by Frobenius, so End_F(N)=F₂. Consequently every F-submodule of N^b is N⊗C for a unique subspace C≤F₂^b. In particular T is S₅-stable; write c=dim C. Since a transposition t has rank one on N,

    dim(T∩(1+t)Y)=c.                                          (7)

Fix a selected marked involution λ over t and put D=(1+λ)E, using the linear conjugation action on the elementary abelian E. Since U is free on restriction to ⟨t⟩, C_Y(t)⊂(1+t)V. Equations (4) and (6) therefore give ℓ_λ(v)=0 for every v∈C_Y(t). Every such v has a λ-fixed lift in E: the obstruction to a fixed lift is exactly ℓ_λ(v). The kernel on Z is C_Z(t), of dimension 3. Thus dim C_E(λ)=3b+3 and

    dim D=b+1,  D∩Z=(1+t)Z.                                  (8)

The second equality follows because D projects onto (1+t)Y, of dimension b, and contains (1+t)Z, of dimension one.

For u∈T∩(1+t)Y, there is a unique w∈W∩E above u, because W∩Z=0; choose any d∈D above u. Define

    Δ(u)=w−d+(1+t)Z ∈ Z/(1+t)Z.

By (8) this is a well-defined linear map. If Δ(u)=0, then w∈D. A nonzero element of W∩D would be the product of λ with an E-conjugate of λ and would lie in H∩Λ², contradicting the folder transversal condition. Hence Δ is injective. The target has dimension 3, and (7) yields the uniform necessary bound

    dim_F₂ C = c ≤ 3.                                         (9)

This conclusion uses the F-action on the shadow, not an assumed F-action or splitting on E. It does not classify the lifted stabilizer, the extension, or the resulting loops. The verifier's added rank test checks vanishing for the entire two-dimensional U–U map space, rather than merely for a chosen sample map.

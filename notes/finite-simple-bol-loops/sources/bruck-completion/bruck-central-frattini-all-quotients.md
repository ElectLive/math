# Central-Frattini core vanishing for every allowed product quotient

**Mira Cult**

**Theorem with scoped independent reviews below, conditional on the cited source and envelope inputs.** This continues [C0010](../../claims/0010.md), without a new ID. Let $`(G,H,K)`$ be the faithful canonical envelope of a finite simple nonassociative right Bruck loop of 2-power exponent. Put $`P=O_2(G)`$, $`\Phi=\Phi(P)`$, $`V=P/\Phi`$, $`W=P\cap H`$, $`U=W\Phi/\Phi`$, and let $`C`$ be the largest $`G/P`$-submodule of $`V`$ contained in $`U`$. Suppose $`\Phi\leq Z(G)`$ and $`G/P=A=\mathop{\mathrm{PGL}}\nolimits_2(q)`$, where $`q\ge5`$ is an odd prime power and $`q-1`$ is a power of two. The conclusion is **$`C=0`$**. It includes $`q=9`$. The final section extends the theorem to every allowed product quotient; neither argument treats noncentral $`\Phi`$.

The envelope, marked-section and original fiber inputs are Baumeister–Stein, [arXiv:0908.2597v1](https://arxiv.org/html/0908.2597v1), Theorem 1 and Lemmas 3.2(1)–(2), 3.4 and 4.1–4.5, in the precise form used by the [central-Frattini constraints](bruck-central-frattini-constraints.md) and [q=9 theorem](bruck-q9-central-core.md). In particular $`B=HP/P`$ is a point stabilizer, $`\Phi`$ and $`W\Phi`$ are elementary, $`W\cap\Phi=1`$, the canonical inversion $`\tau`$ fixes $`W\Phi`$ pointwise and acts trivially on $`A`$, and $`C^A=0`$. A marked involution fiber above the prescribed nonsplit outer involution $`t`$ is nonempty, has positive power-of-two cardinality, and is invariant under lifts of $`C_A(t)`$. We use the **original** fiber, not a putative quotient folder. Prior input reviews recorded in those sources do not review this candidate.

## Module identification and source interface

Write $`L=\mathop{\mathrm{PSL}}\nolimits_2(q)`$, let $`\Omega=\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_q)`$, and let $`D=\mathbb F_2^{\Omega,\mathrm{even}}/\langle\mathbf1\rangle`$. The following identification is deduced below; the source matching and deductions have the scoped independent reviews recorded at the end:

1. $`D`$ is the nontrivial principal absolutely simple $`\mathbb F_2A`$-module. Every other nontrivial absolutely simple $`\overline{\mathbb F}_2A`$-module $`S`$ restricts to a degree-$`q-1`$ defect-zero simple of $`L`$, and is the reduction of one of the two ordinary degree-$`q-1`$ extensions of that $`L`$-character. Its associated nonsplit torus character $`\theta`$ below is nontrivial. Every binary simple other than $`\mathbf1,D`$ is obtained by Frobenius descent from one orbit of these absolute simples.
2. For the canonical trivial-source lift of each such $`S`$, the extension sign is the one with $`\chi(t)=2`$. If $`R\cong C_{(q+1)/2}`$ is the odd subgroup of the nonsplit torus centralizing $`t`$, then
   
```math
\chi(tr)=\theta(r)+\theta(r)^{-1}\quad(r\in R).\tag{1}
```


The following source trail and matching have the scoped independent reviews below. Saunders, [arXiv:2002.04183v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2002.04183v3), §3 notation and Theorem 3.8, gives the characteristic-two $`L`$-modules $`N_i`$ and their projectivity, with dimension $`q-1`$; the split-torus families $`M_i`$ disappear here because the split torus has 2-power order. Koshitani–Lassueur, [7 March 2019 preprint](https://classueur.github.io/maths/lassueur-Dateien/preprints/SplendidDihedral.pdf), §5.1 Lemma 5.1 and §5.2 Lemma 5.3 proof, printed pp.16–19, identify the principal $`A`$-module as $`\mathop{\mathrm{Ind}}\nolimits_L^A S`$. For the ordinary extension step, White, [*Character Degrees of Extensions of PSL₂(q)*](https://www.math.kent.edu/~white/papers/autpsl2.pdf), 25 July 2011 author preprint, Lemma 4.4, printed p.5, and Plaza, [2016 dissertation](https://udspace.udel.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/a1384905-fca7-4229-aa00-8598a7490809/content), Table 2.1, printed p.18, are the ordinary-character sources. Plaza's cuspidal row is $`-\beta(z)-\beta(z)^{-1}`$ on a nonsplit torus element and $`-2\beta(t)`$ at its involution. Choosing the sign with $`\beta(t)=-1`$ gives (1), with $`\theta=\beta|_R`$. The class-count argument below matches and exhausts these reductions, including $`q=9`$.

Set $`m=(q+1)/2`$, which is odd, and let $`R`$ be the order-$`m`$ subgroup of the nonsplit torus. Plaza, §2.4, printed pp.16–18, classifies the PGL conjugacy classes and supplies the character table. Because the split torus has 2-power order, the 2-regular classes are the identity, the single nonidentity unipotent class, and the inverse pairs of nonidentity elements of $`R`$. Thus there are exactly $`2+(m-1)/2`$ such classes and the same number of absolutely simple characteristic-two modules.

For each nontrivial $`\theta\in\widehat R`$ up to inversion, extend $`\theta`$ uniquely to a character $`\beta`$ of the full cyclic nonsplit torus by $`\beta(t)=-1`$. Its order exceeds two, so Plaza's Table 2.1 supplies the irreducible cuspidal character $`\eta_\beta`$ of degree $`q-1`$. Its restriction to $`L`$ is irreducible: $`R`$ is the nonsplit torus of $`L`$, and Plaza's row agrees on every $`L`$-class with the degree-$`q-1`$ row $`\theta_j`$ in Saunders v3, Table 1, using matching inverse-pair parameters on $`R`$. On the two unipotent classes both values are $`-1`$, on nonidentity split semisimple elements both are zero, and on $`R`$ both are $`-\theta-\theta^{-1}`$. Saunders' table includes $`(m-1)/2=(q-1)/4`$ such rows. Their 2-defect is zero because their degree equals $`|L|_2=q-1`$. The defect-zero theorem therefore says reduction modulo two is irreducible and projective on $`L`$. The reduction of any invariant lattice for $`\eta_\beta`$ is consequently irreducible on $`A`$, since its restriction to $`L`$ is irreducible.

Different inverse pairs of $`\theta`$ give distinct Brauer characters on $`R`$: ordinary characters of the odd cyclic group are linearly independent, and odd-order eigenvalues have unique Teichmüller lifts. These $`(m-1)/2`$ modules are distinct from the principal simples $`1,D`$, whose dimensions are $`1,q-1`$ but whose restrictions to $`L`$ lie in the principal block. The class count therefore exhausts all absolute simples. The two extensions of a given defect-zero $`L`$-character differ by the linear character of $`A/L`$, which is trivial after reduction in characteristic two; the choice $`\beta(t)=-1`$ is the extension needed for (1). This argument uses the ordinary and modular defect-zero theorems and Brauer's count of simples by 2-regular classes; its complete source matching and the theorem applications have the scoped reviews below.

The defect and character calculation is otherwise as follows. A covering block's defect group intersects $`L`$ in a defect group of the covered block, after a suitable choice. The statement is reproduced in [*2-Blocks with Homocyclic Defect*](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/files/177287954/FULL_TEXT.PDF), Theorem 2.4.4(2), printed p.38, citing Alperin, *Local Representation Theory* (1993 edition), Theorem 15.1, whose original text has not been accessed. A defect-zero $`L`$-block and $`[A:L]=2`$ force covering defect at most 2. It is exactly 2 because $`\dim S=q-1`$ is not divisible by $`|A|_2=2(q-1)`$, whereas a projective $`A`$-module has Sylow-2-divisible dimension. Hiss–Lassueur, [*The classification of the trivial source modules in blocks with cyclic defect groups*](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Gerhard.Hiss/Preprints/tscyclicrevision.pdf), arXiv:1908.07833v2 (7 April 2020), §4.6, printed p.12, says over an algebraically closed field that the unique nonprojective simple in a defect-$`C_2`$ block is trivial source. The ordinary character of a lift reducing to this simple module is irreducible: otherwise its nonzero ordinary constituents would give a sum of at least two nonzero Brauer characters upon reduction. Its restriction to $`L`$ is the unique ordinary lift of the defect-zero simple, so it is one of the two ordinary extensions just identified. A permutation lattice has nonnegative trace at $`t`$, as does its trivial-source summand via its $`t`$-permutation basis, so the canonical lift has $`\chi(t)=2`$, selecting the claimed sign once the two ordinary lifts have been matched. The original Alperin text was not accessible; the covering-block statement was checked in the cited thesis. The resulting calculation has the scoped reviews below.

For completeness, the Brauer-quotient character step can be checked directly. A trivial-source lattice has a basis permuted by $`T=\langle t\rangle`$; after restriction to $`T`$, fixed basis vectors survive in $`S(T)=S^T/(1+t)S`$ and each exchanged pair vanishes. For $`r\in R`$, odd-order idempotents split the lattice after an unramified splitting extension; each $`r`$-eigensummand is a direct summand of a permutation $`T`$-lattice and is again a permutation lattice for this 2-group. Therefore the trace of $`t`$ on each such summand equals the dimension of the corresponding eigensummand of $`S(T)`$. Equivalently, the Brauer character of $`S(T)`$ at $`r`$ equals $`\chi(tr)`$. Thus (1) yields exactly the weights $`\theta,\theta^{-1}`$, each once, over a splitting field. Frobenius descent replaces these by their 2-power conjugates, all with the same order and kernel in cyclic $`R`$. Duality only inverts weights. Hence if $`F`$ is one nonprincipal binary simple, then every nonzero vector of

```math
H^1(T,(F^*)^m)
```

for any $`m\ge0`$ has $`R`$-orbit length exactly $`d=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits(\theta)>1`$. This uses one binary isotype: different isotypes can have different $`d`$, and mixed-isotype orbit counting is invalid.

## Cohomology and local module calculations

Saunders, Proposition 4.2, directly gives $`H^1(A,D)\cong\mathbb F_2`$ and $`H^2(A,D)=0`$, by absolute scalar extension and descent. The dot form makes $`D`$ self-dual. The nonzero class in $`\mathop{\mathrm{Ext}}\nolimits^1_A(D,\mathbf1)`$ is represented by the even permutation module $`J^*`$ in

```math
0\to\mathbf1\to J^*\to D\to0.\tag{2}
```

Nonsplitting follows from 2-transitivity: an invariant functional on the even module takes one value on all $`e_i+e_j`$, and the three-term relation forces that value to vanish.

The Borel $`B=\mathbb F_q\rtimes\mathbb F_q^\times`$ has cyclic Sylow 2-subgroup of order $`q-1`$. Restriction of $`D`$ to it is the augmentation module on the affine $`q`$ points, and is regular on that Sylow subgroup; moreover $`\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_B(D,\mathbf1)=0`$. The selected $`t`$ is fixed-point-free on $`\Omega`$, so both $`D`$ and $`J^*`$ have $`(1+t)`$-rank $`(q-1)/2`$, and $`D|_T`$ is free.

For a nonprincipal binary simple $`F`$, the interface and Hochschild–Serre give $`H^i(A,F)=H^i(A,F^*)=0`$ for all $`i`$: its restriction to $`L`$ is projective and has no $`L`$-fixed vectors. Its restriction to $`B`$ is projective: the restriction to the index-two cyclic 2-Sylow subgroup of $`B\cap L`$ is free; if a generator of the cyclic 2-Sylow of $`B`$ has a unipotent Jordan block of length $`\ell`$, its square has blocks of lengths $`\lceil\ell/2\rceil,\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor`$. Freeness on the index-two subgroup forces $`\ell`$ to be the full Sylow order, so the original restriction is free. The ordinary degree-$`q-1`$ row has value $`-1`$ at each nonidentity unipotent. Averaging over the odd additive group $`\mathbb F_q`$ therefore gives no fixed vectors, and in particular $`\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_B(F,\mathbf1)=0`$. These assertions survive Frobenius descent and duality.

## Single-factor collision argument

Suppose $`C\ne0`$; take one simple binary submodule $`F\le C`$. The input $`C^A=0`$ excludes $`F=\mathbf1`$. Let $`E`$ be its preimage in $`P`$, $`W_E=W\cap E`$, and $`R_0=C_P(E)`$. Then $`E=W_E\Phi`$ is elementary, $`W\le R_0`$, and the commutator map gives an $`A`$-equivariant injection

```math
P/R_0\hookrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathbb F_2}(F,\Phi).\tag{3}
```

Because $`\Phi`$ is trivial, the target and its submodule $`P/R_0`$ are $`F^*`$-isotypic and semisimple, even when $`F`$ is not absolutely simple. The preceding $`H^2(A,F^*)=0`$ splits $`G/R_0`$, and $`H^1(B,F^*)=0`$ conjugates its complement so that its $`B`$-part is the image of $`H`$. The actual $`W_E`$ is then $`B`$-stable. Since $`\tau`$ fixes $`E`$ pointwise and acts trivially on $`A`$, $`\tau(g)g^{-1}\in R_0`$ for all $`g\in G`$; hence the formal marked group maps to $`G/R_0`$ with $`\widehat\tau\mapsto1`$.

If $`F=D`$, equation (2) and the one-dimensional Ext group decompose the extension $`E`$ into one active $`J^*`$ or $`D`$ summand and residual trivial summands. The $`B`$-stable complement $`W_E`$ has no projection to the residual trivial summands, by $`\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_B(D,1)=0`$. The rank $`(q-1)/2>1`$ gives $`(1+t)E\cap W_E\ne0`$. Since $`D|_T`$ is free, $`H^1(T,P/R_0)=0`$; a selected marked element over $`t`$ is $`P`$-conjugate to one whose quotient image is pure $`t`$. It acts as $`t`$ on $`E`$, because $`R_0`$ and $`\tau`$ centralize $`E`$.

If $`F\ne D`$, $`\mathop{\mathrm{Ext}}\nolimits^1_A(F,1)=0`$ splits $`E=F\oplus\Phi`$, and $`\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_B(F,1)=0`$ makes the actual complement $`W_E=F`$. Project the original marked fiber over $`t`$ to $`H^1(T,P/R_0)`$. Lifts of $`R`$ in the chosen complement preserve that fiber and biject label fibers. By the isotypic calculation, every nonzero label orbit has size $`d>1`$, an odd number. If zero were absent, any prime dividing $`d`$ would divide the fiber size, contradicting its positive power-of-two size. Thus a selected marked element has zero label and, after $`P`$-conjugacy, projects to pure $`t`$. Since a nontrivial simple $`A`$-module is faithful (a nontrivial kernel contains $`L`$, leaving only the trivial simple of $`A/L\cong C_2`$), $`(1+t)F\ne0`$.

In either case choose $`x\in E`$ whose marked displacement is a nonidentity $`w\in W_E`$. Conjugation preserves the marked set and $`\tau(w)=w`$, so the two section elements have the same $`H`$-coset, contrary to transversality. This proves the stated conclusion **conditional on the cited representation-theoretic and envelope inputs**; the source matching and assembled argument have the exact-source reviews below.

No computation is asserted here. The existing q=9 finite controls and their limitations are recorded in [the q=9 manuscript](bruck-q9-central-core.md). The proof reviews below are distinct from those finite controls.

## Product extension

Let A=∏ A_i, A_i=PGL₂(q_i), in the product Bruck-envelope setting of the preceding single-factor argument, with Φ(P)≤Z(G). Assume for each A_i the single-factor module interface and its consequences exactly as stated there, including H²(A_i,F*)=H¹(B_i,F*)=0 for every nontrivial binary simple F, the principal-heart extension and rank facts, and, for each nonprincipal F, the existence of an odd integer d_F>1 such that every nonzero R_i-orbit in H¹(<t_i>,(F*)^k) has length d_F. The conclusion C=0 then extends to this product, without global splitting.

Suppose C≠0 and choose a simple F₂A-submodule D₀≤C. It is nontrivial since C^A=0. For some factor A_i, D₀ restricts nontrivially. Its restriction to A_i is isotypic semisimple: indeed the Jacobson radical of F₂A_i generates a nilpotent two-sided ideal in F₂A and therefore kills the simple A-module. Restriction is thus semisimple. The central idempotents of the semisimple quotient commute with the remaining factors, so each binary isotypic component is A-invariant; simplicity permits only one. Equivalently, the simple module arises from one component of the tensor product of the semisimple quotients of the factor group algebras; finite fields are perfect, so tensoring their finite endomorphism fields gives a product of fields, and restriction to one factor is a direct sum of copies of one binary simple F. This does not assert that F is absolutely simple. Write D₀|A_i≅F^n with n>0.

Put E=π⁻¹(D₀)≤P, W_E=W∩E, R₀=C_P(E). From the central-Frattini input E=W_EΦ is elementary abelian, W≤R₀, and the commutator gives an A_i-equivariant injection P/R₀→Hom_F₂(D₀,Φ). Because Φ is pointwise fixed, the target and its submodule P/R₀ are isotypic semisimple sums of F*. Therefore H²(A_i,P/R₀)=0 and H¹(B_i,P/R₀)=0. In the full preimage of A_i in G/R₀, choose a quotient complement A_i⁰. Let H_i=H∩π⁻¹(B_i), where B_i is embedded in A with all other coordinates 1. It maps onto B_i, and its image in G/R₀ is a complement to P/R₀, since H∩P=W≤R₀. Conjugating A_i⁰ by an element of P/R₀ makes its B_i-complement equal to this actual H_i image. Since R₀ centralizes E, A_i⁰ acts on E and its B_i stabilizes the actual W_E.

The canonical τ fixes E pointwise and induces the identity on A: K generates G, and every K-image in A is an involution or 1. Hence τ(g)g⁻¹∈P centralizes E, so lies in R₀. The marked extension maps to G/R₀ with τhat→1. For any selected marked involution in the original singleton-support fiber over t_i, its image is p t_i with p∈P/R₀ fixed by t_i. P-conjugacy changes p by a (1+t_i)-coboundary. These steps use no global complement and no quotient folder.

If F is the principal heart D_i, the restriction of E as A_i-module is an extension of D_i^n by trivial Φ. The single-factor Ext^1 calculation decomposes this into active J_i* and D_i summands plus residual trivial kernel; W_E is a B_i-stable complement with no projection to the residual kernel. More explicitly, write the active extension as (J_i*)^s⊕D_i^{n−s}, with 0≤s≤n, and remove the residual trivial kernel using Hom_{B_i}(D_i,1)=0. The actual W_E has codimension s in this active space, while (1+t_i) has rank n(q_i−1)/2. Their intersection therefore has dimension at least n(q_i−1)/2−s>0. Since D_i|<t_i> is free, H¹(<t_i>,P/R₀)=0, so conjugate a selected marked involution by P to make its local quotient image the pure t_i. Its action on E is then t_i, since R₀ and τ centralize E. Conjugating it by an element of E whose displacement is a nonidentity w∈W_E produces two selected marked elements with K-coordinates in one H-coset, contradiction.

If F is nonprincipal, Ext^1_{A_i}(F,1)=0 and Hom_{B_i}(F,1)=0 make E≅F^n⊕Φ with the actual W_E=F^n. Map the original marked fiber over the singleton t_i to H¹(<t_i>,P/R₀). Lifts from the chosen local complement of R_i≤C_{A_i}(t_i) preserve this fiber and permute its label fibers bijectively. Every nonzero label has orbit size d_F, even for an arbitrary number of F* copies and even if F has a nontrivial finite endomorphism field, by the explicitly assumed single-isotype interface. The original fiber has size |P:W|·2/(q_i−1), a positive power of two. If the zero label were absent this size would be divisible by a prime divisor of d_F, contradiction. A selected marked element can therefore be P-conjugated to project to pure t_i. The nontrivial simple F is faithful on t_i under the single-factor interface, so its displacement on W_E is nonzero and produces the same collision.

Thus C contains no nontrivial simple A-submodule and contains no trivial one by C^A=0; hence C=0. The result is conditional on the full stated single-factor module interface (including its character matching, reviewed below), and on the central-Frattini, canonical inversion, original-fiber and cohomological inputs. It gives no source-independent verification of that interface, no claim for noncentral Φ, and no bound on e or module multiplicities.

The reviewed central-Frattini shadow bound now becomes d≤B_A m and r≤d(d+1)/2, where d=dim(P/Φ), r=dim Φ, m is the core-reduced nontrivial composition-factor count, and B_A=[1+2^e(3e+1)]|A|. Thus fixed A,m bounds radical order without an independent Frattini parameter. This deduction uses the preceding argument and stated inputs, does not bound A or m, and does not solve the residual extension/section classification.

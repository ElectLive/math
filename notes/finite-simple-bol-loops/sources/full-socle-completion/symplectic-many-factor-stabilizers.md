## Original-group interface

Set P=(I∩S)^(∞). This is characteristic in I∩S, hence normal in I. If P admits a nonzero proper fixed space in the natural module, I preserves it. The [independently reviewed subspace/decomposition result](symplectic-decomposition-actions.md) at `899203054af332971f56c53557c6844e543f3288` excludes every proper nonzero I-invariant natural subspace in characteristic two: maximality is used in the original M to identify the corresponding action; the result uses its exact recorded isotropic and nondegenerate action inputs. The residual P is nontrivial, since I∩S has at least two nonsolvable composition factors. Its natural action is faithful. Thus C_V(P) cannot equal V; consequently C_V(P)=0 in any surviving configuration in natural dimension at least six.

If P fixes a plus-type quadratic refinement Q0 of the natural symplectic form, uniqueness follows: the difference of two refinements is the square of a linear functional, and C_V(P)=0 via the invariant symplectic form. Every semilinear symplectic normalizer of P therefore preserves the unique normalized refinement; I does so. Maximality in M gives the full plus-refinement stabilizer, as this stabilizer is proper. The present plus branches have even natural dimension at least 8, including the boundary (dimension,q)=(8,2). For these parameters Ω⁺(V,Q0) is nonabelian simple and has index 2 in O⁺(V,Q0); Kleidman–Liebeck1990 §2.1 Table2.1.B and Theorem2.1.3, pp.15–16, supply this standard orthogonal simplicity statement and the natural symplectic automorphism description is retained from the reviewed geometric interface. Hence I∩S=O⁺(V,Q0) has exactly one nonsolvable composition factor. Also I/(I∩S) embeds in the solvable group Out(S), so I itself has exactly one nonsolvable composition factor, contrary to the hypothesis. This handles q=2 and q=4 uniformly without a plus-refinement action exclusion. The small rank-two graph case is handled separately below.

Li–Xia Lemma 4.2 may replace M by M*=GS∩IS in order to select maximal overfactors. It preserves I∩S and hence P. No transfer of maximality from I in M to I∩M* in M* is asserted. All normalizer and maximality arguments return to the original M.

## Recursive embedding dichotomy for the extension-field branch

This induction applies to all the surviving §6.5 cases and all even base fields. It is an argument about the actual perfect residual in the natural module, not an inference from a Table1 label or an appeal to a loop structure on an induced factorization. Rank-two graph images are retained explicitly. In Cases3 and5 the first smaller socle is Sp₄ₐ(4), a≥2, so that particular first stage has no exceptional rank-two graph automorphism.

At every extension-field step the chosen actual overfactor A is almost simple with socle T=Sp₄ₐ(qᵇ), b prime, qᵇ≥4, in its natural restricted-scalar embedding. In particular Rad(A)=1: any solvable normal subgroup centralizes T, and C_A(T)=1. The source gives A=H(A∩B), where H is the current actual many-factor side. It retains at least two nonsolvable composition factors. Therefore H cannot contain T, since a subgroup between T and Aut(T) has exactly one such factor. The actual intersection (A∩B)∩T is the proper minus-orthogonal subgroup in the smaller natural space, so A∩B also does not contain T. Thus both factors are core-free and the corrected many-factor classification applies to this smaller actual factorization.

Moreover H/(H∩T) embeds in the solvable Out(T), whence H^(∞)=(H∩T)^(∞). Indeed the perfect residual of H lies in H∩T, while the quotient of H by the perfect residual of H∩T is solvable. The same argument through the original outer quotients, and the unchanged socle intersections in Lemma4.2, identifies this residual with the original P. Hence no radical kernel or extra perfect component is silently discarded. The simple socle order strictly decreases at each proper extension-field step. This verifies the hypotheses and termination of the embedding induction; it uses no smaller loop or maximality of a subgroup intersection.

Trace the source induction by decreasing the simple socle order, retaining the dichotomy: either the actual product residual preserves an unordered equal nondegenerate orthogonal decomposition, or an unequal nondegenerate decomposition, or a plus-type quadratic refinement. In §5, symplectic Table 3 row 1 gives the equal decomposition, and rows 2 and 4 give the nondegenerate two-space and its complement. Row 3 is rank two; the graph image of a product C2 subgroup is the irreducible tensor C8 subgroup, which preserves a plus quadratic form, while its natural image preserves the 2+2 decomposition. In §6.5 Cases 1/2, the factorization is reduced to a strictly smaller extension-field almost-simple group. Apply the same dichotomy recursively to that actual induced factor: do not infer its embedding merely from a Table 1 row label. An actual decomposition descends by restriction of scalars, and an actual plus form descends by trace as below. This also handles rank-two graph images encountered deeper in the chain. Cases3/5 explicitly force the current ambient field F2 and descend to F4, where the same smaller-order induction applies; they cannot recur at a later stage with a strictly larger extension field. Case4 is ruled out in the source. Table4 row2/Lemma6.6 over F2 and row3/Lemma6.7 over F4 directly place the actual residual in a plus-orthogonal overfactor, ending that branch. The primitive-prime-divisor eliminated cases and the sufficiency of the corrected source branch list are retained primary classification inputs, not independently re-proved.

In a decomposition branch, restriction of scalars preserves nondegeneracy for the traced alternating form. The symplectic and G2 factors remain irreducible after restriction, since their standard nonzero-vector transitivity survives on the identical underlying set, as reviewed in 899203; the exceptional small factors are subject to the same cited qualifications. The distinct kernels make the two modules inequivalent. Hence they are the only minimal nonzero P-submodules; the original I normalizes P and preserves the two summands as an unordered pair, or fixes each when their dimensions differ. Maximality in the original M identifies the corresponding full natural decomposition or proper-subspace action, excluded by 899203. In the plus branch, let Q be a plus form over F_(q^b), with original base field F_q. The original alternating form is Tr_(q^b/q)(λ B_Q) for some λ≠0 in the standard extension-field model. Then Tr_(q^b/q)(λQ) has that polar form and has a totally singular half-dimensional F_q-space obtained by restricting a maximal Q-singular F_(q^b)-space; it is plus type and P-invariant. Its uniqueness, or the fixed-space contradiction, applies in the original group. This includes q=2,b=2; for a chain use transitivity of trace. No maximality transfer or smaller-loop property is used.

There is a second interface trap. Setting the source’s maximal overfactor A equal to the original maximal I works directly when GS=M: then Lemma4.1 makes every maximal overgroup of G core-free. The new [many-summand displacement argument](symplectic-decomposition-actions.md#any-number-of-equal-summands-in-characteristic-two) excludes every natural equal-decomposition action with at least three summands in characteristic two. This avoids the source induction entirely for that specified action. A complementary local tensor argument is as follows. Suppose dim(V)≥6 over an even field and the actual I normalizes the full product of natural symplectic groups on a genuine tensor decomposition V=V1⊗⋯⊗Vt, t≥2, with the tensor alternating polar form. Then I preserves a plus-type quadratic refinement and has exactly one nonsolvable composition factor.

For two alternating spaces A,B, choose a symplectic basis e_i,f_i of B and write x=Σ(a_i⊗e_i+b_i⊗f_i). The function Q(x)=Σ B_A(a_i,b_i) has polar B_A⊗B_B and vanishes on every pure tensor. These properties determine it uniquely: the difference of two refinements is the square of a linear functional and pure tensors span. Both natural symplectic factors preserve Q. The subspace A⊗L, with L Lagrangian in B, is totally singular of half the dimension, so Q is plus. A tensor product of alternating forms is alternating in characteristic two: it is symmetric and vanishes on pure tensors, hence on all vectors by bilinearity. Iteration gives a plus refinement for any number of factors. The full natural tensor base has no fixed vector, so its invariant refinement is unique among all refinements. Every semilinear normalizer therefore preserves the normalized form Q. Maximality in the original M puts I equal to the full proper plus-form stabilizer. Since tensor dimension here is at least eight, its Ω⁺ subgroup is simple with solvable remaining quotient, and I has one nonsolvable composition factor. This contradicts the many-factor hypothesis.

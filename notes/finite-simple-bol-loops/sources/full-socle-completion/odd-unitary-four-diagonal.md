# Pure-diagonal canonical PSU4(q), all odd q

Independently reviewed continuation of [0323:C0010](../foundations/research-C0010.md), conditional on the stated inputs. Definitions are in [PROBLEM.md](../foundations/problem-contract.md). Let q be an odd prime power and S=PSU4(q). Under the canonical selected-mark/transversal input C0001 at `9431ade99316d6bc62efb389492fef385b510b45`, canonical inversion interface C0007 at `932ead5db5f39045e2085caa6b062f6b981b2813`, and cited unitary group/automorphism inputs, no finite simple nonassociative left Bol loop has entire canonical group G=S with canonical inversion carrying a nontrivial pure diagonal label in PGU4(q)/S. This does not address semilinear labels, higher dimensions, or G>S.

Throughout GU is the full Hermitian isometry group, not the similitude group. Put μ={z∈F_(q²)^×:z^(q+1)=1}, d=gcd(4,q+1), and D=PGU4(q)/S≅μ/μ⁴, cyclic of order d, via determinant. Standard unitary automorphism theory is retained from Steinberg, *Automorphisms of finite linear groups*, Thm3.2/statements3.3–3.6 p.608 and §7 pp.613–614, https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/16023F257E0F21D57873B1450E9F15E4/S0008414X00010245a.pdf/automorphisms-of-finite-linear-groups.pdf ; see also the explicit PGU/PSU quotient at https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/forum-of-mathematics-sigma/article/abelian-supplements-in-almost-simple-groups/DC431FD5F2999BC34548AE2AB6634AA1 . Since D has one nontrivial involution, the actual selected set Λ lies in its unique coset. It is nonempty, S-invariant, consists of projective involutions, has |Λ|=[S:H], and satisfies H∩Λ²={1}.

Let α be a projective involution in that coset and choose A∈GU with A²=λI, λ∈μ. The square class of λ in μ is independent of the lift.

## Split scalar class

 If λ is square in μ, rescale A so A²=I. The ±1 eigenspaces are orthogonal and nondegenerate. If r is the dimension of the negative eigenspace, det A=(−1)^r. If q≡1 mod4 then d=2, −1 has the nontrivial D-label and r is odd. If q≡3 mod4 then d=4; −1 has the order-two D-label exactly when v₂(q+1)=2, and otherwise has trivial label. Thus the split outside class occurs for q≡1 mod4 and q≡3 mod8, and has r odd. In dimension4, r=1 and r=3 give the same projective class upon multiplication by −I. GU is transitive on nondegenerate lines and C_GU(A)=GU1(q)×GU3(q) has determinant image all μ; therefore this is one S-class. A projective centralizer cannot exchange the 1- and 3-spaces, and the determinant map onto D is onto, so its S-centralizer has order Cs=|GU3(q)|/d.

## Nonsplit scalar class

 If λ is nonsquare in μ, there is a∈F_(q²) with a²=λ and a^(q+1)=−1. The unitary reciprocal-q map interchanges the ±a eigenspaces, so they are paired totally isotropic 2-spaces. In a hyperbolic basis take A=diag(aI2,−aI2). Its determinant λ² has trivial D-label for d=2 and the unique order-two D-label for d=4. Thus this class occurs outside S exactly when q≡3 mod4. All nonsquare λ differ by a square of μ, so projective rescaling and transitivity on paired isotropic decompositions give one PGU-class. The linear isometry centralizer is isomorphic to GL2(q²): B acts as diag(B,bar(B)^(-T)), and its determinant is c/bar(c) for c=det B. The map c↦c/bar(c) is onto μ. Therefore the PGU-class is already one S-class. Its projective centralizer doubles the linear centralizer because the total swap of the two isotropic planes conjugates A to −A. Hence its S-centralizer has order Cn=2|GL2(q²)|/[(q+1)d]=|GL2(q²)|/[2(q+1)].

## A common collision and stabilizer orders

Both possible outside class squares contain the same internal involution class C2, represented by a determinant-one involution with nondegenerate negative 2-space. In the split class take two representatives with negative signs on distinct orthogonal nondegenerate lines. In the nonsplit hyperbolic model let u=diag(−1,1,−1,1). Then u is unitary, has determinant1 and commutes with A. Swapping ONE hyperbolic pair conjugates A to Au. The conjugator's determinant can be corrected by an element of C_GU(A), since that centralizer has determinant image μ. Thus αu∈α^S and u∈(α^S)². As C_GU(u)=GU2(q)×GU2(q) also has determinant image μ, its GU-class is one SU-class; in particular C2 is the image of every noncentral genuine involution of SU4(q). Therefore C2⊂Λ² for every nonempty class selection, and H misses C2.

For q≡1 mod4, only the split class occurs and |H|=Cs=|GU3(q)|/2. Put s=v₂(q−1)≥2; the order formula |GU3(q)|=q³(q+1)(q²−1)(q³+1) gives |H|₂=2^(s+2), while |Z(SU4(q))|=2. For q≡3 mod4, put t=v₂(q+1)≥2, so |Z(SU4(q))|=4. The nonsplit class always occurs and (Cn)₂=2^(t+2); the split class occurs only for t=2, when (Cs)₂=2^5 and (Cn)₂=2^4. If just one class is selected, |H| is its centralizer order. If both classes are selected, then |H|=Cs Cn/(Cs+Cn); if this is not an integer the transversal is already impossible, and if it is integral its 2-part is 2^5. Thus, for t≥3, |H|₂=2^(t+2), while for t=2 it is at least2^4.

## The unique-involution lift exceeds the unitary exponent bound

Let P be a Sylow 2-subgroup of H, and let Ptilde be its full inverse image in SU4(q). Its unique involution is −I: every noncentral SU4 involution projects into C2, which H avoids, and the cyclic scalar center has only one involution. A finite 2-group with a unique involution is cyclic or generalized quaternion; see Keith Conrad, *Generalized quaternions*, Theorem4.10 p.8 and the quaternion presentation in Theorem3.2 p.2, https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/grouptheory/genquat.pdf (PDF downloaded 2026-11-17, SHA256 `e135c1fb2750d3561c1b145410fba6911b7d1772192b78452b1fc4c73022aec9`). Thus a group of order 2^k here contains an element of order at least 2^(k−1). For q≡1 mod4, |Ptilde|=2^(s+3), so it contains an element of order at least2^(s+2). For q≡3 mod4, |Ptilde| is 2^(t+4) if t≥3, and at least2^6 if t=2; these require element orders at least2^(t+3) and 2^5 respectively.

No such element exists in SU4(q). Every 2-element is semisimple in odd characteristic and its eigenvalue multiset is invariant under a↦a^(−q). Each orbit has length at most4. Length1 or 3 gives eigenvalue order dividing q+1 or q³+1, with 2-part 2 if q≡1 mod4 and 2^t if q≡3 mod4. Length2 gives order dividing q²−1, whose 2-part is 2^(s+1) or 2^(t+1), respectively. A length4 orbit fills the entire multiset; determinant one gives a^(1−q+q²−q³)=1, whose absolute exponent is (q⁴−1)/(q+1)=(q−1)(q²+1). Its 2-part is 2^(s+1) in the first case and 4 in the second. Thus every 2-element has order at most 2^(s+1), respectively 2^(t+1), contradicting the forced orders above. The determinant-one step is essential; a GU4 exponent bound would be weaker.

# Stabilizer orbits of marks above an odd normal kernel

**Independently reviewed structural theorem for [0323:C0010](../foundations/research-C0010.md).** Let (G,H,K) be a finite faithful generated left Bol folder in the [canonical marked setting](../odd-completion/elementary-two-marked-lifts.md). Thus E=G⋊⟨t⟩, t²=1, Λ=tK is a conjugacy-invariant set of outside involutions, t∈Λ and H∩Λ²=1. The action of t may be trivial. Let N≤G be normal in E of odd order, with no commutativity assumption. Write π:E→E/N, L=G/N, J=πH, W=H∩N, S=πΛ, and Π for the primes dividing |N|. Use z^g=g⁻¹zg. A point x∈S denotes an outside involution in E/N, not a left translation in L.

For each x∈S define B=J∩Jˣ and take any involution λ lifting x. Then
```
S ∩ xJ = x^B,
[B:C_J(x)] = |C_N(λ)|/|W|,
B = [B,x] C_J(x) = O_Π(B) C_J(x).                            (1)
```
Here x^B is the conjugacy orbit, [B,x] is a normal Π-subgroup of B, and O_Π(B) is its largest normal subgroup with all prime divisors in Π. Each outside double coset of J contains exactly one J-conjugacy class of selected quotient marks. These facts concern the projection of the original folder. The projected set need not be a loop section.

## Lifting a coprime rotation and using all its powers

All involution lifts of a given outside involution form a single N-conjugacy orbit. Indeed the preimage of its cyclic subgroup has order 2|N|. Existence and conjugacy follow from Sylow's theorem, and conjugation by a lift itself fixes that lift, so the conjugator can be taken in N. Thus all such lifts of x∈S belong to Λ and their number is [N:C_N(λ)].

Take x,y∈S with j=xy∈J and x≠y. First suppose ord(j) is coprime to |N|. The subgroup D=⟨x,y⟩ is dihedral of order 2 ord(j): x inverts j and lies outside L. Its preimage has normal Hall subgroup N and has a complement by Schur–Zassenhaus. In H the preimage of ⟨j⟩ has normal Hall kernel W, so contains a cyclic complement mapping bijectively to ⟨j⟩. Let h be the element of that complement over j. The rotation subgroup in the lifted D and ⟨h⟩ are both complements to N in π⁻¹(⟨j⟩); the quotient is cyclic, so Schur–Zassenhaus conjugates them by an element of N. After that conjugation the unique lift of j in the dihedral complement is h. The two reflections in the complement lifting x and y belong to Λ and multiply to h≠1 in H. This contradicts H∩Λ²=1. The argument extends the [previous involution collision lemma](odd-normal-involution-collisions.md) at `59338c6eebe69e352d561b68c870ac842d6047e0` to the coprime rotations needed here.

For arbitrary j=xy, every reflection xjⁱ belongs to S: an even exponent gives a conjugate of x and an odd exponent a conjugate of y by powers of j. All these reflections lie in xJ. If a prime outside Π divides ord(j), take a power jⁱ of that prime order. The preceding coprime argument applied to x and xjⁱ is a contradiction. Consequently ord(j) is a Π-number, in particular odd. Since x inverts j, j∈B. Choose k with 2k≡1 modulo ord(j), giving y=x^{jᵏ}. Thus S∩xJ is contained in x^B. Conversely for b∈B one has xxᵇ∈J and xᵇ∈S. The stabilizer in B is C_J(x), giving the first orbit and its index in (1).

Every outside coset is met because K is a transversal and multiplication by t̄ identifies the outside cosets with the cosets in L. If y∈S∩JxJ, write y=a x b with a,b∈J. Then y^a∈S∩xJ, so y is conjugate to x under J. Conversely that J-class stays in JxJ. This proves the double-coset assertion.

## The centralizer formula and the normal Π-subgroup

There are exactly [N:W] original left H-cosets above the quotient coset t̄xJ, by normality of N. Each y∈S∩xJ contributes [N:C_N(λ)] section elements, since all y there are conjugate and all lifts of each are selected. Original transversality gives their total [N:W], proving the index/centralizer equality. In particular the index in (1) divides both |J| and [N:W], with no containment W≤C_N(λ) required.

The group B is normalized by x. In A=⟨B,x⟩ all conjugates of x have the form xᵇ. The products xxᵇ have Π-order by the preceding proof. No distinct conjugate of x can therefore belong to the same Sylow 2-subgroup as x: the product would be a nonidentity 2-element. Glauberman's Z* theorem gives x central in A/O_(2′)(A). Consequently R=⟨xxᵇ:b∈B⟩=[B,x] is odd. It is normal in A: x inverts the generators, and (xxᵇ)^c=(xxᶜ)⁻¹(xx^{bc}) for c∈B. By Feit–Thompson, R is solvable. Each generator u is inverted by x and has odd order, so u belongs to the cyclic subgroup generated by u⁻²=[u,x], and R=[R,x].

We show R is a Π-group. In general, take a finite odd solvable group R with automorphism x of order dividing two, R=[R,x], and all r⁻¹rˣ of Π-order. These conditions pass to invariant quotients. For R≠1 take a minimal nonzero x-stable normal subgroup D; it is elementary abelian of some characteristic q by solvability. By induction R/D is a Π-group. If q is outside Π then every d⁻¹dˣ is trivial, since it lies in D and has Π-order. Each R-conjugate of x centralizes D as well, so [R,x]=R centralizes D. This central normal Hall subgroup D splits off by Schur–Zassenhaus; every element of Π-order lies in its complement in the resulting direct product. This contradicts generation of R by its commutators with x. Thus q∈Π and R is a Π-group. The commutators in our R satisfy the hypothesis since r is also in B.

The involution x acts trivially on B/R. Each R-coset has odd size and is x-invariant, hence contains a fixed element. Therefore B=R C_J(x); R≤O_Π(B) yields the last equality in (1). The deep inputs for this paragraph are Glauberman, *Central Elements in Core-Free Groups*, Theorem 4 and Corollary 1, and Feit–Thompson; the [source locators](../foundations/references.md) and their existing verification limits apply. The orbit and centralizer-count results above do not require either input.

## Consequences for the classification

For any nontrivial such N in a finite simple proper loop of **even** order, H<HN by core-freeness and HN<G because [HN:H]=[N:W] is odd. There must be an outside coset with more than one selected label. Otherwise |KN/N|=[G:HN], giving a proper nontrivial normal subloop by the [original normal-subloop criterion](../foundations/C0001.md). Explicitly the image is then a transversal in G/N and the coset projection is a loop homomorphism after taking the faithful quotient if necessary. It follows that gcd(|N|,|J|)>1. In particular no nontrivial τ-stable odd normal Hall subgroup of G is possible in this branch.

If N is a p-group each outside coset has a power-of-p multiplicity dividing |J|. In an actual even simple loop there is some multiplicity at least p, so p divides |J|, even for nonsplit extensions and several selected quotient classes. If N is elementary, the negative eigenspace rank of the marked action on N is constant **within each individual projected stabilizer coset**, though it may vary between cosets. With d=dim N and w=dim W its common value r gives the exact coset multiplicity p^{d−w−r} dividing |J|. Thus the mixed-rank partition in the [weighted-fiber control](../odd-completion/odd-layer-weighted-fibers.md#scope-and-control) cannot arise inside one coset of an actual folder. This also excludes multiplicity 2p−1 for odd p. For cyclic Sylow p-subgroups of B a nontrivial multiplicity equals |B|_p: R is cyclic, and x inverts its generators, so C_R(x)=1. A nontrivial p-subgroup of C_J(x) would generate together with the nontrivial normal R a p-subgroup with two disjoint subgroups of order p, impossible in a cyclic Sylow subgroup.

As a check on nontrivial multiplicity, take the [exact-factorization family](../odd-completion/nonbruck-root-graph-parameters.md) for odd q=p^f≥5 with linear map φ of rank k≥1. Write X=Fq²⋊SL₂(q), so G=X×X, t swaps coordinates, and H=B_φ×SL₂(q). Take N=(Fq²)². Then |W|=p^{2f−k}, since a translation is in B_φ precisely when its second coordinate is in ker φ. Every selected involution acts on N by exchanging the two factors through mutually inverse linear maps, so |C_N(λ)|=p^{2f}. Formula (1) gives pᵏ labels in each projected coset. At x=t̄, J=T_φ×SL₂(q), B=T_φ×T_φ, and C_J(x) is the diagonal T_φ. This independently gives the same index. This check uses the pinned family construction; it is an algebraic calculation, not a census.

For fixed L and J a single odd elementary kernel minimal normal in E is bounded without a prescribed prime or dimension. One has p dividing |J|, and N is an irreducible module for E/N, generated by any one nonzero vector, so dim N≤2|L| by the regular module surjection. One can therefore enumerate its finitely many module and extension possibilities from L, its involution, and J. More generally let N be nilpotent of odd order and suppose it has at most c chief factors for E inside N. Each Sylow subgroup P of N is characteristic in N. Applying the prime-divisor conclusion to P and using [N:P] coprime to p shows that p divides |G/N|. Starting from E/N of order 2|L|, reconstruct successive chief factors from the top. Each factor is elementary abelian of such a prime p, irreducible under the group already constructed, and has dimension at most that group's order. This gives a finite recursive bound on |G| at fixed L and c. This induction only bounds ordinary group extensions, and never applies a loop theorem to an intermediate quotient. Neither an absolute chief-length bound nor the same top-down prime restriction for arbitrary nonnilpotent odd radicals is claimed.

## Outside projective classes

Let d=gcd(4,q−1), D=PGL4(q)/S≅Fq*/(Fq*)⁴. The unique nonzero involution label is represented by an outside projective involution [A] with A²=λI; λ square or nonsquare. The actual nonempty Λ is a union of the following S-classes. If λ is square, rescale A²=1; its negative eigenspace has dimension r odd exactly when −1 has the nonzero order-two D-label. Here r=1,3 are projectively the same, and the class is a single S-class, because C_GL(A)=GL1×GL3 has surjective determinant. The centralizer in S has order Cs=|GL3(q)|/d. This class occurs for q≡3 mod4, and for q≡5 mod8; for q≡1 mod8, −1 is a fourth power and it is absent.

If λ is nonsquare, its irreducible quadratic polynomial gives V=Fq²² and C_GL(A)=GL2(q²), determinant norm onto Fq*. Its determinant λ² has the nontrivial order-two D-label exactly for d=4, i.e. q≡1 mod4. All nonsquare λ are scalar-square equivalent, so this is one S-class. A projective centralizer also interchanges ±A via the q-field involution, which on Fq⁴ has determinant (+1), hence Cn=2|GL2(q²)|/((q−1)d)=|GL2(q²)|/[2(q−1)]. These are actual S-class orders; scalar denominators matter.

## A common genuine involution collision

Both class squares contain the same class I of genuine noncentral SL4(q) involutions, diag(−1,−1,1,1). For the split class multiply two representatives supported on two distinct one-dimensional eigenspaces. For the nonsplit class realize A as multiplication by √λ on each Fq² summand and let u act −1 on the first summand and +1 on the second. The field conjugation on one summand sends A to Au and has determinant −1; multiply this conjugator by a centralizing element of any desired determinant (the norm map of GL2(q²) is onto) to make it determinant one. Thus Au is S-conjugate to A. The GL centralizer of u is GL2×GL2 with onto determinant, so all determinant-one noncentral SL4 involutions project into the same S-class I. Consequently H avoids I for any actual nonempty union of outside classes.

## Stabilizer lifts and determinant-one eigenvalue bounds

Let P∈Syl₂(H); its complete inverse image Ptilde in SL4(q) has unique involution −I: every other SL involution is noncentral and projects into I. The finite unique-involution theorem (Conrad, Generalized Quaternions, Thm 4.10 p.8 and Thm 3.2 p.2, as pinned in [the unitary proof](odd-unitary-four-diagonal.md)) implies an element of order at least |Ptilde|/2. We bound all 2-element orders in SL4(q): their eigenvalues lie in finite field orbits of degrees ≤4. Degrees 1,3 have 2-parts at most (q−1)₂, degree 2 at most (q²−1)₂, and if an irreducible degree-4 orbit occurs it fills the space and determinant one forces its order to divide (q⁴−1)/(q−1). Hence the exponent is at most 2^(t+1) for q≡3 mod4, t=v₂(q+1), and at most 2^(s+1) for q≡1 mod4, s=v₂(q−1)≥2; in the second case the degree-4 determinant-one factor has 2-part 4.

For q≡3 mod4, only Cs occurs, |H|₂=2^(t+2), and the scalar kernel has order 2. Thus |Ptilde|=2^(t+3), forcing element order ≥2^(t+2), contradiction. For q≡1 mod4 the scalar kernel has order4, and the nonsplit class always occurs with (Cn)₂=2^(s+2). If s≥3 it is the only class and |Ptilde|=2^(s+4), forcing order ≥2^(s+3). If s=2 the split class also occurs and (Cs)₂=2^5 while (Cn)₂=2^4; a single selection has |H|₂ at least 2^4 and a double selection has 2-part 2^5 by Cs Cn/(Cs+Cn), whenever the order is integral. Hence |Ptilde|≥2^6 and an element of order ≥2^5, exceeding the 2^(s+1)=2³ bound. The inequality is even stronger for split-only. This concludes the proposed proof for actual G=S and every possible nonempty selected union.

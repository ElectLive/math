# Identity stabilizers for characteristic-two symplectic full socles

**Independently reviewed theorem for [0323:C0010](../foundations/research-C0010.md).** Let a finite simple proper left Bol loop have full multiplication group M with nonabelian simple socle S = Sp_d(q), where q is even and d ≥ 4 is even. In the actual action on the loop, the identity stabilizer I has at most one nonabelian composition factor, counted with multiplicity. The inputs are C0008's almost-simple, primitive full-group reduction and the exact group and geometric results cited below. This supplies one structural restriction on the possible operations. The historical many-factor factorization induction remains unverified.

## The original action

Put J = I∩S. Since S is a nontrivial normal subgroup of a primitive faithful group, it is transitive. Thus M=SI, I is a maximal proper subgroup, J is normal in I, and I/J embeds in the solvable outer automorphism group of S. It suffices to bound the factors of J. The actual coset action M/I is important here. If I preserves a structure, its full stabilizer in M is proper whenever some element of S moves that structure. Maximality then gives equality. If S is transitive on structures of the given type, the full coset domain is precisely their natural orbit.

For d≥6 all automorphisms of S are represented by semilinear symplectic similitudes. Holt–Roney-Dougal (2005), Theorem 1.1, gives the intersections with the natural group of geometric maximal subgroups of **each** intermediate M. The even-characteristic scalar center of Sp is trivial. Equivalently one can use the full scalar preimage of projective M in the theorem and take the intersection with Sp. The remaining Aschbacher class consists of projectively almost-simple groups with solvable outer quotient. This argument requires no maximality of J in S and makes no assumption about the image of the left multiplication group in M/S.

## Recovering the geometry in dimensions at least six

The C1 rows in Holt–Roney-Dougal §4 Table 1 give J as a parabolic stabilizing a totally isotropic W, or a product on two nondegenerate orthogonal subspaces of unequal dimensions. In the parabolic case, recover W from the transvections in J. For v≠0 and c≠0 the transvection x↦x+cB(x,v)v preserves W exactly if v lies in W-perp or in W. Since W is totally isotropic this is precisely W-perp. Consequently the lines occurring as images of u−1 for transvections u in J span W-perp. Every semilinear normalizer of J preserves this space and its perpendicular W. In the unequal-product case the two summands are irreducible J-modules with different kernels. They are the only minimal nonzero submodules of their direct sum and have different dimensions. Again I fixes a proper subspace. Both cases contradict the [original-stabilizer invariant-subspace theorem](symplectic-decomposition-actions.md#recovering-the-actual-point-action-from-a-product-residual) at `899203054af332971f56c53557c6844e543f3288`.

In C2 (§5 Table 2), the even-field case is the full wreath product Sp_b(q) wr Sym(t) on t equal nondegenerate summands, b even and t≥2. The complementary totally singular pair entry of the symplectic table requires odd q. Recover the unordered summands from J intrinsically in the ambient representation. If u permutes the summands nontrivially, pick a moved summand U. Then (u−1) restricted to U is injective, so rank(u−1)≥b≥2. Every transvection in J therefore lies in the base product, and its image line belongs to a single block. Conversely every line of every block is such an image, since the block group is the full symplectic group. The blocks are exactly the maximal vector subspaces contained in the resulting union of lines (including zero): adding nonzero vectors in two different blocks gives a vector in neither block nor any other block. Semilinear normalization preserves ranks and image lines. Hence I preserves that unordered decomposition, including over F₂. The full symplectic group is transitive on these decompositions by symplectic bases. The two-block displacement theorem at `899203054af332971f56c53557c6844e543f3288` applies for d≥6, and for t≥3 use [the all-summand theorem](symplectic-decomposition-actions.md#any-number-of-equal-summands-in-characteristic-two) at `898445e11fd53478a1a84e4eeb00b98450424fe4`. Thus C2 cannot be the identity stabilizer.

## Rank two and graph extensions

Here d=4 and simplicity requires q≥4. Bray–Holt–Roney-Dougal (2013), §7.2 and Table 8.14 p.384, classifies the original maximal-subgroup intersections and the action of the graph-field generator γ with γ² a field automorphism. Only two ordinary rows have two nonabelian composition factors: the full stabilizer of a nondegenerate 2+2 decomposition and the plus-type orthogonal group. The graph interchanges their distinct S-classes, and all field automorphisms preserve each class. The two parabolics have a soluble radical with one SL₂(q) factor in the Levi. The extension-field and minus-orthogonal rows have one PSL₂(q²) factor. Each subfield or Suzuki row has at most one, including Sp₄(2)≅S₆ when it occurs. The additional graph-extension intersections are solvable: they are the unipotent-by-torus and torus-by-2-group possibilities of §7.2 and Table 8.14. This includes q=4, where one torus case drops out. See also Lucchini–Marion–Tracey, proof of Proposition 7.1. The e>2 hypothesis of Holt–Roney-Dougal Theorem 12.1 is not extended to q=4 here.

Suppose J is one of the two ordinary rows with two factors. An element of I normalizes J, so its outer class cannot be an odd power of γ. Since M=SI, M lies in the natural semilinear group. If J is on the orthogonal side, transport the **entire coset action** by an automorphism of S exchanging the two classes. More precisely conjugation in Aut(S) identifies (M,I) with (M',I') and mI with its corresponding coset, so the transported action still contains all translations of the relabelled loop. The image M' is natural since the natural outer subgroup is normal in cyclic Out(S). Now the recovered block argument above applies to the actual natural wreath subgroup on two 2-spaces. The two-block theorem covers q≥4 even, giving a contradiction. This finishes the stated bound in all ranks.

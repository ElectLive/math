# Even-dimensional linear canonical overgroups in characteristic two

Continuation of [0323:C0010](../foundations/research-C0010.md), with independent exact-source review recorded below. Let n=2m≥4, q=2^f, and S=PSL_n(q). Under the stated canonical and finite-classical-group inputs, no finite simple nonassociative left Bol loop has canonical left multiplication group G with S≤G≤Aut(S). No hypothesis says S is the full multiplication socle. In particular this closes the socle-containing canonical case for these full socles, but not their occurrence through proper actual factors. The previously reviewed G=S theorem is [the even-dimensional continuation](../primitive-completion/radical-free-odd-linear-inversion.md#even-dimensional-continuation), exact source `1ca7a62efde571600a531c8e352151faca2ab626`; the new obligation is every almost-simple overgroup.

Its original marked set Λ=τK is a G-invariant set of involutions, contains τ, has size [G:H], and satisfies H∩Λ²=1. No quotient folder is formed and no assumption that τ normalizes H is used.

The standard automorphism description is Steinberg, *Automorphisms of finite linear groups*, Theorem3.2, printed p.608, [primary PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/16023F257E0F21D57873B1450E9F15E4/S0008414X00010245a.pdf/automorphisms-of-finite-linear-groups.pdf), retained with the direct source inspection and limitations of the earlier linear proof. Put d=(n,q−1), odd. The diagonal quotient D=PGL_n(q)/S has order d, graph acts on it by inversion, and field acts by raising scalars to the corresponding power of2. The field projection of G/S has exponent at most two: it is generated by the projections of τλ, λ∈Λ, and both factors have order at most two in the cyclic field quotient. The graph projection also has exponent at most two. Thus |G/S|₂≤4; this bound is only for the actual G, not the full outer group.

Every mark lies outside S because τG is disjoint from G. Its field/graph type is graph, field or graph-field; purely diagonal involutions cannot occur in the odd group D. If there are no semilinear marks, all marks have graph type. Then τΛ has trivial field and graph projection, so generation forces G≤PGL_n(q). We first treat this graph-only case.

## Graph-only selections with diagonal overgroups

Write A=G/S≤D and l=|A|. Since G′=S, the actual H maps onto A and |H∩S|=|H|/l. The reviewed graph-class proof at `1ca7a62efde571600a531c8e352151faca2ab626` gives two S-classes within each actual graph label, with centralizer orders

    F=|Sp_(2m)(q)|,      C=q^(2m−1)|Sp_(2m−2)(q)|,
    F/C=q^(2m)−1.

They are respectively alternating and nonalternating symmetric-form types. That proof derives the nonalternating isometry order, determinant-one congruence and projective centralizer descent directly. It also gives these actual square interfaces: the nonalternating class square contains every S-involution, and the alternating class square contains an element u of odd prime order r for any r|q+1. For the latter, the explicit four-dimensional construction uses an order-r a∈SL₂(q), J₄=[[0,I₂],[I₂,0]] and u₄=diag(a,aᵀ), extended by identity and an alternating form. Congruence of J and Ju can be achieved in SL since its determinant has square1 in characteristic two. These are retained proof interfaces, not new numerical evidence.

Conjugation by A permutes the l labels in τA transitively: the relevant change of diagonal coefficient is multiplication by a square, and squaring is bijective on the odd group A. It preserves the two types because their centralizer orders differ. Hence selection of each type is uniform across the l labels. The possible orders of H∩S are

    F/l,       C/l,       F/(l q^(2m)),

for alternating alone, nonalternating alone or both. If only the nonalternating type is selected, its square forces H∩S odd, contradicting the even number C/l.

If the alternating type is selected, choose an odd prime r|q+1. Then r∤l, and |S|_r=F_r: among the degree factors in |SL_(2m)(q)|, exactly the even degrees can be divisible by r, and these are precisely the factors of F; the scalar denominator divides q−1 and is r-prime. In either remaining selection, H∩S has full S-Sylow r-order. The retained alternating square supplies an order-r element of Λ², and Sylow conjugacy places an S-conjugate of it in H∩S. S-invariance of Λ² contradicts H∩Λ²=1. This excludes every graph-only diagonal overgroup.

## Semilinear class descent and exact label counts

Now a semilinear mark occurs. Necessarily f is even; write q=s², s=2^e. The two physical types have standard representatives β=F_s and β=γF_s. On SL_n over the algebraic closure they satisfy β²=F_q, with finite fixed groups SL_n(s) and SU_n(s). The finite field/Hermitian descent and determinant corrections at reviewed source `1ca7a62efde571600a531c8e352151faca2ab626` show that each standard S-label contains one S-class of involutions. The argument works with the entire odd scalar kernel: for an order-two action on an odd abelian group, norm surjects onto its fixed subgroup and the difference map surjects onto the norm-one subgroup, by the ± decomposition using division by2. Thus it does not require d to be prime. Applying the same fixed-lift adjustment to centralizing elements gives

    |C_S(F_s)|=|SL_n(s)|/d_+,       d_+=(n,s−1),
    |C_S(γF_s)|=|SU_n(s)|/d_−,     d_−=(n,s+1).       (1)

The fixed subgroup is the projective image of the indicated simply connected group, not its full inner-diagonal overgroup. Finite classical orders and split/unitary forms are the same retained inputs as the earlier proof; the central adjustment explicitly explains their divisors.

For either physical β, a diagonal coefficient δ yields an involution label only if δ+β(δ)=0 in D. Conversely ker(1+β)=im(β−1), so diagonal conjugation supplies every such label. Each has one S-class, transported from the standard label. The kernel of β−1 has order d_+ or d_−, because (d,s∓1)=(n,s∓1). Consequently the number of selected classes of each type satisfies

    0≤n_+≤d/d_+,             0≤n_−≤d/d_−.             (2)

These bounds overcount all restrictions from the actual outside coset and G-orbits. They remain valid for composite d. In particular the weighted counts A=n_+d_+ and B=n_−d_− satisfy 0≤A,B≤d≤s²−1, and A+B>0. The last strict inequality is exactly the semilinear-selection hypothesis.

Both fixed groups meet every S-involution class. In characteristic two the classes have Jordan types 2^r1^(n−2r), 1≤r≤m. Their GL classes remain single SL classes: a size-one block supplies arbitrary determinant in the centralizer, and at maximal rank the centralizer contains determinants which are arbitrary squares, still every scalar since q is even. The odd scalar center gives a unique involutory lift and does not fuse different ranks. Split transvection blocks realize every type in SL_n(s). For SU_n(s), choose r orthogonal hyperbolic planes and on each take [[1,a],[0,1]] with 0≠a∈F_s. It preserves the hyperbolic Hermitian form because a+bar(a)=0 and has determinant1. Identity on the complement completes each rank; standard Hermitian congruence transports the construction to the fixed form in (1).

For a β-fixed involutory lift û, the same field/graph-field descent sends βû to an S-conjugate of β. Equivalently Lang gives g⁻¹β(g)=û with β²(g)=g, hence g∈SL_n(q). Thus u=β(βu) belongs to the class square. Diagonal conjugacy preserves every internal rank class, so every semilinear S-class has square containing all S-involutions. Therefore B₀=H∩S is odd. The exact index identity gives the stronger necessary valuation bound

    |Λ|₂=|S|₂ |G/S|₂ / |H/B₀|₂ ≥ |S|₂,
    v₂(|Λ|)≥2e m(2m−1).                                (3)

This uses that H/B₀ is a subgroup of G/S, not an assumption of surjectivity in the general semilinear case.

## Uniform cancellation bound in every even dimension

Let c=|S|/|Sp_n(q)|. The graph-class sizes in the actual labels are c and c(q^n−1), with v₂(c)=2em(m−1). Each graph type can be selected at most d times. Write its counts as a,b, so the graph contribution is c[a+b(q^n−1)]. Each semilinear class has 2-valuation

    v=em(2m−1).

If a≠b, then 0<|a−b|≤d<s², so v₂(a−b)<2e. The graph term has valuation 2em(m−1)+v₂(a−b); the term involving bq^n has larger valuation. Every semilinear term has valuation at least v, whose difference from 2em(m−1) is em≥2e. Thus no cancellation changes the lower graph valuation, and it is strictly less than (3). Hence a=b.

If a=b>0, the graph contribution is acq^n. Its valuation is at least v+3em; when a=b=0 there is no graph contribution. From (1), exact order cancellation gives the semilinear sizes

    |S|/|PSL_n(s)| = s^(n(n−1)/2) (d_+/d) ∏_(i=2)^n(s^i+1),
    |S|/|PSU_n(s)| = s^(n(n−1)/2) (d_−/d) ∏_(i=2)^n(s^i+(-1)^i).

All factors in the products are odd. Removing the common 2-power and common odd unit from their selected sum leaves

    A ∏_(j=3,5,...,2m−1)(s^j+1)
      +B ∏_(j=3,5,...,2m−1)(s^j−1).                    (4)

The crucial bounds A,B≤s²−1 come from the actual label count (2), not independent unconstrained multiplicities.

There are m−1 factors in each product. If m is odd, (4) is congruent to A+B modulo s³; its positive leading residue is <2s²≤s³, so its 2-valuation is at most2e. If m is even and A≠B, it is congruent to A−B modulo s³, a nonzero integer of absolute value <s² and valuation <2e. If m is even and A=B>0, the sum of the two products has leading term 2s³ and all remaining terms have strictly larger 2-valuation: their expansion retains exactly the odd-sized subsets, and the next exponent exceeds3. Thus its valuation is3e+1. Multiplying by A<s² gives valuation at most5e. These cases include A=0 or B=0 wherever permitted; a zero semilinear sum is impossible.

The total semilinear contribution therefore has valuation at most v+5e. Since 3em≥6e, it cannot cancel a nonzero paired graph contribution. Also v≥6e for m≥2, so v+5e<2v. Consequently v₂(|Λ|)<2v=2em(2m−1), contradicting (3). This includes n=4 and s=2; no finite exceptional parameter or extrapolated computation remains.

The graph-only and semilinear cases exhaust actual outside marks and prove the proposed canonical-overgroup theorem. Together with the reviewed odd-dimensional theorem at `114ce1b011fd47589eb762ed7cd5492a190002d8` and the reviewed all-PSL2 canonical theorem at `47b07688a82d3df93f537946fe0780112200383b`, it excludes every almost-simple canonical overgroup with a linear socle in characteristic two. Proper actual factors in a larger full multiplication group, odd-characteristic even-dimensional socles, and the complete classification remain separate obligations.

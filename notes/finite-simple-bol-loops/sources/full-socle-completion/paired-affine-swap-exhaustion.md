# Exhaustion of the factor-swapping affine square

**Reviewed theorem for [0323:C0010](../foundations/research-C0010.md), conditional on the pinned canonical-folder, construction and isomorphism inputs below.** Let q=p^f≥4, V=F_q², A=SL₂(q) in its natural action, and X=V⋊A. Suppose a finite simple proper left Bol loop has canonical left multiplication group G=X×X and its canonical inversion interchanges the two displayed factors. Then its multiplication belongs to the [root-graph family](../odd-completion/nonbruck-root-graph-parameters.md). Conversely every member of that family has these data. For each q the nonzero F_p-linear maps φ:F_q→F_q, modulo the semilinear equivalence in the linked construction, give exactly its isomorphism classes. The loop order is q³(q²−1). This identifies all operations under the factor-exchange hypothesis and leaves factor-preserving inversion open.

## Marks, collisions and the order inequality

By identifying the factors via the inversion, write τ as the ordinary swap. In the [canonical marked extension](../odd-completion/elementary-two-marked-lifts.md), E=G⋊⟨t⟩ with t acting by τ. Every outside involution is conjugate to t, as its two coordinates are mutually inverse. The invariant selected set contains t and hence consists of the entire class. Thus the left translations are precisely K={(x,x⁻¹):x∈X}, without prescribing a construction or stabilizer. For the original point stabilizer H one has |H|=|X| and K is a transversal to every conjugate of H. Consequently H contains no nonidentity pair with coordinates conjugate to one another's inverse in X. Such a pair is conjugate to a member of K, and every conjugate of H meets K only in the identity.

Put N=V×V, W=H∩N and let J≤A×A be the image of H with coordinate projections B,C. Since A is transitive on the nonzero vectors in V, the collision restriction places W in the union of the two coordinate modules. A subgroup in this union lies in one coordinate module, so |W|≤q². In G=KH a representation of (u,1) has the form (c,c⁻¹)(b,c), hence the two projection groups of H factor X. Projecting to A gives A=CB=BC. Also

    |J| / |A| = q² / |W| = p^k.

If k=0 the image of K in G/N has |A|=[G:HN] elements. Here H<HN<G because [HN:H]=q² and [G:HN]=|A|. The [normal-subloop criterion](../foundations/C0001.md) contradicts simplicity. Hence k≥1 and |B|_p |C|_p ≥ |J|_p = q p^k > q. We use |D|_p for the p-part of a group order.

## Two proper projections

Assume B,C<A. Their images in PSL₂(q) are proper. Otherwise the index in the perfect group A would be at most two. We use Dickson's subgroup list as stated in Guralnick and Zieve, Appendix A, Theorem A.1, cited in [REFERENCES.md](../foundations/references.md). The table gives abstract types. A p-divisible subgroup of Borel type has a nontrivial normal p-subgroup. A nontrivial p-subgroup of PSL₂(q) lies in a Sylow unipotent group and fixes exactly one point of the projective line, so its normalizer lies in the actual stabilizer of that point. In characteristic two the same applies to A₄ by its normal group of order four.

If neither projective image lies in a point stabilizer, both p-parts are at most √q. Torus normalizers have p-part at most two, and proper subfield groups with parameter r have p-part r≤√q. The exceptional A₄,S₄,A₅ in odd characteristic have p-part at most p for p=3,5 and have p-part one otherwise. For prime q≥5 the only numerical issue is A₅ at q=5, which is the entire projective group. In even characteristic S₄ does not occur. A₅ has 2-part four, bounded by √q for q≥16, is the whole group at q=4, and cannot occur at q=8 since 5 does not divide the group order. This proves the bound in all cases, contradicting |B|_p |C|_p>q.

Consequently assume B is in a point stabilizer and C is transitive on the q+1 projective points by factorization. No group of proper subfield type can act transitively, since q+1 is coprime to r and larger than r²−1 whereas the subfield order divides r(r²−1). Both projections must be p-divisible to violate the bound.

For odd p the torus normalizers have no p-part. Among the proper exceptional projective images with p dividing their order, transitivity requires that q+1 divide 12, 24 or 60. For p=3 and q≥5 only q=9 with A₅ survives (at q=27, 28 divides none). For p=5 the case q=5 has no proper A₅, and q=25 already requires divisibility by 26. All larger powers in these two characteristics exceed the candidate orders. At q=9 the projection onto A₅ is a genuine possibility at the factorization level. Its lift C has order 120 because SL₂(9) has a unique involution and so contains no A₅. Also C is perfect, since its derived subgroup maps onto A₅ and likewise cannot have order 60. B is solvable and C perfect, so Goursat's lemma gives J=B×C. The order identity gives |B|=6·3^k and therefore both B and C contain z=−I. A lift to H of (z,z) has coordinates (v,z) and (w,z). Both are involutions conjugate by translations to (0,z) because 2 is invertible in F₉. This contradicts the collision restriction.

Now suppose p=2. Among proper exceptional images, A₄ fixes a point and a transitive A₅ would require q+1 dividing 60. The smaller fields q=4,8,16,32 give respectively the whole group or a failed divisibility test, and q≥64 is too large. A split torus normalizer of order 2(q−1) cannot be transitive, since the odd integer q+1 is coprime to q−1. The only remaining transitive type has order dividing 2(q+1) and has a cyclic subgroup of odd rotations. The point stabilizer has order q(q−1). Since |J|=|A|2^k divides |B||C|≤2|A| and k≥1, equality holds throughout. Thus J=B×C, B is the full point stabilizer and C is dihedral of order 2(q+1). Also |W|=q²/2.

A rotation of order q+1 in C acts irreducibly on the second V over F₂. Its eigenvalues in F_{q²} have order q+1, since its determinant is one and it is semisimple. The multiplicative order of 2 modulo q+1 divides 2f, and any proper divisor d of 2f is at most f, giving 2^d−1<q+1. Thus the eigenvalue degree is 2f. The J-invariant W must therefore lie in the first coordinate, where it has codimension one over F₂. A generator of the split torus in B acts there with eigenvalues λ,λ⁻¹ for λ of order q−1>1. Its difference from the identity is invertible and hence its action admits no trivial quotient. Any invariant F₂-hyperplane gives a trivial one-dimensional quotient, a contradiction.

## Splitting the original point stabilizer

After interchanging factors assume C=A. The only proper normal subgroups of the quasisimple A are contained in its center, of order gcd(2,q−1). If B=A, Goursat gives |J|/|A| equal to 1, 2 (only in odd characteristic), or |A|. None is a positive power p^k with k≥1. If B<A, any nontrivial quotient of A has order at least |PSL₂(q)| whereas |B| is smaller by perfectness and the absence of index two subgroups. Goursat now gives J=B×A and |B|=p^k>1.

The coordinate restriction on W, its size smaller than |V| and the irreducibility of A on V over F_p put W=W₀×0. Irreducibility also follows directly from transitivity on nonzero vectors. In the split affine coordinates the translation part of H modulo W is a 1-cocycle on B×A with values in (V/W₀)⊕V. Indeed the translations over each (b,a) are one coset of W, and multiplication gives the cocycle equation. Its first component on 1×A is a homomorphism into the additive group V/W₀, hence zero by perfectness. Its second component on B×1 is fixed by A, by comparing the cocycle evaluated on commuting factors. It therefore vanishes. Thus H=Y₀×A_d, where A_d={(d(a),a):a∈A} is the graph of a cocycle and Y₀≤X projects onto B with kernel W₀. The automorphism (v,a)↦(v−d(a),a) of X, applied simultaneously to the two coordinates, preserves K and τ and takes H to Y×A. This works for every cocycle d, including nonprincipal ones.

Now |Y|=q² and any nonidentity y∈Y∩A would give (y,y⁻¹)∈H∩K. Hence Y is regular on V and its nontrivial projection B makes it a nontranslation regular subgroup. The [reviewed regular-subgroup exhaustion](../odd-completion/nonbruck-root-graph-parameters.md) at `508e8f23a95c6817e9ec3c1b29ff4d5836caa843` identifies all such Y with the root graphs after an allowed change of coordinates. The construction, converse, and semilinear isomorphism/counting theorem there at `8ad35e2dbed5179f70be146d73b61ae80bde5555` retain the repaired C0004 input at `87a0895eec2cf7b690dafab5ca3292fb05147a03`. Swapping the two original factors itself preserves the marked data. This proves the stated exhaustiveness under the exhibited group and inversion hypotheses.

# Binary symplectic full multiplication socles in rank at least five

**Independently reviewed theorem for [0323:C0010](../foundations/research-C0010.md).** For every integer m≥5, the full multiplication group of a finite simple proper left Bol loop cannot have socle Sp₂ₘ(2). The argument uses the original loop action, the cited classifications of finite groups and the finite certificate for one spread. The rank-three and rank-four binary groups lie outside this theorem.

## Reduction to the action on extension-field structures

Let M be the full multiplication group, S its socle, I the stabilizer of the identity and G the group of left translations (the subgroup generated by all of them). By [C0008](../../claims/0008.md), M is primitive almost simple. For S=Sp₂ₘ(2), m≥5, the outer automorphism group is trivial, so M=S. The [canonical symplectic exclusion](../factor-permutation/radical-free-symplectic-inversion.md#all-almost-simple-canonical-overgroups) at `a3358d80029053be96219bdf14727de0defbdd9d` forces G proper. Since G is transitive, S=GI. Choose a maximal subgroup A containing G. Then S=AI, where A and the original I are distinct maximal subgroups of S.

Use Li–Wang–Xia, arXiv:2012.09551v6, §3.6, the paragraph preceding Table 3.6 and rows 1–6,10 of that table. This is their record of the Liebeck–Praeger–Saxl **maximal** factorizations. The near-exact hypothesis of their Proposition 3.13 is not imposed on GI or AI here. None of the six small groups excluded before the table has the indicated rank over F₂. In either orientation the entries at q=2 and m≥5 give the following possibilities for I:

* the stabilizer of an isotropic 1-space or maximal isotropic m-space, or of a nondegenerate 2-space,
* the stabilizer of two equal orthogonal nondegenerate summands,
* either orthogonal group O₂ₘ⁺(2) or O₂ₘ⁻(2),
* a prime extension-field group Sp₂ₐ(2ˢ).s, with as=m.

In particular row 10 contributes an extension-field side and a nondegenerate 2-space side. The [subspace and two-summand obstructions](symplectic-decomposition-actions.md) at `899203054af332971f56c53557c6844e543f3288` remove the first two entries. The [operation theorem on the full refinement orbits](binary-refinement-loop-rigidity.md) at `cd10179d20a5f8c739799b664e99093a37e4e4a2` removes both orthogonal sides. For the final entry use the natural restriction-of-scalars model of Holt–Roney-Dougal (2005), §6, Proposition 6.4, also used in the [original stabilizer theorem](symplectic-even-maximal-stabilizers.md). All automorphisms of S in the ranks at issue are inner, so a conjugate model gives the same permutation action.

Put E=F_(2ˢ), V=E^(2a), d=2m. Equip V with the binary symplectic form B given by tracing a nondegenerate alternating E-form to F₂. The E-lines partition the nonzero vectors into the nonzero parts of binary totally isotropic s-spaces. Denote this collection of subspaces by D. The extension-field subgroup preserves D, including its field automorphisms. Hence there is a surjective S-equivariant map from S/I to the orbit of D. Every invariant relation on that orbit pulls back to the original action. For 1≤k≤s, let R_k consist of ordered pairs of spreads for which no member of the first meets any member of the second in dimension ≥k. These are invariant and off the diagonal.

The translation condition used below follows directly from the loop axioms. For a nonempty invariant relation on ordered pairs, move the first point of one pair to the identity e, obtaining (e,y). For every x, left translation by x gives (x,x·y) in the relation. The single right translation by y must therefore send **every** point to a related point. It suffices to exhibit a nonempty R_k and, for **each** g∈S, a spread D₀ in the orbit for which (D₀,gD₀) lies outside R_k. The equivariant surjection lifts both requirements. Equality of I with the full set stabilizer of D is unnecessary.

Every isotropic binary s-space occurs in an orbit member, by transitivity. If a≥2, two disjoint s-spaces with totally isotropic sum occur together in an orbit member. Start with two independent E-lines in an E-Lagrangian and extend a binary isometry of the sums by Witt's lemma.

## A nearly invariant isotropic subspace

Let T be any invertible semilinear symplectic similitude on a 2m-dimensional space over F_q. For each 1≤s≤m there exists a totally isotropic s-space U with dim(U∩TU)≥s−1. To see this, write q=p^f. In prime-field coordinates the s−1 equations B(v,Tʲv)=0, 1≤j<s, give at most f(s−1) homogeneous quadratic equations in 2mf variables. Each power of T is F_p-linear. Their total degree is less than the number of variables, so Chevalley–Warning yields a nonzero common zero, since zero itself is a solution. All pairs among the first s iterates of v are orthogonal, by semilinear preservation of orthogonality. If the iterates are independent their span works. Otherwise their first dependence gives a T-stable isotropic span W of dimension h with 1≤h<s. Induct on s in W-perp/W, of rank m−h, with target dimension s−h. Lifting the resulting space adds h to its dimension and its intersection dimension. The base s=1 follows by choosing any line.

For each g of the binary symplectic group choose such a U and an orbit member D₀ containing it. Then gU belongs to gD₀ and meets U in dimension at least s−1. Thus no element can meet the universal translation condition for R_(s−1).

## Finding separated spreads

Each spread has N=(2ᵈ−1)/(2ˢ−1) members, each containing L binary k-spaces, where L is the Gaussian coefficient [s choose k]₂. These NL subspaces are distinct. Counting ordered isotropic bases one vector at a time and dividing by the number of bases gives the total number
```
I(d,k) = product over 0≤i<k of (2^(d−2i)−1)/(2^(k−i)−1).
```
Transitivity on isotropic k-spaces gives the exact mean (NL)²/I(d,k) for the number of k-spaces shared with a uniform symplectic conjugate. A mean below one proves nonemptiness of R_k. The product formula for L gives L < 4·2^{k(s−k)} because the infinite product of (1−2^(−j)), j≥1, exceeds 1/4 (separate the first two factors and bound the tail by one minus its geometric sum). Also N < 2·2^{d−s}. Each ratio defining I exceeds 2^{d−k−i}. The mean is strictly less than
```
64 · 2^{−(k−2)d + (2k−2)s − k(k+1)/2}.
```
For prime s≥7 take k=s−1 and use d≥2s. The exponent after the factor 64 is at most 2s−s(s−1)/2, at most −7. For s=5 and a≥2 it is −2d+20≤−20. In both cases R_(s−1) is nonempty. For s=3, a≥3 and k=3 the exponent is −d+6≤−12, giving nonempty R₃, used below.

The remaining s=5, a=1 uses [a finite witness](../code/verify_binary_spread_32.py). Work in E=F₂[X]/(X⁵+X²+1) on E² with the trace of its determinant form. Encode a polynomial-basis pair as the low and high five bits. In that basis the binary map with columns (813,201,812,499,143,634,838,938,484,459) is symplectic. For the 33² ordered pairs of one standard line and one transformed line the intersection sizes 1,2,4,8 occur 480,444,144,21 times respectively, including zero in each intersection. The verifier checks the field, form, map and every intersection directly. Thus R₄ is nonempty in this case also.

## Small extension degrees

First suppose s=2 or 3 and a≥3. For every g there is a conjugate spread sharing a whole line with its g-image. If ker(1+g) contains an isotropic s-space, take an orbit member containing it. Otherwise dim ker(1+g)≤2s−2, since an alternating space of dimension f, including a degenerate one, has an isotropic subspace of dimension at least ceil(f/2).

Inductively build a j-space U with W=U⊕gU totally isotropic, initially j=0. To extend U by w, restrict to H=(g⁻¹U+U+gU)-perp, of dimension n≥d−3j, and impose B(w,gw)=0. The function F(w)=1+B(w,gw) on H has Boolean degree at most two and F(0)=1, so its support has size at least 2^{n−2}. Indeed a nonconstant such function has a nonzero derivative in some direction. This affine-linear derivative has support at least 2^{n−1}, and its support is contained in the union of two translates of the support of F. The constant case is immediate. Avoid the three sets W, g⁻¹W, (1+g)⁻¹W to keep w and gw independent modulo W. Their union has size at most 2^{2j}(2+2^{2s−2}). For s=2 and j≤1 the lower bound for the zeros is at least 128 and the bad count at most 24. For s=3 and j≤2 the corresponding bounds are 1,024 and 288. Each addition therefore preserves isotropy and increases the dimension of W by two. At j=s choose one spread D₀ containing U and gU, as above. The latter space belongs to both D₀ and gD₀. This rules out universal displacement into R_s, already shown nonempty for s=3.

For nonemptiness of R₂ when s=2 and a≥3, set K=F_(2ᵃ), X=K², and take X and its K-dual with trace to F₂ of their hyperbolic pairing. The matrix J=[[0,1],[1,1]] acts on X, and the transpose J* acts on the dual. Together these generate an F₄-scalar structure since J²+J+1=0. The trace pairing identifies the second summand with the binary dual of the first. More explicitly the F₄-linear dual of X maps isomorphically to its binary dual by composing functionals with the F₄/F₂ trace. Under this map multiplication by J on the dual corresponds to precomposition with J. Thus the trace of the hyperbolic alternating F₄-form is the given binary pairing, and this is the required orbit of structures by symplectic bases over F₄.

Choose c in K with c different from 0,1 and c²+c+1 nonzero, possible since |K|≥8. Conjugate by A=diag(c,1) on X and inverse transpose on its dual. On X the difference J+AJA⁻¹ has off-diagonal entries 1+c and 1+c⁻¹ and zero diagonal. Its square is tI with t=(c+1)²/c, different from 0 and 1. On the dual the difference is its transpose. Thus the difference on the whole space is invertible, as is the difference plus the identity. On a common binary spread line the two scalar generators induce order-three maps in GL₂(2). Its unique subgroup of order three forces the generators to agree or to differ by the identity on the line. Both possibilities contradict invertibility. This supplies a pair in R₂.

It remains to treat s=3, a=2. Use K=F₆₄ containing E=F₈ and C=F₄. On K² take the trace to F₂ of the determinant form. It is the trace from E of the nondegenerate alternating E-form obtained by tracing the determinant from K to E. Write σ(x)=x⁴ and choose α∈K* with N_(K/C)(α)≠1, using surjectivity of the norm. On K let P(x)=x+ασ(x). This has zero kernel, since a nonzero solution of x=ασ(x) would give N(α)=1. Write M_λ for multiplication by λ. For every λ∈E outside F₂ and μ∈E,
```
P M_λ − M_μ P = M_(λ+μ) + M_(α(λ⁴+μ)) σ
```
is invertible. The two coefficients cannot both vanish since λ⁴≠λ. If both are nonzero, a nonzero kernel vector forces their ratio to have norm one. Each element of E* has K/C norm one (the norm exponent is 21), whereas α does not. This is impossible.

Take the adjoint for the nondegenerate trace pairing Tr_(K/F₂)(xy), so each M_λ is self-adjoint. With T=(P*)⁻¹ one has
```
P* (T M_λ − M_μ T) P* = M_λ P* − P* M_μ = (P M_λ − M_μ P)*.
```
Hence g=diag(P,T) is symplectic, and gM_λ−M_μg is invertible on K² for all the indicated scalars. If an E-line and an image E-line shared two binary independent vectors, their preimages on the latter line would be x,λx, λ outside F₂, and their images would satisfy g(λx)=μg(x). Invertibility rules this out. Thus R₂ is nonempty. The nearly invariant 3-space lemma rules out universal displacement into this R₂.

The primes s dividing m≥5 have now all been treated. Every extension-field action on the maximal-factor list fails the translation condition, completing the exclusion. Other fields, the two lower binary ranks, and classification of the operations in surviving full-group actions are separate obligations. The deduction uses none of the disputed intermediate many-factor claims of Li–Xia.

## Which marked restrictions can survive

X=G^(infinity) is characteristic in G. At least one mark induces a nonidentity automorphism of X: otherwise tau and K=tau Lambda centralize X, contradicting G=<K> and the nonabelian X. By Steinberg, Automorphisms of finite linear groups (1960), section 3 Theorem 3.2, printed p.608, and its A1 specialization, Aut(X)=PΓL2(R); this includes R=9, where PSL2(9)=A6. Its involutions have either identity restriction, a linear split or nonsplit restriction, or a half-field restriction. For a split linear involution, a suitable root subgroup is inverted, so the product of a mark with an X-conjugate is a nonidentity root element. For a half-field involution represented by Aσ, the projective square relation Aσ(A)=cI has c in the fixed field. Surjectivity of the scalar norm rescales to c=1; matrix Hilbert90 then makes it PGL2(R)-conjugate to σ; X-conjugacy is not needed. Its transported root group contains a nonidentity commutator with that involution, obtained from x(t^sigma-t) with t^sigma !=t. A suitable X-conjugate transports the resulting root element into H intersect X, contradicting H intersect Lambda^2=1. Hence every nonidentity mark restriction is a nonsplit PGL2(R) involution. Since tau and every mark restrict to PGL2(R) and G=<tau Lambda>, the restriction of the entire G to X lies in PGL2(R).

The centralizer in PGL2(R) of a nonsplit involution is the normalizer of a nonsplit torus, of order 2(R+1). Its intersection with X has order exactly R+1: the torus contains elements of both determinant square classes, so its normalizer is not contained in X. Hence the X-orbit of the restriction has size exactly c=R(R-1)/2. The X-orbit of an actual mark is no smaller than the orbit of its restriction, even when restriction has a kernel. This exceeds N for q>=5, since R(q−3)>2. It remains to consider q=3.

## The three-element field

Now N=(R^2-1)/2 and H intersect X is the root group U of order R. There is exactly one X-orbit in Lambda whose restriction to X is nonidentity: two such orbits would already exceed N. Restriction is X-equivariant, so the size of this actual orbit is an integer multiple of the restriction orbit size c=R(R-1)/2. Since c<=N<2c, the actual orbit has size exactly c. Every other mark centralizes X, and their number is exactly N-c=(R-1)/2. Because X is transitive and the G action is faithful, C=C_G(X) is semiregular and embeds into N_X(U)/U, which is cyclic of order (R-1)/2. Fix one centralizing mark lambda0. Products lambda0 lambda with other centralizing marks belong to C and are distinct. Consequently equality holds: |C|=(R-1)/2 and all centralizing marks form exactly lambda0 C.

We now show C<=Z(G), without assuming the diagonal outer action on X is inner. The graph-free projective-point action was established before the degree calculation. As q=3 has no base-field automorphism, M<=PGL_n(3). A projective linear element normalizing X lifts to a linear normalizer of SL2(R): conjugation can differ by a scalar character, and perfectness kills that character. The F3-algebra generated by SL2(R) is Mat2(R): subtract identity from upper and lower root transvections to get tE12 and tE21 for every t∈R, and multiply these to get tE11 and tE22. Its center is the scalar field R. Its commutant is R. Thus a linear normalizer normalizes R and belongs to ΓL2(R) over F3. Its field exponent is exactly the field component of its induced automorphism of PSL2(R). The established inclusion G->PGL2(R) under restriction forces this exponent to be zero, including R=9; hence every element of G acts R-linearly. Meanwhile every projective element centralizing X lies in R*/F3*, by the same perfect-lift and commutant argument. Therefore C<=R*/F3* and all of G centralizes C, proving C<=Z(G).

The reviewed central lemma makes tau fix C pointwise; every element of G centralizes C, so every mark tau k also fixes C pointwise. Since lambda0 and lambda0 c are involutions for every c in C, it follows that c^2=1 for all c. But |C|=(3^b-1)/2>2, a contradiction.

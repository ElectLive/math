# Every even-field twisted E6 full socle: proposed assembly

Proposed continuation of [0323:C0010](../foundations/research-C0010.md), with independent exact-source review recorded below. Let Q be a finite simple non-Moufang left Bol loop. Under the canonical, almost-simple factorization, exceptional-automorphism, graph-class and maximal-subgroup inputs stated below, Soc(Mlt(Q)) cannot be ²E6(2^f), for any f≥1. This concerns the actual full multiplication socle; it does not exclude ²E6 as an arbitrary section or component. The even-f, diagonal-free case is the reviewed [diagonal-free proof](e6-even-diagonal-free-socles.md) at source `79273df37632fe7b6143710d0566566d561d6dd8`; the new argument below treats odd f.

## Actual outer labels

Let M=Mlt(Q), S=Soc(M)=²E6(q), q=2^f, G=LMlt(Q), I=M_1, H=G∩I, and Λ the original marked set. Reviewed C0008 makes M almost simple and M=GI. If S is not contained in G then it is not contained in I: normality and faithful transitivity would put S in the action kernel. This gives a proper factorization of an exceptional almost-simple group, which the list quoted in Kazarin–Martínez-Pastor–Pérez-Ramos (2015), Lemma 11 pp.56–57, from Liebeck–Praeger–Saxl 1990 Theorem B excludes for ²E6. Thus S≤G≤Aut(S). All these classification statements are retained inputs.

Assume f is odd. Then q≡2 mod3 and the diagonal group D has order 3. Burness–Guralnick–Harper, arXiv:2006.01421v2, §3.2 Table 2 and Lemma 3.12 pp.19–20, gives Out(S)≅S3×C_f, with D normal in the S3 factor and graph labels the reflections. Restriction embeds Aut(G) into Aut(S); let L=G/S and let r be the order-two label of canonical inversion τ. The reviewed 84eba0ffd4dfab4b6d64853a3131010ad2b945b8 inner-inversion theorem implies r∉L, because O2(G)=1. Since τ inverts every element of the actual generating set K, L is generated by elements inverted by conjugation by r. Projecting to the central C_f factor shows that projection has exponent at most 2, and hence is trivial because f is odd. Also r has trivial C_f coordinate. Thus L≤S3. As r normalizes L, the possibilities are L=1 or L=D: a reflection subgroup C2 is self-normalizing, and S3 itself contains r. No quotient-folder construction is involved.

## The two graph types and actual stabilizer orders

Aschbacher–Seitz, *Involutions in Chevalley groups over fields of even order*, §19 (19.8)–(19.9), pp.80–81, gives two Aut(S)-classes of graph involutions, represented by the pinned graph σ and σt with t a highest-root involution. Their S-centralizers are F4(q) and C_F4(q)(t), respectively. Here C_Out(S)(r)=⟨r⟩×C_f. Writing phi for the standard order-2f field generator, its f-th power is sigma and its square generates the central odd-order C_f factor, by BGH Lemma3.12. These commute, and t has parameter 1, so the actual centralizers of both representatives project onto C_Out(S)(r). Therefore each of the two Aut(S)-classes has exactly three S-classes, one above each S3 reflection label; each fixed graph label has one S-class of each type. This supplies the non-splitting statement needed below, including q=2.

Let c1=|S:F4(q)| and c2=|S:C_F4(q)(t)|. Since t is a long-root 2-central involution,

    c2=L0 c1,        L0=(q^12−1)(q^4+1).

For L=1 the actual nonempty S-invariant Λ is one or both S-classes in its single reflection label. For L=D, conjugation by D cyclically permutes the three graph labels, so Λ is one or both of the corresponding G-orbits of sizes 3c1 and 3c2. In the latter case G'=S; the reviewed all-parity canonical interface G=HG' gives H→D onto and [H:H∩S]=3.

If both types are selected, the factor of 3 cancels when L=D, so in either case

    |H|=|F4(q)|/(1+L0).

But 1+L0=q^4(q^12+q^8−1), and the odd integer q^12+q^8−1>1 is coprime to q^12−1 and q^8−1, reducing respectively to q^8 and q^4. It is therefore also coprime to q^6−1 and q²−1 and hence to |F4(q)|=q^24(q^12−1)(q^8−1)(q^6−1)(q²−1). The displayed integer index is impossible.

If the larger graph type is selected, the actual S-class square lemma in source `79273df37632fe7b6143710d0566566d561d6dd8`, now independently accepted by the three recorded reviewers, contains all three S-involution classes in either rational form, including q=2. Its class is contained in Λ, even when L=D. The relevant actual H∩S has 2-part q^24: |H|=|C_F4(q)(t)| when L=1, and |H∩S|=|C_F4(q)(t)|/3 when L=D. Cauchy gives an involution in H∩S∩Λ², contradicting the canonical folder condition.

## The small graph type

If L=1, |H|=|F4(q)|. If L=D, |H∩S|=|F4(q)|/3. In both cases define B=H∩S. Its 2-part is q^24. A Zsigmondy prime ℓ of order 12 modulo q divides |B|: ℓ≥13, so dividing by 3 does not remove it. The exponent-12 case is not a Zsigmondy exception.

Use Craven, arXiv:2103.04869v4 (16 May 2025), Theorem 1.1 and Tables 3 and 10, the corrected complete maximal-subgroup list for S. Here e'=(3,q+1)=3 and d=(2,q−1)=1; e' is odd and changes no 2-part. A maximal proper overgroup of B exists. The following checks every ordinary maximal row relevant in p=2; the rows marked novelty in the source are not maximals of S itself.

* The four parabolic Levis are PSU6(q), Spin8−(q), PSL3(q²)×SL2(q), and SL3(q)×SL2(q²). Their odd order factors divide products of q^j−1 with j≤10: in particular q^4+1 divides q^8−1, and q^i+1 divides q^(2i)−1. Thus none has ℓ. The unipotent radicals are 2-groups.
* The other same-field nonparabolic rows have 2-parts bounded by q^20 for PΩ10−(q); q^16 for PSL2×PSU6; 2q^9 for each of the PSU3(q)^3.Sym3 and PSL3(q²)×PSL3(q) rows; q^9 for PSU3(q³).3; 2q^12 for PΩ8+(q).Sym3; q^12 for ³D4(q).3; 2^7 for the Weyl-group torus row; 2^3 for the (q²−q+1)^3.(3^{1+2}.SL2(3)) row; q^9 for PSU3(q)×G2(q); and q^6 for G2(q). These are all strictly smaller than q^24 for q≥2. The PSp8(q).2 row has p≠2, and the indicated 3-local row has p≥5.
* The ²E6(q0) subfield row has q=q0^r with r an odd prime and 2-part at most q0^36=q^(36/r)≤q^12; its extra gcd(e',r) factor is odd. Table 3's only ordinary p=2 almost-simple exceptional row is Fi22 at q=2; its 2-part is 2^17<2^24. The Ω7(3) q=2 row is novelty, while the other listed rows have odd characteristic.

The only remaining maximal overgroup is a natural F4(q). Its order is exactly |F4(q)|; Table 10 allows three S-classes because e'=3. For L=1, equality of orders gives B=H=F4(q). For L=D, B has index 3 in that maximal subgroup, impossible because F4(q) is nonabelian simple and cannot act nontrivially on three cosets.

For L=1, source `79273df37632fe7b6143710d0566566d561d6dd8` provides the explicit nonidentity involution in the smaller graph-class square, via the graph-paired root product in both rational forms. Guralnick–Robinson, arXiv:2012.08693v1 §3.3 pp.4–5, states that the natural F4(q) meets every S-involution class. The three F4(q) S-classes are conjugate by inner-diagonal automorphisms, and Burness–Thomas §2 Remark 2.2 states that S-involution classes are not fused by inner-diagonal automorphisms. Hence each actual F4(q) class meets the supported S-involution class, giving H∩Λ²≠1. In the L=D branch the index-three contradiction already suffices.

This finishes the odd-f continuation; together with the independently reviewed even-f source `79273df37632fe7b6143710d0566566d561d6dd8`, it proposes the full stated characteristic-two twisted family.

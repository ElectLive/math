## Odd solvable linear groups of power-of-two dimension

Let J be a finite odd solvable group, faithfully irreducible on a finite-dimensional vector space V over Fq of characteristic p. If dim_Fq V is a power of 2, then J is abelian and p does not divide |J|.

Extend scalars to an algebraic closure. The absolutely irreducible constituents form one Frobenius orbit with equal dimensions; their number divides dim V, so each dimension is a power of 2. Frobenius-conjugate representations have the same kernel. Their direct sum is faithful, hence each constituent W is faithful. Since O_p(J) has nonzero fixed points on W and is normal, it fixes W pointwise; faithfulness gives O_p(J)=1.

For r≠p, an irreducible representation of the r-group O_r(J) over the algebraic closure has r-power degree. The distinct isotypic components of W for this normal subgroup form a transitive J-set, hence their number divides |J| and is odd. This number also divides dim W, a power of 2, so there is one. Its irreducible degree, a power of odd r, likewise divides dim W and is therefore one. O_r(J) acts by scalars. Consequently the Fitting subgroup F(J), generated by its normal Sylow subgroups, acts by scalars and is central, using faithfulness.

For completeness F(J) is self-centralizing in a finite solvable group: if C_J(F)/Z(F) were nontrivial, a minimal normal section N/Z(F) inside it is elementary abelian. Since Z(F) is central in N, N is nilpotent of class at most2 and normal in J, so N≤F; but N≤C_J(F) then forces N≤Z(F), contradiction. Thus J=F(J) is abelian. A faithful irreducible representation in characteristic p has O_p(J)=1, so p∤|J|. The r-group degree input has the following direct proof. Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from r, let W be irreducible for an r-group R, and choose N normal of index r. Maschke makes W|N semisimple, and its isotypic components form a transitive R-set. If there are r components, choose an irreducible N-submodule U in one; its r translates form a direct R-invariant sum, which equals W, so dim W=r dim U. If there is one isotypic component, an intertwiner T for a generator g of R/N can be rescaled so T^r=ρ(g^r), by Schur’s lemma and existence of r-th roots; thus the irreducible N-module U extends to R. Now W=U⊗M, and the remaining cyclic quotient acts on M. Its action is diagonalizable in characteristic different from r, so irreducibility forces dim M=1 and dim W=dim U. Induct on |R| to get r-power degree. The isotypic transitivity used in the main proof is elementary: the sum of translates of any isotypic component is a nonzero invariant subspace and hence is all W. For the scalar-extension step, the image algebra A of Fq[J] on irreducible V has radical acting trivially and is a finite-dimensional simple algebra. By the finite-field Artin–Wedderburn theorem A≅Mat_d(F_(q^e)); after extension to an algebraic closure it becomes the product of e matrix algebras, cyclically permuted by Frobenius. Their simple natural modules are exactly the conjugate constituents used above. This makes the standard algebra input explicit; no classification of odd-order groups is needed for the lemma itself.

## Odd semilinear fusions cannot occur

Let G be any finite odd group with automorphism τ inducing inversion on G_ab, and let V be an elementary abelian p-group with an F_p-linear G-action and a compatible F_p-linear τ-action. In a canonical odd envelope, G is generated by the τ-inverted section K, so τ induces inversion on G_ab. Suppose D is a finite subfield of End_{F_p}(V) normalized under conjugation by both G and τ. Then G centralizes D.

Indeed, conjugation gives an equivariant homomorphism ψ:G→Gal(D/F_p). This Galois group is cyclic, and conjugation by τ acts trivially on it, because τ itself induces a field automorphism and all field automorphisms commute. But ψ factors through G_ab, where τ induces inversion. Hence ψ(g)=ψ(g)^{-1} for all g. Since G has odd order, ψ(G) has odd order and is trivial.

This applies without assuming that V is irreducible for a chosen normal subgroup. Thus taking an existing irreducible representation, enlarging its scalar field to raise the available dimension, and adding odd Frobenius elements to fuse the resulting constituents cannot give a canonical odd envelope so long as the enlarged scalar field is preserved by G and τ. The scalar extension alone is reducible: the action matrices lie in the old field, so an old-field form and its scalar multiples are proper invariant F_p-subspaces. A possible repair must avoid such a normalized field, or change the faithful irreducible action rather than adjoining an odd semilinear quotient. In particular taking D=End_T(V) when T is τ-stable and irreducible is only a special case; D need not be the whole centralizer.

## The natural q²-adic half-support is singular

For any odd prime q and any field of characteristic different from q containing a primitive q²-th root ζ, let R be the nonzero quadratic residues mod q, n=|R|=(q−1)/2, and let S⊂Z/q² consist of all nonzero classes whose first nonzero q-adic digit lies in R. It has n(q+1) elements, with nq units and n nonunits. Consider the Fourier half-minor M=(ζ^{−ij})_{i,j∈S}. Then

rank M ≤ n²+2n < n(q+1)=2n²+2n,

so the minor is singular and its nullity is at least n².

For the proof, partition the unit rows and columns according to residues a,b∈R mod q. Write i=a+qs, j=b+qt, and ω=ζ^q. The (a,b)-block has entries

ζ^{−(a+qs)(b+qt)}=ζ^{−ab} ω^{−at} ω^{−bs}.

It is an outer product and has rank one. The unit-unit block consists of n² such blocks, so its rank is at most n². Adding the n nonunit columns and n nonunit rows increases rank by at most 2n. This proves the bound. Exactly the same argument works with any opposite-sign row index convention; no field characteristic 3 special identity is used.

Consequently the natural attempt to enlarge the scalar field by replacing the exponent-q center and q-dimensional Schrödinger model by exponent-q² and the valuation-defined half-support fails its essential Fourier-minor test, regardless of whether the extra group and simplicity requirements can be met.

Remaining viable boundary: a faithful irreducible module whose scalar field is intrinsically large, but which uses a support other than this q-adic half-set (or a different nonabelian p′-kernel), is not excluded. Varying admissible q is another possibility, but the present sufficient Jacobi criteria do not prove infinitely many q. The two obstructions rule out specific seemingly direct routes; they do not imply a universal bound on defect.

## Projected stabilizers and distributed cocycles

This applies to the common-center Schrödinger model underlying [the higher-defect construction](odd-monolithic-defect-two.md), but does not require its lexicographic support or its specific stabilizer. Let p,q be distinct odd primes with p|(q−1), put d=ord_q(p), and suppose d=2m. Let T be the central product of r Heisenberg groups of order q³, with common center generated by z, and let A=C_p^r act by independent order-p scalars on the r symplectic coordinate planes. Let X=<x₁,…,x_r> be the diagonal elementary abelian q-subgroup. Take E=F_{p^d}, V=E^{F_q^r}, the faithful Schrödinger representation in which z acts by a primitive qth root, and G=V⋊(T⋊A). Let τ be the compatible involution which swaps the x_i and y_i axes, inverts z and A, and acts on V by the tensor Fourier transform composed with the order-two automorphism of E/F_{p^m}. Assume these operators define an involution and that (G,H,K,τ) is a finite odd canonical Bol folder with the actual H-image

HV/V = J = X⋊A.

Writing W=H∩V and U=W∩V^X, one necessarily has

r≤m,   dim_Fp U=m−r,   dim_Fp W=mq^r−r.                     (4)

No simplicity, lexicographic support, or decomposition of W into coordinate lines is assumed. For the reviewed q=7,p=3 model m=3, so no such stabilizer projection can give r≥4, regardless of how its constraints are distributed.

The action of X on V has fixed space V^X=E e₀. Every nontrivial irreducible F_pX-module has dimension d: a nontrivial character has a primitive qth root among its values, and its field of values is E. By Maschke's theorem, the nontrivial X-isotypic part of the J-stable W has dimension divisible by d. Isomorphic coordinate modules may mix, so this is only a dimension statement; it does not assert W is a sum of coordinate E-lines. Put u=dim_Fp U. Then

dim W≡u (mod 2m).                                           (5)

The standard fixed-point count for odd canonical folders gives |H|=|C_G(τ)|; the full-inverted-set and fixed-point interfaces are reviewed in [odd-folder-radicals](odd-folder-radicals.md) at the recorded source revision. The Fourier/field involution is E/F_{p^m}-semilinear on an E-space of dimension q^r, so its fixed space on V has F_p-dimension mq^r. On T⋊A, τ fixes q^r elements: it inverts A and the common center and fixes one axis in each plane. Thus |H|=p^{mq^r}q^r. Since |J|=p^r q^r,

dim W=mq^r−r,  and hence  r+u≡m (mod 2m).                 (6)

The subgroup H/W is a graph for a 1-cocycle J→V/W. Because X is a p′-group, restriction/inflation and exactness of X-invariants give

H¹(J,V/W)≅H¹(A,(V/W)^X)≅Hom(A,E/U),                        (7)

where A acts trivially on the origin. Thus after conjugating H by a vector of V, its graph can be represented by a homomorphism f:A→E/U at the origin. If f has nonzero kernel, the conjugated H contains a nonidentity pure a∈A. Such a is τ-inverted, and so a belongs to Λ²: τhat and τhat a are marked involutions. The set Λ is the full outside involution class and Λ² is G-conjugacy invariant. Therefore conjugating H by V preserves its avoidance of Λ²; this conclusion does not assume that K itself is conjugacy invariant. The canonical full-transversal criterion H∩Λ²=1 rules out the pure a. Consequently f is injective and

r+u≤2m.                                                     (8)

As r+u is nonnegative, (6) and (8) force r+u=m, proving (4). In particular, the origin bound is intrinsic to this projected stabilizer; spreading a cocycle over nonzero A-orbits cannot remove it. The theorem leaves open other stabilizer projections, other irreducible kernels, and other normal forms. Its p-general statement is conditional on the explicitly stated involution and folder hypotheses; it does not assert that such a folder exists for every p,q,r.

## Valuation-defined support at every prime-power exponent

**Continuation with the scoped independent reviews below.** The prime-square singularity above extends to every exponent a≥2. Let q be an odd prime, let the coefficient field have characteristic different from q and contain a primitive q^a-th root ζ, and put n=(q−1)/2. Let S be the nonzero residue classes modulo q^a whose first nonzero q-adic digit is a quadratic residue modulo q. Then |S|=(q^a−1)/2, and the Fourier half-minor

M_S=(ζ^{−ij})_{i,j∈S}

satisfies

rank M_S ≤ n²q^{a−2}+q^{a−1}−1 < (q^a−1)/2.               (9)

In particular it is singular. This is a statement about this support choice; it does not exclude mixed κ-orbit transversals or assert nonexistence of a folder.

For the proof, S contains nq^{a−1} units and (q^{a−1}−1)/2 nonunits. Split the unit rows and columns into their n possible residue classes modulo q. For a block with i=b+qs and j=c+qt, the Fourier entry is

ζ^{−bc} ζ^{−qbt} ζ^{−qcs} ζ^{−q²st}.

After removing invertible row and column factors, this depends only on s,t modulo q^{a−2}; hence each of the n² blocks has rank at most q^{a−2}, and the entire unit-unit block has rank at most n²q^{a−2}. Adding the nonunit rows and columns increases rank by at most q^{a−1}−1, proving the first inequality in (9). The difference between the final size and the bound is

[q^{a−2}(q²−2q−1)+2]/4 > 0,

which proves the second inequality. This argument is independent of characteristic except for the existence of ζ. It shows that any support induction which succeeds for all a must depart from the uniform first-digit sign rule; the existence of suitable mixed supports remains open.

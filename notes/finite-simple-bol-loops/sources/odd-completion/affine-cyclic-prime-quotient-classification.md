# Solvable p-prime affine complements with cyclic p quotient

Mira Cult
Scoped classification for [C0010](../../claims/0010.md), with exact-source reviews below: this classifies, up to loop isomorphism, the exact-factorization loops constructed from **all** nontranslation regular subgroups in the following affine family. It does not classify other affine complements or all loops of the same orders. No parity restriction is imposed on p or |T|; the loop has odd order precisely when both p and |T| are odd. This extends the group mechanism in odd-unbounded-fitting-height.md, using [C0001 Theorem 2.2](../foundations/C0001.md)'s exact normal-subloop criterion rather than the odd-only τ-stable criterion.

## Data

Let p be any prime, T a nontrivial finite solvable p′-group, and φ an automorphism of T of order dividing p such that φ acts fixed-point-freely on T_ab (equivalently, φ−1 is an automorphism of T_ab). Since T is nontrivial solvable, T_ab is nontrivial and this condition forces φ to have exact order p. Put S=T⋊⟨φ⟩. Let V be a finite faithful Fp S-module whose restriction to T is irreducible, and assume dim_Fp V≥2. We use the same symbol φ for its linear action and set X=V⋊S. The natural representation is faithful on S by assumption.

For every nonzero φ-invariant functional ℓ∈V* define W=ker ℓ and

Y_ℓ={(v,φ^i): i∈Fp, ℓ(v)=i} ≤ X,    Y₁=S.             (1)

Nonzero invariant ℓ exist because a p-element acts unipotently on the dual. Every Y_ℓ is regular on V, and every nontranslation regular Y≤X is S-conjugate to Y_ℓ, with ℓ uniquely determined once the Sylow alignment π(Y)=⟨φ⟩ is chosen; different alignments can identify parameters. The factorization X=Y_ℓ S is exact. Each associated Nagy loop is simple and proper, conditional on [C0001](../../claims/0001.md)–[C0002](../../claims/0002.md). Two data sets (p,T,φ,V,ℓ) and (p̃,T̃,φ̃,Ṽ,ℓ̃) yield isomorphic loops iff p=p̃ and there exist an Fp-linear isomorphism L:V→Ṽ and a group isomorphism β:S→S̃ such that

L(sv)=β(s)L(v),     β(φ)=φ̃^r,     ℓ̃ = r ℓ L⁻¹       (2)

for some r∈Fp*. Here β(s) acts through the specified faithful representation on Ṽ. This permits comparison of distinct groups/modules as well as different ℓ in one group. For a fixed (S,V), quotient the invariant nonzero functionals by the actual normalizer action described by (2). No closed orbit count is asserted.

## Group structure and exhaustion

Every α=inn(t)φ^i, with t∈T and i≠0 mod p, acts fixed-point-freely on T_ab: inn(t) is trivial there and every nontrivial power of the order-p linear action has the same fixed space. Thus [T,α]=T, where this denotes the normal subgroup generated by commutators: otherwise the nontrivial solvable quotient T/[T,α] has nontrivial abelianization, but α acts both trivially on it and fixed-point-freely as a quotient of T_ab, contradiction. In the last implication the coprime action is essential: the order-p action on T_ab has order coprime to |T_ab|, so its fixed-point-free property passes to quotients.

By irreducibility and nontrivial faithful T-action, [V,T]=V and C_V(T)=0. Consequently X′=V⋊T, X/X′≅Cp, C_X(V)=V, C_X(X′)=1, and V is the unique minimal normal subgroup of X. Indeed any nontrivial normal N◁X disjoint from V centralizes V and lies in V, impossible. Also S has no nontrivial normal p-subgroup: if P◁S is a p-group, [P,T]≤P∩T=1; every nonidentity element tφ^i∈P would centralize T, contrary to [T,inn(t)φ^i]=T. Hence Fit(X)=V, although only characteristicity of V is needed below.

Let Y be regular on V. It is a p-group, so π(Y)≤S is a p-group of order at most p. If π(Y)=1 then Y=V. Otherwise its projection has order p and, since T is a normal Hall p′-subgroup, Sylow conjugacy by T aligns it with ⟨φ⟩. Let W=Y∩V. It is a φ-invariant hyperplane, because |Y|=|V|. The p-element φ acts trivially on the one-dimensional quotient V/W. The coset of a lift of φ is a nonzero vector in V/W, or the lift could be multiplied by a translation in W to give an element of S, contradicting Y∩S=1. There is thus a unique nonzero invariant functional ℓ with W=ker ℓ and ℓ(v)=1 on that coset, and Y=Y_ℓ. Conversely (1) is closed under the affine product, has order |V|, and its intersection with S is 1, so it is regular. Its core is trivial since every nontrivial normal subgroup of X meets V in V, whereas W<V; S is core-free by faithful action. Both factors are nontrivial.

## Simplicity and properness

By [C0002 Proposition 1.1](../foundations/C0002.md), the generated faithful folder is G={(x,y)∈X²:xy∈X′}, H=G∩(Y_ℓ×S), K={(x,x⁻¹):x∈X}. Its order is |X||X′| and |H|=|X′|. Set M=V×V. The group T×T lies in G; since |T| is prime to p, M is semisimple as an Fp[T×T]-module, with nonisomorphic irreducible summands V×1 and 1×V. Also C_G(M)=M. Hence every nontrivial normal N◁G meets M in an axis or in M.

Suppose N∩M=V×1. Centralizing 1×V puts the second projection of N in C_X(V)=V. Since 1×X′≤G, then [N,1×X′]≤N∩(1×V)=1. Thus that projection centralizes X′ and is trivial, so N≤X′×1. The K images modulo N are distinct, and faithfulness of H implies [G:HN]<|K|=|X|. The other single-axis case is symmetric.

Suppose M≤N. Modulo M, G/M=(T×T)⋊⟨(φ,φ⁻¹)⟩. If N/M contains an element with nonzero exponent in that cyclic factor, commutators with each T factor generate T×T by the preceding lemma, forcing N=G. Otherwise N/M≤T×T; let R be its first projection. Normality of N/M in G/M shows that R is normal in T and φ-stable; consequently V⋊R is normal in X. The image of H in G/M is {(φ^i, tφ⁻i):i∈Fp,t∈T}; hence [G:HN]=[T:R]. On the other hand projection of K into X/(V⋊R) gives |KN/N|≥[X:V⋊R]=p[T:R]>[G:HN]. Thus every nontrivial proper normal N has strict defect |KN/N|>[G:HN]. [C0001 Theorem 2.2](../foundations/C0001.md)'s exact normal-subloop criterion rules out every proper nontrivial normal subloop, without requiring odd order or assuming a quotient transversal.

The properness argument in [C0002 Proposition 1.1](../foundations/C0002.md) applies to this simple core-free exact factorization with both factors nontrivial. Thus each loop is a simple proper left Bol loop, conditional on the stated folder inputs. Its order is |X|=|V|p|T|, so it is odd exactly when p and |T| are both odd.

## Reconstruction and isomorphisms

The two axes of M are exactly the minimal normal subgroups of G, since every nontrivial normal subgroup meets M. They are distinguished by H: H∩(V×1)=W×1 is nontrivial (dim V≥2), whereas H∩(1×V)=1. Any folder isomorphism therefore preserves their order. Moreover

C_G(1×V)=X′×V,    O^p(C_G(1×V))=X′×1,

and similarly for the other axis. Here O^p is the intersection of normal subgroups with p-group quotient. Indeed X′=V⋊T has no nontrivial p-group quotient: any map to a p-group kills the p′-group T and then kills [V,T]=V. The V factor has p-power order. These subgroups intrinsically recover both X′ coordinate axes.

Conjugation of X on X′ is injective since C_X(X′)=1. A folder isomorphism carrying the X′ axes to the corresponding axes therefore induces two group isomorphisms α₁,α₂:X→X̃, acting coordinatewise on G (the coordinate projections of G are onto X). Preservation of K forces α₁(x)=α₂(x) for every x∈X; write the common map α. Its preservation of H is equivalent to α(Y_ℓ)=Y_ℓ̃ and α(S)=S̃: both projections of H are onto their displayed factors because X′Y_ℓ=X′S=X. Conversely such an α induces a folder and loop isomorphism. The axes also force the same characteristic p across isomorphic data.

Since V is characteristic in X and α preserves S, α is determined by L=α|V and β=α|S satisfying the first equation of (2). The subgroup projection of Y_ℓ is ⟨φ⟩, so α(Y_ℓ)=Y_ℓ̃ forces β(φ)=φ̃^r with r∈Fp*. Formula (1) then gives ℓ̃L=rℓ, the final equation in (2). Conversely all equations (2) define an α mapping both factors, completing the criterion. ∎

The theorem does not assert a Fitting-height value without additional hypotheses or classify all solvable or odd Bol loops.

## Necessity of the fixed-free condition

More generally, retain p arbitrary, nontrivial solvable p′-group T, φ of order dividing p, S=T⋊Cp, and a faithful S-module V whose restriction to T is irreducible, with dim V≥2; here Cp is a formal complement, including when φ acts trivially on T. For any nontranslation regular Y, Sylow conjugacy in S aligns π(Y) with Cp. Put D=T′[T,φ]◁T. Since [V,T]=V, we have X′=V⋊D. The image of X′Y in S is D⟨φ⟩, so X′Y<X iff D<T. In this case [C0002 Proposition 1.1](../foundations/C0002.md)'s exact-factorization necessity gives a proper nontrivial normal subloop; the required core-freeness holds as before since V is irreducible and C_X(V)=V. On the other hand, D=T iff φ−1 is surjective on T_ab, equivalently fixed-point-free: φ has p-power order coprime to |T_ab|, so fixed points and coinvariants are simultaneously trivial. Therefore, within these ambient hypotheses and assuming C0001/C0002, the loop constructed from a nontranslation regular Y is simple iff φ acts fixed-point-freely on T_ab. The forward condition forces the automorphism φ to have exact order p because a nontrivial solvable group has nontrivial abelianization. No other ambient family is covered.

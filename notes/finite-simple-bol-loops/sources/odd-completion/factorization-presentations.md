## Setup and conventions

All loops below are finite simple nonassociative left Bol loops. A folder is the canonical faithful folder `(G,H,K)`, with `G = ⟨K⟩`, `H` the identity stabilizer, and `K` the left translations. Let `τ` be the unique automorphism of `G` satisfying `τ(k)=k⁻¹` for `k∈K`. For such a folder, let `W(G,H,K)` be the set of normal subgroups `N◁G` satisfying

N∩τ(N)=1,   [G:N]=|K|,   HN∩Hτ(N)=H.                (1)

Every folder automorphism commutes with `τ`, by uniqueness, and hence acts on `W`. The map `N↦τ(N)` is also an involution of `W` and commutes with that action: the first two conditions in (1) are symmetric under `τ`, and the third merely exchanges its two factors. This map on witnesses need not itself be a folder automorphism, since `τ(H)` need not be `H`.

An **ordered presentation** is an exact factorization triple `(X,Y₀,Y₁)` of finite groups, `X=Y₀Y₁`, `Y₀∩Y₁=1`, whose Nagy loop is simple and nonassociative. Ordered triples are equivalent if a group isomorphism `α:X→X'` maps `Yᵢ` to `Yᵢ'` for `i=0,1`. An **unordered presentation** is an ordered presentation considered up to that equivalence and swapping its two factors. The construction and faithfulness under the present hypotheses are established in the criterion manuscript, citing Nagy, *A class of simple proper Bol loops*, arXiv:math/0703919v1, Proposition 3.2.

## Proposition: decorated folders and presentation triples

There is a bijection between equivalence classes of ordered presentations and isomorphism classes of pairs `(folder,N)` with `N∈W`. Here a decorated-folder isomorphism is a folder isomorphism carrying the designated subgroup to the designated subgroup. Swapping the factors corresponds to replacing `N` by `τ(N)`. Consequently, for a fixed loop `Q` with folder `(G,H,K)`, its ordered presentation classes are

W(G,H,K) / Aut(G,H,K),

and its unordered presentation classes are the orbits for the action generated by `Aut(G,H,K)` and the involution `N↦τ(N)`.

### Proof

Given a witness `N`, put `X=G/N` and embed

ι_N:G → X×X,     g↦(gN,τ(g)N).

Its kernel is `N∩τ(N)=1`. As shown in the criterion, `ι_N(K)` is `S_X={(x,x⁻¹):x∈X}`, its image is

F_X = ⟨S_X⟩ = {(u,v)∈X×X : uv∈X'},

and the projections of `ι_N(H)` are factors `Y₀,Y₁` with `X=Y₀Y₁`, `Y₀∩Y₁=1`, and `ι_N(H)=F_X∩(Y₀×Y₁)`. The designated normal subgroup is exactly `ker(pr₁|F_X)=1×X'` under this embedding.

Conversely, for an ordered presentation `(X,Y₀,Y₁)`, use its faithful generated folder `(F_X,J_X,S_X)` where `J_X=F_X∩(Y₀×Y₁)`, and designate `N_X=1×X'=ker(pr₁|F_X)`. The criterion proves `N_X` satisfies (1). It also proves the coordinate projections of `J_X` are exactly `Y₀,Y₁`. Thus extracting a triple from this decorated folder recovers the original triple up to the canonical isomorphism `F_X/N_X≅X`, and constructing the decorated folder from a witness recovers the original decorated folder via `ι_N`.

It remains to check isomorphisms. If `β:(G,H,K,N)→(G',H',K',N')` is a decorated-folder isomorphism, uniqueness gives `βτ=τ'β`, and `β(N)=N'` induces a group isomorphism `α:G/N→G'/N'`. The two embeddings satisfy

ι_{N'} β = (α×α) ι_N.

Since `β(H)=H'`, taking the two coordinate projections shows `α(Yᵢ)=Yᵢ'` for each `i`. Conversely, if `α:X→X'` is an isomorphism carrying `Yᵢ` to `Yᵢ'`, then `α×α` maps `F_X,J_X,S_X,N_X` onto their primed counterparts, giving a decorated-folder isomorphism. This proves the ordered bijection, including the iff assertion for two decorated folders.

For a witness `N`, define the natural isomorphism `b:G/τ(N)→G/N` by `b(gτ(N))=τ(g)N`. The embedding associated to `τ(N)`, followed by `b` on both coordinates, equals coordinate swap composed with `ι_N`. Therefore the triple extracted from `τ(N)` is equivalent to `(X,Y₁,Y₀)`. This proves the unordered assertion.

Finally, the faithful-folder correspondence identifies loop isomorphisms with folder isomorphisms. Fixing one folder for `Q`, any presentation of a loop isomorphic to `Q` transfers its witness to this folder; two transfers differ by a folder automorphism. The ordered and unordered orbit descriptions follow. ∎

The orbit result is an exact obstruction to an unqualified factorization-data isomorphism criterion. Two triples have isomorphic underlying loops if and only if, after identifying their folders by some folder isomorphism, their witnesses belong to the same underlying folder but may lie in distinct witness orbits. They are equivalent as unordered triples exactly when an identification carries one witness to the other or its `τ`-mate. Thus uniqueness of the unordered witness orbit for each underlying loop is sufficient and necessary for the naive unordered group-isomorphism criterion on that class. This observation does **not** establish that multiple unordered orbits actually occur; no such example is claimed here. In particular, the rigidity argument for the almost-simple family in [C0002](../../claims/0002.md) cannot by itself be extended to all exact factorizations without controlling the witness orbit set.

## Example: the order-24 presentation

Take `X=S₄`, `Y₀=C₄`, `Y₁=S₃` (a point stabilizer), as in Nagy, arXiv:math/0703919v1, §4 Example II. The generated folder is

F={(u,v)∈S₄×S₄ : sgn(u)=sgn(v)},
    J=F∩(C₄×S₃),       S={(x,x⁻¹):x∈S₄}.

Here `|F|=24·12=288`, `|S|=24`, `|J|=12`, `S₄'=A₄`, and `τ` is coordinate swap. Every witness has order 12 by `[F:N]=24`. We show that the only **normal subgroups of order 12** in `F` are `A₄×1` and `1×A₄`.

The two projections `F→S₄` are surjective, so for `N◁F` of order 12 each `prᵢ(N)` is a normal subgroup of `S₄` whose order divides 12. The possibilities are `1`, `V₄`, and `A₄`.

* If one projection is trivial, then `N` lies in the other coordinate copy of `A₄`, and order forces equality.
* If both projections are `V₄`, then `N≤V₄×V₄`, impossible because `12` does not divide `16`.
* If one projection is `A₄` and the other is `V₄`, the first projection is an isomorphism `N≅A₄`, inducing an epimorphism `A₄→V₄`. Its kernel would have order 3 and be normal in `A₄`, which is impossible.
* If both projections are `A₄`, they are isomorphisms and `N` is the graph of an automorphism of `A₄`, so `N∩(A₄×1)=1`. But normality under `A₄×1≤F` gives, for each `(a,b)∈N` and `c∈A₄`, a commutator `([a,c],1)∈N∩(A₄×1)`. Surjectivity of the first projection would make `A₄` abelian, a contradiction.

The two remaining groups are witnesses by the necessity direction of the criterion, and `τ` exchanges them. Their ordered orbits are distinct: a folder automorphism carrying one to the other would, by the decorated equivalence, induce an automorphism of `S₄` exchanging `C₄` and `S₃`, which have different orders. Thus `Q₂₄` has precisely two ordered presentation classes and one unordered presentation class.

This is a bounded exact calculation, not a proof of witness uniqueness for arbitrary simple loops. The scope of the independent review is recorded below.

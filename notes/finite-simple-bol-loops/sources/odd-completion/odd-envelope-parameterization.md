## Equivariant elementary-extension recursion

Begin with the trivial group Q and its identity automorphism α. At each finite stage choose an odd prime p, a nonzero finite-dimensional F_p Q-module V with action ρ, and an involution T of V such that

    T ρ(x) T⁻¹ = ρ(α(x))  (x∈Q).

Thus V is a module for the **formal** semidirect product Q⋊⟨s⟩, with s of order 2 acting by α, even if α=1. Require V to be irreducible under this whole formal semidirect product; it need not be irreducible as a Q-module and need not be central. Choose normalized maps c:Q×Q→V and u:Q→V with

    c(1,x)=c(x,1)=0,  u(1)=0,
    c(x,y)+c(xy,z)=ρ(x)c(y,z)+c(x,yz),
    T c(x,y)+u(xy)=u(x)+ρ(α(x))u(y)+c(α(x),α(y)),
    T u(x)+u(α(x))=0.

All equations hold for all indicated elements of Q. Define

    (v,x)(w,y)=(v+ρ(x)w+c(x,y),xy),
    τ(v,x)=(Tv+u(x),α(x)).

The first equation makes G=V×_c Q a group; the second makes τ a homomorphism, and the third makes τ²=1. The semilinearity equation and normalization ensure these formulas are compatible with V and the identity. Replace (Q,α) by (G,τ) and repeat, allowing any finite number of stages and odd primes. All maps and equations at each fixed finite stage are finite and explicitly enumerable. Stop at any stage and retain the resulting pair (G,τ); τ=1 is allowed at intermediate stages.

This recursion is exhaustive for pairs consisting of a finite odd-order group G and an involutory automorphism τ. Feit–Thompson makes G solvable. In the formal semidirect product G⋊⟨τ̂⟩, choose a minimal nontrivial normal subgroup contained in G; it is elementary abelian of odd prime order power and is irreducible under the quotient formal semidirect product. Repeat in the quotient and reverse the resulting τ-stable series. Choosing sections gives the displayed actions, cocycles and lift maps. This does **not** assume that an arbitrary central C_p series can be chosen τ-stable.

## Stabilizer geometry

For a resulting odd-order pair define

    C=C_G(τ),  K={g∈G:τ(g)=g⁻¹},  Λ=τ̂K⊂G⋊⟨τ̂⟩.

All involutions in the nonidentity coset τ̂G form the single G-conjugacy class Λ, by conjugacy of complements to the odd-order normal subgroup G. Consequently |K|=[G:C] and K⁻¹K=Λ² is G-invariant. Enumerate subgroups H≤G and retain those satisfying

    Core_G(H)=1,  |H|=|C|,
    H∩H^λ=C_H(λ)  for every λ∈Λ.                              (1)

The last condition is equivalent to H∩K⁻¹K={1}. Indeed D=H∩H^λ is λ-stable. If H∩K⁻¹K=1 and d∈D is λ-inverted, then λ and λd are involutions of τ̂G and hence both lie in Λ, so d∈Λ²∩H=1. For every x∈D the element x⁻¹x^λ is λ-inverted, so it is 1; thus D=C_H(λ). Conversely, if 1≠h∈H∩Λ², write h=λλ' with λ,λ'∈Λ. Then h^λ=h⁻¹∈H, so h∈H∩H^λ; (1) makes h λ-fixed, and odd order forces h=1, a contradiction.

The equality |H|=|C| makes |K|=[G:H]; the intersection condition and G-invariance of K⁻¹K give a transversal to H and all its conjugates. The full inverted set K contains 1, is inverse closed and is closed under x y x, so it defines a faithful left Bol folder. Require additionally

    ⟨K⟩=G,                                                     (2)
    |H∩N|<|C_N(τ)| for every 1≠N◁G, N≠G, τ(N)=N.              (3)

The reviewed τ-stable-normal criterion makes (3) equivalent to simplicity for a faithful generating odd-order folder. Finally require the [C0001 folder Moufang criterion](../foundations/C0001.md) to fail for at least one pair: for x,y∈K, the unique z∈K∩xyH must fail z∈Hxy. This finite test retains precisely the proper left Bol outputs; it can be evaluated from group data without presenting a loop table. Every subgroup, normal subgroup and displayed equality is finite and checkable.

## Exhaustiveness and isomorphism interface

Conditional on the cited inputs, every retained datum produces a finite simple proper left Bol loop of odd order. Conversely, for such a loop the cited FKP result gives odd canonical left multiplication group; C0001 gives a faithful generating folder, and simplicity and nonassociativity give trivial left nucleus, so C0007 supplies its unique inverting τ. The reviewed odd-folder results identify K as the full inverted set and establish (3). Feit–Thompson and the recursion supply the extension data, and (1) follows from its folder transversality. Thus the construction covers the stated branch without assuming an exact-factorization theorem.

Two retained data (G,τ,H) and (G',τ',H') define isomorphic loops exactly when a group isomorphism φ:G→G' satisfies

    φ(H)=H',  φτ=τ'φ.

Such a map carries the full inverted set K to K' and hence the faithful folders to one another. Conversely a loop isomorphism induces an isomorphism of canonical left multiplication groups carrying H and K to H' and K'; uniqueness of inversion on the generating set gives φτ=τ'φ. The chosen chief towers, module bases, sections, cocycles and lift maps are **not** required to match under φ. This finite isomorphism test removes their duplication.

This is a candidate extension-and-stabilizer parameterization, not a proof of a new explicit exact-factorization witness or a certified solution of the parent classification. It does not cover even-order proper Bol loops, or by itself supply the group and nonassociative Moufang portions of the broader program. The recursion, geometry equivalence and isomorphism interface have the scoped reviews below. The following exact-factorization specialization supplies an algebraic control.

## Exact-factorization control

Let X be an odd-order group with an exact factorization X=Y₀Y₁ and X′Y₀=X′Y₁=X. In the canonical construction of [C0002](../../claims/0002.md), put G={(u,v)∈X²:uv∈X′}, H=G∩(Y₀×Y₁), and τ(u,v)=(v,u). Then K={(x,x⁻¹):x∈X}. Since X/X′ has odd order, C_G(τ)=diag(X′), and the surjective map Y₀×Y₁→X/X′ gives |H|=|X′|=|C_G(τ)|.

Exact factorization implies Y₀∩xY₁x⁻¹=1 for every x∈X: write x=ab with a∈Y₀,b∈Y₁, and conjugate the intersection by a⁻¹. If (b,c)∈H∩K⁻¹K, write it as (xy,x⁻¹y⁻¹); then c=x⁻¹b⁻¹x, so b∈Y₀∩xY₁x⁻¹=1 and c=1. Hence the new stabilizer-intersection criterion holds for every λ∈Λ by its proved equivalence above. This checks the geometry directly without a loop table or a subgroup census; it does not separately prove the other admissibility conditions for an arbitrary factorization.

For the p=3,q=13 construction in [C0003, Theorem 1.1](../foundations/C0003.md), X=F₂₇⋊(C₁₃⋊⟨φ⟩), φ(v)=v³. Its trace construction gives |Y₀|=27, |Y₁|=39 and exact factorization. ## Proposed characteristic-section inversion obstructions

**Necessary conditions with scoped independent reviews below.** These are group-theoretic conditions in [0323:C0010](../../claims/0010.md). Let a finite group G be generated by a set K inverted by an automorphism τ. In particular they apply to the canonical section under the scoped [C0007](../foundations/C0007.md) inversion input at [the source revision](https://github.com/Mira-Cult/math-wip/commit/932ead5db5f39045e2085caa6b062f6b981b2813). For every τ-invariant quotient Q of G, τ acts by inversion on `Q_ab`, because the images of K generate that abelian group.

More generally, let `A◁Q` be cyclic and τ-invariant. Conjugation gives `ρ:Q→Aut(A)`, whose target is abelian. The restrictions of τ and conjugation by q to A commute, so `ρ(τ(q))=ρ(q)`. But τ induces inversion on the abelian quotient `Q/C_Q(A)`, by the preceding observation. Thus `ρ(q)=ρ(q)⁻¹` for every q: `Q/C_Q(A)` has exponent at most two. If Q has odd order, A is central in Q. No assertion is made for an arbitrary section without an induced τ-action.

As a useful special case, suppose a characteristic quotient of G is `Q=E⋊C`, where E is cyclic and characteristic in Q, C is nontrivial of odd order, and C acts faithfully on E. Any τ descends to Q and preserves E. The preceding conclusion makes `Q/C_Q(E)≅C` have exponent at most two, a contradiction. The quotient need only be τ-invariant; characteristicity is a convenient sufficient condition.

A second obstruction concerns odd-dimensional Singer actions. Let `Q=V⋊C_n`, where V is a nonzero elementary abelian p-group of odd dimension d, and `C_n` is a nontrivial odd cyclic group acting faithfully and irreducibly on V over `F_p`. Then no generating set of Q can be inverted by an automorphism. First p does not divide n: the p-part of the cyclic complement would be normal in it and have a nonzero invariant vector, hence act trivially by irreducibility and faithfulness. The nontrivial irreducible action gives `[V,C_n]=V`, so `Q_ab≅C_n`; it also makes `O_p(Q)=V`, since the complement quotient has order prime to p. Thus any inverting automorphism preserves V and acts by inversion on C_n. The faithful irreducible cyclic representation identifies V with `F_{p^d}` and a generator with multiplication by an element of order n. An F_p-linear conjugator inducing inversion would normalize that field and send the generator to its inverse, so `p^j≡−1 (mod n)` for some j. Since d is odd, the order of p modulo n divides d and is odd; for odd n>1 it cannot contain −1. This is a contradiction. The field-normalizer fact follows from the centralizer of the irreducible cyclic action being `F_{p^d}` and the Galois group of that finite field being generated by Frobenius.

For a concrete scoped consequence, consider the standard subgroup `E=3³:(13:3)` of `PSL4(3)`, with C13 irreducible on `3³`. One explicit embedding uses upper block translations in SL4(3) and the lower 3×3 block generated on F27 by multiplication by an element of order 13 and Frobenius x↦x³. Both linear generators have determinant one, and the odd-order subgroup intersects the order-two center trivially, so it embeds in PSL4(3). Any subgroup L≤E transitive in a degree-117 action has order divisible by `117=9·13`. Its intersection with the normal `3³` is nonzero and C13-invariant, hence is all of `3³`; its image in `13:3` is either `13` or `13:3`. Therefore L is `3³:13` or `3³:(13:3)`. The first is excluded by the odd-dimensional Singer obstruction. In the second, `O₃(L)=3³` is characteristic because `13:3` has no nontrivial normal 3-subgroup; the characteristic quotient `13:3` is excluded by the cyclic-normal obstruction. Consequently no such L can be the canonical left multiplication group. This applies to every transitive subgroup inside this **specified** E, not to every embedding, outer extension, or Li–Xia factorization row. It does not assert that a degree-117 group action constructs a loop.

These arguments are elementary, use no new computation, and do not classify classical full-multiplication socles or settle C0010. The finite-field normalizer and standard irreducible cyclic-module facts above are stated as mathematical inputs.

## Proposed exclusion of the PSL4 symplectic-factor row

**Conditional exclusion with scoped independent reviews below.** Let X be a finite simple nonassociative left Bol loop of odd order. Under C0001/C0008 and the odd-envelope inputs pinned in the preceding full-multiplication restriction, write `M=Mlt(X)`, `S=Soc(M)`, `L=LMlt(X)` and `I=M_1`. Assume that their actual factorization `M=LI` lies in Li–Xia, [arXiv:1408.0350v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.0350v3), Theorem 1.1(d), Table 1.1 row 2: `S=PSL4(q)`, `L∩S` lies in the displayed Singer parabolic `q³:((q³−1)/d).3`, and `I∩S` contains a normal `PSp4(q)`; here `d=gcd(4,q−1)`. Then this configuration is impossible. This is only row 2, not a classification of classical socles. The C0008 aggregate review qualification in the preceding section remains in force.

First determine the possible odd degree. The natural symplectic group preserves, up to scalar, a unique alternating form: its transvections force any invariant alternating form to be a scalar multiple of the defining one. Hence its normalizer in SL4 is the corresponding conformal symplectic group intersected with SL4. For odd q a similitude with multiplier μ has determinant μ², so μ=±1; both occur, the second via `diag(−I2,I2)`. The normalizer has order `2|Sp4(q)|`. Passing to PSL4 divides by the full scalar center of SL4, of order `d`; this full preimage description is legitimate because every projective normalizer lift normalizes the inverse image of PSp4, namely the product of Sp4 with the scalar center, and conjugation preserves its derived subgroup Sp4, since Sp4(q) is perfect for odd q, including q=3. Equivalently, the potential scalar discrepancy is a homomorphism from this perfect group to the scalar center and is trivial. Consequently the normalizer index in S is

`q²(q³−1)/2`,

and its quotient by PSp4 has order `4/d`. If `q≡1 mod4`, this index is even. If `q≡3 mod4`, the normalizer has index two over PSp4, and PSp4 itself has even index; odd degree forces `I∩S` to be the full normalizer and the degree is `q²(q³−1)/2`. For even q, the only multiplier with μ²=1 is 1, and the normalizer index is `q²(q³−1)`, also even. Here S is transitive because it is a nontrivial normal subgroup of primitive M, so the loop degree is exactly `[S:I∩S]`. Thus `q=p^f≡3 mod4`, with p odd and f odd.

Let `U≅F_q³` be the unipotent radical in the row-2 Singer parabolic containing `L∩S`. By Li–Xia Lemma 2.2 and Theorem 2.3, a Zsigmondy prime r for `p^(3f)−1` exists in this odd-p, exponent-at-least-three case. It has `ord_r(p)=3f`, hence `r>3f`, and divides `q³−1` but not `q−1`. It divides the odd loop degree and therefore |L|. Since the odd part of `Out(PSL4(q))` divides f (Li–Xia Table 2.1; the diagonal and graph parts are 2-groups here), r lies in `|L∩S|`. The p-part of |L∩S| is at least `p^(2f)/(f)_p`, while the p-part outside U in the indicated socle-intersection group divides 3. The strict inequality `p^(2f)>(3f)_p` follows from `p^(2f)≥9^f>3f≥(3f)_p`. Thus `(L∩S)∩U` is nontrivial. The r-element is in the Singer part and acts irreducibly on U as an `F_p`-space of dimension `3f`, so this intersection is all of U.

The quotient `(L∩S)/U` lies in a cyclic p′ Singer group extended by C3, and has a characteristic cyclic Sylow-r subgroup R. A normal p-subgroup centralizes R, while the centralizer of R in this Singer semilinear quotient lies in its p′ scalar subgroup; it is therefore trivial. Hence `U=O_p(L∩S)`. As `L∩S◁L`, the entire actual L normalizes U. The common fixed line of U on the natural four-space (or, for the dual row, its common fixed hyperplane) forces this normalizer to preserve the corresponding parabolic. An odd L has no graph-automorphism image. Its projective semilinear action on U has kernel U: for a Levi block and a field map this action is `v↦(A/a)v^{p^j}`; if it fixes all vectors, varying `F_q`-scalars makes the field map trivial on `F_q`, then `A/a=1`, so the element is projectively trivial modulo U.

Since R is characteristic in `(L∩S)/U`, the faithful action of L/U on U normalizes R. The centralizer of the irreducible r-action is `F_(p^(3f))^×`, and its linear normalizer is `ΓL1(p^(3f))`. Thus the entire actual L/U embeds in that semilinear group, whose Frobenius quotient has order 3f. Because `r>3f`, its cyclic Sylow-r subgroup is unique and characteristic in L/U. A normal p-subgroup centralizes it, hence lies in the p′ scalar kernel, so `O_p(L/U)=1` and `O_p(L)=U`. The canonical inversion of L consequently descends to L/U. The preceding characteristic-cyclic-normal obstruction forces R central in this odd quotient; Frobenius acts faithfully on R because `ord_r(p)=3f`, so L/U lies in the cyclic scalar subgroup.

The quotient L/U is a p′ cyclic group containing r, so Schur–Zassenhaus gives `L=U⋊C` with C cyclic and acting faithfully and irreducibly on U. The dimension `3f` is odd. The preceding odd-dimensional Singer inversion obstruction rules out such an L. This contradiction uses the original canonical inverting automorphism, not a projected loop or merely the factorization table. The argument cites Li–Xia's classification and Zsigmondy input rather than re-proving those sources. It leaves all other classical rows, other actions and the unrestricted classification open.

## Proposed exclusion of the linear Singer row

**Conditional exclusion with scoped independent reviews below.** Assume the scoped canonical inversion and odd-left-envelope inputs of the characteristic-section obstruction above, and the full-multiplication almost-simple conclusion C0008, whose aggregate review metadata remains unreviewed. In the odd-order case of the full-multiplication factorization, Li–Xia [LX16, Table 1.1 row 1](../foundations/references.md#lx16-solvable-factor) cannot occur. This is a necessary restriction on the full multiplication group, conditional on those inputs; it does not classify the remaining factorization rows.

Write q=p^f and S=PSL_n(q), n≥2. Let M be the full multiplication group, I its point stabilizer and L its odd canonical left multiplication group. Li–Xia's row asserts that L∩S is contained in the normalizer of a projective Singer torus T_S in S, of shape T_S:n. Write T_PGL for the full projective Singer torus of order (q^n−1)/(q−1); its intersection T_S has index gcd(n,q−1). The row also asserts that J=I∩S has a **normal** subgroup

 E=q^(n−1):SL_(n−1)(q).

Its unipotent translation subgroup U=q^(n−1) is O_p(E), including n=2 and the small SL2 cases. Hence U is characteristic in E and normal in J. Its normalizer in S is the corresponding point parabolic P, or dually the hyperplane parabolic. Thus J≤P and

 (q^n−1)/(q−1)=[S:P] divides [S:J] divides [M:I]=|X|.          (10)

The last divisibility is [M:I]=[M:SI][S:J]; it does not require I itself to equal a parabolic.

Suppose ℓ is a primitive prime divisor of p^(nf)−1, meaning ord_ℓ(p)=nf. Then ℓ is odd, ℓ≡1 mod nf, ℓ>nf and ℓ∤q−1. By (10) it divides |X|, hence |L|. The standard outer-automorphism bounds—diagonal part dividing n, field part dividing f and graph part of order at most two—give ℓ∤|M/S|. Thus ℓ divides |L∩S|. ## Right-translation sign and the odd alternating full group

**Elementary lemma and conditional assembly with three scoped independent reviews below.** This is a manuscript-local continuation of [C0010](../../claims/0010.md) and resolves only the odd-order portion of [0323/S1](../foundations/problem-contract.md#0323s1-permutation-rank-and-loop-conjugacy) under the inputs below.

**Sign lemma.** For every finite left Bol loop Q, the map `ε(y)=sgn(R_y)` is a loop homomorphism to `{±1}`. Consequently every right translation is even if Q has odd order, or if Q is simple and nonassociative.

Indeed the left Bol identity, regarded as an identity of permutations of y, says

    L_x R_(xz) = R_z L_x R_x.

Composition is read right-to-left: both sides send y to `x(y(xz))=(x(yx))z`. Taking signs and cancelling `sgn(L_x)` gives `ε(xz)=ε(x)ε(z)`, and `ε(1)=1`. If ε is nontrivial, a translation by an element a with `ε(a)=−1` bijectively interchanges its two fibers, so the order is even. Its kernel is a normal subloop. If Q is simple, a nontrivial ε has trivial kernel, hence both fibers have size one and Q has order two. This is impossible for a nonassociative Q. The lemma uses only the left Bol identity, not the odd-envelope or classification inputs.

**Conditional odd-order theorem.** Let Q be any finite simple nonassociative left Bol loop of odd order n. Under the canonical-folder, odd-envelope, C0008/C0011, Li–Xia classification and standard group inputs explicitly pinned in this manuscript, its full multiplication group is exactly `Alt(Q)=A_n`, in its natural action on Q.

# Odd prime-power regular tops: cyclic affine reduction

Mira Cult
This contributes to [0323-finite-simple-non-moufang-bol-loops:C0010](../../claims/0010.md). Let P be a finite group of odd prime-power order `n=p^e`, e≥1, let `S=A5^P⋊P` for the regular left-coordinate action, and put

`G={(x,y)∈S² : ab(top(x))+ab(top(y))=0 in P_ab}`,  `τ(x,y)=(y,x)`.

The group G is generated by the anti-diagonal of S; for nonabelian P it is the fiber over P_ab, not an inverse-top graph. Let `(G,H,K,τ)` be a faithful generated canonical folder of a finite simple nonassociative left Bol loop. Use the C0001 faithful-folder, exact normal-quotient and simple-Moufang primitivity inputs, scoped canonical inversion/class invariance, the [regular-top strip/normalizer argument](radical-free-a5-abelian-top.md), and the subgroup and construction inputs pinned in [the factor-permutation construction](../../advances-2026-11-16/manuscripts/radical-free-factor-permutation-construction.md). The A5 subgroup and full-strip data are those of [the A5 path source](radical-free-a5-path-classification.md). These pins are prerequisites, not reviews of this continuation.

The proposed reduction is: p is 3 or 5; after a global row orientation and actual τ-compatible coordinate gauges, H is determined by a codimension-e P-invariant subspace of Fp[P] and a cocycle of the top fiber `F=G/A5^(2P)` into its cyclic e-dimensional quotient M, subject to an explicit finite collision condition below. Conversely every such admissible datum constructs a faithful simple proper folder. The loop-isomorphism relation is the explicitly described actual normalizer orbit, not arbitrary GL(M)-equivalence. This is a finite structural parameterization for a specified P, not a closed classification of all its module/cocycle orbits and not an unrestricted classification of Bol loops.

## Actual full mark and stabilizer

Let N=A5^(2P). Each outside N-conjugacy class is indexed by one p0∈P and has size 60^n; its K representative has first S-top p0. Conjugation of the outside mark τK by `(a,b)∈F={(a,b)∈P²:ab(a)+ab(b)=0}` acts on that label as `x↦b^-1 x a`. This action is transitive because P_ab has odd order: for x,y choose a with `2 ab(a)=ab(y)−ab(x)` and take b=x a y^-1. Thus the G-conjugacy-invariant actual Λ=τK contains all outside classes, so K is the full anti-diagonal of S. This argument does not assume P abelian.

F is a finite p-group. If HN/N were proper in F, it would lie in a proper normal maximal subgroup; its inverse image would contain H, while the actual section covers the resulting quotient, contradicting the exact strict normal-quotient criterion. Thus H maps onto F. Write H0=H∩N. Then `|H0|=60^n/n`; integrality implies p∈{3,5}. For n=1 use the trivial-top paired A5 classification; the remainder assumes n>1.

The original H0 full-strip and proper-residual decomposition is as in the pinned regular-top source. H→F makes the coordinate projections conjugate along each row. If both rows have full projections, assign an involution to every independent full strip; both ends of every pair then have conjugate involutions, contradicting H0∩K²=1. If exactly one row has full projections, its strip partition is P-invariant. A singleton strip would, by transitivity, force every coordinate in that row to be singleton, putting the full G-normal row in H, contrary to corefreeness. Thus the number m of strips is ≤n/2. The residual on the proper row has order 60^(n−m)/n, divisible by both 3 and 5 because v3(n),v5(n)<n/2. Its projections are conjugate, but no proper A5 subgroup contains both primes. So all coordinates are proper.

The 3- and 5-parts of H0 and transitivity force one row to have C5/D10 projections and the other C3/S3/A4 projections. Orient the first row as C5/D10. The C3 case has insufficient 2-part. The S3 case has a Sylow-2 subgroup of dimension 2n in C2^n×C2^n; its intersection with the n-dimensional matched-support diagonal is nonzero, giving an involution in H0∩K². Thus the second projections are A4.

Let Q≤F3^P be the image of H0 in (A4/V4)^P. It has dimension n−v3(n), and its nonzero coordinate characters are permuted transitively by P. If two were proportional, their blocks would all have a common size at least p, so dim Q≤n/p≤n/3, contradicting v3(n)<n/2. The corresponding V4 coordinate modules are pairwise nonisomorphic simple F2[Q]-modules. Because `H0′≤C5^P×V4^P` and projects onto every V4 coordinate, `O2(H0′)=V4^P` by semisimplicity. This exhausts |H0|_2=2^(2n), so all first projections are C5. Goursat gives

`H0=W5×preimage(Q3)`,                                               (1)

with W5≤F5^P of codimension v5(n) and Q3≤F3^P of codimension v3(n). One codimension is e and the other zero. All factors in (1) are actual subgroups; no projection-intersection identity or residual independence is assumed.

Use even simultaneous coordinate conjugations to standardize each paired (C5,A4) containment, possible because D10=N_A5(C5) acts transitively on the five A4 point stabilizers. The first-side reflection signs of H define a cocycle of F into F2^P through the first P-coordinate. Its kernel in F is a p-group and has no nontrivial homomorphism into the 2-module, so the cocycle factors through P. The regular permutation-module cocycle is explicitly a coboundary: if r(g)(x) is its x-coordinate, `b(x)=r(x)(x)` gives `r(g)(x)=b(x)+b(g^-1x)`. Common coordinatewise even reflections in D10∩A4 remove all signs and commute with τ. The second-side base lies in A4^P, and the relevant W5 or Q3 is invariant under the corresponding regular shift.

## Cyclic module and exact collision condition

Put `M5=F5^P/W5`, `M3=F3^P/Q3`, and M=M5⊕M3. For p=5, M=M5 has dimension e and its F-action is through the first P-coordinate; for p=3, M=M3 has dimension e and its F-action is through the second P-coordinate. The other summand is zero. M is a cyclic quotient of Fp[P]. The H lifts form a cocycle `η:F→M` for this actual action.

Call `f=(a,b)∈F` **relevant** if b is conjugate in P to a^-1. For every nonidentity relevant f, the required condition is

`η(f)∉im(1−ρ(f))`.                                                  (2)

Indeed, membership is necessary for a collision: if the two wreath coordinates are conjugate inverses, their cycle products have the same conjugacy types; first-side products have orders 1 or5, second-side products 1,2 or3, so they are all identity. The corresponding base cycle sums vanish after applying the actual quotient map. Conversely, if f is relevant and η(f) lies in that image, use the independent C5-side or A4/V4-side H0 factor in (1) to make all relevant cycle sums zero, and the independent V4^P to kill the remaining A4 cycle products. The two wreath elements are conjugate inverses, yielding an actual H∩K² element. At top zero, the disjoint-order argument excludes nonidentity collisions. Thus (2), for all relevant f≠1, is exactly H∩K²=1; it is not required for irrelevant f.

## Converse and actual isomorphism interface

Fix P with p∈{3,5}, an actual P-invariant subspace U≤Fp[P] of codimension e, the canonical quotient M=Fp[P]/U, and a cocycle η:F→M satisfying (2). The quotient map is part of these actual coordinate data. On the active row take U as the kernel in (1), and take the other row's full C5^P or A4^P factor. Since n−e>0 and U is invariant under a transitive coordinate group, every coordinate projection of U is nonzero, hence onto Fp. Thus the H0 projections are exactly C5 on the first row and A4 on the second. Let `L=G∩[(C5^P⋊P)×(A4^P⋊P)]`. Then H0 is normal in L and `L/H0≅M⋊F`; take H to be the graph-preimage of η there. It has H0 of order 60^n/n, maps onto F, and satisfies `|H||K|=|G|`. By (2), H∩K²=1. Since Λ=τK is G-conjugacy invariant, so is K²; the collision and cardinality give a transversal to all H conjugates. K contains 1, is inverse-closed and sandwich-closed, and generates G because the anti-diagonal generates S′×S′ and the inverse graph in S_ab², with `S′=A5^P⋊P′`. Thus this is a canonical Bol folder once faithfulness is checked.

Here C_G(N)=1, and every nontrivial normal subgroup L of G meets N. The intersection with N is P-invariant and contains an entire socle row; H contains neither because its coordinate projections are proper, so H is core-free. If L misses the second row, then `[L,N2]=1` and its right S-projection is trivial. Projection of K to G/L is injective, so `|KL/L|=|K|=[G:H]>[G:HL]` by corefreeness; the symmetric case handles L missing the first row. If L contains N, then HL=G and the K-image generates the nontrivial quotient, giving strictness. These cases cover every nontrivial proper L, proving simplicity by C0001, including possible one-sided subgroups involving P′. G has nontrivial proper normal socle rows, so the loop is nonassociative. H is strictly inside the proper endpoint-normalizer group `G∩[(D10^P⋊P)×(A4^P⋊P)]`; the canonical action is imprimitive, so the pinned simple-Moufang obstruction makes the loop proper.

For isomorphism, the C5 and A4 coordinate orders distinguish the two minimal normal socle rows after a global orientation. A loop isomorphism conjugates the left translations, and hence intertwines their canonical inversion τ. It therefore uses the same coordinate permutation and local automorphism on corresponding socle factors. The coordinate permutation normalizes the regular P-action, hence belongs to Hol(P), generated by right translations and Aut(P). Once the paired (C5,A4) containments are standard, local maps lie in the common S5 normalizer C4. Each first-side H lift has C5 base; comparing its image at x and a^-1x forces the ratio of the two local C4 multipliers to lie in `C5∩C4=1`. Since every a∈P occurs as a first top, transitivity makes this multiplier uniform. An automorphism fixing N pointwise fixes G because C_G(N)=1. These arguments give the complete actual ambient normalizer between normalized data.

Explicitly, let f be a common coordinate permutation in Hol(P), let σ∈Aut(P) be the automorphism induced by its conjugation of left P, and put σ_F(a,b)=(σ(a),σ(b)). Let T_f be its permutation operator on Fp[P]. A uniform common C4 gives a scalar μ∈Fp*: all four scalars for p=5, and both signs for p=3. The exact equivalence is generated by

`U↦T_f U`, `η'(h)=μ·T_f[η(σ_F^-1(h))]` in `Fp[P]/T_f U`.       (3)

Every operation in (3) is induced by an actual automorphism of G preserving K and τ, and the preceding normalizer proof shows that there are no additional identifications. Quotient basis changes merely redescribe the same datum; arbitrary linear transformations or arbitrary cocycle coboundaries are not extra equivalences. For varying P choose one representative of each group-isomorphism type, or include common coordinate group isomorphisms. Thus (1)–(3) are an exact construction, exhaustion and isomorphism interface, with the admissible module and cocycle orbits still to be solved.

## Regularity for a class-two Camina top

Assume additionally that P has class two, `Z(P)=P′`, and `[x,P]=P′` for every x outside P′. Let F act on any set M of size |P|, and assume that no nonidentity relevant f fixes 0. The |P| anti-top elements `(x,x^-1)` send 0 to distinct points: the difference of any two has second entry conjugate to the inverse of its first. Their orbit is all M, so the action is transitive and its point stabilizer T has order |F|/|P|=|P′|. If (p,q)∈T with p∉P′, then pq∈P′ and the Camina hypothesis makes q conjugate to p^-1, contradiction. Thus p,q∈P′≤Z(P), so T≤Z(F). The central stabilizer T is normal and equals the kernel of the transitive action; the image of F therefore acts regularly on M.

This lemma uses only the stated class-two/Camina hypotheses and collision interface; it even holds for a permutation action on any set of size |P| with a distinguished point. Extraspecial p-groups, including the nonabelian order-27 exponent-9 modular group, satisfy the hypotheses. It does not assert that a suitable cyclic module or admissible cocycle exists, nor that arbitrary odd p-groups satisfy them.

There is an explicit central-quotient parameter. Write W=P′ additively. For central pairs, relevance means exactly membership in the anti-diagonal `{(w,−w):w∈W}`. The point stabilizer T has order |W| and meets this anti-diagonal trivially. Therefore the sum map T→W is an isomorphism and uniquely

`T=T_α={(αw,(1−α)w):w∈W}`,  `α∈End(W)`.                         (4)

The regular affine image is consequently `B_α=F/T_α`, of order |P|. Conversely an affine regular representation of B_α whose linear part factors through the active P-coordinate and whose e-dimensional module is cyclic gives an admissible datum after choosing a cyclic generator for the actual quotient map: no nonidentity relevant f lies in T_α, and every other element of B_α is fixed-point-free. Thus in this class the collision condition becomes a regular affine realization problem for the explicit central quotients (4), retaining the linear-action and actual coordinate-isomorphism requirements.

One further structural calculation is useful. Since P has class two, the commutators in F have the form `(w,w)`, and they generate diag(W). The map `(w1,w2)↦(1−α)w1−αw2` identifies W²/T_α with W: its kernel is T_α and it is onto, as seen on (w,−w). Here α and 1−α commute. Under this identification

`B_α′=(1−2α)W`.                                                   (5)

In particular the quotient is abelian exactly when 1−2α=0 on W. In that case the elementary regular-abelian cyclic-module theorem in the pinned [abelian-top source](radical-free-a5-abelian-top.md#the-regular-cyclic-module-algebra) forces `B_α≅1+tFp[t]/(t^(e+1))`. The nonabelian regular affine representations remain a substantive classification problem.

Its scope is the displayed regular odd-prime-power top model; even tops, mixed-prime or nonregular actions and arbitrary canonical groups remain outside it.

# Abelian regular-top A5 envelopes

**Independently reviewed classification under the pinned inputs.** A manuscript-local continuation of [classification frontier](../../claims/0010.md). Let A be a finite abelian group, n=|A|, and `S=A5^A⋊A` with A acting regularly on its coordinate set. Let `G={(x,y)∈S²:top(x)+top(y)=0}` and let τ swap the two S factors. Under the canonical-folder, full-strip, normal-quotient, exact-factorization and simple-Moufang primitivity interfaces pinned in [the factor-permutation construction](radical-free-factor-permutation-construction.md), every faithful generated canonical folder of a finite simple nonassociative left Bol loop with this exact G and τ has

`A ≅ U_a(F3) × U_b(F5)`,  `U_e(Fp)=1+t Fp[t]/(t^(e+1))`,  `a,b≥0`.

Conversely each such A occurs, and for each ordered pair (a,b) there is exactly one loop-isomorphism class in this model. Its order is `n·60^n`, where `n=3^a5^b`. The case e=0 means the trivial group. These are not assertions about arbitrary factor-permuting groups or arbitrary top actions.

The faithful-folder, normal-subloop and simple-Moufang primitivity interfaces are C0001; canonical inversion and actual marked-class invariance use the scoped C0007 input. ## Mark and full-strip reduction

Write N=A5^(2A), H0=H∩N. In every top coset of G, the outside involutions form a single N-class of size 60^n, consisting of the formal marks corresponding to `(x,x^-1)` for x∈S with that top. The actual mark K is a union of such classes, indexed by D⊆A with 0∈D. K generates G, so D generates A; sandwich closure gives 2d+e∈D for all d,e∈D. Since D is finite, it is invariant under translations by the group generated by 2D, which is 2A. The set D need not yet equal A.

Simplicity and the exact normal-quotient criterion give HN=G; otherwise HN is a proper normal subgroup containing H, while the projected K covers its quotient and violates strictness. For the trivial top this conclusion is automatic. Hence `|H0|=60^n/|D|`. Put d_p=v_p(|D|). As |D|≤n, d_p≤log_p(n)<n/2 for p=3,5 when n>1. The coordinate projections of H0 are conjugate along each of the two regular A-orbits.

If both orbits have full projections, the original full-strip splitting supplies independent strips covering every coordinate. Choose a nonidentity involution in each strip: every τ-pair then has conjugate involutions, producing `H0∩K²≠1`. If only one orbit is full, the transitive regular action preserves its full-strip partition. If one strip were a singleton, transitivity would make every coordinate in that row a singleton strip; H would then contain the entire normal socle row, violating core-freeness. Thus every strip has at least two coordinates and there are m≤n/2 strips. The residual on the other orbit has order `60^(n−m)/|D|`, divisible by both 3 and 5 because d3,d5<n/2 for n>1. All its proper coordinate projections are conjugate under H; one proper A5 subgroup would therefore contain both primes, impossible. The n=1 case is covered by the two-full-orbit collision or directly by the same corefreeness observation. Thus all coordinate projections are proper.

No proper A5 subgroup contains both 3 and 5. Since both divide |H0| for n>1, up to swapping the two S factors the first orbit has projections C5 or D10, and the second has projections C3, S3 or A4. If the second projection is C3, the available 2-part is at most 2^n, less than the required 2^(2n−d2) because d2≤log2(n)<n for n>1. If it is S3, a Sylow-2 subgroup embeds in C2^n×C2^n with dimension 2n−d2>n and hence contains a nontrivial matched-support involution, contradicting the actual marked square. Thus the second projections are A4. For n=1 the same conclusion follows directly from |H0|=60 and the proper-subgroup order bound.

Use simultaneous even coordinate conjugations to put every paired (C5,A4) containment in a common standard position; this is possible because the A5 normalizer D10 of a C5 acts transitively on the five A4 point stabilizers. Such paired even conjugations normalize the regular-wreath model and commute with τ. Write Q≤F3^A for the image of H0 under coordinate A4/V4 maps. Its dimension is n−d3, since the first-side D10 groups are 3-free and all omitted kernel factors are 2- or 5-groups. Every coordinate functional λ_x on Q is nonzero, and the top A action permutes their projective proportionality classes transitively. If any two were proportional, all blocks would have the same size at least two, giving dim Q≤n/2, contradicting d3<n/2.

Let E=O2(H0′). Since `H0′≤C5^A×V4^A` and every second-side projection is A4, E projects onto every V4 coordinate. The V4_x are pairwise nonisomorphic simple F2[Q]-modules: their kernels are the distinct projective characters λ_x. By semisimplicity, E is their full direct sum V4^A. This supplies a subgroup of order 2^(2n), while |H0| has 2-part 2^(2n−d2). Hence d2=0 and E is a Sylow-2 subgroup of H0, and the first-side projections cannot contain D10 reflections and are C5. Goursat now gives

`H0 = W5 × preimage(Q3)`,  `W5≤F5^A`, `Q3≤F3^A`,

where W5 has codimension d5, Q3=Q has codimension d3, and the second factor is the preimage of Q3 under A4^A→F3^A. No equality between a projection/intersection and a product of residual projections has been used.

Since |D| is odd and D is a union of 2A cosets, |2A| divides |D| and is odd. Thus the Sylow-2 subgroup A2 is elementary abelian and `D=E_marks×A_odd` for a subset E_marks⊆A2 containing 0 and spanning A2. The equality of the first-side H0 projection with C5 makes the D10 reflection sign of every H lift a well-defined cocycle `r:A→F2^A` for the regular translation module. This cocycle is a coboundary for every finite A: with `b(x)=r(x)(x)` in multiplicative coordinate notation, the cocycle identity gives `r(g)(x)=b(x)+b(g^(-1)x)`. Simultaneous coordinatewise conjugation by the corresponding even reflections in `D10∩A4=C2` removes all signs while commuting with τ. The second-side base normalizes each standard A4 and hence lies in A4^A. Now W5 and Q3 are translation-invariant under the forward and backward actions, respectively, and the H lifts define a cocycle η on `M=M5⊕M3`, where `M5=F5^A/W5`, `M3=F3^A/Q3`. Since |D| divides 60^n and is odd, it has only 3- and 5-prime divisors, so the underlying set of M has odd order `|M|=|D|`.

The affine action of A2 on M has a common fixed point: average an orbit over A2 in each field, since its order is invertible in F3 and F5. If A2 were nontrivial, choose 0≠c∈E_marks, so c∈D. The fixed point says η(c) lies in (1−ρ(c))M. Using W5 and Q3 representatives, multiply an actual H lift over c by H0 to make the first C5 and second A4/V4 sums zero on every translation-by-c cycle. The literal independent V4^A in H0 clears each remaining second-side cycle product. Both S elements are now conjugate by base elements to the pure translations c and −c. Thus they are conjugate inverses by a base element z, whose top is zero. The exact anti-diagonal square formula then puts this nonidentity H element in K_0 K_c: if its first coordinate is v, write it as (z,z^-1)(z^-1v,(z^-1v)^-1), with the factors' tops 0 and c. Both are actually selected. This contradicts H∩K_D²=1, using no hypothetical full section.

Therefore A2=1 and A is odd. Multiplication by 2 is now onto A, so the established 2A-translation invariance gives D=A. Consequently `d3=v3(n)=a`, `d5=v5(n)=b`, and n=3^a5^b. All subsequent collision and affine arguments use this full actual mark, now proved rather than assumed.

## Actual collision criterion and primary affine actions

Set `M5=F5^A/W5`, `M3=F3^A/Q3`, and M=M5⊕M3. The normalized H projects to a graph over A in the affine semidirect product: its cocycle η=(η5,η3) has linear actions given by forward translation on M5 and backward translation on M3. Its order parameters satisfy `|M5|=5^b`, `|M3|=3^a`, and `|M|=|A|`.

For nonzero c∈A, an actual element of H over c lies in K² exactly when `η(c)∈(1−ρ(c))M`. To see sufficiency, use W5 and Q3 representatives to make the first C5 and second A4/V4 sums zero on each orbit of translation by c. The independent V4^A in H0 then removes each second-side A4 cycle product. Both wreath elements have identity cycle products, so they are conjugate inverses in S. Conversely, any such conjugacy forces the first C5 and second A4 cycle products to have matching orders. Since their possible orders are respectively {1,5} and {1,2,3}, both must be identity; their orbit sums therefore vanish, which is exactly the stated image condition. At top zero the same order argument excludes nonidentity collisions. Thus `H∩K²=1` exactly when the affine action `c:z↦ρ(c)z+η(c)` is free. Its acting group and underlying set have equal size, so this action is regular.

Write A=A3×A5 for its primary factors. A3 acting affinely on M5 has a common fixed point by averaging, since |A3| is invertible in F5; its action on M3 is therefore free, hence regular because the orders agree. Similarly A5 acts regularly on M5. The induced A5 action on M3 centralizes the regular abelian A3 permutation group, whose centralizer is A3 itself. Coprime orders make that induced action trivial; likewise A3 acts trivially on M5. These are statements about the full affine actions, not merely their linear parts. Thus η splits into regular affine A3- and A5-actions on cyclic group-algebra modules M3 and M5.

## The regular cyclic-module algebra

We give the algebra step to avoid importing an abstract affine-classification claim. Let a finite abelian group B act regularly and affinely on an Fp-space J. For each x∈J let its unique group element be `g_x:z↦L_xz+x`. Commutativity gives `(L_x−1)y=(L_y−1)x`, so `x*y=(L_x−1)y` is commutative and bilinear. From `g_xg_y=g_(x+y+x*y)` follows `L_(x*y)−1=(L_x−1)(L_y−1)`, which is associativity of *. The unitization R=Fp⊕J is a finite-dimensional commutative algebra in which each 1−z, z∈J, is a unit: its multiplication map has diagonal blocks 1 and L_(−z), both invertible. The descending powers of J stabilize at some I=JI. Write a finite Fp-basis e of I as e=Be with entries of B in J. The adjugate identity gives det(1−B)e=0; this determinant lies in 1+J and is a unit. Hence I=0. Thus J is nilpotent, and B is its principal-unit group 1+J.

If J is cyclic as Fp[B]-module, a generator t satisfies `J=Fp·t+J²`, hence successive powers of t span J. Nilpotence and the unit factorization of any polynomial with nonzero constant show that the first dim(J) positive powers are independent. Therefore

`J≅t Fp[t]/(t^(e+1))`,  `B≅U_e(Fp)`,  `e=dim_Fp J`.

The two modules are cyclic because they are quotients of the regular group algebras Fp[A], and after primary splitting the opposite primary factor acts trivially. This proves the asserted necessary group types. Conversely the displayed principal-unit action is regular and cyclic.

## Coordinate-realizable isomorphism and existence

It remains to normalize actual quotient maps and cocycles using transformations of the specified folder. Omit a prime with zero-dimensional module. For each remaining p let `J_p=tFp[t]/(t^(e_p+1))`, and let `φ_p:A_p→1+J_p` be the group isomorphism obtained from its regular affine action and the preceding algebra identification. Reindexing both socle rows by this product group isomorphism is an actual isomorphism of the regular-top models and commutes with τ.

The actual quotient map from the regular coordinate module to J_p sends δ_0 to a cyclic generator `t u_p(t)`, where u_p is a unit. With x=(x3,x5) define the standard maps

 π5(δ_x)=φ5(x5)t,       π3(δ_x)=φ3(x3)^(-1)t.                    (1)

The inverse in π3 records the backward shift: translating its coordinate by c3^-1 multiplies the quotient by φ3(c3). Thus both induced affine linear actions on J_p are multiplication by φ_p(c_p). Equivariance shows that the actual quotient map equals u_p times (1), so its kernel is exactly the standard kernel. This is an identification of the original coordinate kernels, not an arbitrary change to them.

In the standard quotient coordinates the actual cocycle is

 η_p(c)=u_p^-1(φ_p(c_p)−1).

Write u_p^-1=α_p+r_p with `α_p=u_p(0)^-1∈Fp*` constant and r_p∈J_p. Its difference from α_p(φ_p(c_p)−1) is the coboundary (ρ_p(c)−1)r_p. All simultaneous coboundaries can be removed by actual common coordinate translations: translation by b in A acts on the two cocycles with coboundary parameters `(η5(b),η3(−b))`, because the two shifts are opposite. After the primary split, each primary orbit map is bijective and cross-primary actions are trivial; b5 and b3 may therefore be chosen independently to give any pair of parameters. These coordinate permutations preserve the standard kernels, normalize G and commute with τ.

Only the two nonzero constant coefficients α_p remain. For a constant c_p∈Fp*, substitution `R_(c_p):t↦c_p t` induces a group automorphism f_p of 1+J_p and hence a common coordinate reindexing of A. It preserves the standard kernels and induces `c_p^-1 R_(c_p)` on the quotient, as follows directly from (1), for either orientation. The new cocycle is

 c_p^-1 R_(c_p)[α_p(φ_p(f_p^-1(c))−1)]
   =(α_p/c_p)(φ_p(c)−1).

Taking c_p=α_p independently for the two primary factors gives the standard cocycle. This uses constant substitutions only, after removing every nonconstant term by a realized coboundary. Every folder has thus been normalized through genuine common coordinate permutations and the earlier even paired gauges; no arbitrary GL equivalence is used. Consequently there is at most one loop-isomorphism class for each (a,b).

For the converse choose `A=U_a(F3)×U_b(F5)` and the standard quotient maps above. The maps in (1) are surjective: their images span t,t²,…,t^e because 1+J_p ranges over all principal units; for e=0 use the zero map. Let H0 be the product of their kernels, with the entire V4^A included on the A4 side. These kernels are invariant under the two coordinate actions. Define H as the preimage of the graph of the standard cocycle: its two components are `φ5(c5)−1` and `φ3(c3)−1`; the inverse in π3 together with the backward coordinate shift makes the latter action multiplication by φ3(c3). Multiplication by φ_p(c_p) followed by translation φ_p(c_p)−1 is the regular multiplication action on the principal units after identifying z with 1+z. The product affine action is therefore regular, so the established collision criterion gives H∩K²=1. Its order is

`|H|=|A|·5^(n−b)·4^n·3^(n−a)=60^n`,

and `|H||K|=|G|`. The full anti-diagonal K contains 1, is inverse- and sandwich-closed, and its outside marked set is G-conjugacy invariant; hence K² is G-conjugacy invariant. The collision and order conditions make K a transversal to H and all conjugates. K generates G because S′=A5^A. Every nontrivial normal subgroup of G meets N, as C_G(N)=1; its N-intersection is invariant under the regular top and contains one entire socle side. Since every H0 coordinate projection is proper, H is core-free. If a normal subgroup meets N in exactly one socle side, it centralizes the other side and equals the first, because the regular top action is faithful and the corresponding centralizer is that side. A normal subgroup disjoint from N is trivial; all others are preimages of subgroups of A above N. In a quotient by one side, K projects onto all S and H has nontrivial image, containing W5 or V4^A; here |W5|=5^(n−b)>1. Thus |πK|=|S|>[S:πH]. In a proper quotient above N, H and K both project onto the nontrivial top quotient, so |πK|>1=[Gbar:Hbar]. These cover all proper nontrivial normal quotients and prove simplicity by C0001.

The stabilizer is nontrivial, so the loop is nonassociative. Finally H lies properly in the proper subgroup `G∩[(D10^A⋊A)×(A4^A⋊A)]`, so the faithful canonical action is imprimitive. The cited simple-nonassociative-Moufang primitivity obstruction proves properness. This proves existence in every stated case; the distinct values n=3^a5^b and strictly increasing orders n·60^n distinguish the resulting classes.

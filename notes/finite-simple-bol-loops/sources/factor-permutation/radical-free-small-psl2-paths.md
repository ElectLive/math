**Mira Cult**

**Independently reviewed continuation of [0323:C0010](../../claims/0010.md).** In the faithful canonical setting G=PSL₂(p)^k×PSL₂(p)^k with inversion swapping the two blocks, every finite simple nonassociative Bol folder is a single k-edge path for p=7,11,19,29. The exhaustive endpoint list and number of loop-isomorphism classes for each k≥1 are:

| --- | --- | --- |
| 7 | (7:3,D₈), (C₇,S₄) | 2 |

All these paths exist, are proper and non-Bruck under the reviewed construction, and retain its exact isomorphism criterion. ## p=7

Let T=PSL2(7), |T|=168=2³·3·7. Dickson's classification is Guralnick–Zieve, *Polynomials with PSL(2) monodromy*, Annals of Mathematics 172 (2010), Appendix Theorem A.1 and Lemma A.2, printed p.1354, https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v172-n2-p11-p.pdf ; the classification proof has not been independently reverified here. Every proper subgroup containing 7 lies in a Borel 7:3, and has odd order. Every proper subgroup with full 2-part 8 lies in an S4: either it is a Sylow-2 D8, or its maximal overgroup is S4. All involutions form one T-class by Lemma A.3, printed p.1355. A 7-Borel, a Sylow 7-group and a Sylow 2-group are unique up to T-conjugacy; Lemma A.2 includes the q=7 torus-normalizer exception where D8 lies in S4.

Use the strip-forest reduction, with c components, l proper-coordinate leaves, m full strips and leaf projection R. The forest equation is |R|=168^(l−c). Any proper leaf has 7-valuation at most 1 and 2-valuation at most 3, and the sets with nonzero 7- and 2-valuation are disjoint. Thus at least l−c leaves of each type are required, l≤2c, and the tree lower bound l≥2c gives l=2c. Every component is a path with two leaves; exactly c leaves are 7-type and c full-2-type. Embed the former in fixed Borels 7:3 and the latter in fixed S4 subgroups, enlarging the chosen right ambient S4 if needed. The Sylow 7-subgroup of R has order 7^c and hence equals the product of all c ambient normal C7 factors. Likewise a Sylow 2-subgroup of R has order 8^c and projects isomorphically onto a full product of chosen Sylow-2 D8 subgroups in the c right factors. These product subgroups lie in R.

Each path has one leaf of each kind. Otherwise some path has two full-2 leaves. Choose nonidentity involutions independently from the D8 factors on that path and identity on all other leaves, and choose involutions on all strips of this path and identity on other strips. Strip twisting preserves the unique involution class. Every paired coordinate is then conjugate inverse, so the nonidentity element of H lies in K², contradicting folder transversality. Index paths so left leaves are 7-type and right leaves are full-2-type.

The full normal C7^c lies in R, so Rbar=R/C7^c ≤ C3^c×S4^c and |Rbar|=24^c. The ambient index is 3^c. Its normal 2-subgroup V4^c lies in Rbar: for a normal 2-subgroup N, |NRbar:Rbar| is a power of 2 dividing the odd index. Divide by V4^c to obtain L≤C3^c×S3^c of order 6^c. Its Sylow 2-subgroup has order 2^c and projects isomorphically onto the full right quotient C2^c. Conjugate inside the ambient product to take this Sylow subgroup to the standard product E=C2^c; this is a harmless change of coordinate identifications.

Let W=L∩(C3^c×C3^c), where the right C3^c consists of the normal A3 factors in the S3's. Then W has F3-dimension c and is E-invariant. E acts trivially on the left and by independent inversion in each right coordinate. If a vector of W has a nonzero ith right coordinate, subtract its image under the ith involution; the result is a nonzero scalar multiple of that right coordinate vector, so the whole ith right axis belongs to W. Subtracting all right components gives W=B⊕⊕_{i∈S}F3_i for some B≤F3^c in the left coordinates and S⊆{1,...,c}; dim B=c−|S|. We do NOT claim B is supported on S^c: graph correlations can remain.

Suppose c>1. If S is proper choose i∉S. The right projection of W has no 3-component at i, and the image of L there has order at most 2; hence the right projection of R has order at most 8, while the left projection has order at most 21. Therefore the leaf projection on component i has order at most 168, contradicting the strict forest quotient bound |π_i R|>|T|. If S is all indices, B=0 and W is the entire right C3^c. Since L contains E, it equals the full right S3^c and has no left C3 component; lifting V4^c and C7^c gives R=C7^c×S4^c. Each component then has leaf projection of order 168, again violating strictness. Thus c=1.

For c=1, either S is empty and B=C3, giving R=(7:3)×D8, or S contains its sole index and B=0, giving R=C7×S4. This follows from the quotient description and equality of orders, not from any unsupported elimination of graph correlations. Both endpoint pairs have coprime orders with product |T|, hence are exact factorizations. The reviewed path construction supplies existence for every k. The forest is one path; simultaneous automorphisms on paired coordinates and reparameterization of strips gauge all its twists away. For (7:3,D8), Dickson's lemma gives a single D8 class and D8 acts regularly on the 8 projective points, hence all pairs are simultaneously conjugate. For (C7,S4), the S4 classes fuse under PGL2(7), verified by the finite certificate below; a fixed S4 acts transitively on the 8 Sylow-7 subgroups, because its stabilizer of any projective point is contained in a Borel and has order dividing gcd(24,21)=3, so all point orbits have size at least 8. Thus all pairs of this type are Aut(T)-conjugate. Their distinct endpoint isomorphism types are distinguished by the reviewed path isomorphism criterion.

## p=11

The prime-11 valuation and full Sylow-2 rank force the forest into paths and produce c leaves of 11-type and c leaves of full-2 type. An 11-leaf lies in its Borel B=11:5. By Dickson's list, a full-2 leaf lies in D12 or A5; the possible A4 subgroup lies in A5. A Sylow 2-subgroup of R has order 4^c, equal to the full Sylow 2-order of the ambient product, and is therefore a product of independent right-leaf Sylow subgroups. Thus any path with two full-2 ends collides: choose independent involutions on both endpoints and every full strip, and identity on all other leaves and strips, to obtain a nonidentity element of H∩Λ². Equal total counts therefore force one 11 end and one full-2 end on every path.

Let R be the subdirect stabilizer on the 2c leaf coordinates after the full strips. After orienting paths, put each left leaf in B and each right leaf in either A5 or D12. Write a+d=c for their numbers. The forest equation gives |R|=660^c; the ambient product B^c×A5^a×D12^d has index 5^a over R. The normal subgroup C11^c of the ambient lies inside R since its order is coprime to this index. Consequently Rbar=R/C11^c has order 60^c.

Every normal D12 direct factor also lies inside R, since [D12:D12∩R] divides both |D12| and 5^a. If d>0, project R to the two leaf coordinates of a path with D12 end. It contains D12 and has order at most |B||D12|=660. The reviewed strict component inequality requires that projection to have order >660 whenever c>1. Thus d>0 implies c=1.

Now suppose d=0. Modulo C11^c the ambient is C5^c×A5^c and |Rbar|=60^c. Since its Sylow 2-subgroup has order 4^c, every A5 projection has full 2-part; since its 3-part has order 3^c, every A5 projection has a factor 3. The subgroup list then makes every right projection A4 or A5. Let s be the number of full A5 projections and d=c−s. The projection to A5^s is subdirect, and its 2-part has order 4^s because the other d projections contribute at most 4^d. Scott's lemma expresses a subdirect subgroup of A5^s as a product of diagonal simple strips. Each strip contributes exactly 4 to the 2-part, so there are s singleton strips: the projection is all A5^s. Goursat's lemma then splits this factor off from the remaining solvable C5^c×A4^d, because the latter has no A5 composition factor. Thus Rbar=A5^s×Y with Y≤C5^c×A4^d.

The 2- and 3-parts force Y onto A4^d (the ambient 2·3-part is already 12^d). Goursat again splits Y=W×A4^d: any common quotient with C5^c is an elementary 5-group, and A4^d has no nontrivial such quotient. Counting yields |W|=5^d. If d>0, an A4-ended component projects to a subgroup of B×A4, order at most 55·12=660, again violating strictness when c>1. If d=0, W=1, and each component projects to C11×A5, order 660; the same argument forces c=1. Therefore every simple folder in this bounded paired setting has one path component.

For c=1, the right projection possibilities and total order force exactly three endpoint types (up to swapping): (B55,D12), (B55,A4), (C11,A5). In each case the product of endpoint orders is 660 and their intersection is trivial, hence it is an exact factorization of T. The previously reviewed connected-path construction supplies a simple proper non-Bruck folder for each k, and its within-family isomorphism theorem reduces the count to Aut(T)-orbits of endpoint pairs.

The asserted GAP 4.15.1 certificate linked above gives subgroup conjugacy-class counts 1,2,1,2 for orders 11,12,55,60; the order-12 classes are A4,D12 and Aut(T) interchanges the two A5 classes. Normalizer sizes are 55 for C11 and 12 for each order-12 group; B55 intersects these normalizers trivially, so acts transitively on each 55-element subgroup class. Each A5 class has 11 members and B55 intersects its normalizer in order 5, so B55 acts transitively on each; the outer automorphism interchanges them after aligning B. Since N_T(C11)=B55, it likewise gives a single Aut(T)-orbit on pairs (C11,A5). Thus the three types are distinct isomorphism classes for every k.

## p=19

Use the strip-forest notation of the reviewed source: c connected components, ℓ leaf coordinates, and R the projection of H onto all leaves. Dickson gives |T|=3420=2²·3²·5·19. Every proper 19-bearing subgroup lies in a Borel B of order 171, and every 5-bearing proper subgroup lies in a nonsplit normalizer D of order 20 or an A5 subgroup of order 60. Their supports are disjoint. The valuation argument with primes 19 and 5, together with |R|=|T|^{ℓ−c} and ℓ≥2c, forces ℓ=2c, all components paths, exactly c leaves of each support, and full 19- and 5-parts on those leaves. Thus R is contained in B^c×∏X_i with X_i∈{D,A5}.

Its index in this ambient product is 3^a, where a is the number of A5 choices. Consequently a Sylow 2-subgroup of R is a Sylow 2-subgroup of the ambient product: as each B is odd, it is the direct product of c Klein four groups, one on each 5-bearing leaf. If a path had two 5-bearing leaves, choose matching nonidentity involutions in those two Sylow factors, set other leaves to 1, and choose conjugate involutions on the internal full strips. PSL₂(19) has one involution class, so this gives a nonidentity member of H∩Λ². Equal support counts then imply every path has one 19-bearing and one 5-bearing end.

If some X_i=D and c>1, project H onto both halves of all edges in that component. Its projection has order at most |T|^{e_i−1}|B||D|=|T|^{e_i}, where e_i is its number of edges. The normal-quotient criterion of the reviewed strip-forest source requires this projection to have strictly greater order than |T|^{e_i}; contradiction. For c=1 and X_i=D, |R|=|B||D| makes R=B×D, giving the standard path.

It remains to exclude all X_i=A5. The index of R in B^c×A5^c is 3^c. Projection S of R to A5^c also has 3-power index. Its projection to each A5 is all of A5, because A5 has no proper subgroup of index a power of 3 (its maximal subgroup indices are 5,6,10). A subdirect subgroup of A5^c is a product of diagonal strips; any nontrivial strip makes its index divisible by |A5|=60, impossible for a 3-power. Therefore S=A5^c. Goursat applied to R≤B^c×A5^c now gives R=R_left×A5^c: every quotient of B^c is solvable, whereas every quotient of A5^c is a direct product of copies of A5. Since |R|=|T|^c, |R_left|=(19·3)^c, and Cauchy gives a nonidentity x∈R_left of order 3.

Each nonidentity coordinate of x is an order-three element of PSL₂(19); these lie in a single T-conjugacy class, invariant under inversion. On each path whose B-end coordinate is nonidentity, choose an order-three element independently at the A5 end and choose suitable conjugate order-three elements in its full internal strips; set all other paths to identity. The direct-product decomposition of R and the full strip factors allow these choices simultaneously. The resulting element is nonidentity and lies in H∩Λ², a contradiction. This is an original-folder collision, not a quotient-loop assertion.

The surviving B/D pair is exact: B is odd, |B||D|=|T| and B∩D=1. The reviewed path construction gives existence and properness, and its path-isomorphism criterion plus transitivity of D on projective points gives uniqueness. The subgroup classification and conjugacy facts are source dependencies, not reproved here; this deduction has the independent exact-source reviews below.

## p=29

Let T=PSL2(29), with |T|=12180=2²·3·5·7·29. Its Sylow 2-subgroups are V4 and it has one involution class. These standard facts and the subgroup classification are in Guralnick–Zieve, *Polynomials with PSL(2) monodromy*, Annals of Mathematics 172 (2010), Appendix Theorem A.1 and Lemmas A.2–A.3, printed pp.1354–1355, https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v172-n2-p11-p.pdf ; the classification proof is not reverified here. A proper subgroup containing 29 lies in a Borel 29:14 of order 406, whose Sylow 2-subgroup is C2 and which has no 5-divisor. A proper subgroup containing 5 lies in A5 or the nonsplit normalizer D30; the remaining Dickson types have no 5-divisor. Every such subgroup has 2-rank at most 2, and in D30 the Sylow 2-subgroup is C2.

Apply the forest reduction. Let c be the number of components, l their total leaves, m their full strips, and R the projection on leaf coordinates. Since |R|=|T|^(l−c), disjoint 29- and 5-valuations force at least l−c leaves of each type and hence l≤2c; a forest component has at least two leaves, so l=2c. All components are paths and there are exactly c 29-leaves and c 5-leaves. Embed the former in B=29:14 and the latter in an A5 or D30 as available. A Sylow 2-subgroup P of R has order 4^c and, since the ambient Sylow 2-subgroups are elementary abelian, is a 2c-dimensional F2 space.

Use a support-matching argument. For each path, require that its two leaf projections of x∈P are simultaneously trivial or simultaneously nontrivial. A one-dimensional leaf coordinate has nonzero-indicator z, and a two-dimensional leaf coordinate (y,z) has nonzero-indicator y²+yz+z². Thus matching on a path is one polynomial equation of degree at most 2, and of degree at most 1 when both its leaf ambient 2-ranks are at most 1. If one path has two 29-leaves, another has two 5-leaves; the two equations then have total degree at most 1+2, and all others degree at most 2. The total degree is at most 2c−1. Likewise, once every path is mixed, any path whose 5-leaf lies in D30 has degree at most 1 and yields the same strict bound. In either situation the polynomial system has a nonzero solution by the following direct parity proof: modulo 2, its number of common zeros is the sum over F2^(2c) of ∏(1+f_i); every monomial of this product misses a variable, so its sum is zero. The zero solution exists, hence the number of solutions is even and another solution exists.

Any such nonzero x gives a contradiction to H∩K²=1. For each active path, both leaf values are involutions and the full strips can independently be set to involutions; every paired coordinate then consists of conjugate inverses since T has one involution class and automorphisms preserve it. Set all other strips to identity. Hence every path is mixed, and no 5-leaf lies in D30: all right ambient factors may be chosen A5.

Consequently R≤B^c×A5^c and the index is 2^c, because |B|·60=2|T|. The normal odd subgroup C29^c of the ambient product lies in R: for a normal odd-order N, |NR:R| divides both |N| and the 2-power index. Quotient by C29^c, obtaining a subgroup of C14^c×A5^c of the same index, and similarly it contains the normal C7^c. Quotient by C7^c to obtain L≤C2^c×A5^c of order 60^c and index 2^c.

The projection J of L onto A5^c has 2-power index. Each coordinate projection of J is all A5, since A5 has no proper subgroup of 2-power index: a power of two dividing |A₅|=60 is at most four, and the resulting nontrivial coset action would embed the simple group A₅ into S₄, impossible. By Goursat/Scott, a subdirect subgroup J of A5^c is a product of full diagonal strips; a nontrivial diagonal identification has index divisible by 60, contrary to its 2-power index. Hence J=A5^c. Goursat between C2^c and A5^c gives no nontrivial common quotient (the latter is perfect), so L is a product of its projection onto C2^c with A5^c. Since |L|=60^c, the left factor is trivial. Pulling back the two full normal odd groups gives R=(29:7)^c×A5^c as a subgroup on the leaves.

If c>1, retaining one component gives leaf projection order |29:7|·|A5|=203·60=|T|, contradicting the strict forest normal-quotient criterion. Thus c=1 and its path has the stated endpoints. Tree gauge using the same automorphism on the two coordinates of each paired edge and independent strip parameter changes removes all twists. The endpoint orders are coprime and multiply to |T|, so their intersection is trivial and the reviewed construction gives existence for each k.

For uniqueness, all A5 subgroups are fused by Aut(T): the asserted GAP 4.15.1 certificate linked above checks orders 12180 and 24360, exactly two maximal T-classes of A₅, and their fusion under PGL₂(29). GAP Reference Manual 39.19-5 defines MaximalSubgroupClassReps as representatives of maximal-subgroup conjugacy classes; the calculation uses GAP group methods and is not an independent proof of the subgroup algorithm. For a fixed A5, its action on the 30 projective points has stabilizer dividing gcd(60,406)=2. Each orbit has length at least 30, hence the action is transitive with point stabilizer 2. The Borel 29:14 has a unique subgroup 29:7 of index 2, so the A5 action is also transitive on such subgroups as the projective point varies. Thus endpoint pairs form one Aut(T)-orbit and the reviewed path criterion proves uniqueness. Its exact-source reviews are recorded below.

## Internal involutions

The scalar center of each determinant-one covering group has odd order dividing 3. Every projective involution consequently has an actual involutory lift: if $`x^2`$ is central, multiply $`x`$ by the unique central square root of $`x^{-2}`$. In odd characteristic the eigenspaces of a nonidentity determinant-one involution have dimensions 2 and 1; in the unitary case they are nondegenerate and mutually orthogonal. Thus every involution is conjugate under the full general linear or unitary group to

```math
u=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}\nolimits(-1,-1,1). \tag{1}
```

The corresponding class remains one class under the special group: the centralizer on the one-dimensional eigenspace realizes every nonzero determinant, respectively every norm-one determinant, correcting a general conjugator to determinant 1. Passage through the odd center preserves this single projective class. The cited inner-inversion theorem therefore excludes all inner $`\tau`$, including the identity case under its stated repointing and Bruck inputs.

## Linear outer involutions

In $`T=\mathop{\mathrm{PSL}}\nolimits_3(q)`$, the diagonal quotient has odd order dividing 3. The field/graph quotient is the product of the cyclic field group and the graph involution $`\gamma(A)=(A^t)^{-1}`$. An outer involution has image represented by $`\gamma`$, or, if $`q=s^2`$, by the order-two field map $`\sigma:x\mapsto x^s`$ or $`\gamma\sigma`$. Its diagonal component $`d`$ satisfies $`d+\alpha(d)=0`$; for an involution $`\alpha`$ of an odd abelian group, $`\ker(1+\alpha)=\mathop{\mathrm{im}}\nolimits(1-\alpha)`$. Conjugating by an inner-diagonal automorphism therefore reduces to the actual coset $`\alpha T`$.

Each such coset contains one $`T`$-class of involutions. For the graph case, an $`A\in\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_3(q)`$ representing a projective outside involution satisfies $`A=zA^t`$ with $`z`$ central. Transposing gives $`z^2=1`$, and the odd center gives $`z=1`$. The nonsingular symmetric form $`A`$ has determinant 1; symmetric forms of dimension three over a finite odd field are classified by determinant square class, so $`A=B^tB`$ for some $`B\in\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits_3(q)`$. Since $`\det B=\pm1`$, an orthogonal reflection changes its sign if necessary, producing $`B\in\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_3(q)`$. The needed form classification has an elementary proof: diagonalize the form. Any nondegenerate binary diagonal form ax²+by² represents 1, since the sets of values ax² and 1−by² each have (q+1)/2 elements and intersect. Split off a vector of norm 1 twice; the remaining one-dimensional coefficient is a square precisely when the original determinant is a square, and then rescale it to 1.

For the field case, the projective square condition is $`A\sigma(A)=zI`$ with $`z`$ in the odd scalar center and fixed by $`\sigma`$. The norm map on this odd center onto its fixed subgroup allows a central rescaling to $`A\sigma(A)=I`$. For completeness, descent in odd characteristic uses a nonzero η with σ(η)=−η: for a semilinear involution F, both x=(v+F(v))/2 and y=(v−F(v))/(2η) are fixed and v=x+ηy. Thus a fixed-space basis spans the whole extension-field space. This semilinear descent (matrix Hilbert 90) gives $`A=B\sigma(B)^{-1}`$. Since $`\det A=1`$, $`\det B\in\mathbb F_s^\times`$; multiplying the fixed-basis matrix $`B`$ on the right by a matrix over $`\mathbb F_s`$ of inverse determinant makes $`B\in\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_3(q)`$. For graph-field, the projective square condition gives $`A=z\sigma(A)^t`$, with $`z\sigma(z)=1`$; central rescaling uses surjectivity of $`c\mapsto c/\sigma(c)`$ onto the norm-one subgroup of the odd center. The resulting nonsingular Hermitian form is congruent to identity; its determinant-one condition makes the congruence determinant norm one, and a unitary isometry of arbitrary norm-one determinant corrects it to 1. These are the same descent and determinant arguments as in [the reviewed linear source](../primitive-completion/radical-free-odd-linear-inversion.md), at `6d3a97daa9cf20bbae0416295c8c2374c2cd8ede`, with odd-characteristic symmetric forms treated separately above.

## Unitary outer involutions

For $`T=\mathop{\mathrm{PSU}}\nolimits_3(q)`$, Steinberg's unitary case gives an odd diagonal quotient of order dividing 3 and a cyclic field quotient of order $`2f`$. Its unique involution is represented by entrywise conjugation $`\alpha=\bar{\phantom{x}}:x\mapsto x^q`$ on the standard Hermitian space over $`\mathbb F_{q^2}`$. The same odd-diagonal argument reduces every outer involution to the actual coset $`\alpha T`$.

Let $`A\in\mathop{\mathrm{SU}}\nolimits_3(q)`$ and suppose $`A\alpha`$ has projective square 1. Then $`A\bar A=zI`$ for a scalar $`z`$ in the odd special-unitary center. Conjugating and comparing the two product orders gives $`z=\bar z`$; a unitary scalar also has $`\bar z=z^{-1}`$, so $`z^2=1`$ and $`z=1`$. The semilinear involution $`F=A\alpha`$ has a three-dimensional fixed $`\mathbb F_q`$-space $`E`$, whose scalar extension is the whole space. The Hermitian form restricts to a nondegenerate symmetric form on $`E`$. For a fixed-basis matrix $`B`$, $`A=B\bar B^{-1}`$; $`\det A=1`$ gives $`\det B\in\mathbb F_q^\times`$, and the Gram determinant is

```math
\det(\bar B^tB)=N_{\mathbb F_{q^2}/\mathbb F_q}(\det B)=(\det B)^2.
```

Thus this symmetric form has square discriminant and is isometric to the standard fixed-space form. The isometry extends by scalars to a unitary conjugator. Its determinant is $`\pm1`$, and an orthogonal reflection of the standard fixed space corrects the sign while preserving the form and commuting with $`\alpha`$. Hence all involutions in $`\alpha T`$ form one $`T`$-class. This determinant argument is specific to the actual special-unitary coset and does not identify distinct diagonal cosets.

## Marked collision and even-characteristic corollary

In every outer case, the involution $`u`$ in (1) is fixed by the standard $`\alpha`$, so $`\widehat\alpha u`$ is an outside involution in the same actual coset as $`\widehat\alpha`$. Its unique $`T`$-class contains both, and its square contains $`u`$, hence every internal involution. The actual canonical mark is nonempty and contains $`\widehat\alpha^T`$; uniqueness makes it exactly that class. The condition $`H\cap\Lambda^2=1`$ forces $`H`$ to have odd order. But $`|H|=|C_T(\alpha)|`$, and this centralizer contains $`u`$, so it has even order. This contradiction completes the odd-characteristic proof.

Together with the separately reviewed [characteristic-two linear theorem](../primitive-completion/radical-free-odd-linear-inversion.md) at `6d3a97daa9cf20bbae0416295c8c2374c2cd8ede` and [unitary theorem](../primitive-completion/radical-free-unitary-inversion.md) at `fa462cdffd3082b37b0f100ea7e0364a97832473` and its recorded review, this excludes both three-dimensional families for all prime powers. The corollary retains the cited dependencies and does not cover these groups as proper factors of a larger canonical group.

#### One actual T-class in each outside coset

Write a formal element as Int(A)α, A∈SL_n(q), α one of those representatives. Its square is inner by a scalar z∈Z. For α=σ the equation is Aσ(A)=z. The scalar z is σ-fixed. Replacing A by cA, c∈Z, changes z by cσ(c); this norm is surjective onto Z^σ because for fixed z its square root c within the odd cyclic Z is also σ-fixed. Normalize z=1. Matrix Hilbert 90 gives A=Bσ(B)⁻¹ with B∈GL_n(q). Since det A=1, det B∈F_s×, and right multiplication by a fixed-field matrix of reciprocal determinant gives B∈SL_n(q).

For α=γ, the square condition is A=zAᵀ. Transposition gives z²=1 and Z odd, hence z=1. A is a nonsingular symmetric matrix of determinant one. Over F_q, q odd, nonsingular symmetric forms in a fixed dimension are classified by determinant square class, so A=BBᵀ with B∈GL_n(q). Then det B=±1; if needed right-multiply B by an isometry of determinant −1, obtaining B∈SL_n(q).

For α=γσ, the square condition is A=zσ(A)ᵀ, with zσ(z)=1. Since σ(z)=z⁻¹ and Z is odd, the map c↦c/σ(c) is surjective on this norm-one subgroup: for c in that subgroup it is c². Rescale A to get z=1. Hermitian-form congruence gives A=Bσ(B)ᵀ. Norm(det B)=det A=1, so right multiplication by a unitary diagonal matrix with determinant (det B)⁻¹ makes B∈SL_n(q). In each case the standard representative α and the original involution are T-conjugate. These are PSL-level conclusions, not merely PGL-level or algebraic-closure conjugacies.

Every internal involution class has an α-fixed representative. Since Z has odd order, every projective involution has an actual involution lift (multiply an arbitrary lift by the unique correcting square root in Z). Its eigenvalues are ±1, and determinant one forces negative-eigenspace dimension 2r. Its GL_n(q) class does not split in SL_n(q): the GL centralizer GL_{2r}(q)×GL_{n−2r}(q) has determinant image all F_q×. Hence u_r=diag(−I_{2r},I_{n−2r}), 1≤r≤(n−1)/2, represents every T-involution class and is fixed by γ, σ and γσ.

For each r, αu_r is an involution in the same Tα coset, hence belongs to α^T. The actual Λ is T-conjugacy invariant, consists of outside involutions in that coset, and contains the standard representative after conjugation. The class uniqueness therefore gives Λ=α^T. It follows u_r∈Λ² and, by T-invariance, every T-involution belongs to Λ². The faithful-folder condition H∩Λ²=1 forces H to have odd order. Yet |Λ|=[T:H]=[T:C_T(α)] gives |H|=|C_T(α)|, which is even because u₁∈C_T(α). Contradiction.

For clarity, the finite-field form and descent arguments used above work in every dimension: diagonalize a symmetric form and split off a vector of norm 1 while dimension≥2, using the binary square-set intersection argument in the three-dimensional proof. The last coefficient is determined by determinant square class. For Hermitian forms, diagonalization and surjectivity of the finite-field norm normalize each diagonal coefficient. Nondegeneracy supplies a nonzero-norm vector at each step by polarization. For semilinear descent with field involution σ, choose η≠0 with σ(η)=−η and write v=(v+Fv)/2+η(v−Fv)/(2η); both displayed components after division are fixed by F. This proves that the fixed space has a basis spanning the original space. The automorphism classification remains an external input. ### Unitary groups

Let n≥5 be odd and q odd, and suppose the *entire* canonical group is T=PSU_n(q). The n=3 case is already reviewed in the preceding three-dimensional theorem, source b436485f3c07c3413c19b319e51e8c7bc2c3a580. The diagonal quotient has odd order gcd(n,q+1), and the field quotient is cyclic of order 2f. Its unique involution is represented by α:x↦x^q on the standard Hermitian space over F_(q²). As above, H¹(C₂,D)=0 for the odd diagonal quotient D, so an outside involution is diagonally conjugate into the actual Tα coset.

Write a coset involution as Aα with A∈SU_n(q). The projective square equation is A\bar A=zI with z in the odd scalar center Z. Since α fixes the square of Aα, z=\bar z. But z is a unitary scalar, so \bar z=z⁻¹. Thus z²=1 and z=1. Semilinear descent gives a fixed F_q-space E of dimension n whose scalar extension is the full Hermitian space and a fixed-basis matrix B with A=B\bar B⁻¹. The Hermitian form restricts to a nondegenerate symmetric form on E. Since det A=1, det B=\overline{det B}∈F_q×, and its Gram determinant det(\bar BᵀB)=(det B)² is a square. By finite-field symmetric-form classification the restricted form is congruent to the standard real form. A fixed-basis change therefore gives a unitary B with A=B\bar B⁻¹. Now det B belongs both to F_q× and to the norm-one group, hence is ±1; a determinant−1 orthogonal reflection in the standard fixed space corrects its sign. Thus B∈SU_n(q) and every involution in Tα is T-conjugate to α. This is an actual special-unitary-class assertion, not a GU-class or group order comparison.

Every projective internal involution lifts to an actual order-two element of SU_n(q), because its scalar center has odd order and one can use the unique central square-root correction. Its −1 eigenspace has even dimension 2r, the eigenspaces are mutually orthogonal nondegenerate Hermitian spaces, and GU-conjugacy is determined by their dimensions. Its GU-centralizer realizes every norm-one determinant on either nonzero block, so this class does not split in SU. Therefore the matrices u_r=diag(−I_{2r},I_{n−2r}), 1≤r≤(n−1)/2, represent all T-involution classes and are fixed by α. The same actual-mark argument as for PSL gives every internal involution in Λ², so H is odd, but unique outside-class counting gives |H|=|C_T(α)| even because u₁ centralizes α. Contradiction.

### A central-extension obstruction using the original marks

We first prove an abstract sufficient criterion. Let L be a characteristic quasisimple subgroup with odd center in a hypothetical canonical G=LZ(G). Let α be the restriction of canonical inversion τ to L. Suppose the formal coset tL in L⋊⟨t⟩ implementing α has exactly one L-conjugacy class C of involutions, C² contains every nonidentity involution of L, and C_L(α) has even order. These hypotheses are about actual involutions, not just their automorphism labels. They force a contradiction as follows.

The independently reviewed [all-parity center lemma](exact-factorization-criterion.md#canonical-inversion-fixes-the-center-at-every-parity) gives τ|Z(G)=1. Since L is perfect and G=LZ(G), G′=L. Inversion on G/G′ then gives Z(G)²≤Z(L). Every odd-order central element lies in L because squaring is invertible on its cyclic subgroup. The Sylow2 subgroup E of Z(G) is elementary abelian and intersects L trivially, since Z(L) has odd order. Consequently

    G=L×E,       τ=(α,1_E).                            (20)

The original marked set lies in tL×E. Each E-label has one L-class of involutions, so Λ=C×B for some B⊆E containing0. Projection of K=tΛ generates E, hence B spans E. The class C×{0} is selected, and its square contains every L-involution. Thus H∩L has odd order by H∩Λ²=1.

The canonical simple nonassociative quotient argument gives G=HG′=HL, so H→E is surjective. A Sylow2 subgroup P of H maps isomorphically onto E: its kernel lies in the odd group H∩L, and |H|₂=|E|. Suppose B contains b≠0. Choose h=(l,b)∈P mapping to b. It is an involution, and l≠1 because H∩E=1 by centrality and core-freeness. Write l=c1c2 with c1,c2∈C, using the square hypothesis. Both (c1,0) and (c2,b) belong to Λ, so h∈H∩Λ², a contradiction. Therefore B={0}; generation gives E=1. Now G=L and Λ=C, forcing |H|=|L|/|C|=|C_L(α)| even, again contradicting the oddness of H∩L=H. This proves the criterion without constructing a quotient folder on L or imposing a bound on the central rank.

The identity G=HG′ used here holds at either parity: projecting the canonical section to the abelian quotient G/HG′ is a surjective homomorphism of loops. Simplicity makes it trivial, unless the loop itself is associative of prime order. Thus the nonassociative case has HG′=G. This is the same reviewed identity used in the preceding almost-simple and full-socle deductions.

### Lifting the local projective involution facts

Set S=L/Z(L). The subgroup L=G′ is characteristic. Restriction of Aut(L) to S is injective: an automorphism acting trivially on S has the form x↦x z(x), with z:L→Z(L) a homomorphism, and perfection forces z=1. If the induced automorphism αbar on S were inner, choose l∈L lifting its inner representative. Injectivity would make α=Inn(l). Since τ fixes Z(G), it would then be inner on all of G. The reviewed [inner-inversion theorem](exact-factorization-criterion.md#inner-inversion-and-the-generalized-fitting-subgroup), exact source `84eba0ffd4dfab4b6d64853a3131010ad2b945b8`, forbids this: inner inversion forces F*(G)=O2(G), whereas the normal quasisimple L is a nontrivial component. This uses the component conclusion, not an assumption that O2(G)=1. Therefore αbar is an outer involution of S.

The reviewed local inputs summarized in the preceding section, exact sources `b436485f3c07c3413c19b319e51e8c7bc2c3a580`, `d5e26dd6aef4ad09f61e0569e383782dea0d468b`, `6d3a97daa9cf20bbae0416295c8c2374c2cd8ede` and `fa462cdffd3082b37b0f100ea7e0364a97832473`, establish that the actual coset αbar S has one S-class of involutions, that its square contains every S-involution, and that C_S(αbar) has even order. Their reduction by inner-diagonal conjugacy transports these assertions to every actual outer involution, not just a chosen standard representative. These statements retain their separate characteristic-two and odd-characteristic proofs and external automorphism qualifications.

Because τ fixes Z(L), the natural map

    L⋊⟨t⟩ → S⋊⟨tbar⟩

has central odd kernel Z(L). Every involution downstairs has exactly one involutory lift in its fiber: for a lift x with x²=z∈Z(L), multiply x by the unique element c∈Z(L) satisfying c²=z⁻¹. Conjugacy therefore lifts uniquely, so the outside coset tL has one L-class C, with the same class size as its projective image. In particular |C_L(α)|=|Z(L)|·|C_S(αbar)| is even.

To lift the square property, take an involution u∈L and a factorization ubar=abar bbar into two outside involutions in the projective class. Conjugating this commutator by a inverts it, while centrality fixes it; hence it has order at most two and is trivial. Thus ab is an involution lifting ubar and must equal u by uniqueness. It follows that every nonidentity involution of L lies in C². All hypotheses of the central-extension criterion hold, proving the stated exclusion.

## Odd-dimensional unitary normalizers without field automorphisms

**Conditional continuation of 0323-finite-simple-non-moufang-bol-loops:C0010, with three independent exact-source reviews below.** Let d≥3 be odd and let q=p^f with PSU_d(q) nonabelian simple. Under the pinned canonical and unitary automorphism inputs, no finite simple nonassociative left Bol loop has canonical group G isomorphic to an intermediate group

    SU_d(q) ≤ G ≤ GU_d(q).                            (21)

Here GU denotes the full isometry group of the Hermitian form, with scalar center of order q+1. For the semilinear enlargement SU_d(q)≤G≤GU_d(q)⋊Gal(F_(q²)/F_p), a necessary condition is that its field image is exactly the subgroup of order two, generated by q-power conjugation. The latter is a residual necessary condition, not an exclusion of those semilinear groups.

Put L=SU_d(q), Z_L=Z(L) of odd order d0=gcd(d,q+1), and S=L/Z_L. The natural linear centralizer of L consists of the scalar unitary matrices C≅C_(q+1). The determinant gives GU_d(q)/L≅C_(q+1), while the image of C under determinant has index d0. Thus GU_d(q)/(LC) is cyclic of order d0. For every intermediate G in (21), L=G′ is characteristic, C_G(L)=G∩C=Z(G), and

    G/(LZ(G)) embeds in GU_d(q)/(LC),

so it has odd order. These are statements about the actual intermediate group, with no equality to the full normalizer presumed. The scalar-centralizer/natural-module input is the one used in the reviewed line-normalizer argument in [the full-action manuscript](../odd-completion/odd-envelope-parameterization.md#independent-review-of-rows-36), exact source `b8c237d84f001b9a3d70ab725adab6b67e57c05b`.

If d0=1, then G=LZ(G), excluded by the odd-central extension theorem at exact source `e7476945cf987cf1c654a385ac8f78fe2beb320a`. Suppose d0>1. The center-fixing theorem at that source makes canonical inversion τ act trivially on Z_L≤Z(G). On the other hand, an involutory automorphism of L inducing an outer automorphism on S has unitary field label f in the cyclic group C_(2f): the diagonal subgroup has odd order d0, so it has no nontrivial involution. Inner and diagonal automorphisms fix Z_L, while this field label sends z to z^q=z⁻¹. Thus an outer involutory restriction would invert the nontrivial odd Z_L, contrary to center-fixing. This uses the standard unitary automorphism description and odd-diagonal reduction already pinned to Steinberg60, statements3.2–3.6, p.608, and §7, pp.613–614, in REFERENCES.md. Restriction Aut(L)→Aut(S) is injective by perfection as proved above. Hence τ|L=Inn(l) for some l∈L.

Let γ=Inn(l)⁻¹τ. It fixes L and Z(G) pointwise. Moreover τ(l)=l and l²∈Z_L≤Z(G), so γ²=1. For g∈G, compatibility with its conjugation on L gives

    χ(g)=g⁻¹γ(g) ∈ C_G(L)=Z(G).

Since these values are central, χ is a homomorphism, trivial on LZ(G). Since γ fixes its own values and γ²=1, its image has exponent at most two. The odd order of G/(LZ(G)) therefore forces χ=1. Thus τ=Inn(l) on all of G, contradicting the component form of the reviewed inner-inversion theorem at `84eba0ffd4dfab4b6d64853a3131010ad2b945b8`. This proves (21), including possible central 2-torsion.

For a semilinear intermediate G, the quotient G/L is solvable and L perfect, so L=G^(∞) is characteristic. Its conjugation action gives ρ:G→Aut(S). In the standard unitary outer description Out(S)=D⋊F, D has odd order and F=C_(2f) is the field quotient. Let π:G→F be the composite field map.     ρ(τ(g))=βρ(g)β⁻¹.

Because F is abelian, this implies π(τ(g))=π(g). For each canonical k∈K, inversion also gives π(τ(k))=π(k)⁻¹. As K generates G, the field image has exponent at most two, and is therefore either trivial or the unique subgroup of order two in F. A trivial image places G in (21), already excluded. Hence the nontrivial image is forced. This argument does not assert that the induced action on S is a loop quotient, or that τ itself lies in the ambient semilinear group.

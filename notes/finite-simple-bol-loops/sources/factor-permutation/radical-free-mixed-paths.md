# Correlated two-type path family

Mira Cult

**Independently reviewed continuation of [classification frontier](../../claims/0010.md)**, conditional on the canonical folder/normal-subloop and Moufang interfaces of [Claim 0001](../../claims/0001.md), and the [Bruck-stabilizer criterion](exact-factorization-criterion.md#bruck-stabilizers-and-necessary-witness-tests). For every a,b≥1 it gives a simple proper non-Bruck left Bol loop of order 60^a168^b with canonical group A5^(2a)×PSL2(7)^(2b), paired inversion, and stabilizer obtained from two cross-type correlated paths. It lies in the existing exact-factorization family if and only if a≡b mod2, and has no Bruck isotope for any a,b. No exhaustive or literature novelty claim is made.

The base construction and subgroup embeddings are [the mixed-simple source](radical-free-mixed-simple-quotients.md), independently reviewed; the canonical-inversion source and its scope are recorded there.

Write T=A5, U=PSL2(7), and use endpoint subgroups D=D10,A=A4<T and S=S4,B=7:3<U. Let ε:D→C2, δ:S→C2, λ:A→C3, μ:B→C3 be the surjective maps with kernels C5,A4,V4,C7 respectively. Construct a path with a edges for the T-factor and a path with b edges for the U-factor. Each edge is a τ-pair of coordinates of its type. Each internal vertex supports a full diagonal simple strip on the incident edge coordinates. The endpoint vertices carry D,A on the T path and S,B on the U path. Define H as the subgroup of the product of all independent vertex groups satisfying ε(d)=δ(s) and λ(x)=μ(y), where d,x,s,y are the four endpoint variables. These two equations involve disjoint variables and are independent; hence

 |H| = 60^(a−1)168^(b−1)·(10·12·24·21)/6 = 60^a168^b = |K|=[G:H].

Here G=T^(2a)×U^(2b), τ swaps the two coordinates of each edge, and K is the product over edges of anti-diagonals (z,z^−1). The correlation equations are imposed on actual endpoint variables, not on projections alone.

First H∩K²=1. On every active path, successive paired coordinates are conjugate inverses, while each internal strip identifies its two incidence values. Thus, along that path, the endpoint values have the same order, and if one is 1 the entire path is 1. A common nonidentity order for D and A is only 2, whereas for S and B it is only 3. If the T path is active, d and x are involutions; λ(x)=0, so y∈C7. As S has no element of order 7, the U path must be inactive; then s=1 and ε(d)=δ(s)=0, contradiction because d is a reflection. If the T path is inactive, x=1 forces y∈C7, so the U path is also inactive. Hence there is no nonidentity collision. Invariance of K² under G-conjugacy and the order equation make K a transversal to every conjugate of H. K is inverse-closed and closed under sandwiching, so this is a Bol folder. K generates G because T and U are perfect; H is core-free: endpoint projections are proper, and an internal coordinate is tied diagonally to the other coordinate of its strip. Hence no entire simple coordinate factor is contained in H. C0001 makes this a faithful generated canonical folder with paired τ, since τ inverts the generating K.

For a≡b mod2, two-color the two paths so D and S have the same color and A and B have the same color. Regard the product of vertex groups of either color, with the equations involving its endpoints, as subgroups L,R of N=T^a×U^b after identifying each edge's two coordinates with N×N. The stabilizer above is L×R. The coloring is possible precisely under the displayed parity assumption: the endpoint colors differ exactly when their path has odd length. The folder calculation gives L∩R=1, and |L||R|=|N| gives N=LR. Thus this is an exact factorization of the characteristically non-simple perfect group N, within the existing construction. No coordinate swap is claimed to preserve the correlated H; coordinate orientation is chosen once in making the identification.

Simplicity holds for EVERY proper nontrivial normal subgroup N0 of G, including those not fixed by τ. Such a subgroup is a product of coordinate factors. Retain the complementary nonempty proper set J of coordinate incidences. For a path X of length n with simple group V, let f_X be its number of edges both of whose incidences are retained, t_X the number of touched internal vertices, and let E_X be the product of orders of its selected endpoint groups. The projection of H to J has order

 60^t_T 168^t_U E_T E_U / (2^e2 3^e3),

where e2=1 precisely if D and S are both selected and e3=1 precisely if A and B are both selected. This holds because the two constraints are independent, and a constraint whose one endpoint is omitted is freely soluble. Meanwhile |G/N0|/|π(K)|=60^f_T168^f_U. It suffices to show the quotient of these quantities is >1.

For either path put ρ_X=|V|^(t_X−f_X)E_X. If every incidence of that path is retained, ρ_T=120/60=2 and ρ_U=504/168=3. If a path is not fully retained then f_X≤t_X, because a selected full-edge component in a path with more edges than touched internal vertices would have to include both endpoints and hence the whole path. Thus ρ_X≥E_X. If a path is nonempty and no endpoint is selected, the stronger f_X≤t_X−1 follows, so ρ_X≥|V|.

If both paths are full, J is all coordinates, excluded. If precisely one path is full and the other is empty, the ratio is 2 or 3. If precisely one is full and the other is nonempty but partial, the ratio is >1: if the partial path selects no endpoint there is no cross-endpoint divisor and its ratio is ≥60 or 168; if it selects an endpoint, its ratio is at least that endpoint's order, which is at least 10, while the entire cross divisor is at most 6. If neither path is full and no endpoint is selected anywhere, some nonempty path supplies a factor ≥60. Otherwise the product E_T E_U contains at least one selected endpoint. With one selected endpoint there is no divisor and its order is at least 10; with at least two, its product is at least 10² while the divisor is at most 6. Thus the ratio is always >1. (The smallest endpoint order here is 10.) C0001's strict quotient criterion proves simplicity.

H is nontrivial, so the loop is nonassociative. H has index six in the product of all independent vertex groups, which is itself proper in G because its endpoint projections are proper. Thus the canonical action is imprimitive and the simple nonassociative Moufang primitivity criterion gives non-Moufangness. For a=1, the two T-endpoint projections D and A have different orders; for a>1, τ sends an endpoint proper projection to an interior full-T projection. Thus τ does not normalize H, proving non-Bruckness by the cited criterion. In fact O2(G)=1, so the independently reviewed generalized-Fitting/Bruck-isotope theorem in [the generalized-Fitting source](exact-factorization-criterion.md#inner-inversion-and-the-generalized-fitting-subgroup), exact, excludes every Bruck isotope. The deductions have the independent exact-source reviews below, retaining the input scopes.


## Exact-factorization route boundary

The intrinsic exact-factorization witness criterion (*) is the reviewed criterion in [the intrinsic criterion](exact-factorization-criterion.md). It requires a normal coordinate-product subgroup M◁G with [G:M]=60^a168^b, M∩τM=1 and HM∩HτM=H, together with the remaining conditions of (*). The same-parity coloring above explicitly supplies all conditions and an exact factorization, proving the forward inclusion.

Conversely, suppose a witness M exists. The condition M∩τM=1 permits at most one of the two coordinates of each τ-pair in M. Taking 5-valuations in [G:M]=60^a168^b forces exactly a T coordinates outside M, hence exactly one outside M in each of the a T-pairs. Taking 7-valuations similarly forces exactly one outside M in each U-pair. Thus M and τM partition all simple coordinates. The equality HM∩HτM=H says that H is the direct product of its coordinate projections onto these two sides.

Each full internal strip must lie entirely on one side: otherwise its two nontrivial simple coordinate values are diagonally constrained, whereas the direct-product split independently varies their projections. The C2-linked D/S endpoints also lie on the same side: if they were separated, independently selecting endpoint values in the split projections would violate their surjective ε=δ equation. The independent C3-linked A/B endpoints lie on the same side by the same argument. (The other equation can always be satisfied by setting its endpoint variables to the identity.) Hence the sides color path vertices so adjacent vertices have opposite colors, with D and S the same color and A and B the same color. Such a coloring exists exactly when a≡b mod2. Therefore when the parities differ no witness (*) exists, and the reviewed criterion excludes membership in the exact-factorization route. This is an iff statement within this explicitly constructed family, not an exhaustion of other simple Bol loops.


## Intrinsic cycle and isotope boundary

The reviewed [bipartite witness criterion](radical-free-bipartite-witness.md) gives an equivalent description. The finest coordinate splitting blocks of H are exactly its independent full internal strips and the two endpoint fiber products D×_{C₂}S and A×_{C₃}B. A full strip cannot split between its two coordinates, and neither endpoint fiber product splits because its quotient equation is surjective and nontrivial. These blocks already form a direct product on disjoint coordinate supports, so they are precisely the atoms, with no omitted further splitting. The τ-pair graph is one cycle of length a+b, with the two-parallel-edge cycle allowed when a=b=1. Therefore its bipartiteness is exactly a≡b mod2; there are two actual witness subgroups in that case and none otherwise. This count is not a loop-isomorphism count.

The isotope-invariance deduction in the same reviewed source strengthens the opposite-parity conclusion: these loops admit no exact-factorization isotope. They already admit no Bruck isotope by the reviewed O₂(G)=1 obstruction. For example (a,b)=(2,1) gives order 604800. This is an even-order radical-free family outside both isotope routes, under the stated construction and graph/isotope dependencies, with the independent reviews below. No literature novelty or exhaustion of the even branch is asserted.

## Isomorphisms within the full construction family

Independently reviewed continuation of the construction [radical-free-mixed-paths.md](radical-free-mixed-paths.md), reviewed at source recorded source version. For each a,b≥1 the loops in that construction, allowing every embedding of the specified endpoint subgroups, every quotient identification, arbitrary twists of full strips, and arbitrary orientations of the τ-pairs, form exactly one loop-isomorphism class. Different ordered pairs (a,b) give nonisomorphic loops. This concerns only the stated family, not arbitrary mixed paired folders.

The canonical group is A5^(2a)×PSL₂(7)^(2b); uniqueness of its simple normal factors makes a,b intrinsic. Conversely a folder isomorphism commuting with τ preserves the full anti-diagonal K and induces a loop isomorphism. Thus it suffices to put all family data into the same actual subgroup H by an automorphism of G commuting with τ.

On a path, independent simultaneous automorphisms on the two coordinates of each τ-pair, together with reparametrization of internal strip variables, remove all twisted diagonal embeddings successively from one end to the other. The absence of cycles in each individual path is essential: the two cross-type quotient correlations are at the endpoint variables and introduce no compatibility into this strip gauge. After gauging, a simultaneous automorphism of the underlying simple group on every edge of that path still acts on both endpoints. Pair flips orient the underlying abstract paths as desired and commute with τ.

For A5, identify the points with F5. Every D10 is the normalizer of a Sylow 5-subgroup, and every A4 is a point stabilizer in the natural five-point action; the reviewed endpoint subgroup inputs include these identifications. A 5-cycle in D10 acts transitively on those five stabilizers. Consequently all ordered endpoint pairs (D10,A4) are simultaneously A5-conjugate. For the standard pair, take
 D10={x↦±x+b : b∈F5},  A4=even permutations fixing 0.
The permutation m:x↦2x normalizes D10, fixes 0, and is an odd 4-cycle on F5\{0}. Conjugation by m, an automorphism of A5, preserves both endpoint subgroups and inverts A4/V4≅C3. It preserves the unique nontrivial quotient D10/C5≅C2.

For U=PSL₂(7)≅GL₃(2), B=7:3 is the normalizer of a Sylow 7-subgroup. Every Sylow 7-subgroup acts regularly both on the seven points and on the seven lines of the Fano plane: its order-seven elements are irreducible on F₂³, so fix neither a point nor a line. Thus B is transitive on each of the two classes of S4 endpoint stabilizers, and all Sylow 7-subgroups are conjugate. The graph automorphism of GL₃(2) exchanges points and lines. Therefore every ordered endpoint pair (S4,7:3) is simultaneously Aut(U)-conjugate. The point/line subgroup identification and its scoped subgroup/fusion dependencies are those of the reviewed [small-prime path source](radical-free-small-psl2-paths.md) at recorded source version; the present argument is not a new primary-source audit.

Every surjection D10→C2 or S4→C2 is the unique nontrivial map, and Aut(C2)=1, so the C2 identification has no freedom. Every surjection A4→C3 or 7:3→C3 has the indicated characteristic kernel, and its two choices differ by inversion. Thus there are exactly two possible C3 fiber-product equations. Applying the simultaneous A5 automorphism induced by m to every A5 path edge inverts the A4 quotient while preserving D10 and its C2 quotient; it exchanges those two equations without changing any other condition. Hence the fiber products are isomorphic. This does not require, and in fact cannot use, inversion of the 7:3 quotient by a normalizing automorphism: its C7-kernel is characteristic and the two C3 generators act as distinct automorphisms of C7, whose automorphism group is abelian.

Thus all choices at fixed (a,b) produce isomorphic canonical folders and loops. The argument has the scoped independent reviews below; it does not claim all canonical groups of these isomorphism types arise from this family or identify isotopy classes.

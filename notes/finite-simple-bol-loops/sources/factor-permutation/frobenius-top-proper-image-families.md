# Frobenius-top exact-factorization family with proper top image

**Mira Cult**

**Construction independently reviewed, conditional on the pinned inputs; [0323:C0010](../../claims/0010.md).** Let T be a finite nonabelian simple group with an exact factorization T=BC by nontrivial proper subgroups B,C, and let χ:C→C_m be an epimorphism, where m>1 is odd. Let F be a nontrivial finite odd group admitting an action of C_m in which every nonidentity complement element acts fixed-point-freely on F. Put n=|F| and P=F⋊C_m. The construction below gives, conditional on the cited canonical-folder, normal-quotient and properness inputs, a finite simple proper non-Bruck left Bol loop of order mn|T|^n. Its radical-free canonical group has socle T^(2n), an odd pure-permutation top Q of order mn² with two τ-paired transitive factor orbits, and a proper stabilizer top image of index n. It lies in the existing exact-factorization route; no classification or literature novelty is claimed. The argument does not assume F abelian or elementary abelian.

## Exact factorization and canonical group

Let S=T^F⋊P, with the faithful affine action (f,c):x↦f c(x) of P on the set F. Let φ:P→C_m be the quotient map and put

 Y0=B^F⋊C_m,
 Y1={(a,z)∈C^F⋊P : Σ_{x∈F}χ(a_x)=φ(z)}.                    (1)

The coordinate sum is invariant under permutation by P, so Y1 is a subgroup. As |Y0|=m|B|^n and |Y1|=n|C|^n, their intersection is trivial: B^F∩C^F=1, and for a pure C_m element in Y1 its φ-image must be 0. Therefore |Y0||Y1|=mn|T|^n=|S| and S=Y0Y1 is an exact factorization.

For any nonidentity c∈C_m, the map f↦f^−1 c(f) on F is injective because equal values imply the quotient of the two arguments is c-fixed; it is thus bijective. Hence [F,c]=F even if F is nonabelian. Consequently P′=F, and since T^F is perfect,

 S′=T^F⋊F,     S/S′≅C_m.                                       (2)

With K={(s,s^−1):s∈S} in S², the group generated by K is

 G={(s,t)∈S² : φ(s)=−φ(t)}=N⋊Q,
 N=T^F×T^F,   Q={(x,y)∈P² : φ(x)=−φ(y)}≅F²⋊C_m.              (3)

Here φ on S denotes S→P→C_m. The first equality follows because K generates S′×S′ and maps onto the anti-diagonal in the abelianization. Let τ swap the two S coordinates and θ be the induced swap on Q. Set H=G∩(Y0×Y1). Nagy's exact-factorization construction, *A class of simple proper Bol loops*, arXiv:math/0703919v1, Proposition 3.2 and Lemma 3.4, https://arxiv.org/html/math/0703919v1, supplies the Bol-folder transversal for K; equivalently, the exact factorization makes K a transversal to every conjugate of Y0×Y1 in S², and its intersection with G gives the claimed action. Here |K|=[G:H]=|S|. By definition K generates G. The Q permutation action on the two F-orbits is faithful, so C_G(N)=1; H∩N has proper coordinate projections, hence H is core-free. The faithful generated-folder interface [C0001](../../claims/0001.md) at [the source revision](https://github.com/Mira-Cult/math-wip/commit/9431ade99316d6bc62efb389492fef385b510b45), together with the scoped canonical inversion input [C0007](../foundations/C0007.md) at [the source revision](https://github.com/Mira-Cult/math-wip/commit/932ead5db5f39045e2085caa6b062f6b981b2813), identifies G as the canonical left multiplication group and τ as canonical inversion. The intrinsic exact-factorization criterion is independently reviewed in [the exact-factorization source](exact-factorization-criterion.md) at [the source revision](https://github.com/Mira-Cult/math-wip/commit/e3a39be22fe1390d3717bdeee8f81d82f6817aef); no claim is made that this family exhausts that route.

The stabilizer top image is

 U=HN/N=Q∩(C_m×P),   |U|=mn,   [Q:U]=n>1.                    (4)

The first projection of Y0 is C_m; in Y1 the epimorphism χ lets its coordinate sum realize every prescribed φ-value, so the second top element is unrestricted apart from the anti-abelianization relation. Moreover

 H∩N=B^F×{a∈C^F:Σχ(a_x)=0},
 |H∩N|=|B|^n |C|^n/m=|T|^n/m.                                 (5)

The image of K in Q is I={(x,x^−1):x∈P}, so |I|=mn. The construction therefore has a genuinely proper top image; no assumption that τ stabilizes U is made.

## All normal quotients

Let N1,N2 be the two T^F factors. Each is a minimal normal subgroup of G because P is transitive on F. Any normal L◁G has L∩N∈{1,N1,N2,N}. Trivial intersection forces L=1 by C_G(N)=1. If L∩N=N1 then [L,N2]=1, so

 N1≤L≤C_G(N2)=N1⋊ker(Q→P on the second F-orbit),

whose last kernel is the first copy of F. Intermediate normal subgroups can occur; the bound below treats all of them. The analogous statement holds after exchanging the orbits. If L≥N then L=N⋊M for M◁Q.

For L=N1 and L=N2 respectively,

 [G:HN1]=mn|B|^n,       [G:HN2]=n|C|^n.                       (6)

The identities follow from |K|=mn|T|^n and (5). Both indices are strictly less than |T|^n=|B|^n|C|^n. Indeed |C|≥m≥3 and |B|≥2. The fixed-point-free C_m-orbits on F\{1} have length m, so n≥m+1. Consequently mn<m^n≤|C|^n and n<2^n≤|B|^n. If L lies between either Ni and its displayed centralizer, its quotient stabilizer index is no larger than the corresponding value in (6). Moreover |KL/L|≥|T|^n: the identity-top anti-diagonal marks project onto every tuple in the opposite T^F orbit, and that orbit intersects L trivially. Thus |KL/L|>[G:HL] in both one-orbit cases, including all intermediate normal subgroups.

Now let L=N⋊M be a proper normal subgroup containing N. If M has nontrivial image D≤C_m, take z∈M mapping to 1≠c^j∈D. Its conjugation action on F² is the pair of automorphisms c^j and c^−j, composed with inner automorphisms from its F² part. Rather than assume those inner twists are fixed-point-free, conjugate z by an element of F² to the pure complement element c^j: for each coordinate the map f↦f^−1 c^±j(f) is bijective. The conjugate remains in M. Then [F²,c^j]=F² by the same bijection, so F²≤M and M is the full preimage of D. Since L is proper, D<C_m. The image of U in Q/M is all C_m/D; I={(x,x^−1):x∈P} also maps onto all of it. Hence |KL/L|=|C_m/D|>1=[G:HL].

Otherwise M≤F². Put A=pr1(M)◁P; then A≤F. The first projection pr1:Q→P maps U onto C_m and M onto A, with its kernel contained in U, so

 [Q:UM]=[P:C_m A]=n/|A|.

As I projects bijectively to P, its image in Q/M has at least |P/A|=mn/|A| elements. Therefore |KL/L|≥mn/|A|>n/|A|=[G:HL]. This includes arbitrary coupled normal subgroups of F² and does not assume that A is abelian. Every normal quotient has now been considered, whether τ-stable or not. The exact normal-subloop criterion of C0001 gives simplicity.

H is nontrivial, so the loop is nonassociative. The action is imprimitive because H<N⋊U<G; the reviewed simple nonassociative Moufang-primitivity criterion makes it proper. The solvable radical and O2(G) are trivial: any solvable normal subgroup is disjoint from N and hence centralizes N, so is 1. The generalized-Fitting/Bruck-isotopy result in [the exact-factorization source](exact-factorization-criterion.md#inner-inversion-and-the-generalized-fitting-subgroup), reviewed at [the source revision](https://github.com/Mira-Cult/math-wip/commit/84eba0ffd4dfab4b6d64853a3131010ad2b945b8), excludes every Bruck isotope. Two explicit inputs use A₅=A₄C₅, whose factors have coprime orders. For m=3 take B=C₅, C=A₄, χ the quotient A₄/V₄, and F=C₇ with complement generator acting by multiplication by 2 modulo 7. This yields order 21·60⁷ and top order 147. For m=5 take B=A₄, C=C₅ with χ the identity, and F=C₁₁ with complement generator acting by multiplication by 3 modulo 11. This yields order 55·60¹¹. The displayed multipliers have orders 3 and 5 respectively; on their prime fields every nonidentity power is fixed-point-free on the additive group. These are concrete instances of the theorem, not an enumeration or classification of the family.

## Explicit isomorphism criterion

**Independently reviewed deduction, conditional on the pinned inputs.** Consider two inputs to the construction, written

```math
(T;B,C,\chi:C\twoheadrightarrow C_m;F,\rho)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 (\widetilde T;\widetilde B,\widetilde C,
 \widetilde\chi:\widetilde C\twoheadrightarrow C_{\widetilde m};
 \widetilde F,\widetilde\rho).
```

Their loops are isomorphic if and only if there are isomorphisms

```math
\gamma:T\to\widetilde T,\qquad
 \beta:F\to\widetilde F,\qquad
 r:C_m\to C_{\widetilde m}
```

such that

```math
\gamma(B)=\widetilde B,\qquad \gamma(C)=\widetilde C,\qquad
 \widetilde\chi\gamma|_C=r\chi,\qquad
 \beta\rho(c)\beta^{-1}=\widetilde\rho(r(c))\quad(c\in C_m). \tag{7}
```

In particular the **same** cyclic isomorphism occurs in the character and Frobenius-action equations. This is an exact equivalence of the displayed construction data, not an enumeration of their orbits or a classification of all simple Bol loops.

### Recovery from the canonical folder

Write $`M=T^F`$, $`S_{\rm der}=[S,S]=M\rtimes F`$, and denote target groups with tildes. The subgroup $`M`$ is the unique minimal normal subgroup of $`S`$: any normal subgroup disjoint from $`M`$ centralizes it, while $`C_S(M)=1`$ by the faithful permutation action and centerlessness of $`T`$. Similarly the only minimal normal subgroups of the canonical $`G`$ are $`N_1=M\times1`$ and $`N_2=1\times M`$. They are intrinsic, as is their unordered pair. Their centralizers are

```math
C_G(N_2)=S_{\rm der}\times1,\qquad C_G(N_1)=1\times S_{\rm der}. \tag{8}
```

For example centralizing $`N_2`$ forces the second $`S`$-coordinate to be 1, and the anti-abelianization relation puts the first in $`S_{\rm der}`$. These centralizers are generally larger than $`N_1,N_2`$. Both coordinate projections $`G\to S`$ are onto, so the two corresponding quotients in (8) recover $`S`$.

A loop isomorphism induces a canonical-folder isomorphism and intertwines $`\tau`$: $`K`$ generates $`G`$, and inversion on $`K`$ determines $`\tau`$. After possibly composing with the target coordinate swap, it preserves the ordered blocks and induces isomorphisms $`\alpha_1,\alpha_2:S\to\widetilde S`$. Commutation with the swaps gives $`\alpha_1=\alpha_2=\alpha`$; the two coordinate projections jointly embed $`G`$ in $`S^2`$, so the folder isomorphism is the restriction of $`\alpha\times\alpha`$, possibly followed by the swap.

The two projections of $`H=G\cap(Y_0\times Y_1)`$ are all of $`Y_0,Y_1`$: each maps onto $`C_m=S/S_{\rm der}`$, so any chosen element on one side has an opposite-side element with inverse abelian image. Thus $`\alpha`$ carries the endpoint pair in order or swapped. The swapped case cannot occur: the images of $`Y_0,Y_1`$ in the characteristic quotient $`S/M`$ have distinct orders $`m`$ and $`m|F|`$. Here $`|F|`$ is recovered as the number of simple factors of $`M`$, and $`m=|S/S_{\rm der}|`$. Hence every isomorphism carries the endpoint pair in order.

### Coordinate gauges and the cyclic map

The self-centralizing characteristic subgroup $`M`$ embeds $`S`$ faithfully in $`\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(M)`$. The restriction of $`\alpha`$ to $`M`$ is described by a coordinate permutation $`f:F\to\widetilde F`$ and component isomorphisms $`a_x:T\to\widetilde T`$. The induced map on $`S/M`$ is conjugation by $`f`$ on its faithful permutation action. Conjugating any pure permutation in $`P=F\rtimes C_m`$ produces only inner local corrections in $`\widetilde S`$; hence the outer classes of $`a_x`$ are constant on the transitive $`P`$-set. Choose one isomorphism $`\gamma:T\to\widetilde T`$ and write $`a_x=\mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(t_x)\gamma`$.

Since $`F=P_{\rm der}`$, $`f`$ conjugates the regular $`F`$-actions and has the affine form $`f(x)=u\beta(x)`$, where $`\beta:F\to\widetilde F`$ is an isomorphism. The image of $`Y_0`$ in $`S/M`$ is the designated $`C_m`$, so $`f`$ conjugates it to the designated target complement. Each complement has the identity as its unique common fixed point: every nonidentity complement element acts fixed-point-freely on the Frobenius kernel. Therefore $`f(1)=1`$, $`u=1`$, and conjugation by $`f`$ defines the cyclic isomorphism $`r`$ satisfying the last equation of (7).

The projection of $`Y_1\cap M`$ onto any one coordinate is all of $`C`$: another coordinate can balance the prescribed $`\chi`$-value, since $`|F|>1`$ and $`\chi`$ is onto. Therefore every $`a_x`$ carries $`C`$ to $`\widetilde C`$. Relabel target coordinates by $`f`$, and write the induced target permutation of $`q\in P`$ also as $`q`$. For every $`q`$, $`Y_1`$ has an element of top $`q`$; comparison of its target coordinates gives

```math
t_x t_{q^{-1}x}^{-1}\in\widetilde C. \tag{9}
```

The $`P`$-action is transitive, so $`t_xt_1^{-1}\in\widetilde C`$ for every $`x`$. Absorb $`t_1`$ into $`\gamma`$, obtaining $`a_x=\mathop{\mathrm{Inn}}\nolimits(c_x)\gamma`$ with $`c_x\in\widetilde C`$ and $`\gamma(C)=\widetilde C`$.

Equality of $`Y_1\cap M`$ with its target says that the two coordinate-sum epimorphisms have equal kernels. They therefore differ by a unique cyclic isomorphism $`\lambda:C_m\to C_{\widetilde m}`$, so $`\widetilde\chi\gamma=\lambda\chi`$. Inner conjugation by $`c_x\in\widetilde C`$ does not change $`\widetilde\chi`$. For a $`Y_1`$-element of arbitrary top $`q`$, the $`\widetilde\chi`$-sum of the coordinate corrections $`c_xc_{q^{-1}x}^{-1}`$ telescopes to zero. The target graph equation therefore gives $`\lambda\phi(q)=r\phi(q)`$, and surjectivity of $`\phi`$ yields $`\lambda=r`$.

The $`Y_0`$ base subgroup gives $`c_x\gamma(B)c_x^{-1}=\widetilde B`$. Absorbing $`c_1`$ into $`\gamma`$ makes $`\gamma(B)=\widetilde B`$ while preserving $`\gamma(C)=\widetilde C`$ and the character equation. The image of a pure complement element of $`Y_0`$ has all base coordinates in $`\widetilde B`$, so its coordinate corrections lie in $`\widetilde B\cap\widetilde C=1`$. The residual $`c_x`$ are constant on complement orbits and impose no further existence condition. This proves necessity of (7).

Conversely, given (7), apply $`\gamma`$ at each base coordinate and relabel the regular index set by $`\beta`$. The action equation extends this map to an isomorphism $`S\to\widetilde S`$ taking the distinguished complements to one another; the first three equations carry $`Y_0,Y_1`$ to their target subgroups. Its diagonal square maps $`G,H,K,\tau`$ to their counterparts, giving a canonical-folder, and hence loop, isomorphism. This equivalence does not itself enumerate the resulting data orbits; the following application evaluates the elementary abelian A5 cases.

## Elementary abelian A5 kernels: exact counts

**Independently reviewed application of the preceding data-equivalence criterion.** Take $`T=A_5`$, $`F\cong C_p^d`$, where $`p`$ is an odd prime and $`d\ge1`$, and either of the displayed endpoint types

```math
m=3:\quad (B,C,\chi)=(C_5,A_4,A_4/V_4),\qquad
 m=5:\quad (B,C,\chi)=(A_4,C_5,\mathrm{id}).
```

The counts below classify the loops **within these displayed construction data**, conditional on the construction and the same-cyclic-map isomorphism criterion (7). They do not classify all simple Bol loops or all exact factorizations.

All ordered $`A_4,C_5`$ pairs are conjugate in $`A_5`$: $`A_5`$ is transitive on its five point-stabilizer $`A_4`$'s, and the chosen $`A_4`$ is transitive on the six Sylow-five subgroups, since its intersection with each $`D_{10}`$ normalizer has order two: the intersection order divides $`\gcd(12,10)=2`$ and is at least two by the product bound inside $`A_5`$. The common ordered-pair normalizer in $`\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(A_5)=S_5`$ is $`C_4`$: $`N_{S_5}(C_5)=\mathop{\mathrm{AGL}}\nolimits_1(5)`$ has order 20, $`N_{S_5}(A_4)=S_4`$ fixes the point of the natural action, and their intersection is the affine point stabilizer of order four. Its action on $`C_5`$ realizes all units in $`\mathbb F_5^\times`$, and its odd elements invert $`A_4/V_4\cong C_3`$. Therefore every automorphism of the complement $`C_m`$ is realized by an endpoint-pair automorphism with the SAME cyclic map required in (7).

If $`p=m`$, there are no admissible nontrivial kernels: an order-$`p`$ linear automorphism in characteristic $`p`$ is unipotent and has a nonzero fixed vector. For $`p\ne m`$, the representation is semisimple, and because $`m\in\{3,5\}`$ is prime, the fixed-point-free condition is exactly that no trivial $`C_m`$-constituent occurs.

For $`m=3`$:

* If $`p\equiv1\pmod3`$, the two nontrivial one-dimensional constituents have multiplicities $`a,b\ge0`$, $`a+b=d`$. Complement inversion interchanges them, giving exactly $`\lfloor d/2\rfloor+1`$ classes.
* If $`p\equiv2\pmod3`$, the two characters form one Frobenius orbit and one irreducible constituent of degree 2. There is exactly one class when $`2\mid d`$, and zero otherwise.

For $`m=5`$, let $`e=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\nolimits_5(p)\in\{1,2,4\}`$. The four nontrivial characters split into $`4/e`$ Frobenius orbits of size $`e`$; admissible modules have multiplicities summing to $`d/e`$, and the full $`C_4=\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(C_5)`$ rotates these orbit types. There are zero classes unless $`e\mid d`$. When $`e\mid d`$, the numbers are:

* If $`p\equiv1\pmod5`$,
  
```math
\frac{1}{4}\left(\binom{d+3}{3}+[2\mid d](d/2+1)+2[4\mid d]\right).
```

* If $`p\equiv4\pmod5`$, $`\lfloor d/4\rfloor+1`$ (and zero for odd $`d`$).
* If $`p\equiv2 \text{ or }3\pmod5`$, one when $`4\mid d`$, and zero otherwise.

Here brackets denote indicators. In the first case Burnside's lemma counts weak four-compositions of $`d`$: the half-turn fixes $`d/2+1`$ when $`d`$ is even, and each quarter-turn fixes one tuple exactly when $`4\mid d`$. In the second case the two Frobenius-orbit multiplicities are interchanged, giving unordered pairs summing to $`d/2`$. No finite census is needed for these counts.

The construction gives loop order $`mp^d60^{p^d}`$. Different $`m,p,d`$ give nonisomorphic loops within this family: the recovery in the preceding section determines $`m=|S/S'|`$ and $`|F|=p^d`$ from the number of simple factors in a minimal socle block. These conclusions retain the source qualifications of the construction and (7).

## Alternating-map orbits within the family

**Independently reviewed continuation, conditional on the pinned inputs.** This section uses the reviewed construction at [the source revision](https://github.com/Mira-Cult/math-wip/commit/c9b83d37421da818e57ef69227bd04293862540d) and the explicit isomorphism criterion above, reviewed together with this section at the revision recorded below. Fix an odd prime `p≡1 (mod 3)` and a primitive cube root `ω∈F_p`. Let `V` and `W` be finite-dimensional vector spaces over `F_p`, with `V≠0`, and let

 `β:Λ²V→W`

be surjective with zero radical, meaning `{v∈V:β(v,V)=0}={0}`. These conditions force `W≠0`: otherwise the radical would be all of `V`. Define `F_β=V⊕W` with multiplication

 `(v,w)(v′,w′)=(v+v′, w+w′+β(v,v′)/2)`.

Bilinearity proves associativity; the identity is `(0,0)`, inverse is negation, and the commutator is `(0,β(v,v′))`. The group has exponent `p` and nilpotency class two. Surjectivity and the zero-radical condition give

 `[F_β,F_β]=Z(F_β)=W`,  `F_β/Z(F_β)≅V`.

The map `α(v,w)=(ωv,ω²w)` is an automorphism of order three. Its nonidentity powers have no nontrivial fixed points, since neither `ω` nor `ω²` is 1. Thus it supplies an allowed Frobenius kernel and complement in the reviewed construction. With fixed data `T=A5`, `B=C5`, `C=A4`, `χ:A4→C3` the quotient, and this action, the resulting simple proper non-Bruck loop has order

 `3 p^(dim V+dim W) · 60^[p^(dim V+dim W)]`.

For two such alternating maps over the same `F_p`, a group isomorphism `F_β→F_{β′}` induces `F_p`-linear isomorphisms `A:V→V′` and `B:W→W′`, since `W=Z(F_β)` is characteristic and the center and quotient are elementary abelian. Commutators imply

 `B β(v,v′)=β′(Av,Av′)`.

Possible central shifts in the group isomorphism do not change these induced maps or this equation. Conversely every pair `(A,B)` satisfying the equation gives the group isomorphism `(v,w)↦(Av,Bw)`, and this isomorphism commutes with the displayed `α` actions.

Criterion (7) permits either automorphism of the cyclic complement `C3`, but inversion cannot occur here: on the nonzero quotient `F_β/Z(F_β)` the source action is scalar `ω`, while the inverse target action is scalar `ω²`, and an `F_p`-linear isomorphism cannot intertwine these distinct scalars. Thus the cyclic isomorphism must be the identity. With the fixed `A5,C5,A4,χ` data, the identity automorphism of `A5` and any pair `(A,B)` above give the converse in (7). Conditional on that criterion, the assignment `β↦` its constructed loop therefore induces a bijection from `GL(V)×GL(W)`-equivalence classes of surjective radical-zero alternating maps, allowing all finite dimensions, onto the loop-isomorphism classes in this displayed subfamily. Group isomorphism already identifies `dim V` and `dim W`, so allowing different dimensions introduces no further identifications.

This is an explicit equivalence-problem embedding. It does not assert formal wildness, a computational hardness bound, or a classification of loops outside this subfamily. The new deduction and the isomorphism criterion it invokes remain subject to independent exact-source review.

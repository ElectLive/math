# Bipartite recognition of exact-factorization witnesses in paired semisimple folders

**Mira Cult**

**Independently reviewed continuation of [0323:C0010](../../claims/0010.md).** Let Q be a finite simple nonassociative Bol loop with its faithful generated canonical folder (G,H,K,τ), and suppose G=∏_{i=1}^m(T_i×T_i), for finite nonabelian simple T_i, with τ exchanging the two factors of each pair (after identifying each through τ). The canonical marked set τK is conjugacy-invariant and contains τ, and every outside involution in the pair-swapping extension is conjugate to τ. Thus τK is the full outside class, as in the [paired-envelope source](radical-free-paired-simple-envelopes.md); this argument applies to unequal simple types too. Consequently K is the full anti-diagonal, |K|=∏|T_i| and |G|=|K|². The criterion (*) and its exact-factorization interpretation are from [the intrinsic criterion](exact-factorization-criterion.md), independently reviewed at [the source revision](https://github.com/Mira-Cult/math-wip/commit/e3a39be22fe1390d3717bdeee8f81d82f6817aef), with its recorded C0001/C0007 dependencies. The result is an intrinsic test for that construction route inside this paired model; it is not an exhaustion theorem for simple Bol folders.

Let Ω be the set of the 2m simple coordinate factors. For A⊆Ω let π_A be the coordinate projection embedded back in G. Say A splits H when π_A(H)≤H. Then H=π_A(H)×π_{Ω\A}(H), so A splits iff its complement does. If A and B split, π_A and π_B preserve H; hence π_{A∩B}=π_Aπ_B preserves H. The splitting subsets form a finite Boolean algebra, whose atoms partition Ω into unique nonempty indecomposable blocks. A subset splits H exactly when it is a union of these atoms. Coordinates on which H projects trivially are included: they form singleton splitting atoms, and must not be discarded.

Form a multigraph Γ whose vertices are these atoms, with one edge for each τ-pair of simple coordinates, joining their respective atoms; loops and parallel edges are allowed. Then Q admits an intrinsic witness N for (*) if and only if Γ is bipartite. More precisely, all witnesses N correspond bijectively to proper two-colorings of Γ: select in N every coordinate belonging to an atom of one color. In particular, if Γ is loop-free and bipartite with c connected components, there are 2^c witnesses as actual subgroups; global color reversal gives τ(N) for N. The count is of witnesses, not of isomorphism classes of factorizations or loops.

Proof. Every normal subgroup of a direct product of nonabelian simple groups is a product of coordinate factors. If N satisfies N∩τN=1, it contains at most one coordinate of each τ-pair. Since [G:N]=|K| and |G|=|K|², |N|=|K|. Its order is at most the product of one |T_i| per pair, and equality forces exactly one from every pair, with no argument based on distribution of prime divisors. Consequently G=N×τN. Under this decomposition,

 HN∩HτN = π_N(H) × π_{τN}(H).

Thus the last equality in (*) holds exactly when the selected coordinate set splits H, equivalently is a union of atoms. Having exactly one coordinate per τ-pair means precisely that every edge of Γ joins atoms of opposite colors. Conversely every proper two-coloring selects such a normal N and verifies all three conditions in (*). A loop prohibits a coloring, so no witness exists in that case.

A canonical-folder isomorphism carries simple direct factors to simple direct factors, carries H to H′, and intertwines τ with τ′, so it sends splitting atoms to splitting atoms and preserves τ-edges. The graph up to isomorphism, its bipartiteness, and the set of witnesses are therefore loop-isomorphism invariants within the stated canonical model. Parallel edges record multiplicity of coordinate pairs. Independent reversal on connected components gives all colorings; whether different witnesses lead to isomorphic factorizations is not decided by the graph alone.

This result applies in particular to any reviewed mixed-type path folder once its H-splitting blocks have been computed from the actual stabilizer, including correlated endpoint groups. It would be invalid to infer the graph merely from a support picture that does not prove a direct-product decomposition of H. No claim is made here for its bipartiteness without that calculation.

## Isotopy invariance of the witness route

For every finite simple nonassociative Bol loop, not just the paired semisimple model, the existence of a witness for (*) is invariant under isotopy, subject to the reviewed principal-isotope interface in [the Bruck-isotopy source](exact-factorization-criterion.md#bruck-isotopy-as-a-stabilizer-conjugacy-test) at [the source revision](https://github.com/Mira-Cult/math-wip/commit/ea836a5f328d6bc52913aac8fd1841a87072f5f8). Indeed a principal isotope has folder (G,H,k^-1K) with k∈K and canonical inversion τ_k=Inn(k^-1)τ. For any normal N◁G, τ_k(N)=τ(N); the cardinality of the section remains |K|, and H is unchanged. Hence all three conditions (*) hold for precisely the same normal subgroups N in both presentations. Every loop isotope is isomorphic to a principal isotope under the reviewed interface, so the exact-factorization membership characterized by (*) is isotopy invariant. This does not assert that the explicit factorizations or their parameterizations are the same. In the paired model, it follows in particular that failure of the bipartite test excludes every exact-factorization isotope. The shifted τ_k need not retain the original visible pair-swapping normal form, but normality makes the witness argument independent of that issue.

## Connectedness and bridges

Independently reviewed 0323:C0010 continuation of the preceding graph theorem at [the source revision](https://github.com/Mira-Cult/math-wip/commit/9d1c0af909166459fbbeb87b881c28dff353aa3f), under its exact paired semisimple, canonical-folder and intrinsic witness hypotheses and the reviewed C0001 normal-quotient interface. Γ has splitting atoms as vertices and τ-pairs as edges, including loops/multiplicity.

### Simplicity forces connectedness

Suppose Γ has a nonempty proper union C of connected components. Let G_C be the product of all coordinate factors in those vertices, and G_D its complementary coordinate subgroup. Because no τ-edge crosses, both subgroups are τ-stable and K=K_C×K_D, with K_C,K_D their full paired anti-diagonals. Because C is a union of splitting atoms, H=H_C×H_D where H_C=H∩G_C and H_D=H∩G_D. The original transversal property shows K_C is a transversal to H_C: injectivity follows from H_C≤H, and |K_C| is at most [G_C:H_C]. Likewise |K_D|≤[G_D:H_D]. Their product is |K|=[G:H]=[G_C:H_C][G_D:H_D], so both inequalities are equalities. Thus the coordinate projection of K to G_C has size exactly [G_C:H_C]. As G_D is nontrivial and proper normal in G, the reviewed simplicity criterion requires this size to be strictly larger than [G_C:H_C], a contradiction. Hence Γ is connected. Equivalently the direct product of coordinate folders gives a nontrivial direct-product loop, but the cardinality argument does not assume that conclusion. The graph has at least one edge, and each component under consideration contains a nontrivial coordinate factor; H_C or H_D is allowed to be trivial.

For a singleton-coordinate atom {v}, H projects onto a proper subgroup A<T_v. If A=T_v, the embedded projection is T_v≤H because the atom splits H, contradicting core-freeness. A may be trivial. Its vertex has degree one because each coordinate is incident with exactly its single τ-pair edge. Conversely every degree-one atom contains exactly one coordinate, counting loops twice; so degree-one vertices are precisely the singleton-coordinate atoms.

### What a bridge gives

Let e be a bridge of the connected multigraph, joining distinct vertices. Delete e and let U,V be the two nonempty vertex sides. Let G_U,G_V be their coordinate products. Each is normal in G, and H=H_U×H_V because each side is a union of atoms. Exactly one τ-pair crosses, with boundary factors T_U,T_V≅T; all other pairs lie wholly on one side. Thus the original anti-diagonal section has the concrete form

 K={(k_U(a),k_V(a^-1)): a∈T, k_U(a) ranges independently over all internal anti-diagonal pairs on U, and k_V(a^-1) ranges independently over all internal anti-diagonal pairs on V},

where the notation fixes the boundary coordinate to the displayed value. Equivalently, K is a fiber product over the full boundary value T, not generally a product K_U×K_V. In each quotient by the complementary side, the image of K has size |T| times the product of the orders of the internal pair factors; the reviewed simplicity criterion forces its size to exceed the corresponding quotient stabilizer index. These are necessary strict density constraints, not a quotient-folder or exact-factorization assertion.

This decomposition isolates the exact obstruction to splitting at a bridge: the stabilizer splits across U,V, but τ exchanges the two boundary factors, and their K-values are coupled by inversion. Deleting e therefore does not give two independent canonical paired folders, nor by itself produce a witness (*). A valid witness exists exactly when the entire atom graph is bipartite, as shown in the preceding note; bridges alone do not decide bipartiteness because cycles may occur within either side. Conversely, an odd cycle with a bridge attached is a graph-theoretic countercontrol to any assertion that a bridge automatically yields bipartiteness; it is not asserted to be realized by a simple folder.

When Γ is connected and bipartite, its two colorings are complementary and give exactly two witness subgroups N and τN. If Γ is connected and contains an odd cycle or loop, there is no witness, even when it has bridges. These statements identify witnesses, not their resulting factorization isomorphism types. No general bridge existence/exclusion theorem or unrestricted classification is claimed.

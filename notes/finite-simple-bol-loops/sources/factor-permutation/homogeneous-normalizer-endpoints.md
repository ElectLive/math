# Every nilpotent transitive socle action is a 2-group, with unpermuted and entirely paired marks

**The local exclusion and its application.** Let $`G`$ be the faithful canonical left group of a finite simple proper left Bol loop and $`H`$ its actual point stabilizer. Suppose its **whole** socle is $`S=T^\Omega`$ for a nonabelian finite simple $`T`$, $`n=|\Omega|\ge2`$, and the factor-permutation image $`C`$ is nilpotent and transitive. Then:
* $`C`$ is a 2-group, and $`n`$ is a power of two.
* Some physical outside mark has identity coordinate permutation; some other physical outside mark exchanges all coordinates in pairs.
* With $`U_i=\pi_i(H\cap S)`$, no $`N_{T_i}(U_i)`$ can factor its simple group with any automorphic conjugate. More strongly, in the [rectangular incidence construction](rectangular-stabilizer-incidence.md) **every** target color pattern of the signed orbit of the actual local-subgroup tuple is realized by a selected mark with identity factor permutation.

This closes all four homogeneous-factorization possibilities in the separate rectangular reduction. The action need not be regular, the true outer kernel is arbitrary, and surjectivity of $`H\to G/S`$ is not assumed. The conclusions exclude neither all transitive 2-groups nor nonnilpotent actions; this is **not** the unrestricted classification. The proof also gives a local exclusion with no homogeneous-factorization assumption.

## An almost-simple projection cannot hide a common real class

Use the [whole-socle projection theorem](../../manuscripts/0105-nilpotent-socle-stabilizer-projections.md), which gives $`H\to C`$ and equivalent $`1<U_i<T_i`$ under actual transports through $`H`$. Work in such frames, with common projection $`U`$. Put $`L=|T|,\ o=|\operatorname{Out}(T)|`$.

**Local exclusion.** It is impossible that all three of the following hold:
1. $`U`$ is almost simple with nonabelian simple socle $`R`$;
2. a nonidentity $`\operatorname{Aut}(R)`$-invariant $`R`$-conjugacy class lies inside a single $`\operatorname{Aut}(T)`$-invariant **real** $`T`$-conjugacy class (real means inverse-closed);
3. $`[T:U]\ge3o/2`$.

Write $`A=H\cap S`$. Its perfect derived residual $`W=A^{(\infty)}`$, the eventual term of the finite derived series, projects onto $`U^{(\infty)}=R`$ at every site. Indeed derived terms commute with genuine surjections, $`R`$ is perfect and the quotient is solvable by the Schreier theorem. Hence $`W\le R^\Omega`$ is subdirect and is a product of full twisted diagonal strips; the elementary Goursat argument and recovery of the intrinsic partition by pair projections are given in [the projection theorem](../../manuscripts/0105-nilpotent-socle-stabilizer-projections.md#a-true-normal-layer-strips-in-a-nonregular-transitive-action). It is $`H`$-invariant since $`W`$ is characteristic in $`A`$, so the strip partition is invariant under its full permutation image $`C`$.

The product $`R^\Omega`$ is **not** being assumed normal in $`E=G\rtimes\langle t\rangle`$. The following collision is made in the actual ambient socle $`S`$. For any selected signed involution $`\lambda\in\Lambda=tK`$, join sites that lie in one strip or form an exchanged pair. A connected component with no fixed site is impossible. In each strip of that component put an element of the class in (2) together with its specified strip twists; use identity on the other strips. This gives a single $`1\ne w\in W`$. All its active coordinates are in the same real, automorphism-invariant ambient $`T`$-class. On each exchanged pair, the desired entries are consequently inverse-conjugate through its actual arrow. More explicitly, we can choose an arrow $`g`$ in the given inner coset with $`g w_i^{-1}g^{-1}=w_j`$; then $`g`$ and $`g w_i`$, with their inverse return arrows in opposite sites, factor the corresponding entries (order the two conjugates to give $`w_i`$ at the chosen site). For inactive sites use identical arrows in both conjugates. These choices on disjoint cycles assemble two genuine $`S`$-conjugates of $`\lambda`$, whose product is $`w`$. This contradicts the actual left-folder avoidance $`H\cap\Lambda^2=\{1\}`$. The order of the class elements is immaterial; realness, not necessarily order two, is needed here.

For clarity, we recall how this fixed-site statement forces **one** strip and odd degree, rather than invoking a normal-layer conclusion on the nonnormal product. Choose a site fixed by the selected $`t`$, use $`\Omega=C/P`$ rooted there, and write $`\theta(c)=tct^{-1}`$ on $`C`$. The origin strip is $`M/P`$, $`P\le M\le C`$. Each mark can be conjugated by $`G`$ to fix the origin, so its coordinate label there is $`pt`$ for some $`p\in P`$, acting by $`\eta=\operatorname{inn}(p)\theta`$ on coset representatives. With $`V=\langle M,\theta M\rangle=\langle M,\eta M\rangle`$, the component of $`xP`$ is $`xV/P\cup\eta(x)V/P`$: strip steps are right multiplications by $`M`$, and conjugating such steps by the involution gives right multiplications by $`\eta M`$. If the two pieces were distinct the involution would exchange them without a fixed site. Thus $`x^{-1}\eta(x)\in V`$ for all $`x`$, both for $`p=1`$ and for every actual signed origin label. Subtraction in the group gives $`x^{-1}px\in V`$ in the latter case: use $`x^{-1}p\theta(x)p^{-1}`$, $`x^{-1}\theta(x)`$ and $`p\in V`$. The coordinate images of original translations are generated as elements by $`t[x(pt)x^{-1}]=\theta(xp)x^{-1}`$; these lie in $`V`$ by the preceding two statements (use the distinguished-mark condition at $`(xp)^{-1}`$). Hence $`C=V`$. Since $`\theta`$ inverts the generator images $`K`$, it is inversion on $`C_{\rm ab}`$, giving

```math
C=M[C,C],\qquad C=P[C,C]C^2 .
```

For the second equality reduce each same displayed translation modulo commutators and squares, where its image is that of $`p`$. If $`M<C`$ it lies in a normal maximal subgroup of nilpotent $`C`$, contradicting the first equation. There is just one strip. If $`[C:P]`$ were even, an index-two normal subgroup containing $`P`$ would be obtained by extending a maximal subgroup containing its proper Sylow 2-projection in $`C_2`$ by all other Sylow factors, contradicting the second equation. Thus $`n`$ is odd, at least three. Faithfulness and transitivity give $`|C|`$ odd: the equal orbit lengths of its normal Sylow 2-group divide $`n`$, so it acts trivially.

In abstract coordinates of $`R`$, $`W`$ is now one full diagonal. Each coordinate projection $`W\to R_i`$ is an isomorphism. If $`a\in A`$ is identity at any site, its conjugation on $`W`$ is identity by this injective projection; at every site it therefore centralizes the local $`R_i`$. But $`C_{U_i}(R_i)=1`$ since $`U_i`$ is almost simple. Thus $`A\to U_i`$ is injective as well as onto, and

```math
|A|=|U|.
```

The strip twists have not been extended to automorphisms of $`T`$; no hypothesis on $`C_{\operatorname{Aut}(T)}(R)`$ was used.

## The sharp rank-three end of the mass estimate

We spell out the counting inputs to avoid treating this as full-top surjectivity. By the [uniform simple-type bounds and top estimates](../../manuscripts/0105-nilpotent-socle-stabilizer-projections.md#counting-the-actual-stabilizer-fiber-not-the-entire-outer-group), $`I=|\{g\in\operatorname{Aut}(T):g^2=1\}|<L/2`$, identity included, and $`o<L^{1/3}`$. In particular $`\sqrt{Lo}<L/2`$ since $`L\ge60`$. For odd transitive nilpotent $`C`$ one has $`|C|\le3^{(n-1)/2}`$: its Sylow groups act faithfully on coset factors of degrees $`n_p`$ with product $`n`$, hence have orders at most $`p^{(n_p-1)/(p-1)}`$. Here $`p\ge3,\ \sum(n_p-1)\le n-1`$. Take any maximal $`C_0\ge P`$; it is normal of odd prime index $`p`$, and $`\theta`$ preserves it by inversion on $`C_{\rm ab}`$. Each permutation in $`tC`$ induces an affine inversion on the $`p`$ equal blocks, fixing exactly one block in the quotient. Its number $`s`$ of fixed sites is consequently at most $`n/p\le n/3`$.

Over any one such permutation there are at most $`I^s(Lo)^{(n-s)/2}`$ physical marked actions on $`S`$: choose any square-one local automorphism at a fixed site, and any one local automorphism per exchange whose return is forced. The map on the signed coset is injective, since the ratio of two elements acting identically lies in $`G`$ and centralizes its whole centerless socle, and $`C_G(S)=1`$. Summing with $`I<L/2`$, $`\sqrt{Lo}<L/2`$ and $`s\le n/3`$ gives

```math
|K|<3^{(n-1)/2}(L^2o/2)^{n/3}.
```

Indeed the bound after replacing $`I`$ by $`L/2`$ increases with $`s`$; equality at $`s=n/3>0`$ is strict by the actual $`I`$-inequality, and all smaller $`s`$ give a strictly smaller bound. The true transversal, without $`HS=G`$, gives $`|K||A|=[G:SH]L^n\ge L^n`$. With $`m'=[T:U]`$, this requires $`m'L^{n-1}\le |K|`$. But

```math
\frac{m'L^{n-1}}{3^{(n-1)/2}(L^2o/2)^{n/3}}
 =\frac{2m'}{3o}
   \left(\frac{(2L/o)^{1/3}}{\sqrt3}\right)^{n-3}\ge1 .
```

The last base is greater than one by the stated bound on $`o`$, and (3) of the local exclusion includes equality $`m'=3o/2`$; the strict estimate is essential at rank three in that endpoint. This proves the local exclusion.

## All four homogeneous possibilities contain such a class

Suppose $`T=N N^\alpha`$, $`N=N_T(U)`$, for some $`\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(T)`$. Here $`1<U<T`$ and so $`N<T`$. Use Baddeley and Praeger, [*On classifying all full factorisations and multiple-factorisations of the finite almost simple groups*, Theorem 1.1, Table I and the accompanying notes (ii), (iv), pp. 133–135](https://doi.org/10.1006/jabr.1997.7275). This factorization is full because the equal factor orders cover every prime divisor of $`|T|`$. The equal-order, isomorphic entries are the four types already identified in the [rectangular theorem](rectangular-stabilizer-incidence.md). We use the table itself here for their **embeddings and indices**, not just the four abstract isotypes. Its equivalence is simultaneous ambient automorphism and independent inner conjugation of the two factors. Put $`R=\operatorname{Soc}(N)`$; the nontrivial normal subgroup $`U\lhd N`$ satisfies $`R\le U\le N`$, hence $`U`$ is almost simple with socle $`R`$.

* $`T=A_6,\ N=R=A_5`$. Each group has just one involution class, and these elements in $`T`$ are double transpositions. The local index is six and $`o=4`$.
* $`T=M_{12},\ N=R=M_{11}`$. $`M_{11}`$ has one involution class. The two involution classes of $`M_{12}`$ have different centralizer orders 240 and 192, so each is automorphism-invariant. See the [ATLAS $`M_{12}`$ table](https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/v3/spor/M12/) and [ATLAS $`M_{11}`$ table](https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/v3/spor/M11/). Here the index is twelve and $`o=2`$.
* $`T=\operatorname{Sp}_4(q)`$ with $`q=2^f\ge4,\ R=\operatorname{Sp}_2(q^2),\ N=R.2`$; the different-order alternative in Table I does not give a pair of isomorphic factors. We show that the unique involution class of $`R`$ lands in a distinguished class of $`T`$ even without knowing the embedding. Write $`x=1+D`$ for an ambient involution, $`D^2=0`$. Then $`D`$ is self-adjoint for the alternating form since $`x=x^{-1}`$; its image is totally isotropic. Nonzero rank one gives the transvections, one class of size $`q^4-1`$: each projective direction gives $`q-1`$ choices, and every nonzero scalar is a square. For rank two, its image is a Lagrangian plane $`P`$, its kernel also $`P`$; in a symplectic basis starting with $`P`$, $`D:V/P\to P`$ is an invertible symmetric $`2`$-by-$`2`$ matrix. Its type under the full $`\operatorname{GL}(P)`$ is alternating or nonalternating. In the latter case split off a vector of nonzero norm; its one-dimensional orthogonal complement is nondegenerate, and both diagonal coefficients can be scaled to one. Among the $`q^3`$ symmetric matrices exactly $`q^2`$ are singular: in each pair of diagonal coefficients the off-diagonal has a unique square root of their product. Exactly $`q-1`$ invertibles are alternating. The number of Lagrangians is $`(q+1)(q^2+1)`$ (double-count incidences with projective points). The three class sizes are therefore $`q^4-1,\ q^4-1,\ (q^4-1)(q^2-1)`$. In $`R=\operatorname{SL}_2(q^2)`$ there are exactly $`q^4-1`$ involutions, all in one class. If that class lay in either smaller $`T`$-class it would exhaust the latter, whose normal closure is $`T`$, contradicting $`R<T`$. It lies in the unique largest involution class, which is $`\operatorname{Aut}(T)`$-invariant. Here $`[T:N]=q^2(q^2-1)/2`$ and $`o=2f`$.
* $`T=\operatorname{P}\Omega_8^+(q)`$, $`N=R=\Omega_7(q)`$ (with projective interpretation); for $`q=2`$ keep the $`\operatorname{Sp}_6(2)`$-versus-$`\operatorname{Sp}_6(2)`$ possibility of Table I line 6, not its unequal-order entries. The applicable factors are ambient automorphic transports of the nonsingular-point orthogonal subgroup. In its natural model take a hyperbolic basis $`e_i,f_i`$, $`1\le i\le4`$, and a nonsingular vector $`e_4+c f_4`$, $`c\ne0`$. The copy of $`\operatorname{SL}_3(q)`$ acting on the first three $`e`$'s and contragrediently on the corresponding $`f`$'s fixes this vector and lies in the derived orthogonal subgroup there (it is perfect). Its elementary transvection $`I+sE_{12}`$, $`s\ne0`$, is the root element $`x_{e_1-e_2}(s)`$ both in the ambient $`D_4`$ root system and in the $`B_3`$ orthogonal root system on the perpendicular space. In characteristic two the seven-dimensional perpendicular space has its usual one-dimensional polar radical; this orthogonal long-root class becomes the short-root class in $`\operatorname{Sp}_6(q)`$ on the symplectic quotient, not the transvection class there. See Kantor, [*Subgroups of classical groups generated by long root elements*, §2](https://pages.uoregon.edu/kantor/PAPERS/longrootelt.pdf).

  In either group the Weyl group is transitive on roots of the chosen length, and the coroot $`e_2-e_3`$ pairs by $`-1`$ with $`e_1-e_2`$, giving all nonzero parameters by torus conjugation. The class is real because inversion changes $`s`$ to $`-s`$. More explicitly on the shared $`\operatorname{SL}_3`$ this parameter rescaling is already furnished by diagonal matrices of determinant one. By Steinberg's [automorphism theorem, §§3.2–3.6](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1960-054-6), diagonal and field automorphisms preserve these classes, as do the ambient diagram automorphisms (all $`D_4`$ roots have the same length); the rank-three $`B_3`$ or $`C_3`$ has no length-interchanging diagram automorphism. Inner conjugation is built in. Thus the indicated intrinsic $`R`$-class is automorphism-stable and lands in one real automorphism-stable $`T`$-class. The claim transports to the other Table I embeddings, including triality transports, without requiring triality to preserve the chosen point stabilizer. In this row $`[T:N]=q^3(q^4-1)/(2,q-1)`$, where $`(2,q-1)`$ denotes a greatest common divisor, and $`o\le24 f`$ for $`q=p^f`$.

The displayed indices follow from the named factor orders; the outer data are also in [Lucchini–Marion–Tracey, Proposition 3.11 and Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2019.43). In every row $`[T:U]\ge[T:N]\ge3o/2`$. For the symplectic row compare with $`3f`$, using $`f\le q-1`$. For the orthogonal row $`q^3(q^4-1)/2\ge60(q-1)\ge36f`$ for $`q\ge2`$. The alternating row is exactly the endpoint that must allow equality. The local exclusion now contradicts $`T=NN^\alpha`$ in all four cases.

## Back to actual signed marks

If any distinct color pair were flat, the final section of the [rectangular theorem](rectangular-stabilizer-incidence.md#a-flat-pair-forces-a-genuine-homogeneous-factorization-inside-the-simple-group) would give precisely the impossible automorphic factorization of $`T`$ just excluded. Equal colors are already nonflat. Hence, for **every** target color pattern in $`X'=tG u`$, the rectangular incidence test supplies an actual selected mark in its specific block that permutes no coordinate. Consequently the permutation cosets of $`E`$ and $`G`$ agree; conjugation by $`t`$ on $`C`$ is inner but induces inversion on $`C_{\rm ab}`$. Nilpotence then makes $`C`$ a 2-group, since each nontrivial odd Sylow direct factor has a nontrivial abelian quotient. Finally if all selected marks fixed a coordinate, their now genuine $`C`$-labels would all belong to conjugates of a point stabilizer. A proper point stabilizer in a transitive 2-group lies in a normal maximal subgroup, which would contain all those labels, including $`t`$, and hence the images of $`t\Lambda=K`$ generating $`C`$. This is impossible. There is an all-paired mark, necessarily distinct from an unpermuted one.

The [author controls](../code/verify_homogeneous_strip_endpoints.py) exercise the actual Mathieu involutions, the two kinds of symplectic rank-two forms and all embedded rank-one involutions over a quadratic extension, simultaneous orthogonal roots and parameter inversion, and the strict numerical rank-three boundary. They test specific mechanisms, not the classification by enumeration. An unrestricted list of finite simple proper left Bol loops, including a structural treatment of all remaining transitive 2-group cases, is not obtained.

# Primitive almost-simple actions on nondegenerate classical subspaces (candidate)

**Mira Cult**

This provisional interface for [C0010](../../claims/0010.md) extends the normalizer argument of [classical-point-normalizers.md](classical-point-normalizers.md) and uses the natural-action geometric exclusions in [classical-nondegenerate-subspaces.md](classical-nondegenerate-subspaces.md). It introduces no claim ID. The interface and applications have the scoped review recorded below. The scope is a specified natural T-set, not every action of the abstract group T.

Let V be a nondegenerate finite classical space of dimension n and Ω the set of its nondegenerate k-dimensional subspaces. Suppose G≤Sym(Ω) is faithful, primitive and almost simple, with socle T, and the restricted T-action is the stated natural action. In the unitary case take T=PSU_n(q), n≥3, q≥3, over F_{q²}, 1≤k<n. In the symplectic case take T=PSp_n(q), n even, 2≤k<n even and n≥6, over F_q. Then, in either case, G is contained in the corresponding natural projective semilinear similitude action, namely PΓU_n(q) or PΓSp_n(q). Moreover k≠n/2. Here the symplectic range n≥6 includes the case k=4,n=6; for the geometric exclusion below, min(k,n−k)≥4 forces n≥8.

Proof. The socle T is nonabelian simple in these ranges. It is transitive on Ω. Witt's lemma gives transitivity of the full isometry group. In the symplectic case this is Sp(V), whose projective image is T. In the unitary case, after an isometry sends U to W, correct its determinant by an isometry stabilizing W: a nondegenerate one-dimensional summand of W or W⊥ carries arbitrary norm-one determinant, so the correcting block has the inverse determinant. Thus SU(V), and hence T, is transitive.

Put H=G_U and K=T_U. K is proper, since Ω has more than one member, and nontrivial: it contains the relevant nontrivial isometry group acting on a nondegenerate summand (or its projective image). Since H normalizes K, it normalizes N_T(K). If N_T(K)>K, then HN_T(K)>H, so primitivity gives HN_T(K)=G; intersecting with T gives N_T(K)=T, impossible because K is nontrivial proper and T is simple. Hence N_T(K)=K. The centralizer of transitive T in Sym(Ω) is N_T(K)/K=1, so the conjugation map from N_Sym(Ω)(T) to Aut(T) is injective.

Kleidman–Liebeck, Theorems 2.1.3–2.1.4 and Table 2.1.B, pp.15–16, give Aut(PSU_n(q))=PΓU_n(q) for n≥3,q≥3. In the symplectic case they give Aut(PSp_n(q))=PΓSp_n(q) for even n≥6: the exceptional graph automorphism in their theorem occurs only in dimension 4 in even characteristic. Every such semilinear similitude preserves the set Ω of nondegenerate k-subspaces. Given g∈G, choose a natural semilinear representative inducing the same automorphism of T; by injectivity of conjugation on the permutation normalizer, its permutation of Ω is g. Thus G lies in the stated natural action. The field automorphisms, odd-characteristic diagonal similitudes and, in the unitary case, the graph-field involution are included.

If n=2k, orthogonal complementation U↦U⊥ is a fixed-point-free involution on Ω commuting with T: U≠U⊥ because U is nonzero and nondegenerate. This contradicts C_Sym(Ω)(T)=1. Therefore no primitive almost-simple action with the specified T-set exists in the middle dimension. This is stronger than assuming a particular extension respects complement pairs: it follows from transitivity of T and primitivity of G alone. ∎

Conditional application of the separately reviewed geometric arguments: in the symplectic case, if min(k,n−k)≥4, those arguments exclude any loop whose multiplication group lies in PΓSp_n(q). In the unitary case, if q≥3 and min(k,n−k)≥2, they exclude any loop whose multiplication group lies in PΓU_n(q): the min=2 case additionally uses the reviewed unitary nonorthogonality obstruction, and min≥3 uses the subspace completion argument. For k>n/2, complementation identifies the natural k-action with the (n−k)-action. Thus, under the interface and those results, no loop can have such a primitive almost-simple group as its full multiplication group. This says nothing new for the symplectic two-plane actions or unitary point actions, and does not assert that all subspace actions are primitive.

## Which alternating groups are ambivalent

A permutation's S_n-class splits into two A_n-classes exactly when its cycle lengths, including fixed points, are distinct odd numbers. Indeed the centralizer contains an odd permutation if there is an even cycle or two equal odd cycles; otherwise it is generated by powers of the disjoint odd cycles and contains only even permutations. The orbit-stabilizer formula then gives the splitting criterion.

A nonsplit class is inverse-stable. For a split class with k cycles, a conjugator reversing each cycle has sign (−1)^((n−k)/2). All other conjugators to the inverse differ by an even centralizer element. Thus the class is inverse-stable exactly when this sign is positive.

For odd n≡3 mod4, the n-cycle gives a negative sign. For odd n≡1 mod4 and n≥9, the distinct odd partition 1+3+(n−4) does so. For n≡0 mod4 use 1+(n−1). For n≡2 mod4 and n≥18 use 1+3+5+(n−9). This excludes every simple alternating degree except 5,6,10,14. At degree5 the only distinct-odd partition is 5, with positive reversal sign. At degrees6,10,14 every distinct-odd partition has exactly two parts, since four distinct positive odd integers sum to at least16; its sign is again positive. This proves the exact ambivalence list without a character-table calculation.

For degrees10 and14 use the following two stated primary classification inputs, whose detailed locators and inspection limits are in [REFERENCES](../foundations/references.md#paired-alternating-classification-sources).

Wiegold–Williamson, §2 Theorem A and its following remarks, classifies exact factorizations of A_n. Case I has factors A_(n−k) and a sharply k-transitive even group, 1≤k≤5. The allowed degrees in cases II–III omit10 and14: the variable rows require q≡3 mod4, so q+1 cannot equal either degree. In case I, k=1 is impossible because a regular-group involution would have n/2 odd transpositions. Neither degree is a prime power, excluding k=2. The k=3 degree criterion admits10=3²+1 but excludes14=13+1; k=4,5 have degrees11,12. The source states uniqueness of the even sharply 3-transitive degree10 group M10 in its standard representation. Once M10 is fixed, it is transitive on ordered triples, so all pointwise A7 stabilizers form one simultaneous endpoint orbit. Hence A10 has one product-folder class, and A14 has none.

Galt–Gubarev, arXiv:2404.14078v1, §7 Theorem4(1), supplies the **necessary** degree condition for a nonsplitting RB map: n=q^m or q^m+1, where q is a prime power, every prime divisor of m divides q−1, q≡3 mod4 and m≡2 mod4. Degree14 is absent, so A14 has no remaining folder. Degree10=3²+1 qualifies, and Theorem4(2)(c) gives at most one nonsplitting Q(T)-equivalence class there. We use only this necessity and upper bound; the explicit construction below establishes existence. In particular we do not use the literally misstated reverse implication in Theorem4(1), which would wrongly make every RB map at an allowed degree nonsplitting. The source's proof assumes a nonsplitting map, and its §7 k=3(a) discussion addresses the degree10 case. Its classification-dependent group inputs are cited dependencies, not independently reproved here.

The reviewed correspondence turns the last upper bound into an isomorphism upper bound. Product and nonproduct stabilizers cannot be identified by the canonical simultaneous-automorphism/swap action. The explicit representatives below therefore finish the two A10 classes under the stated inputs.

## Explicit representatives at degree ten

Work in T=A10 on {1,…,10}. Let A=A7 on {1,…,7}, and put

    Y=⟨A,(1,2)(9,10)⟩,
    u=(1,9,6,7,5)(2,10,3,8,4),
    v=(1,10,7,8)(2,9,4,6),
    Z=⟨u,v⟩,       D=Z′.

Restriction to the first seven points identifies Y with S7: it fixes8, preserves {9,10}, and evenness determines its action there. Thus A◁Y has index2. The finite certificate linked below checks, in the actual permutation groups,

    |T|=1814400, |A|=2520, |Y|=5040,
    |Z|=720, |D|=360, |Y∩Z|=2,
    A∩Z=1,       Y∩Z≤D,

as well as all ambient memberships, normalities, simplicity and 3-transitivity. Since |Z|=10·9·8, the latter is sharp; the group-classification input above identifies this concrete even group as the standard M10. The construction itself needs its checked properties, not that name.

The orders and A∩Z=1 give the exact factorization T=AZ. Thus H_split=A×Z with full anti-diagonal K in T² gives the first loop. Let ν:Y→Y/A≅C2 and ε:Z→Z/D≅C2 be the unique quotient identifications, and put

    H_corr={(z,y)∈Z×Y : ε(z)=ν(y)}.

The nontrivial element r of Y∩Z lies in D, so ε(r)=0. The independently reviewed [nonproduct construction](paired-simple-nonproduct-construction.md), exact source [the source revision](https://github.com/Mira-Cult/math-wip/commit/393f6714639ad5dd6dfe46756220acde5b690502), applies because T is ambivalent. It proves that H_corr gives a simple proper loop, and excludes both an exact-factorization isotope and a Bruck isotope under its pinned route interfaces. Its common quotient is C2, whereas H_split is a product, establishing distinctness independently of their presentation labels. No novelty claim is made for this construction or either class.

## Unitary involution classes and their 2-parts

Work on a nondegenerate Hermitian n-space over F_{q²}. In U_n(q), involutions are 1+N with N²=0 and rank r∈{1,…,⌊n/2⌋}. The image of N is totally isotropic. The induced nondegenerate Hermitian form on the r-dimensional quotient dual to im N is unique up to isometry over F_{q²}; Witt extension gives one U_n(q)-class for each r. Its centralizer has order

 q^{r(2n−3r)} |U_r(q)| |U_{n−2r}(q)|.                 (1)

To see the centralizer order, fix the image L=im N. Its parabolic stabilizer has Levi factor GL_r(q²)×U_{n−2r}(q) and unipotent radical of order q^{r²+2r(n−2r)}: the Hermitian r×r block contributes q^{r²} and the mixed r×(n−2r) block contributes q^{2r(n−2r)}. At fixed L, the nonsingular induced Hermitian forms constitute one GL_r(q²)-orbit with stabilizer U_r(q). This gives the class size and centralizer (1). The matching total count of involutions and the unitary group order appear in Fulman–Guralnick–Stanton, [“Asymptotics of the number of involutions in finite classical groups,” Proposition 3.4 and equation (10)](https://dornsife.usc.edu/jason-fulman/wp-content/uploads/sites/224/2023/06/involutions2_18_2017v2.pdf), pp. 15–16 of the authors' version. The coordinator directly inspected the displayed order and count in the authors' version; Proposition 3.4 states a total count, not the individual class claim. The class, splitting and parabolic-order argument in this note is a separate deduction with the scoped reviews below; the source proof was not reverified.

The involutions have determinant one. Their U-class remains one SU-class: if n−2r>0, the U_{n−2r} centralizer factor supplies every norm-one determinant; if n=2r, the U_r centralizer factor acts on the paired spaces with determinant d/\bar d=d², and squaring is surjective on the norm-one group of odd order q+1. Passing to PSU, the scalar center has odd order and is injective on involutions; projective centralizers cannot enlarge by a nontrivial scalar because such a scalar would square to 1. Hence T has one involution class C_r per r and the class size is the U-class size. Equation (1) and |U_k(q)|₂=q^{k(k−1)/2} give

 |C_r|₂=q^{r(r−1)/2},     |T|₂=q^{n(n−1)/2}.             (2)

If r is the least rank in S, the class valuations in (2) strictly increase with r, so |Λ|₂=q^{r(r−1)/2} without cancellation. Conjugate a Sylow 2-subgroup Q of H into a fixed Sylow 2-subgroup U of T. Then

 [U:Q]=q^{r(r−1)/2}.                                    (3)

## A Hermitian collision

Choose an r-dimensional totally isotropic space and its dual, and take the elementary abelian group X of transformations

 1+N(M),   N(M)=[[0,M,0],[0,0,0],[0,0,0]],   M=\bar M^T∈Mat_r(F_{q²}),

in a hyperbolic basis (the displayed block ordering is chosen so N maps the dual isotropic space into the first). Its order is q^{r²}: r diagonal entries lie in F_q and each off-diagonal conjugate pair contributes q². It lies in a Sylow 2-subgroup, so choose U to contain it before conjugating Q. Since r²>r(r−1)/2, equations (3) and |X| give a nonidentity element 1+N(M)∈X∩Q, of rank j≤r.

For q>2 and all r≥1, and for q=2 with r≥2, every rank-j Hermitian r×r matrix M is a sum of two invertible Hermitian matrices. Hermitian congruence reduces M to diag(I_j,0). If q>2 choose a diagonal matrix with α∈F_q\{0,1} in the first j places and 1 elsewhere. For q=2 and even j, use blocks [[0,1],[1,1]] on the first j coordinates and identity elsewhere; the block and its sum with I are invertible. For q=2 and odd j<r, use those blocks on j−1 coordinates, then the swap block [[0,1],[1,0]] on one M=1 and one M=0 coordinate, and identity elsewhere. For q=2 and odd j=r≥3, use 2×2 blocks for r−3 coordinates and the Hermitian matrix

 A=[[0,1,1],[1,0,ω],[1,ω²,0]],   ω²+ω+1=0,

on the remaining three coordinates. Both det A and det(A+I₃) are 1 in F₄. All blocks and their prescribed sums are invertible. Undoing the congruence gives the required two Hermitian matrices; the corresponding commuting unitary involutions lie in C_r and multiply to 1+N(M). Thus Q∩C_r²≠1, contradicting H∩Λ²=1.

The only exception to that matrix statement is q=2,r=1: F₂ has only one nonzero scalar, so a nonzero 1×1 Hermitian matrix is not a sum of two invertible such matrices. Here (3) makes Q=U. If n≥4, choose a totally isotropic 2-space: U may be chosen to contain its Hermitian block subgroup. The rank-two matrix diag(1,1) is the sum of diag(1,0) and diag(0,1), so Q contains a nonidentity product of two class-C₁ involutions, the same contradiction. The remaining n=3,q=2 has |PSU₃(2)|=72 and is not simple, so it is excluded by the hypothesis. (The rank-one failure inside a 1×1 block is genuine; it is not bypassed by assuming the linear matrix lemma.)

All group and collision statements use the original folder. The source inspections, unitary class-splitting/determinant argument, Hermitian decomposition and whole deduction have the scoped independent review recorded below. This completes the inner case; the outer case follows below, and no classification of all simple Bol loops is claimed.

## Automorphisms and fixed-space descent

The needed automorphism description is the unitary case of Steinberg, *Automorphisms of finite linear groups*, Theorem 3.2 and statements 3.3–3.6, printed p.608, DOI 10.4153/CJM-1960-054-6: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/16023F257E0F21D57873B1450E9F15E4/S0008414X00010245a.pdf/automorphisms-of-finite-linear-groups.pdf . In this case the outer automorphism group is the odd diagonal group D of order gcd(n,q+1), extended by the cyclic field group of order 2f, with the stated natural action. In particular its unique field involution is σ:x↦x^q. An outer involution has this field component; its diagonal component is a cocycle for an involution of odd D, and ker(1+σ)=im(1−σ) on D. Thus PΓU conjugation puts τ in the actual coset σT. The coordinator inspected the published automorphism statements; their application to the simple unitary groups, including the small-rank cases retained here, is covered by the scoped independent reviews below. No reproof of Steinberg’s classification is claimed.

For an involution t=σA in U_n(q)⋊⟨σ⟩, its fixed Fq-space W spans the natural Fq²-space: choose η with η+η^q=1, and decompose v as x+ηy with y=v+t(v), x=v+ηy, both fixed. The restricted Hermitian form is nonsingular, symmetric and Fq-valued on W. Conversely such a symmetric form gives a semilinear involution. Two are U-conjugate exactly when the fixed forms are isometric. Hence there is one type when n is odd, and alternating and nonalternating types when n is even. In a model with Hermitian matrix I, representatives are σ and, for even n, σJ with J nonsingular alternating and det J=1.

Within the actual σSU coset these U-classes do not split: if B∈U conjugates two elements whose linear components have determinant one, det B has square one in the odd norm-one group μ_(q+1), hence det B=1. The U-centralizer is the isometry group of the fixed form, and its determinant lies in Fq*∩μ_(q+1)=1. It is therefore already in SU. A lift t of a projective outside involution satisfies t²=z in the odd scalar center. As t commutes with z and σ inverts that center, z=1. Projective centralizers lift by rescaling by a central square root. Thus these results pass to T with unchanged centralizer orders.

For nonalternating even dimension 2m, the centralizer has order h=q^{2m−1}|Sp_(2m−2)(q)|; for alternating type it is |Sp_2m(q)|=h(q^{2m}−1). To see the former, the canonical vector w defined by b(v,v)=b(v,w)² is nonzero and isotropic. In a decomposition Fw+Fe+U, its isometries are parametrized by S∈Sp(U), u∈U and c∈Fq via g(w)=w, g(v)=Sv+b(Sv,u)w, g(e)=e+u+cw. Odd dimension gives an orthogonal centralizer containing involutions. Both even-dimensional orders have 2-part q^{m²}.

The actual selected marked set Λ is a nonempty union of these T-classes, not assumed to contain all outside involutions. Its size equals [T:H], and H∩Λ²=1.

## Odd dimension and the nonalternating class

For each r≤floor(n/2), let V have columns e_(2i−1)+e_(2i), and u_r=I+VVᵀ. These F2 matrices are symmetric involutions and are unitary for the identity Hermitian form. Each is fixed by σ and has rank r. Their associated symmetric forms are nonalternating when r<n/2, so σu_r is conjugate to σ. At maximal rank r=n/2≥2 choose u=I+VCVᵀ instead, with C nonsingular symmetric having a zero diagonal entry: take C alternating for even r, or H₂⊕I_(r−2) for odd r. This again is a symmetric unitary involution and its associated form is nonalternating. The unitary involution-class input identifies one T-class for each rank. Thus the nonalternating outside-class square contains every T-involution. In odd dimension it is the only outside class, while in even dimension this applies whenever it is selected. Hence H is odd whenever the nonalternating class occurs. If it alone is selected, |H| is its even centralizer order, a contradiction. If both even-dimensional types are selected the next paragraph applies instead.

## Alternating selected, q>2 and q=2,n≥8

Put n=2m, and use a hyperbolic Hermitian model with matrix J and α=σ. Its fixed-space form is J, so α represents the alternating class C_A. If C_A alone is selected, |H|=|Sp_2m(q)|; if both classes are selected, |H|=|Sp_2m(q)|/q^{2m}.

For q>2 choose an odd prime r|q−1. The r-divisible factors in the unitary order formula are exactly the even-j factors in |Sp_2m(q)|; the scalar center divides q+1. Consequently |H|_r=|T|_r. Choose λ∈Fq* of order r, let D=diag(λ,λ^-1), P=[[0,1],[1,0]], and select η with η+barη=1. The matrix B=[[η,1],[barη,1]] has determinant 1 and bar B=PB. Put a=B^-1DB. Since PDP=D^-1, bar a=a^-1 and det a=1. On a J₄-hyperbolic summand set u=diag(a,bar a^-T), extending by I. Then u∈SU(J), has order r and satisfies bar u=u^-1. An appropriate power C∈⟨u⟩ has C^-2=u, whence C^-1α(C)=u and αu∈C_A. Thus u∈C_A² and Sylow conjugacy puts a nonidentity element of Λ² in H.

For q=2,n≥8 let A be multiplication by an element of order 5 in F16 on its four-dimensional F2-space. It has order 5 and is inverted by the involution P:x↦x⁴. Semilinear descent supplies B∈GL4(F4) satisfying bar B=PB. Set a=B^-1AB and u=diag(a,bar a^-T)⊕I on a hyperbolic eight-space. Then det a=1, bar a=a^-1, u∈SU(J), and α(u)=u^-1. The same power-of-u argument gives u∈C_A². Since ord_5(2)=4, the 5-parts of SU and Sp agree; the scalar center has order dividing 3. In either class selection H contains a Sylow 5-subgroup, giving the same contradiction.

## q=2,n=6: the three symplectic subgroups

If both classes are selected, the nonalternating-square argument makes H odd but its order has 2-part 2^{m²−2m}=8, a contradiction. If only C_A is selected, |H|=|Sp6(2)|=1,451,520. The ATLAS [U6(2) maximal-subgroup list](https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/clas/U62/), version 2.0 updated 15 April 2005, corroborated by the [v3.004 order table](https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/v3/clas/U62/), supplies exactly three T-classes of maximal subgroups Sp6(2); the divisibility check on that list leaves these as the only maximal overgroups of a subgroup of this order. The [ATLAS S6(2) page](https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/clas/S62/), version 2.0 updated 16 November 2004, supplies Out(Sp6(2))=1. These finite group data remain external classification inputs with the source inspections recorded below. The v2 U6(2) page warns that its subgroup word programs were not checked, and the v3 port warns of incompleteness; the proof relies on the v2 classification list, not on unchecked programs. The distinct listed maximal orders are 13,685,760; 13,271,040; 10,321,920; 6,531,840; 1,474,560; 1,451,520; 443,520; 155,520; 93,312; 40,320. Only 1,451,520 is divisible by |Sp6(2)|, giving the asserted restriction by Lagrange. The S6(2) page reports checking level 0.

Let H0=Sp6(2) be the natural subgroup of SU6(J). Its centralizer in PGU is trivial: if a projective transformation centralizes H0, its commutators define a character H0→F4*, which is trivial because H0 is perfect. Its linear lift centralizes all symplectic transvections, hence preserves each F2-vector line; applying this to a basis and all pairwise sums forces it to be scalar. Since H0 has no outer automorphisms, N_PGU(H0)=H0. The diagonal quotient PGU/T has order 3; consequently the PGU-class of H0 splits into exactly three T-classes. The ATLAS three-class input shows these exhaust the order-|H0| possibilities. Each contains a rank-two unitary involution, because H0 contains a product of two suitable symplectic transvections and PGU conjugation preserves rank.

The unique rank-two T-class lies in C_A²: on a four-dimensional hyperbolic summand take u=diag(s,s), s=[[0,1],[1,0]], extended by I. It is unitary and α(u)=u^-1; moreover Ju is alternating, so αu has alternating fixed-space form by the criterion below. Fixed-space descent makes αu T-conjugate to α. Thus H meets C_A², a contradiction.

## q=2,n=4: finite subgroup input

If only C_A is selected, |H|=|Sp4(2)|=720; if both types are selected, |H|=45. The [ATLAS U4(2) page](https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/clas/S43/), version 2.0 updated 16 December 2004, checked Level 0, lists maximal groups 2^4:A5, S6, 3^{1+2}:2A4, 3^3:S4 and 2.(A4×A4).2. Only S6 has order divisible by 720 or 45. The class completeness is also corroborated by a GAP 4.15.1 maximal-subgroup computation recorded in [the GAP certificate](../code/verify_unitary_four_two_maximals.g), with output [[576,1],[648,2],[720,1],[960,1]]; the ATLAS and library inputs retain their independent-review scope. Under these inputs, every order-720 H is conjugate to C_T(α)=Sp4(2). The involution u=diag(s,s) above lies in that fixed group and in C_A², a contradiction. S6 has no subgroup of order 45: its Sylow 5-subgroup would be normal and centralized by a Sylow 3-subgroup, producing an element of order 15, which S6 has no room to realize.

## Fixed-form test used above

If t is a semilinear involution and b is the fixed-space form, write v=x+ηy with x,y t-fixed and η+barη=1. Then h(v,tv)=b(x,x)+barη² b(y,y), which vanishes for every v exactly when b is alternating. In the identity Hermitian model, for t=σu with u symmetric unitary, this is equivalent to diag(u)=0. Thus the representatives in the nonalternating-square section really have nonalternating type. In the hyperbolic J model with α=σ, the expression for t=αu is bar vᵀJ bar(u) bar v. As J has entries in F_q, it vanishes identically exactly when Ju is alternating. In the displayed finite-case matrices u is fixed by bar. These statements use the actual semilinear representative and do not identify σ with Int(J)σ.

Let q=p^f≥4 be a prime power, T=PSL₂(q), and k≥3. Assume the manuscript's folder hypotheses, including a set Λ of involutions in the extension G̃=G⋊⟨t⟩, invariance of Λ under G̃, |Λ|=|T|^(k−1), Λ²∩H={1}, and injectivity of its action on B=T^k. Assume also that T has one involution class I and I⊆(α^T)² for every nonidentity involutory α∈Aut(T). Then no such primitive SD_k folder exists. Cases q=4,5 are handled for every k≥3 in [the all-factor-count A₅ proof](sd-a5-all-factors.md); take q≥7 below.

Put d=gcd(2,q−1), n=q(q²−1)/d, a=dfn and

    b=q²−(2−d)+1 + [f even] √q(q+1).

Here a=|Aut(T)| and b is one plus its number of involutions. For even f, √q is an integer. These exact inputs, including the field-involution contribution, are not established by the computation below.

## Counting and uniform estimates

Define F_0(x)=1, F_1(x)=x, F_m(x)=xF_{m−1}(x)+(m−1)aF_{m−2}(x). The [general square-class counting lemma](sd-a5-all-factors.md#general-counting-lemma) yields

    n^(k−1) ≤ A_k := F_k(b)−F_k(b−1) ≤ kF_{k−1}(b).

The last inequality uses nonnegative coefficients and F'_k(x)=kF_{k−1}(x), from the matching formula or the exponential generating function exp(xz+az²/2). For m≥1,

    F_m(b) ≤ [b+√((m−1)a)]^m.

Indeed, setting c=√((m−1)a), induct on h≤m using F_h=bF_{h−1}+(h−1)aF_{h−2} and (h−1)a≤c(b+c).

For all q≥7, b/n≤1/3 and a≤b². For odd f, b≤q²+1 and 3(q²+1)≤q(q²−1)/2. For even f, q≥9 and √q≤q/3, so b≤(4q²+q)/3+1; 4q²+q+3≤q(q²−1)/2 holds for q≥9. In both cases n≥q(q²−1)/2. Also a=fq(q²−1)<f q³≤q⁴≤b², since f≤q and b≥q².

We also use b≤(5/3)q². For odd f this is immediate. For even f, the preceding bound b≤(4q²+q)/3+1 is at most (5/3)q² for q≥9.

## Initial finite ranges

Choose a prime r with q+1<r<2(q+1), by Bertrand's postulate. Because n=q(q−1)(q+1)/d, r does not divide n. Set K=2r−1≤4q+1.

For q≥32, all 3≤k≤K are excluded by the unrestricted count. Indeed, f≤q/4: if f≥4, then 2^f≥4f, and otherwise q≥32≥4f. Since k−2<4q and a=fq(q²−1),

    [b+√((k−2)a)]/n
      ≤ (10/3)q/(q²−1)+4√f/√(q²−1)
      ≤ (10/3)q/(q²−1)+2√q/√(q²−1) < 1/2.

The last expression decreases with q and is <1/2 at q=32. Thus A_k/n^(k−1)≤k/2^(k−1)<1 for k≥3.

For q<32, the following exact integer certificate enumerates all prime powers q≥7, selects the first prime r>q+1, checks A_k<n^(k−1) for 3≤k≤max(K,24), and checks the stronger K F_{K−1}(b)<n^(K−1) needed for the alternating-top tail. It asserts the enumerated list, so an omission or duplicate cannot silently pass.

The complete certificate is [verify_psl2_diagonal_bounds.py](../code/verify_psl2_diagonal_bounds.py), using only Python standard-library exact integers. Its checks are mathematical finite-range inequalities, not a test of the infinite or group-theoretic arguments.


## Tail for arbitrary primitive top group

Let P be G's image on the k factors. P is primitive: B is transitive, so G=BH, and H induces P. Any nontrivial P-invariant partition yields the subgroup of B consisting of tuples constant on each block, strictly between Δ(T)=H∩B and B; H normalizes it because H normalizes Δ(T), so it acts on all factor labels by one common automorphism followed by a permutation. This subgroup gives a proper subgroup strictly between H and G, a contradiction.

If P does not contain A_k and k≥25, Maróti, *On the Orders of Primitive Groups*, Corollary 1.2, p. 3, https://www.renyi.hu/~maroti/primitive.pdf, gives |P|<2^k. The action of t on the factors need not lie in P, but π(Λ)⊆π(t)P has at most |P| elements. The general counting lemma, together with a≤b², bounds the number of actions above each top permutation by k b^(k−1). Hence

    |Λ|/n^(k−1) < 2k (2b/n)^(k−1) ≤ 2k (2/3)^(k−1) < 1.

The last expression decreases for k≥25 and its value at 25 is <1, checked in the certificate. For k≤24, the initial-range arguments already apply.

If P contains A_k, products of two elements of Λ cannot have order divisible by r. Indeed, all powers of λμ lie in Λ² because Λ is G̃-conjugation-invariant: (λμ)^(2h)=[(λμ)^h λ (λμ)^(−h)]λ and (λμ)^(2h+1)=[(λμ)^h λ (λμ)^(−h)]μ. Every nonidentity member of Λ² is fixed-point-free by G-invariance and Λ²∩H={1}; hence the cyclic group generated by λμ acts freely and its order divides n^(k−1).

A top involution with j≥(r−1)/2 transpositions and at least one fixed point can be paired with a conjugate so their product has an r-cycle: use two near-perfect matchings on r points. A fixed-point-free top involution with j≥r can similarly be paired with a conjugate using two perfect matchings on 2r points whose union is a 2r-cycle; their product has two r-cycles. The conjugator can be taken in A_k because the involution centralizer contains an odd transposition. Lift it through G and use conjugation-invariance of Λ. Thus for k≥K only j≤(r−3)/2 can occur.

At k=K the normalized sum of surviving derivative bounds is at most K F_{K−1}(b)/n^(K−1), which is <1 by the uniform estimate or the finite certificate. For each fixed surviving j, the exact normalized summand is

    U_j(k)= [k!/(2^j j!(k−2j−1)!)] · (a/n²)^j · (b/n)^(k−2j−1),

and

    U_j(k+1)/U_j(k) = [(k+1)/(k−2j)](b/n)
                      ≤ [2r/(r+2)](b/n) < 1

for k≥K, because b/n≤1/3. Thus their sum stays <1 for all k≥K, contradicting |Λ|=n^(k−1). The two top cases cover all P.

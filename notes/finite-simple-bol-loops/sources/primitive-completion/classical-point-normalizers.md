# Primitive classical nonsingular-point actions

**Mira Cult**

This is an **interface with the scoped review recorded below** for the natural-action arguments in [C0010](../../claims/0010.md), [the orthogonal continuation](orthogonal-odd-linear.md), [the odd-unitary continuation](unitary-odd-dimension.md), and [the unitary nonorthogonal continuation](unitary-nonorthogonal.md). It introduces no claim ID. It concerns a specified natural point action, not all permutation actions of the same abstract socle.

Let $`G\leq\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits(\Omega)`$ be faithful, primitive and almost simple with socle $`T`$, and suppose its restricted $`T`$-set is the indicated natural nonsingular-projective-point action. The intended conclusion is that, after the specified identification of points, $`G`$ lies in the natural semilinear action on that same set, in either of these cases:

1. $`T=\mathop{\mathrm{PSU}}\nolimits_m(q)`$, $`m\ge3`$, $`q\ge3`$, on all nonsingular one-spaces of a nondegenerate Hermitian space over $`\mathbb F_{q^2}`$; the ambient group is $`P\Gamma U_m(q)`$.
2. $`T=P\Omega_{2m}^{\varepsilon}(q)`$, $`m\ge3`$, with $`q\ge5`$ odd or $`q\ge4`$ even, on one $`T`$-orbit of nonsingular one-spaces; the ambient group is the **setwise stabilizer of that orbit** in $`P\Gamma GO_{2m}^{\varepsilon}(q)`$.

Here $`P\Gamma GO`$ denotes projective semilinear quadratic similitudes. For odd $`q`$ the square and nonsquare norm types are distinct sets: a nonsquare multiplier exchanges them. Their permutation-isomorphism after an outer automorphism is not an isomorphism of the two $`T`$-sets with the same fixed $`T`$-action. In even characteristic there is one nonsingular point type.

## Permutation kernel and fixed orbit

Put $`H=G_\omega`$ and $`K=T_\omega`$. The natural stabilizer $`K`$ is nontrivial and proper in the stated ranges. Since $`H`$ normalizes $`K`$, it normalizes $`N_T(K)`$, so $`HN_T(K)`$ is a subgroup of $`G`$. If $`N_T(K)>K`$, maximality of $`H`$ forces $`HN_T(K)=G`$; intersecting with $`T`$ gives $`N_T(K)=T`$, contradicting simplicity of $`T`$. Thus $`N_T(K)=K`$. The centralizer of a transitive $`T`$-action in $`\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits(\Omega)`$ is $`N_T(K)/K`$, hence trivial. Consequently conjugation embeds the permutation normalizer into $`\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(T)`$, and two permutations inducing the same automorphism of $`T`$ agree.

For a semilinear representative $`a`$ of the automorphism induced by an element $`g\in G`$, the subgroups $`K^a`$ and $`K^g`$ are $`T`$-conjugate: $`g`$ sends $`\omega`$ to some $`t\omega`$. When $`a`$ preserves $`\Omega`$, it defines a permutation on the same set and therefore equals $`g`$ by the trivial-centralizer argument. In the odd-orthogonal case, a representative interchanging norm types cannot have $`K^a`$ $`T`$-conjugate to $`K`$: $`K`$ fixes a unique nonsingular projective point in the full projective space. Indeed it contains the natural $`\Omega_{2m-1}(q)`$ on the perpendicular complement of $`\omega`$; that group acts irreducibly there and has no invariant one-space, while its fixed space on the full module is the line $`\omega`$. Here $`2m-1\ge5`$ and $`q\ge5`$; this uses the standard irreducibility of the natural odd-dimensional orthogonal module. Thus conjugate point stabilizers have points in the same $`T`$-orbit. Equivalently, a type-swapping similitude cannot represent an automorphism realized by $`g`$ on the fixed orbit. This is the step that must be checked independently along with the cited classical-group inputs.

## Automorphisms and exceptional dimensions

For the unitary case, Kleidman–Liebeck, *The Subgroup Structure of the Finite Classical Groups* (1990), §2.1, Table 2.1.B (p. 15) and Theorems 2.1.3–2.1.4 (p. 16), identify the projective semilinear unitary group with $`\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathop{\mathrm{PSU}}\nolimits_m(q))`$ whenever $`m\ge3`$, $`q\ge3`$. The table identifies the defining field as $`\mathbb F_{q^2}`$ and the unitary $`A`$ as $`\Gamma U`$; Theorem 2.1.3 excludes $`U_3(2)`$, and the exceptions in Theorem 2.1.4 concern symplectic and triality groups, not these unitary groups. The original theorem statement and table were directly inspected in the [publisher preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780511892394_A23680360/preview-9780511892394_A23680360.pdf). Burness–Guralnick, [arXiv:2112.03967v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.03967v2), Proposition 5.11 and Lemma 5.14, use the nonsingular one-space action and realize the involutory unitary graph automorphism as a field automorphism on the $`\mathbb F_{q^2}`$ module. Thus the graph involution is included in the semilinear group.

The earlier Tits citation is retained only as historical source context: the handwritten note “Automorphismen von Quadriken”, dated 9 June 1975 and signed J. Tits, [booklet PDF](https://www.math.uni-bielefeld.de/chronik/booklets/1975.pdf), PDF p. 22, displays the automorphism relation but does not enumerate the small exceptions there. It was visually checked; the precise finite-group hypotheses above come from Kleidman–Liebeck, not an inferred exception-free reading of that note.

For orthogonal dimension six, Giudici–Glasby–Praeger, [arXiv:2012.07213v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07213v1), proof of Theorem 3.5, explicitly identifies $`P\Gamma O_6^+(q)`$ with $`\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathop{\mathrm{PSL}}\nolimits_4(q))`$ via $`P\Omega_6^+(q)\cong\mathop{\mathrm{PSL}}\nolimits_4(q)`$. Their §4.1 proof of Theorem 4.2 identifies $`P\Gamma O_6^-(q)`$ with $`P\Gamma U_4(q)=\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathop{\mathrm{PSU}}\nolimits_4(q))`$ via $`P\Omega_6^-(q)\cong\mathop{\mathrm{PSU}}\nolimits_4(q)`$. Both proofs explicitly state the index-two orbit restriction in odd characteristic. These natural semilinear identifications include the diagonal, graph and field automorphisms; $`q=2`$ is outside the stated range.

For orthogonal dimension at least eight, Burness–Guralnick Proposition 5.26 and Lemmas 5.30–5.31 treat primitive almost-simple nonsingular-one-space actions for odd and even characteristic respectively. They explicitly exclude triality for $`P\Omega_8^+(q)`$ using maximality of the point stabilizer. Kleidman–Liebeck Theorem 2.1.4 and Table 2.1.B give the other classical automorphisms as semilinear orthogonal similitudes; the only exceptional triality case is therefore excluded for these primitive actions. Table 7 of that paper identifies the relevant point-space conventions; its square/nonsquare permutation-isomorphism statement does not bypass the fixed-orbit argument above.

# Odd-field linear orthogonal continuation

Mira Cult
The [primitive point-action interface](classical-point-normalizers.md) is a separate unreviewed candidate for identifying almost-simple extensions of these natural actions; it makes no assertion about other actions of the same socle.

Bounded obligation: develop the orthogonal frontier of [0323-finite-simple-non-moufang-bol-loops:C0010](../../claims/0010.md), using the construction pattern in [C0009](../foundations/C0009.md) and [C0016](../foundations/C0016.md). This manuscript allocates no claim ID. The scoped independent review below accepts the following bounded result:

Let q>=5 be an odd prime power, V a nondegenerate quadratic space of even dimension n=2m>=6 over F_q, and δ nonzero. Put S={v:Q(v)=δ}, Ω=S/{±1}. If any loop on Ω has full multiplication group contained in the natural projective linear similarity group PGO(V,Q), then n=8, Q is of plus type, and the loop is the Paige loop M(q). Thus no simple proper Bol loop occurs in these actions. (For prime q this also covers PΓGO, since the field automorphism group is trivial.) This does not cover nonlinear semisimilarities over nonprime fields or characteristic 2. It uses the standard Hurwitz classification of unital composition algebras and the splitness of finite-field octonions, as in C0016; the review scope is recorded below.

Write B(u,v)=Q(u+v)-Q(u)-Q(v). Any projective linear similarity permuting Ω has square multiplier: if Q(Ae)=λδ and the image point has norm square class δ, then λ is a square. Rescaling A gives an isometry; its only isometric scalar multiples are ±A, exactly one sending e to each chosen representative of the image point. Denote the lifts of left and right translations by L_x,R_y, with L_x e=x, R_y e=y for x,y∈S; hence L_-x=-L_x and R_-y=-R_y. Their common projective action gives L_x y=s(x,y)R_y x with s(x,y)∈{±1}; s(x,e)=1 and s is invariant under replacing either entry by its negative.

We need the following elementary four-subspace bound. The standard count for nondegenerate even-dimensional Q gives |S|≥q^(n-1)-q^(m-1). A hyperplane H satisfies |H∩S|≤q^(n-2)+q^(m-1): its radical has dimension at most 1, and the standard level counts on its nondegenerate quotient give this bound. Let four proper subspaces W_{++},W_{--},W_{+-},W_{-+} have the property that a member of the first pair meets a member of the second pair only at 0. If a is the largest dimension in the first pair and b in the second, then a+b≤n. If a=n-1, b≤1, and their union meets S in at most 2(q^(n-2)+q^(m-1))+2(q-1). Similarly if b=n-1. Otherwise all four have dimension ≤n-2, and the bound is 4q^(n-2). Both bounds are strictly less than q^(n-1)-q^(m-1) for q≥5,m≥3: the second follows from q^(n-2)(q-4)>q^(m-1), and the first follows from q^(n-2)(q-2)>3q^(m-1)+2(q-1). Hence S is not covered by the four subspaces.

For independent y,z∈S with B(y,z)=-δ, y+z∈S. From linearity of L_x and the projective signs, S is covered by the four kernels ker(R_{y+z}-aR_y-bR_z), a,b∈{±1}. A kernel from the equal-sign pair and one from the unequal-sign pair meet only at zero, since the difference of their defining operators is ±2R_y or ±2R_z. Thus one kernel is V. Evaluation at e, using independence of y,z, forces its signs both +1: R_{y+z}=R_y+R_z. Substitution back and left cancellation make R_y x and R_z x independent, so s(x,y)=s(x,z) for all x∈S.

The corresponding graph on Ω, joining [y] and [z] when representatives y,z are independent and satisfy B(y,z)=-δ, is connected. Indeed for independent y,z solve B(w,y)=B(w,z)=-δ; its solution space is an affine space w0+U of dimension n-2, and rank(Q|U)≥n-4≥2 because rad(U)⊆U⊥. A quadratic polynomial on U with rank r≥2 attains every field value: after splitting its radical, either a radical linear term is nonzero, or a translation completes squares and its nondegenerate r-dimensional part represents every field element. Moreover every attained value has at least q^(n-4) solutions: for rank 2 its nonzero values have ≥q-1 representatives and zero has at least one; for higher rank fix r-2 coordinates and use the remaining nondegenerate binary part. Thus at least q²>2 choices of w solve Q(w)=δ, so one can avoid the at most two collinear candidates with y or z (in fact each intersection with S contains only ±y or ±z; excluding all four still leaves enough). The chosen w is adjacent to each. For dependent y,z the only possibilities are z=±y, which represent the same vertex and have the same sign value. Therefore every s(x,y)=s(x,e)=1.

The sphere S spans V: its cardinality exceeds the hyperplane bound. Choose a basis b_i∈S and define the bilinear product by u⋆v=Σ_i u_i L_{b_i}v. Since L_x y=R_y x on S×S, first u⋆y=R_y u for all u and y∈S, then x⋆v=L_x v for all x∈S and v∈V. It is unital with identity e. Put N=δ^-1 Q. For fixed v, the quadratic form F_v(u)=N(u⋆v)-N(u)N(v) vanishes on S.

To see F_v=0, first each Q-singular u has F_v(u)=0. For u≠0, choose x∈u⊥ with Q(x)=δ (the quotient u⊥/<u> is nondegenerate of dimension n-2≥4 and represents δ). All x+tu lie in S; the polynomial F_v(x+tu) vanishes for all t∈F_q and has degree≤2, so its t² coefficient F_v(u) is zero. A quadratic form F vanishing on the singular cone of nondegenerate Q of dimension ≥4 over F_q, q≥5, is a scalar multiple of Q: choose a hyperbolic pair e1,f1 and write V=<e1,f1>⊥W. Since e1,f1 are singular, F(e1)=F(f1)=0. For v∈W and each nonzero t, the vector te1+v-Q(v)t^-1 f1 is singular. Multiplying its F-value by t gives a polynomial of degree≤2 in t with at least four roots, showing B_F(e1,v)=0 and F(v)=B_F(e1,f1)Q(v), and Q(v) B_F(f1,v)=0. Nonzero-norm vectors span W (the level counts or a nondegenerate basis suffice), so B_F(f1,v)=0 too. Hence F=B_F(e1,f1)Q. As F vanishes on S and δ≠0, its scalar is zero. Therefore N(u⋆v)=N(u)N(v) for all u,v, so (V,⋆,N) is a unital composition algebra. Hurwitz gives dimension 1,2,4 or 8; here n=8. Every 8-dimensional composition algebra over a finite field is the split octonion algebra, with plus-type norm. Its norm-one vectors modulo ±1 form M(q).

The later odd-field semilinear section removes the restriction on field automorphisms.

## Odd-field semilinear continuation

For the C0010 orthogonal frontier, the following extends the reviewed linear result above: let q=p^f≥5 be odd, V nondegenerate of dimension n=2m≥6, Q normalized so the identity representative e has Q(e)=1, and Ω={v:Q(v)=1}/{±1}. Any loop on Ω with full multiplication group in PΓGO(V,Q) is M(q); necessarily n=8 and Q has plus type. No Bol or simplicity assumption is used. The independent review is recorded below; its composition-algebra conclusion uses the reviewed linear result above. For an arbitrary initial nonzero level δ, replace Q by δ⁻¹Q: this preserves the orthogonal type in even dimension, and the projective semisimilarity group is unchanged, with each multiplier adjusted by the corresponding scalar and its field-automorphism image.

A normalized lift of a projective semisimilarity A with field automorphism σ satisfies Q(Av)=Q(v)^σ: indeed its multiplier is a square because it maps the identity projective point to Ω; rescale and fix its sign by Ae=x. Thus each left/right translation has a unique normalized lift L_x or R_x taking e to x for each x in S={Q=1}. These are invertible F_p-linear semilinear maps, with L_-x=-L_x, R_-x=-R_x and L_x(y)=s(x,y)R_y(x), s=±1. Signs are invariant under negating x or y and s(x,e)=1.

**Sphere and prime-field subspace bound.** Let N=nf, and U≤V have F_p-codimension c≥1. Then

  |S∩U| ≤ |S|/p^c + q^m.                                      (1)

To derive it, select the annihilator U^⊥ of the trace pairing Tr_{q/p}(B(z,v)); it has p^c elements. Orthogonality gives |S∩U|=p^-c sum_{z∈U^⊥} sum_{x∈S} ψ(B(z,x)), with ψ a nontrivial additive character of F_q induced by the prime-field trace. The z=0 term is |S|/p^c. For z≠0, expand the level-set indicator with all additive characters t∈F_q. The t=0 inner sum is 0 by nondegeneracy, and each t≠0 inner quadratic Gauss sum has absolute value q^{n/2}=q^m, by completing the square and nondegeneracy. The resulting Fourier coefficient has absolute value ≤(q-1)q^{m-1}<q^m. Summing the other annihilator terms proves (1). The even-dimensional level count gives |S|≥q^{n-1}-q^{m-1}.

**Numerical inequality.** If four proper F_p-subspaces have the property that each of the first two intersects each of the other two only at zero, their union cannot cover S. Let a,b be their respective maximum F_p-dimensions; then a+b≤N. If either maximum is N-1, the other is ≤1 and (1) bounds the union's S-points by 2(|S|/p+q^m)+2(p-1). Otherwise every dimension is at most N-2 and (1) bounds it by 4(|S|/p²+q^m). Both are strictly less than |S|: for all p≥3, q≥5, m≥3, |S|≥q^{2m-1}-q^{m-1}, and thus |S|/3>2q^m+2(p-1), and 5|S|/9>4q^m. For the first, divide the lower bound by q^m: q^{m-1}-1/q≥q²-1/q, so its third exceeds 2+2(p-1)/q^m, using p≤q and q≥5. For the second, 5(q^{m-1}-1/q)/9>4. In particular (1) also proves S spans V over F_p, since |S|/p+q^m<|S|.

**Alignment of signs.** If y,z∈S are independent and B(y,z)=-1, then y+z∈S. As in the reviewed linear argument above, F_p-linearity of L_x gives a four-kernel cover of S by ker(R_{y+z}-aR_y-bR_z) for a,b∈{±1}. Cross-pair kernels have zero intersection because the difference of their defining maps is ±2R_y or ±2R_z, invertible over F_p. The preceding estimate forces one kernel to be V; evaluation at e forces a=b=1. Thus R_{y+z}=R_y+R_z, and cancellation makes R_yx and R_zx independent over F_q for each x∈S, yielding s(x,y)=s(x,z). The norm-sphere projective graph with these edges is connected by the rank-at-least-n-4 affine quadratic argument in the reviewed linear argument above, which applies to all odd q≥5. Sign-invariance under ± and s(x,e)=1 therefore give L_x(y)=R_y(x) for all x,y∈S.

For independent x,y∈S with B(x,y)=-1, equality of signs and additivity of every R_z yield L_{x+y}(z)=R_z(x+y)=R_z(x)+R_z(y)=(L_x+L_y)(z) for all z∈S. As S spans V over F_p, L_{x+y}=L_x+L_y as F_p-linear maps.

**Automorphisms coincide.** If A,B are invertible semilinear maps and A+B is an invertible semilinear map, their field automorphisms are equal. Indeed let the automorphisms be σ,ρ,τ, respectively, and suppose σ≠ρ. Choose t with t^σ≠t^ρ. For all v,

 (t^σ-t^τ)A(v)+(t^ρ-t^τ)B(v)=0.

The two coefficients cannot both vanish. If one vanishes, the invertibility of A or B gives a contradiction; if neither vanishes, B is a nonzero scalar multiple of A, which forces σ=ρ. Consequently adjacent projective points [x],[y] have the same automorphism for their L-lifts. The automorphism is unchanged by negation, so connectedness and L_e=identity force every L_x to be F_q-linear. For the right translations, R_{y+z}=R_y+R_z for adjacent y,z was already proved. The same sum lemma and connectivity give the same automorphism for all R_y, and R_e=identity makes it trivial.

Thus all translations are projective linear and the reviewed odd-linear result applies, proving the stated candidate. General semilinear group elements cannot occur in Mlt because that group is generated by the translations. Independent reviewer a separate automated checker accepted this odd-field semilinear extension at its recorded source revision, under the reviewed linear theorem and standard additive-character and quadratic Gauss-sum facts. The review checked both cross-kernel estimates including p=3, q≥9, m=3, the normalization, the sphere span and the semilinear-sum argument. It covers all odd q≥5 and even n≥6 for arbitrary loops, and does not cover the even-field candidate below.

## Even-field semilinear orthogonal continuation

Bounded result for C0010: let q=2^f≥4, and let V be a nondegenerate quadratic space of even dimension n=2m≥6 and either type. On the projective nonsingular points, any loop whose full multiplication group lies in PΓGO(V,Q) is the Paige loop M(q), and consequently n=8 and Q has plus type. No simplicity or Bol hypothesis is used. The scoped independent review below applies to this proof; the Hurwitz and finite-field octonion conclusions use the sources documented in the reviewed odd-linear argument above, including Nagy–Vojtěchovský, arXiv:math/0701707v1, §3.1 Theorem 3.1, §3.2 pp. 73–74 and §4.1 Theorem 4.1.

Every nonsingular projective point has a unique representative x with Q(x)=1, since squaring is a field automorphism. Let S={x:Q(x)=1}, identify the point set with S, fix its identity e, and denote by L_x,R_x the unique semilinear similarity lifts with L_x e=R_x e=x. If σ is the field automorphism of such a lift, then Q(L_x v)=Q(v)^σ: its multiplier is 1 by evaluation at e. Corresponding normalized lifts are unique because a nonzero scalar preserving Q must square to 1. Thus for all x,y∈S, exactly L_x(y)=R_y(x), not merely projective equality. Every lift is additive over F_2.

**Prime-field span.** Put N=nf. For any F_2-subspace U of codimension c≥1,

 |S∩U| ≤ |S|/2^c+q^m.                                        (1)

Indeed let ψ(a)=(-1)^Tr(a), and use additive-character orthogonality over U's annihilator under the nondegenerate trace pairing Tr B(z,x). For each nonzero z the sphere Fourier coefficient is

 q^-1 Σ_{t∈F_q^*} ψ(-t) Σ_{x∈V} ψ(tQ(x)+B(z,x)).

The t=0 sum vanishes, and each remaining sum has absolute value q^m: its associated F_2-quadratic form has nondegenerate polar form Tr(tB), so its squared modulus is q^n (equivalently, complete the square and use the quadratic Gauss sum). Bound the coefficient by (q-1)q^{m-1}<q^m and sum over the annihilator. The standard even-dimensional nonsingular quadratic count gives |S|=q^{n-1}∓q^{m-1}≥q^{n-1}-q^{m-1}>2q^m, for q≥4,m≥3. Therefore (1) rules out S lying in any F_2-hyperplane, so S spans V over F_2.

**Connected sphere graph.** Join distinct x,y∈S when B(x,y)=1; then Q(x+y)=1. For any distinct x,y∈S they are linearly independent over F_q, since Q(cx)=1 implies c=1. Solve B(w,x)=B(w,y)=1. The solution set is w0+U with dim U=n-2 and rank(B|U)≥n-4≥2. To see that at least q^(n-4) of these w have Q(w)=1 without odd-characteristic diagonalization, split U as W⊕rad(B|U), with W a nonsingular symplectic subspace. Fix the radical coordinate arbitrarily; the quadratic polynomial on W has polar B|W, and translating W removes its linear part. Split off a nondegenerate quadratic plane from W. Every nonsingular binary quadratic form over F_q represents every field element: the hyperbolic form represents values as ab, while the anisotropic form is the norm from F_{q²} and is surjective on F_q^*. For each fixed choice of remaining coordinates there is at least one point on the plane achieving the required value. Thus at least q^(n-4)≥q² choices of w exist. The two candidates x,y cannot satisfy their own corresponding pairing condition, so every solution gives edges to both. Hence the graph has diameter at most two.

**Eliminate field automorphisms.** For adjacent x,y, z=x+y is in S and for every w∈S,

 L_z(w)=R_w(z)=R_w(x)+R_w(y)=(L_x+L_y)(w).

Since S spans V over F_2, L_z=L_x+L_y as additive maps. Similarly R_z=R_x+R_y, using additivity of L_w. If invertible semilinear maps A,B have invertible semilinear sum A+B, their field automorphisms σ,ρ coincide: write τ for the sum's automorphism and choose t with t^σ≠t^ρ. For every v, (t^σ-t^τ)Av+(t^ρ-t^τ)Bv=0. If exactly one coefficient is zero, invertibility gives a contradiction; if both are nonzero then B is a nonzero scalar multiple of A, forcing σ=ρ. (Subtraction equals addition in characteristic 2.) Each adjacent pair of left translations therefore has equal field automorphism, and connectedness plus L_e=I forces all L_x to be F_q-linear. The identical argument for R gives all R_y F_q-linear.

**Composition.** S spans over F_q as well. Pick a basis b_i∈S and define u⋆v=Σ_i u_i L_{b_i}v. Because L_x(y)=R_y(x), F_q-linearity first gives u⋆y=R_y u for y∈S and all u, then x⋆v=L_x v for x∈S and all v. The product is bilinear and e is its identity. For fixed v, F_v(u)=Q(u⋆v)-Q(u)Q(v) vanishes on S. It is a quadratic form.

For each nonzero Q-singular u, choose x∈u^⊥ with Q(x)=1: the quotient u^⊥/<u> is a nonsingular quadratic space of dimension n-2≥4 and contains a nonsingular binary plane, which represents 1. Since x+tu∈S for every t∈F_q, the degree-2 polynomial F_v(x+tu) vanishes on at least four field elements, so its t² coefficient F_v(u) is zero. Thus F_v vanishes on the Q-singular cone. A quadratic form F with that property is a scalar multiple of Q: choose a hyperbolic pair e1,f1 with B(e1,f1)=1 and put V=<e1,f1>⊥W. Then F(e1)=F(f1)=0, and for each v∈W and nonzero t the vector te1+v+Q(v)t^-1 f1 is Q-singular. Multiplying its F-value by t yields a polynomial of degree≤2 with at least q-1≥3 roots. Its coefficients imply B_F(e1,v)=0, F(v)=B_F(e1,f1)Q(v), and Q(v)B_F(f1,v)=0. Nonzero-Q vectors span W: a nonsingular binary plane represents nonzero values, and for each singular v, adding a suitable vector from the plane (or using |{Q≠0}|>|W|/q) expresses v as a sum of two nonzero-Q vectors. Hence B_F(f1,v)=0 for all v, giving F=B_F(e1,f1)Q. Since F vanishes on S, its scalar is zero. Therefore Q(u⋆v)=Q(u)Q(v) universally.

The resulting unital composition algebra has dimension 8 by Hurwitz; over a finite field it is the split octonion algebra, and its projective norm-one loop is M(q). In characteristic 2 its center is trivial, so the projective identification does not quotient a nontrivial scalar. Both signs and all n≥6 are included until the final plus-type conclusion.

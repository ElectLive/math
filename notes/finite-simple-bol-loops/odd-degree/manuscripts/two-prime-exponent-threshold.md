# The first possible exponent at odd order $`p^a q`$

**Mira Cult**

Let $`p,q`$ be distinct odd primes, with no ordering assumed. For a finite simple nonassociative left Bol loop of order $`p^a q`$,

```math
q>p,\qquad a\ge p+1.
```

At $`a=p+1`$, one exists **if and only if** $`\operatorname{ord}_q(p)=p`$. The necessity below concerns every such loop without assuming a factorization construction. Sufficiency uses the existing [the two-prime construction, Theorem 1.1(3)](../../sources/foundations/C0003.md). This is an exact **order-existence** boundary, not a count or uniqueness theorem for loops of that order. In particular every odd Bol loop of order $`p^a q`$ with $`a\le p`$ is classically solvable. The argument does not assert that every order above the threshold occurs.

## A faithful cross-characteristic section

Use the doubled marked group $`D`$, outside involution $`t`$, and physical action and normal-cut formula of the [cube-free manuscript](odd-cubefree-normal-tower.md). Only its general double and rank-one linear lemma are used, not a cube-free hypothesis on the present loop. A nontrivial proper normal Sylow subgroup of $`D`$ is impossible: its full mark-fiber size equals its orbit size, giving a nontrivial normal subloop of prime-power order strictly less than $`p^a q`$. For any characteristic $`q`$-subgroup $`C`$, a nontrivial action of $`t`$ would similarly give a normal subloop of order $`q`$, as its physical orbit size divides $`q`$. Thus $`C=O_q(D)`$ is centralized by $`t`$ and by the group generated by its conjugates. Put $`N=O_p(D)`$. This is nontrivial since $`F(D)=N\times C`$ cannot be central (otherwise $`D`$ is abelian, and the physical loop is an abelian group). It is not a full Sylow subgroup. Let

```math
X=D/F(D),\qquad W=N/\Phi(N).
```

Here $`X`$ has a faithful linear action on the $`\mathbb F_p`$-space $`W`$, with compatible $`t`$; no assertion that $`N`$ is abelian is needed. Indeed $`O_p(X)=1`$: the preimage of a normal $`p`$-subgroup is the product of its central $`p'`$-subgroup $`C`$ with its unique Sylow $`p`$-subgroup, which is normal in $`D`$ and hence lies in $`N`$. Also $`C_D(N)\le F(D)`$, since $`C`$ is central and $`F(D)`$ contains its centralizer. The kernel of $`\operatorname{Aut}(N)\to\operatorname{GL}(W)`$ is a $`p`$-group by the Burnside basis theorem: fixing an ordered basis of $`W`$, the automorphism kernel acts freely on the $`|\Phi(N)|^{\dim W}`$ ordered lifts, which all generate $`N`$. Inner automorphisms of $`N`$ and $`C`$ act trivially on $`W`$, so the kernel in $`X`$ is a normal $`p`$-group and is trivial. Since $`N`$ is not Sylow and $`C`$ is prime to $`p`$, we have $`p\mid |X|`$.

Write $`b_N=[N:C_N(t)]=p^s`$. The class of $`t`$ projects to an outside class of size $`p^{a-s}q`$ in $`X\rtimes\langle t\rangle`$, since $`t`$ fixes $`C`$. The negative dimension $`r`$ of $`t`$ on $`W`$ satisfies $`r\le s`$: every coset fixed in $`N/\Phi(N)`$ has a fixed representative by Sylow 2-conjugacy in the odd kernel, or equivalently the negative class size in the quotient divides the original one.

As $`X`$ is solvable with prime spectrum contained in $`\{p,q\}`$ and $`O_p(X)=1`$, its Fitting subgroup is the nontrivial $`R=O_q(X)`$. It is not centralized by $`t`$: otherwise it would be central in $`X`$, forcing $`X=R`$ despite $`p\mid |X|`$. Its outside-class fiber divides $`p^{a-s}q`$, so $`[R:C_R(t)]=q`$. The outside class of $`X/R`$ now has $`p`$-power size. Multiplying by $`t`$ gives the full inverted set, a generating, 2-divisible twisted subset of the odd group $`X/R`$; its cardinality is a $`p`$-power, so [FKP Theorem 4.17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0208231v2) makes $`X/R`$ a $`p`$-group. Hence $`R`$ is a normal Sylow $`q`$-subgroup and there is a $`t`$-stable Sylow $`p`$-subgroup $`P`$ complementary to $`R`$: $`t`$ acts on the odd number of Sylow $`p`$-subgroups and fixes one. We mean a Sylow $`p`$-subgroup $`P`$ with $`X=R P`$, $`R\cap P=1`$.

We claim

```math
X=V\rtimes P,\quad V=R\text{ elementary abelian over }\mathbb F_q,\quad
V\text{ a faithful absolutely irreducible reflection module for }P\rtimes\langle t\rangle.
```

As in the cube-free proof, $`[R,t]`$ is central cyclic of order $`q`$, and every signed conjugate of $`t`$ has the same property on $`R`$. The span of the commutator lines is $`E_X`$-normal, central in $`R`$, and elementary abelian, where $`E_X=X\rtimes\langle t\rangle`$. All of $`E_X`$ is generated by those involutions. Thus $`M=[R,P]`$ is central in $`R`$ and elementary abelian. It is nonzero because otherwise $`P\le C_X(R)\le F(X)=R`$; it is $`P,t`$-stable and $`R`$-normal. Coprime action gives $`R=M C_R(P)`$: for each $`x\in R`$, its $`P`$-conjugation displacements give a 1-cocycle in the central $`q`$-space $`M`$; averaging over $`P`$ makes that cocycle a coboundary, so a suitable translate of $`x`$ centralizes $`P`$. This equality gives $`M=[M,P]`$; coprime averaging on $`M`$ now gives $`M^P=0`$ and $`R=M\times C_R(P)`$. The action of $`t`$ on $`M`$ is nontrivial (else this $`E_X`$-normal group would be central, contrary to $`[M,P]=M`$). Therefore all the $`q`$-fiber at $`R`$ is spent on $`M`$, and $`t`$ fixes $`C_R(P)`$ pointwise. The direct factor $`C_R(P)`$ is normal in $`E_X`$, hence central there. The resulting direct product $`X=(M\rtimes P)\times C_R(P)`$ is $`t`$-stable factor by factor. The generating inverted set projects trivially onto the fixed odd factor, so $`C_R(P)=1`$.

The action of $`P`$ on $`V`$ is faithful by Fitting self-centralization. Since $`|P\rtimes\langle t\rangle|`$ is prime to $`q`$, its action is semisimple. The group $`P\rtimes\langle t\rangle`$, identified with the signed quotient modulo $`R`$, is generated by the conjugates of $`t`$. The negative dimension on $`V`$ is only one. Thus at most one constituent is nontrivial, and $`V^P=0`$ eliminates trivial constituents. The same Frobenius-conjugate argument as in prime descent makes the active constituent absolutely irreducible. By the [rank-one lemma](odd-cubefree-normal-tower.md#rank-one-prime-descent) we obtain $`p<q`$, and the representation over the algebraic closure is monomial of dimension two or three with weight permutation group $`S_d`$. Dimension one would give $`P=1`$. Dimension three requires $`p=3`$, since the odd part of the weight permutation quotient contains 3. In dimension two, odd $`P`$ is diagonal, and has determinant one since all rotations are even products of reflections. Thus $`P=C_h`$ with $`h=p^k`$, its eigencharacters are faithful reciprocals, and $`t`$ inverts it. In particular this conclusion holds whenever $`p\ge5`$. Conversely whenever $`P`$ is cyclic, its faithful irreducible $`P\rtimes\langle t\rangle`$ representation has dimension two: its abelian normal $`P`$ has weights in orbits of size at most two, and a single scalar weight or a nonsimple multiplicity cannot give this representation.

The exact budget is

```math
a=s+k,\qquad k=\log_p[P:C_P(t)]\ge1. \tag{1}
```

Indeed the outside class in $`X`$ has $`q[P:C_P(t)]`$ elements by the $`t`$-stable split extension and the fiber $`q`$. The complement is generated by its $`t`$-inverted elements and is nontrivial, so the index is greater than one.

## Physical prime field versus the character orbits

Extend $`W`$ to the algebraic closure of $`\mathbb F_p`$ and decompose it by weights of the elementary abelian $`V`$. It is faithful on $`V`$, so there are nontrivial characters. Each nontrivial weight has order $`q`$; Frobenius acts on it as multiplication by $`p`$, with orbit length

```math
f=\operatorname{ord}_q(p)\ge3.
```

Here $`q>p`$ is odd and hence cannot divide $`p-1`$ or $`p+1`$. The signed group permutes weight spaces and commutes with arithmetic Frobenius on the weight set.

Always $`r\ge f`$. Otherwise no conjugate of $`t`$ can interchange two different Frobenius orbits: that would move at least $`f`$ pairs of weight spaces, contributing at least $`f`$ negative dimensions. If every generator preserves every such orbit, on each it acts as a power of the Frobenius cycle, hence with order at most two. Their generated permutation image is abelian of exponent at most two, and the odd group $`X`$ acts trivially on all weights. Faithfulness on $`V`$ would make $`P`$ centralize $`V`$, impossible. By (1), at $`p=3`$ this already gives $`a\ge4`$. If $`p=3`$ and $`a=4`$, then $`f=3`$, as required for the boundary.

For $`p\ge5`$ we know $`P=C_h`$, $`h=p^k`$, acting with faithful reciprocal eigencharacters. Every nonzero character of $`V`$ has a **free $`P`$-orbit** of size $`h`$: every nonidentity element of $`P`$ has no eigenvalue 1 on $`V`$ or its dual. If $`t`$ interchanges two such orbits, it costs at least $`h`$ negative dimensions. Otherwise choose any $`t`$-stable nonzero $`P`$-orbit. Since $`t`$ inverts $`P`$, on this odd regular cycle it acts as a reflection with exactly one fixed point, contributing at least $`(h-1)/2`$ negative dimensions. There are at least $`f`$ distinct Frobenius translates of this orbit, all $`t`$-stable: a Frobenius power preserving the orbit commutes with both the regular cycle and inversion, and so fixes the orbit pointwise (the dihedral centralizer on an odd cycle is trivial). Its exponent must then be divisible by $`f`$. Consequently

```math
r\ge\min\{h,\, f(h-1)/2\}=h .
```

Equation (1) gives $`a\ge k+\max\{p^k,f\}\ge p+1`$.

Suppose $`p\ge5`$ and $`a=p+1`$. Then $`k=1`$, $`r=s=p`$. A $`t`$-stable $`P`$-orbit would cost at least $`f(p-1)/2>p`$. Thus the weight set contains a pair of $`P`$-orbits exchanged by $`t`$, contributing the entire negative budget. Its Frobenius translate is again such a pair, so it must be the same pair: disjoint pairs would each cost $`p`$. This set of $`2p`$ characters is a **regular dihedral orbit**; Frobenius acts in the centralizer of the regular dihedral group of order $`2p`$. The latter is an opposite regular dihedral group and therefore has element orders $`1,2,p`$, not $`2p`$. The order of Frobenius on this orbit is precisely $`f`$. Thus $`f=p`$. Together with the $`p=3`$ calculation, this establishes global necessity at the boundary.

For sufficiency, $`f=p`$ means $`q\mid(p^p-1)/(p-1)`$; conversely any prime there has order $`p`$, since $`\gcd(p-1,(p^p-1)/(p-1))=1`$. [the two-prime construction, Theorem 1.1(3)](../../sources/foundations/C0003.md) supplies a finite simple proper left Bol loop of the claimed degree for every such pair. Its [reviewed folder calculation](../../sources/odd-completion/odd-stabilizer-alignment.md#the-known-simple-family-realizes-the-orbit-obstruction) is context, not a claim that this construction exhausts isomorphism classes at a fixed degree.

Finally for $`a\le p`$ every noncyclic simple factor of a hypothetical nonsolvable loop would have order dividing $`p^a q`$, by [FKP Strong Lagrange](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0208231v2). A pure odd prime-power Bol loop is solvable: its left multiplication group is a prime-power group by FKP 6.7, a maximal subgroup containing the proper point stabilizer is normal of index the prime, and reduction to the cyclic quotient respects the loop product. Induct on the kernel. The only other possible nonassociative simple degrees $`p^b q`$, $`1\le b\le a`$, were just excluded. Equivalently induct directly on proper normal subloops and quotients, using their smaller orders. This proves classical solvability, not the existence of any prescribed Sylow subloop.

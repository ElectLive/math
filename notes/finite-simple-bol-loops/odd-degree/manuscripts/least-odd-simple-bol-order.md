# The minimum odd degree of a simple nonassociative Bol loop

**Mira Cult**

The least odd order of a finite simple nonassociative left Bol loop is **1053**. Nagy supplied the example. The issue settled here is that no smaller odd order is possible, not uniqueness at 1053. The proof uses the [cube-free normal tower](odd-cubefree-normal-tower.md), the [two-prime threshold](two-prime-exponent-threshold.md), and two further obstructions proved below.

## Frattini detection on the other primes

Throughout the argument by contradiction $`Q`$ is simple nonassociative of the indicated odd order $`n`$. Use the doubled odd group $`D`$, formal involution $`t`$, conjugate generating signed marks, and the physical normal-cut argument of the cube-free manuscript. Put

```math
N_p=O_p(D),\quad F=F(D)=\prod_p N_p,\quad X=D/F,\quad
W_p=N_p/\Phi(N_p).
```

An absent core contributes a zero space. On each $`W_p`$ conjugation by $`D`$ factors through $`X`$. For nontrivial $`N_p`$, write $`r_p=\dim W_p^{-}(t)`$, $`p^{s_p}=[N_p:C_{N_p}(t)]`$. If the degree has at least two primes, the original physical cut gives

```math
r_p\le s_p\le v_p(n)-1. \tag{1}
```

The first inequality follows by passage modulo the $`t`$-stable Frattini subgroup (odd-kernel centralizer lifting). For the second, the full physical orbit size $`\Delta_p\le p^{v_p(n)}`$ is a multiple of $`p^{s_p}`$, and equality with the mark-fiber size gives a genuine normal subloop of that positive prime-power order. The orbit is nontrivial even in the doubled presentation by the two projections. Thus equality is impossible by simplicity. In particular a prime occurring once has $`t`$ centralizing its core; all of $`D\rtimes\langle t\rangle`$ then centralizes it.

For any prime $`p`$, the normal group $`O_p(X)`$ injects into the product of the conjugation images on the **other** $`W_\ell`$, $`\ell\ne p`$. This does not assume that $`X`$ acts faithfully or semisimply on each individual module. Indeed let $`A`$ be the kernel in $`O_p(X)`$ on these other spaces, and $`L`$ its preimage in $`D`$. A Sylow $`p`$-subgroup $`S`$ of $`L`$ acts trivially on each $`N_\ell`$, $`\ell\ne p`$: it acts trivially modulo $`\Phi(N_\ell)`$ and that automorphism kernel is an $`\ell`$-group, by the ordered-basis lift argument in the two-prime source. Now $`L=(\prod_{\ell\ne p}N_\ell) S`$ by orders, with commuting factors. Thus $`S`$ is unique, characteristic in $`L`$, normal in $`D`$, and contained in $`N_p`$. This forces $`A=1`$.

## A low-rank obstruction that allows central odd rotations

Let $`Y`$ be odd, $`Y\rtimes\langle t\rangle`$ generated by the conjugates of $`t`$, and $`W`$ any $`\mathbb F_p`$-representation with negative dimension $`r`$. It need not be irreducible. For an odd prime $`\ell\ne p`$, let $`A`$ be a normal $`\ell`$-subgroup of this signed group. If

```math
r<\min\{\ell,\mathrm{ord}_\ell(p)\}, \tag{2}
```

then the image of $`A`$ on $`W`$ is abelian and **centralized by $`Y`$**. It need not be centralized by $`t`$, nor is the image asserted trivial.

Restrict over the algebraic closure to $`A`$, semisimply by coprimality. Every nonlinear irreducible constituent has dimension at least $`\ell`$, since irreducible degrees of an $`\ell`$-group in cross characteristic are powers of $`\ell`$. For an elementary proof of the needed bound, factor out the kernel of an irreducible representation. In the nonabelian faithful image, the center is scalar and an element of the second center outside the center has a nontrivial scalar commutator. Conjugation cycles its eigenvalues in strings of length at least $`\ell`$, yielding the same degree bound. In either the linear or nonlinear case, a nontrivial irreducible representation of $`A`$ has a nonidentity scalar in its image: take an element central in the nontrivial image group. Taking a power gives a primitive $`\ell`$-th root. Its isomorphism type therefore has Frobenius orbit length divisible by $`f=\mathrm{ord}_\ell(p)`$, since any returning Frobenius power fixes that scalar on the same group element.

The involution must preserve every nonlinear isotypic component individually, as swapping two would cost at least $`\ell`$ negative dimensions. Its negative ranks on the Frobenius conjugates of any such component are equal, so $`r<f`$ makes them all zero. The sum of all nonlinear components is invariant under the signed group; all conjugates of $`t`$ act trivially on it, impossible for a nonzero such sum. Thus only linear weights occur. Conjugates of $`t`$ cannot exchange two distinct Frobenius orbits of nontrivial weights, at a cost of at least $`f`$ negative dimensions. Each acts on each preserved Frobenius cycle as a commuting permutation, hence as a power of the cycle. The generated permutation image on the weight set is consequently abelian of exponent at most two; odd $`Y`$ fixes each weight. On an individual weight space $`A`$ acts by scalars, so its image commutes with $`Y`$. This proves (2).

## Exclusion I: $`3^a m`$, $`a\le3`$, $`m`$ odd square-free and coprime to 3

The cube-free case is already known in this chain. Assume $`a=3`$, $`m>1`$. The cores $`N_\ell`$ for $`\ell\mid m`$ are central by (1). The cross-prime injection gives $`O_3(X)=1`$. For each $`\ell\mid m`$, the group $`O_\ell(X)`$ injects into the action on $`W_3`$, as all the other possible non-$`\ell`$ actions are trivial. In characteristic three, $`r_3\le2`$, every prime $`\ell>3`$ has $`\mathrm{ord}_\ell(3)>2`$ (the odd divisors of $`3^2-1`$ are absent), and $`O_\ell(X)`$ is normal in the signed group. Thus (2) makes $`O_\ell(X)`$ central in $`X`$. We have proved $`F(X)\le Z(X)`$. By solvable Fitting self-centralization, $`X`$ is abelian. Yet $`3\mid |X|`$: if not, $`N_3`$ would be a nontrivial normal Sylow subgroup of $`D`$, giving a nontrivial proper normal 3-subloop by the physical cut. This contradicts $`O_3(X)=1`$. This excludes, in particular, order 945. By induction on subloops and quotients, every Bol loop of these odd orders is classically solvable: all non-prime-power divisors have the same form, and odd prime-power Bol loops are solvable as in the two-prime source.

## Exclusion II: $`675=3^3 5^2`$

Here $`O_5(X)`$ still injects on $`W_3`$ and is central in $`X`$ by (2), as $`\mathrm{ord}_5(3)=4`$. The other group $`O_3(X)`$ injects on $`W_5`$ and has order at most three. To see this, $`r_5\le1`$. For zero negative rank the action is trivial. Otherwise the proof of the cube-free rank-one lemma applies to the single active absolutely irreducible constituent of $`W_5`$: the kernel on it, in the total linear image, is a 5-group. In dimension one the odd rotation image is trivial. Dimension three in characteristic 5 is impossible: the three monomial weight lines are prime-field rational, while their diagonal kernel is odd and all its entries have order dividing $`5-1=4`$; the required nonscalar abelian diagonal subgroup would vanish. In dimension two all odd rotations are diagonal over the algebraic closure, of determinant one, and the eigenvalues lie in $`\mathbb F_{25}`$ and are either both rational or conjugate reciprocals. Their orders divide 4 or 6, respectively; the odd diagonal rotation subgroup is cyclic of order at most 3. Hence $`O_3(X)`$ has order at most three and is central in the odd group $`X`$ (the automorphism group of $`C_3`$ has order two). Again $`F(X)`$ is central, so $`X`$ is abelian.

Unlike exclusion I, this does not alone contradict simplicity: a central 5-subgroup in the quotient can still act by nontrivial $`3`$-module rotations. Use the physical stabilizer $`H`$. Its normal closure in $`D`$ is $`D`$, because projection to $`D/\langle H^D\rangle`$ is a surjective loop-to-group homomorphism (all cosets are occupied and $`ab\in cH`$ projects to equality). A simple nonassociative loop has no nontrivial group quotient. As $`X`$ is abelian, each $`H^g`$ surjects onto $`X`$. The same is true in the second projected physical action, whose point stabilizers are conjugates of $`tHt`$.

Let $`L`$ be the preimage in $`D`$ of the full 5-part of $`X`$. It is normal in $`D`$, has normal Hall 3-subgroup $`N=N_3`$, and contains the full 5-part of $`D`$. Fix a Sylow 5-subgroup $`S`$ of $`L`$. Consider first the physical action by the first projection; for any $`N`$-orbit $`B`$, take a point $`x\in B`$. The image of $`N H_x`$ on $`B`$ is solvable transitive of degree $`3^i\le27`$, so has no prime divisor 5. Indeed a faithful primitive solvable action of such degree has an elementary abelian regular minimal normal subgroup, hence lies in $`\mathrm{AGL}_i(3)`$. For $`i=1,2,3`$, none of $`3^j-1,\ 1\le j\le i`$, is divisible by 5. For an imprimitive action use an invariant partition: the kernel of the action on its blocks embeds into the product of the induced block stabilizers on individual blocks, while the action on blocks has smaller 3-power degree. Induct on $`i`$. The degree-one case is immediate.

Choose a Sylow 5-subgroup $`T_x`$ of $`H_x\cap L`$. It surjects onto the 5-part of $`X`$, since $`H_x`$ surjects onto $`X`$. It fixes $`B`$ pointwise by the preceding paragraph. Extend it to a Sylow 5-subgroup $`S_x`$ of $`L`$; then $`S_x=N_5 T_x`$ by orders and $`N_5\triangleleft D`$. Since $`N_5`$ centralizes $`N`$, the entire $`[N,S_x]`$ acts trivially on $`B`$. All Sylow 5-subgroups of $`L`$ are $`N`$-conjugate since $`L=SN`$ and Sylow conjugacy holds. The pointwise kernel on $`B`$ inside $`N`$ is normal in $`N`$, so $`[N,S]`$ also fixes $`B`$ pointwise. This holds on **every** physical block, hence $`[N,S]`$ vanishes under the first projection. Apply the identical argument to the second projection and its stabilizers. The two kernels intersect trivially, so $`[N,S]=1`$ in $`D`$. Now $`L=NS`$ with commuting factors; $`S`$ is unique, characteristic in $`L`$, and a normal Sylow 5-subgroup of $`D`$. This contradicts the proper normal-subloop cut.

## The exhaustive order sieve, and the upper witness

Below 1053, split the odd degrees into prime powers, cube-free degrees, degrees $`p^a q`$ with two distinct odd primes, and the rest. Pure prime powers give no nonassociative simple Bol loops (the elementary prime-index projection induction is in the two-prime source). Remove all cube-free degrees. In the two-prime one-singleton case remove $`q<p`$, $`a<p+1`$, and $`a=p+1`$ with $`\mathrm{ord}_q(p)\ne p`$ by the pinned two-prime theorem. Elementary trial division of the odd integers from 3 through 1053 inclusive leaves exactly

```math
675=3^3 5^2,\quad 945=3^3\cdot5\cdot7,\quad 1053=3^4\cdot13.
```

The first two are impossible above. The last has $`\mathrm{ord}_{13}(3)=3`$; [the two-prime construction, Theorem 1.1(3)](../../sources/foundations/C0003.md) supplies the full simple proper loop of order 1053. [Nagy, Example IV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0703919) is the original order-1053 construction; that preprint proves a nonassociative simple quotient there and records a computational check of simplicity of the full loop. The lower bound just proved forces that nonassociative simple quotient to have at least 1053 elements, so its kernel is trivial. This uses his analytic quotient argument without relying on a computer assertion about simplicity. No uniqueness or historical priority is claimed.

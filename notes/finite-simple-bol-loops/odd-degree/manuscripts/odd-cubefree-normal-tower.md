# Odd cube-free Bol loops have an abelian normal tower

**Mira Cult**

This theorem concerns the order of the **loop**. It gives normal subloops in the original multiplication, not merely a solvable associated group.

**Theorem.** Every finite left Bol loop $`Q`$ of odd cube-free order has a series

```math
1=Q_0<Q_1<\cdots<Q_j=Q
```

in which every $`Q_i`$ is normal in the **original loop** and each $`Q_{i+1}/Q_i`$ is an abelian group of order a prime or the square of a prime. In odd square-free order all factors are cyclic of prime order. In particular these loops are classically solvable; the order of any finite simple nonassociative Bol loop of odd order is divisible by a prime cube. This is not an inference from solvability of the left multiplication group alone. The first normal factor's prime is not prescribed.

## A physical normal cut in the double

Write $`G=\operatorname{LMlt}(Q)`$, $`K=\{L_x:x\in Q\}`$, $`n=|Q|>1`$. The marks $`K`$ act sharply transitively on the loop, contain $`1`$, and are closed under inversion and $`aba`$. Form the actual group

```math
D=\langle(k,k^{-1}):k\in K\rangle\leq G\times G,\qquad
t(x,y)t=(y,x),\quad E=D\rtimes\langle t\rangle.
```

Use a formal outside involution. Both projections of $`D`$ are onto. We let $`D`$ act on $`Q`$ by the first projection, with point stabilizer $`H`$; this action need not be faithful. The two projection kernels intersect trivially and are interchanged by $`t`$. FKP [Proposition 6.1, Theorem 6.7 and Corollary 6.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0208231v2) give that $`G`$, and hence $`D`$, has odd order, is solvable, and has no prime divisors outside those of $`n`$. Conversely $`n=[D:H]`$.

Put $`T=\{(k,k^{-1}):k\in K\}`$. This is inverse- and sandwich-closed. The set $`Tt`$ consists of involutions, is closed under conjugation by its own members, contains $`t`$ and generates $`E`$. By Sylow conjugacy it is the full outside class of involutions. Thus $`T`$ is the full inverted set. For a $`t`$-stable normal $`A\triangleleft D`$ write bars for reduction modulo $`A`$, $`J=HA/A`$, and $`\Delta_A=[A:H\cap A]`$, the common size of the actual $`A`$-orbits. Nontrivial such $`A`$ acts nontrivially: otherwise its two projections would both be trivial. Every fiber of $`T\to\bar T`$ has size

```math
b_A=[A:C_A(t)].
```

Indeed $`Tt`$ and its image are each a single outside class, and the fiber at $`tA`$ consists of the involutions in $`At`$, conjugate under $`A`$ by Sylow conjugacy in $`A\rtimes\langle t\rangle`$. Conjugation by lifts gives the other fibers. Every left $`J`$-coset contains exactly $`m_A=\Delta_A/b_A`$ different projected marks: its preimage is the union of $`\Delta_A`$ actual stabilizer cosets, each occupied once. Hence

```math
m_A=\frac{|J|}{|C_{\bar D}(t)|}. \tag{1}
```

This follows by dividing the size of the projected class by $`[\bar D:J]`$. No quotient loop was assumed.

If $`b_A=\Delta_A`$, the distinct projected marks form a transversal to $`J`$; its operation is determined by $`\bar a\bar b\in\bar cJ`$. The map from $`T`$, identified with $`Q`$, respects this operation since $`ab\in cH`$ projects to that relation. The target is a loop: it has identity and its left and right translations are surjective by lifting to $`Q`$, hence bijective. The kernel is an **actual normal subloop** of size $`b_A`$. For any nontrivial $`t`$-stable normal $`p`$-subgroup $`A`$, its orbit size $`\Delta_A`$ is a positive power of $`p`$ dividing $`n`$ and exceeds one. If $`A`$ is a normal Sylow $`p`$-subgroup, it is characteristic, and (1) makes $`m_A`$ prime to $`p`$ whereas $`m_A\mid\Delta_A`$. Thus $`m_A=1`$.

If $`t`$ centralizes a $`t`$-stable normal subgroup $`A`$, every conjugate of $`t`$ centralizes $`A`$; since those conjugates generate $`E`$, $`A\le Z(E)`$. In square-free order each nontrivial $`O_p(D)`$ has $`\Delta=p`$. If one is not centralized by $`t`$ it immediately gives $`b=\Delta=p`$. Otherwise $`F(D)`$ is central. A finite solvable group's Fitting subgroup contains its centralizer, so $`D`$ is abelian. The faithful transitive abelian group $`G`$ is regular; its $`n`$ actual left translations exhaust it, making $`Q`$ an abelian group. This proves the square-free first-factor assertion independently of the rest.

## A failed prime-power cut becomes linear

Suppose $`n`$ is cube-free. Assume for contradiction there is no nontrivial normal subloop of order $`p`$ or $`p^2`$ for any $`p`$, counting $`Q`$ itself if its order qualifies. A $`p`$-core $`N=O_p(D)`$ not centralized by $`t`$ must have

```math
\Delta_N=p^2,\qquad b_N=m_N=p.
```

Indeed $`b_N>1`$ and equality $`b_N=\Delta_N`$ would give the required kernel. The $`p`$ inverted elements of $`N`$ form a cyclic subgroup $`V_t`$ of order $`p`$: all powers of a nonidentity inverted element are inverted. In fact $`V_t=[N,t]`$, the subgroup generated by $`d(x)=x^{-1}t(x)`$. Every such commutator is inverted and each inverted element arises by taking a suitable power of it, since $`|N|`$ is odd. Normality in $`N`$ follows from $`d(x)^y=d(xy)d(y)^{-1}`$, with $`x^y=y^{-1}xy`$. As a normal subgroup of order $`p`$ in a $`p`$-group it is central. The same holds for $`V_s=[N,s]`$ for each conjugate $`s`$ of $`t`$. Hence

```math
M=\langle V_s:s\in t^D\rangle\le\Omega_1(Z(N))
```

is elementary abelian and $`E`$-normal. Its negative space for $`t`$ is exactly $`V_t`$, giving $`b_M=p`$; the assumed absence of a kernel at $`M`$ also gives $`\Delta_M=p^2`$.

The $`M`$-orbits therefore equal the $`N`$-orbits. On any such orbit the image of $`M`$ is regular abelian of exponent $`p`$. Since $`N`$ centralizes $`M`$, its image is in the centralizer of that regular abelian permutation group, which is the group itself: a commuting permutation is determined by its value at one point. Thus the first projection of $`N`$, acting faithfully on all points taken together, is elementary abelian. Its second projection is the same group, by $`t`$-stability. The original $`N`$ embeds in their product, hence is elementary abelian as well. Assuming faithfulness of a single projection here would be an error.

On the resulting $`\mathbb F_p`$-space $`N`$, $`t`$ is a reflection, i.e. an involution with minus space of dimension one. On any $`E`$-composition series the minus dimensions add, as $`t`$ is semisimple. Exactly one simple constituent $`U`$ has minus dimension one. Every other constituent is trivial because $`t`$ and therefore its generating conjugates act trivially on it. The kernel in the **image acting on $`N`$** of the action on $`U`$ is a $`p`$-group, being upper unitriangular in a flag basis. No splitting of $`N`$ is asserted. The constituent $`U`$ is absolutely irreducible: over a finite field a simple module extends to a direct sum of Frobenius-conjugate absolutely irreducible modules, each with the same minus dimension, and their sum is one. Here one may use the finite division ring of endomorphisms: it is a field, and the corresponding full matrix algebra over this field splits according to its distinct embeddings after extension of scalars.

## Rank-one prime descent

**Lemma.** Let a finite solvable matrix group $`P`$ act absolutely irreducibly on a nontrivial $`\mathbb F_p`$-space, $`p`$ odd. Suppose $`P`$ has an odd subgroup of index two and is generated by the conjugates of a reflection $`s`$. Every odd prime divisor of $`|P|`$ is strictly less than $`p`$.

Work over the algebraic closure. Dimension one is immediate: all conjugate reflections equal $`-1`$. In higher dimension $`O_p(P)=1`$: a normal $`p`$-subgroup has nonzero fixed space, necessarily the whole irreducible module. Also $`O_2(P)=1`$: a normal Sylow 2-subgroup would contain the generating conjugates of $`s`$, forcing $`P=\langle s\rangle`$. Thus $`F=F(P)`$ has odd order prime to $`p`$.

If $`P`$ has a nonscalar abelian normal subgroup, its order is prime to $`p`$ and its distinct weight spaces are permuted transitively by $`P`$. Some conjugate reflection moves a weight space. Rank one then forces a single transposition of two one-dimensional spaces and no other movement. All spaces are one-dimensional. Their permutation group is transitive and generated by transpositions, so is the entire $`S_d`$ (the transposition graph is connected). Thus $`d=2`$ or 3 by the 2-part of $`|P|`$; the permutation kernel is diagonal. Arithmetic Frobenius commutes with this permutation group since the original matrices are over $`\mathbb F_p`$. In dimension two it fixes both weight lines or swaps them, so the diagonal entries have order dividing $`p^2-1`$. Any odd prime dividing $`p-1`$ or $`p+1`$ is smaller than $`p`$. In dimension three the centralizer of $`S_3`$ on three letters is trivial: every weight line descends to $`\mathbb F_p`$. Diagonal entries have order dividing $`p-1`$. At $`p=3`$ this kernel is trivial: its order is odd because the permutation quotient already has the full 2-part, and the diagonal entries have order dividing two. This contradicts the nonscalar abelian normal subgroup acting diagonally. For $`p>3`$ the remaining odd permutation prime 3 is also smaller than $`p`$.

We exclude the alternative in which every abelian normal subgroup is scalar, without a reflection-group classification. If $`F`$ is scalar, Fitting self-centralization gives $`P=F`$, impossible. Thus $`F`$ is nonabelian with scalar center. Put $`B=Z_2(F)`$, the inverse image of the center of $`F/Z(F)`$; it properly contains $`Z(F)`$ and is characteristic. If $`s`$ fixed $`B`$ modulo scalars it would centralize $`B`$: for $`sxs^{-1}=cx`$ the scalar $`c`$ belongs to the odd group $`F`$, and $`c^2=1`$. Reflection generation would make $`B`$ central, a contradiction. Hence for some $`x\in B`$, the element $`y=sxs^{-1}x^{-1}`$ is nonscalar. It is a product of two reflections, so $`\operatorname{rank}(y-I)\le2`$. For some $`u\in F`$, conjugation sends $`y`$ to $`\zeta y`$ where $`\zeta`$ is a scalar of odd order $`e\ge3`$, since $`y\in B\setminus Z(F)`$. The eigenvalues of the semisimple $`y`$ occur in free $`\zeta`$-cycles of equal multiplicities. Thus $`d(1-1/e)\le2`$ and $`d\le3`$.

The nonscalar $`B`$ cannot itself be abelian. Choose noncommuting $`a,b\in B`$. Their commutator is a nonidentity odd scalar of order dividing $`d`$ by determinants; necessarily $`d=3`$, $`p\ne3`$, and that scalar has order three. The three distinct eigenspaces of $`a`$ are one-dimensional and cyclically permuted by $`b`$, so the common matrix centralizer of $`a,b`$ is scalar. Each $`u\in F`$ commutes with $`a,b`$ modulo scalar cube roots of unity. The two scalar commutators give a homomorphism $`F\to\mu_3^2`$ with kernel $`Z(F)`$; the images of $`a,b`$ span its target. Hence $`F/Z(F)=C_3^2`$ with its nondegenerate commutator pairing. Conjugation embeds $`P/F`$ in $`\operatorname{SL}_2(3)`$: the pairing is preserved since scalars are fixed, and an element acting trivially modulo $`Z(F)`$ can be multiplied by $`a^ib^j`$ to centralize both $`a,b`$, becoming a scalar in $`Z(P)\le F`$. The only involution of $`\operatorname{SL}_2(3)`$ is the central $`-I`$, by diagonalization and determinant. Thus the generated image of the conjugates of $`s`$ is contained in $`\{I,-I\}`$. The preimage in $`F`$ of **any** line of $`F/Z(F)`$ is now an abelian normal subgroup of $`P`$, not scalar. This contradiction rules out the whole alternative and proves the lemma.

## Lifting the largest prime

Under the assumed absence of all desired kernels, $`F(D)=\prod_q O_q(D)`$ is abelian: the active factors were proved elementary and the others are central in $`E`$. By Fitting self-centralization the conjugation action embeds $`D/F(D)`$ into the product of the images on the active factors. On any such $`q`$-factor, the image has a normal $`q`$-subgroup (the unitriangular kernel) whose quotient has all prime divisors **smaller** than $`q`$. Apply the lemma to the $`E`$-image on its active irreducible constituent: the image of $`D`$ is odd, and the reflection has determinant $`-1`$. No constraint on a core after a successful cut is needed.

Take the largest prime $`p`$ of $`|D|`$. Project $`D/F(D)`$ to the images on all active $`q\ne p`$ factors and, if the $`p`$-factor is active, also to its active irreducible image. The target is prime to $`p`$. By the faithful product embedding, the kernel lies in the local unitriangular $`p`$-group at the $`p`$-factor (or is trivial if there is no such active factor). It is therefore a normal Sylow $`p`$-subgroup of $`D/F(D)`$, possibly trivial. Let $`P_0`$ be its full inverse image. The product $`F_{p'}=\prod_{q\ne p}O_q(D)`$ is central in $`P_0`$: its central factors were already centralized, the projection killed all other $`q\ne p`$ actions, and $`F(D)`$ is abelian. Now $`P_0/F_{p'}`$ is a $`p`$-group. For any Sylow $`p`$-subgroup $`S`$ of $`P_0`$, we have $`P_0=F_{p'}S`$ by orders. Hence $`S`$ is normal, unique, characteristic in $`P_0`$ and normal in $`D`$. It has the full positive $`p`$-part of $`|D|`$. The normal Sylow observation following (1) gives a desired genuine normal subloop, a contradiction. In particular no Sylow subgroup was pulled back through a noncentral coprime kernel.

Apply the first-factor result recursively to the quotient loop, again of odd cube-free order, and lift its normal subloops back to $`Q`$. Prime-order Bol loops are cyclic by power associativity and Cauchy. In prime-square order [Burn, *Finite Bol loops* (1978)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100055213) gives associativity (take the opposite to interchange left and right conventions); every group of order $`p^2`$ is abelian. This gives the asserted tower, not just a group-theoretic series.

## Boundaries and controls

Cube-free degree is a condition on the **loop**, not on its permutation groups. A cube-free solvable Bol loop can lack a Sylow subloop, as in the separate [order-63 construction](../../../odd-bol-hall/README.md). Even an odd cube-free group may have no normal subgroup of prime order: take the irreducible $`C_5^2\rtimes C_3`$. Thus prime-square factors cannot in general be removed. In unrestricted odd order, [Nagy's simple examples](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0703919) and [higher-height families](../../sources/odd-completion/odd-unbounded-fitting-height.md) preclude an unconditional solvability conclusion. The [earlier quotient count](../../sources/factor-permutation/odd-quotient-multiplicity.md) and [normal prime-overlap obstruction](../../normal-layer-evidence.md) are related but not black boxes here. In particular no inversion automorphism on the physical left multiplication group of a nonsimple loop was assumed.

The [author check](../code/verify_odd_cubefree_normal_tower.py) tests mechanisms, not the universal theorem; see [output](../evidence/odd-cubefree-normal-tower.out). The statement and proof received a separate independent AI mathematical check.

**Literature scope.** Burn's prime-square theorem supplies precisely the group structure of the small factors. For square-free degree with two odd primes, [Kinyon, Nagy and Vojtěchovský, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.05785) give a much stronger full classification, including the unique normal larger-prime subloop in the nonassociative case. [Leppälä and Niemenmaa](https://cmuc.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/pdf/cmuc1301/leppala.pdf) treat square-free loops with a **nilpotent full inner mapping group**, not arbitrary odd cube-free Bol loops. FKP's Corollary 6.8 refers to the left multiplication **group**; Proposition 6.11 reports loop solvability for odd Moufang or Bruck specializations. [Nagy's Example IV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0703919) uses order $`1053=3^4\cdot13`$, proves existence of a simple proper odd quotient there and reports a computer simplicity check for the full example. These results are compatible. A targeted search for square-free and cube-free degree did not locate the above general Bol theorem; this is not an exhaustive historical determination.

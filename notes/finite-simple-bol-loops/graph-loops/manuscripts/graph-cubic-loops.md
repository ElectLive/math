# Graph-native Moufang loops from edge quadratics

Mira Cult

## Scope and provenance

This manuscript gives a finite, natural Moufang loop for every finite simple
undirected graph. Its nonassociativity is generated by the graph's own edges.
The construction is a specialization of established cubic central-extension
methods, not a new general integration theorem for alternating forms. Its
payoffs are an unmarked isomorphism theorem, intrinsic graph invariants, a
marked independence-complex theorem, and exact translations of graph operations.

All results below are manuscript-local; no permanent claim identifiers are
allocated. Independent review and executable verification are recorded in the
owning research workspace, not inferred from the algebraic calculations here.
This does not classify finite simple proper Bol loops: these loops are Moufang,
and their central associator subloop is generally nontrivial.

The ordinary-graph construction and proofs below use only the field F2.
Colored and directed repairs, including Eisenstein coefficients, are separate
constructions and must not be inferred from this binary undirected law.

## 1. Elements and multiplication

Let Γ=(V,E) be a finite simple undirected graph, with n=|V| and e=|E|. Set

```math
 X=\mathbb F_2^V,\qquad W=\mathbb F_2^E,\qquad
 A=\mathbb F_2\oplus X.
```

Write an element of A as a=(t,x); t is one auxiliary marker coordinate.
The standard edge vector in W for an edge {i,j} is denoted w_ij. Define

```math
 Q_\Gamma(x)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}x_ix_jw_{ij},
 \qquad
 B_\Gamma(x,y)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}
 (x_iy_j+x_jy_i)w_{ij}.
 \tag{1}
```

No orientation or ordering of the endpoints is involved. The identities

```math
 Q(x+y)=Q(x)+Q(y)+B(x,y),\qquad B(x,x)=0
 \tag{2}
```

hold coordinate by coordinate. Define

```math
 L_\Gamma=W\times\mathbb F_2\times X,
```

with product

```math
 (z,t,x)(z',s,y)=
 \bigl(z+z'+tB(x,y)+sQ(x),\ t+s,\ x+y\bigr).
 \tag{3}
```

Thus |L_Γ|=2^(e+n+1). One central bit is used for each edge, one bit for each
vertex, and one additional marker bit. The compact law takes O(n+e) storage and
O(n+e) bit operations per multiplication; an explicit full multiplication table
is exponentially larger. No claim of a polynomial-size multiplication table is
made.

### A graph operation, rather than a table encoding

Identify x with a vertex subset X_0 and z with an edge subset Z. Then Q(x)
is exactly the edge set induced by X_0. The edge set B(x,y) is

```math
 E[X_0\mathbin\triangle Y_0]\mathbin\triangle E[X_0]
       \mathbin\triangle E[Y_0].
```

Multiplication takes symmetric differences of the vertex and edge subsets and
adds the adjacency correction tB(X_0,Y_0)+sE[X_0]. Each correction is computed
from the endpoints of an actual graph edge. An edge added or removed changes
one independent central coordinate of this law and of its associators.

## 2. Loop axioms and exact Moufang identity

Put

```math
 f((t,x),(s,y))=tB(x,y)+sQ(x).
```

The identity is (0,0,0). In either equation ab=c or ba=c, the marker and vertex
coordinates of the missing element are first determined uniquely by addition
in A, and then its central coordinate is determined uniquely in W. Hence (3)
is a loop. Its two-sided inverse is

```math
 (z,t,x)^{-1}=(z+tQ(x),t,x).
 \tag{4}
```

We use the associator convention

```math
 (ab)c=a(bc)\,[a,b,c],
```

where the associator is central. Direct expansion gives, for a=(z,t,x),
b=(z',s,y), and c=(z'',r,u),

```math
 [a,b,c]=
 \bigl(tB(y,u)+sB(x,u)+rB(x,y),0,0\bigr).
 \tag{5}
```

For completeness, the central discrepancy is

```math
 \alpha(a,b,c)=f(a,b)+f(a+b,c)+f(b,c)+f(a,b+c),
 \tag{6}
```

with a,b,c on the right denoting their images in A. Substituting f and using
(2) leaves exactly the three terms in (5). Thus α is trilinear and alternating:
when two arguments coincide, two terms cancel and the remaining value of B
has repeated arguments. This is a calculation from the product, not an
assumption that an arbitrary alternating tensor integrates.

**Theorem 1 (Moufang and left Bol).** The loop (3) satisfies

```math
 (ab)(ca)=a((bc)a)
 \tag{7}
```

and, with the exact left-Bol convention,

```math
 a\bigl(b(ac)\bigr)=\bigl(a(ba)\bigr)c.
 \tag{8}
```

**Proof.** In a central extension of this form, the central discrepancy in
(7) is

```math
 \alpha(a,b,c+a)+\alpha(b,c,a).
```

This follows by substituting (6): the intermediate f(b,c+a) terms cancel,
leaving exactly the six factor-set terms from the two sides of (7).
Trilinearity and alternation make the displayed expression
α(a,b,c)+α(b,c,a)=0 in characteristic two.

Similarly, the central discrepancy in (8) is

```math
 \alpha(b,a,c)+\alpha(a,a+b,c)=0.
```

The noncentral coordinates on both sides agree by addition in A. This proves
both identities. ∎

### Squares, commutators, and cubic compatibility

Use ab=ba[a,b] for the commutator. The associated functions on A are

```math
 \sigma(t,x)=tQ(x),
 \tag{9}
```

```math
 \kappa((t,x),(s,y))
  =(t+s)B(x,y)+sQ(x)+tQ(y),
 \tag{10}
```

and α from (5). They satisfy

```math
 \sigma(a+b)+\sigma(a)+\sigma(b)=\kappa(a,b),
```

```math
 \kappa(a+b,c)+\kappa(a,c)+\kappa(b,c)=\alpha(a,b,c).
 \tag{11}
```

These are the square/commutator/associator compatibilities of binary cubic
central extensions. In particular the square, commutator and associator data
are not independently prescribed. The square map is the vector-valued cubic
(t,x)↦tQ(x).

## 3. Graph dependence and basic intrinsic structure

Let

```math
 m=(0,1,0),\quad v_i=(0,0,e_i),\quad z_{ij}=(w_{ij},0,0).
```

Every m and v_i is an involution, and the individual v_i commute with one
another and with m. Nevertheless,

```math
 (mv_i)v_j=
 \begin{cases}
 z_{ij}\,m(v_iv_j),&\{i,j\}\in E,\\
 m(v_iv_j),&\{i,j\}\notin E.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{12}
```

Equivalently,

```math
 [m,v_i,v_j]=
 \begin{cases}z_{ij},&\{i,j\}\in E,\\1,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
 \tag{13}
```

The product v_iv_j has coordinates (0,0,e_i+e_j), while

```math
 (v_iv_j)m=(Q(e_i+e_j),1,e_i+e_j),\qquad
 m(v_iv_j)=(0,1,e_i+e_j).
```

Thus the graph affects genuine products and commutators of graph-derived
subsets, as well as associators. The loop is nonassociative if and only if
E is nonempty.

**Theorem 2 (associator subloop, center and nucleus).** The normal subloop
generated by all associators is exactly W×{0}×{0}. It is characteristic.
If E is nonempty and I⊆V is the set of isolated vertices, then

```math
 N(L_\Gamma)=Z(L_\Gamma)=W\times\{0\}\times\mathbb F_2^I.
 \tag{14}
```

If E is empty, L_Γ is the elementary abelian group of order 2^(n+1), so
its center and nucleus are all of L_Γ.

**Proof.** Formula (5) puts every associator in central W. Formula (13)
generates every basis vector w_ij, proving the first assertion. This identifies
W intrinsically as the smallest normal subloop whose quotient is associative.

Assume an edge exists. An element with t=1 cannot be in the nucleus: take
its other two associator arguments to be v_i,v_j for that edge. For t=0,
taking the other arguments to be m and (0,0,y) shows that the nucleus condition
is B(x,y)=0 for every y. By (1), this holds exactly when x is supported on
isolated vertices. Formula (5) is alternating, so the left, middle and right
nucleus conditions agree. Such x also has Q(x)=0, and (10) shows that these
nuclear elements commute with every element. Hence the center equals the
nucleus in (14). The edgeless case follows directly from (3). ∎

The graph's vertex and edge numbers are intrinsic:

```math
 e=\log_2|\mathop{\mathrm{Assoc}}\nolimits(L_\Gamma)|,\qquad
 n=\log_2|L_\Gamma|-e-1.
 \tag{15}
```

For a nonempty graph, its number of isolated vertices is
log2|Z(L_Γ)|−e. The chosen edge basis is not asserted to be intrinsic;
the full vector space W is intrinsic, and the reconstruction below allows
arbitrary linear changes of its basis.

## 4. Naturality and graph operations

**Theorem 3 (specified functor).** On the category of finite simple graphs
and induced embeddings, (3) defines a functor to loops and injective loop
homomorphisms. In particular a graph isomorphism φ induces the specified
isomorphism

```math
 (z,t,x)\longmapsto(\phi_Ez,t,\phi_Vx).
 \tag{16}
```

**Proof.** An induced embedding sends each vertex coordinate to the coordinate
of its image, and each edge coordinate to the coordinate of the image edge.
The zero extensions satisfy Q_Δ(φ_Vx)=φ_EQ_Γ(x), and likewise for B,
because the image has exactly the edges of Γ. Substitute in (3). These linear
maps preserve identities and compose exactly as the graph embeddings do. ∎

This is not asserted for arbitrary edge-preserving graph homomorphisms:
extra edges between image vertices or identifications of vertices can violate
the required equality for Q. Nor is the functor full. For example, each linear
h:A→W gives a loop automorphism (z,a)↦(z+h(a),a), with no corresponding
permutation of graph vertices.

### Edge deletion is a central quotient

If F⊆E and Γ−F keeps all vertices but removes F, coordinate projection gives

```math
 L_\Gamma/\mathbb F_2^F\ \cong\ L_{\Gamma-F},
 \tag{17}
```

where F2^F is the central subspace spanned by the removed edge coordinates.
This follows immediately by projecting Q and B. Adding an edge is therefore
an explicit central extension adding one associator direction. Repeated edge
deletions compose in the expected order.

### Induced vertex deletion is a retraction and a quotient

For S⊆V, restriction of vertex coordinates to S and edge coordinates to E[S]
gives a surjective loop homomorphism

```math
 \rho_S:L_\Gamma\longrightarrow L_{\Gamma[S]},\qquad
 (z,t,x)\longmapsto(z|_{E[S]},t,x|_S).
 \tag{18}
```

Together with the induced-subgraph embedding ι_S, it satisfies ρ_Sι_S=id.
Its kernel is the elementary abelian normal subloop

```math
 K_S=\{(z,0,x):z|_{E[S]}=0,\ x|_S=0\},
```

of order 2^(e−|E[S]|+n−|S|). Consequently L_Γ/K_S≅L_{Γ[S]}.
The kernels generally are not central. For T⊆S⊆V, the retractions satisfy
ρ_T^Sρ_S^V=ρ_T^V.

This construction uses the specified vertex subset S. It does not identify
vertex deletion with deleting one element from a loop, nor assert that the
ordinary unmarked loop deletion deck determines the graph deletion deck.

### Disconnected graphs share the marker

For a disjoint union Γ=Γ1⊔Γ2,

```math
 L_\Gamma\cong L_{\Gamma_1}\times_{\mathbb F_2}L_{\Gamma_2},
 \tag{19}
```

where both maps to F2 take the marker coordinate, and the fiber product
requires the two marker coordinates to agree. Formula (3) separates into the
two edge-coordinate blocks. Isolated vertices contribute a direct elementary
abelian factor: L_{Γ⊔I_k}≅L_Γ×F2^k. If all vertices are isolated,
L_{I_n}=F2^(n+1).

## 5. Unmarked graph reconstruction

The next theorem concerns abstract loop isomorphisms, with no vertex basis,
edge basis, or chosen marker required in the input to the isomorphism problem.

**Theorem 4 (unmarked isomorphism theorem).** For finite simple graphs Γ,Δ,

```math
 L_\Gamma\cong L_\Delta\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Gamma\cong\Delta.
 \tag{20}
```

The reverse implication is Theorem 3. The forward implication uses the
published graph alternating-map reconstruction theorem [HQ, Proposition 1.2]
over F2, together with the intrinsic recognition argument below. Thus graph
reconstruction from alternating maps is an external input; the assertion here
is its transfer through this graph-sensitive Moufang construction.

### Recognizing the vertex hyperplane

W is characteristic by Theorem 2, so A=L/W and its F2-vector-space structure
are intrinsic. The square map σ:A→W is well-defined: multiplying a lift by
a central element of W does not change its square. A **square-zero hyperplane**
is a codimension-one subspace H≤A with σ(H)=0. The vertex hyperplane
U={0}⊕X is always one.

**Lemma 4.1.** For E nonempty, all other square-zero hyperplanes are as follows:

- For each vertex v incident with every edge, the hyperplane x_v=0.
- If E consists of the single edge {i,j}, also the hyperplane t+x_i+x_j=0.

In particular there is one square-zero hyperplane for a nonstar graph, two
for a graph consisting of a star with at least two edges and isolated vertices,
and four for a single edge with isolated vertices. Here "nonstar" means that
no one vertex meets every edge; disconnected graphs are allowed.

**Proof.** A hyperplane other than U has equation a t+ℓ(x)=0 with
ℓ≠0. At t=1, all x satisfying ℓ(x)=a must have Q(x)=0. For an edge {i,j},
the entire forbidden subcube x_i=x_j=1 must therefore satisfy ℓ(x)=1−a.
Varying every other coordinate freely proves

```math
 \mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits\ell\subseteq\{i,j\},\qquad
 \ell_i+\ell_j=1-a.
 \tag{21}
```

Conversely these conditions for every edge ensure that the hyperplane is
square-zero. If a=0, a nonzero ℓ satisfying (21) is the coordinate functional
of one endpoint, and that endpoint must belong to every edge. If a=1,
ℓ=x_i+x_j, and (21) for every edge forces that one edge to be the entire edge
set. This gives the complete list. ∎

### Proof of Theorem 4

A loop isomorphism preserves e,n from (15), the square map, and the number of
square-zero hyperplanes. If e=0 both graphs are edgeless and determined by n.
If e=1 both are one edge and n−2 isolated vertices. If e≥2 and there are two
square-zero hyperplanes, both graphs are the star K1,e with n−e−1 isolated
vertices, again determined by n,e.

In the remaining case U is the unique square-zero hyperplane. It is therefore
intrinsic. For any a∈A\U define

```math
 \beta(u,v)=\alpha(a,u,v),\qquad u,v\in U.
 \tag{22}
```

Formula (5) gives β=B_Γ. The choice of a has no effect because α(U,U,U)=0.
An abstract loop isomorphism therefore induces an equivalence of the
alternating maps B_Γ and B_Δ, allowing arbitrary invertible maps on both U
and W. By [HQ, Proposition 1.2], graphical alternating maps with independent
edge coordinates are equivalent over any field if and only if their graphs
are isomorphic. Apply it over F2. ∎

The theorem recovers the graph's isomorphism class. It does not identify a
canonical vertex basis inside every loop, or claim that every loop automorphism
comes from a graph automorphism. In particular no individual marker is proved
intrinsic. In the nonstar case the hyperplane U is intrinsic, and (22) is
independent of all choices outside it; the star cases are classified separately.

## 6. Independence sets from subloops and involutions

Let i(Γ) denote the number of independent vertex sets, including the empty set.
Equation (1) gives Q(x)=0 exactly when the support of x is independent, because
individual edges have independent coordinates. Consequently

```math
 |\{g\in L_\Gamma:g^2=1\}|=2^e\bigl(2^n+i(\Gamma)\bigr).
 \tag{23}
```

The identity is included on the left. Since e and n are intrinsic, the
independent-set count is an intrinsic loop invariant with this direct counting
formula. This is an exact identification of an existing graph invariant, not
a claim of a new algorithm for counting independent sets.

There is also a self-contained reconstruction theorem using one marker class,
without a chosen vertex or edge basis and without [HQ]. Let mW be the class
of m in A. For an independent set S, define

```math
 G_S=W\times\mathbb F_2\times\mathbb F_2^S\ \le L_\Gamma.
 \tag{24}
```

Here all vertex coordinates outside S are zero. Both Q and B vanish on this
coordinate subspace, so G_S is elementary abelian of order 2^(e+1+|S|).

**Theorem 5 (one-marker independence arrangement).** The maximal elementary
abelian subloops of L_Γ containing W and m are exactly the subloops G_S for
maximal independent sets S of Γ. Their intersection orders, together with
e,n, determine Γ up to isomorphism. Thus the pair (L_Γ,mW) admits a
self-contained reconstruction proof requiring no vertex basis.

**Proof.** Let H be an elementary abelian subloop containing W and m. Its image
in A is a linear subspace of the form F2m⊕K, with K≤U. Every x∈K has
Q(x)=0, because a lift of m+x belongs to H and squares to the identity.
Let S be the union of the supports of all x∈K. Then S is independent.
Indeed, if {i,j} were an edge in S, take x,y∈K with x_i=y_j=1. The conditions
Q(x)=Q(y)=0 force x_j=y_i=0; then Q(x+y) has nonzero ij-coordinate,
a contradiction. Hence H⊆G_S. Maximality on both sides proves the first claim.

For the second claim, let the maximal independent sets be S1,...,Sk, indexed
by the maximal marked elementary abelian subloops. For nonempty J⊆[k],

```math
 d(J)=\log_2\left|\bigcap_{j\in J}G_{S_j}\right|-e-1
      =\left|\bigcap_{j\in J}S_j\right|.
 \tag{25}
```

Every vertex belongs to at least one maximal independent set. Its membership
pattern is the nonempty set P⊆[k] of maximal independent sets containing it.
The number μ(P) of vertices with exactly that pattern is recovered by finite
inclusion-exclusion:

```math
 \mu(P)=\sum_{J\supseteq P}(-1)^{|J|-|P|}d(J).
 \tag{26}
```

Construct μ(P) vertices for each nonempty P. Two distinct vertices with
patterns P,Q are nonadjacent exactly when P∩Q is nonempty: a nonadjacent pair
extends to a maximal independent set, and an adjacent pair cannot be contained
in one. Thus the patterns and their multiplicities reconstruct the graph.
The case n=0 has no vertices and is immediate. ∎

A consequence with a short formula is

```math
 \alpha(\Gamma)=\max_H\log_2|H|-e-1,
 \tag{27}
```

where H ranges over elementary abelian subloops containing W,m, and α(Γ)
is the graph independence number. The marker restriction in this formula is
essential: the preimage of U is always an elementary abelian subloop of order
2^(e+n), even when Γ has small independent sets.

## 7. Small cases and controls

The following are exact consequences of the formulas, separate from executable
checks of them.

| Graph | n | e | Loop order | Elements with square 1 | Associator behavior |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Empty graph on two vertices | 2 | 0 | 8 | 8 | All associators trivial |
| One edge | 2 | 1 | 16 | 14 | [m,v1,v2]=z12 |
| Path 1–2–3 | 3 | 2 | 64 | 52 | Edge associators z12,z23; [m,v1,v3]=1 |
| Triangle | 3 | 3 | 128 | 96 | Three independent edge associators |
| One edge and one isolated vertex | 3 | 1 | 32 | 28 | One edge associator; isolated coordinate central |

For the path, (0,1,e1+e2+e3)^2=(w12+w23,0,0), while
(0,1,e1+e3)^2=1. Adding the edge {1,3} creates a new independent associator
w13 and changes the latter square to w13. Deleting that edge is the central
quotient in (17).

For the complete graph K_n with n≥2, every vertex subset of size at least two
has nonzero Q. Its loop has order 2^(1+n+binom(n,2)), its center and nucleus
are W, and (23) becomes 2^binom(n,2)(2^n+n+1). For n=0 or 1 the graph is
edgeless and the loop is elementary abelian. Disconnected behavior is exactly
(19), not an unspecified direct product.

### Why one scalar edge sum is insufficient

A cheaper construction replaces W by F2 and Q by

```math
 q_\Gamma(x)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}x_ix_j.
 \tag{28}
```

The same product and proof give a code loop of order 2^(n+2), and it is
nonassociative exactly when an edge exists. But this scalar construction loses
graph information even before asking whether its marker is intrinsic.

For Γ=P3, q_Γ=x1x2+x2x3=x2(x1+x3). For Δ=K2⊔I1,
q_Δ=y1y2. The invertible linear substitution

```math
 (y1,y2,y3)=(x1+x3,x2,x3)
```

takes q_Δ to q_Γ. It also transports their polar forms, so
(z,t,x)↦(z,t,(x1+x3,x2,x3)) is an explicit isomorphism of the scalar loops.
The graphs are nonisomorphic: one is connected and has two edges, the other
is disconnected and has one. Thus even a preserved marker does not repair the
scalar collapse. Keeping independent edge coordinates is a material change,
not a different notation for the scalar invariant.

### Comparison with group-only and fixed-factor controls

A graph group already offers graph-dependent associative multiplication; known
graph-group or graph-alternating-map reconstruction theorems are legitimate
baselines. Multiplying such a group by a fixed nonassociative loop supplies
nonassociativity independently of the graph. In contrast, (13), (14), and (17)
show that the associator subloop here has one independently removable generator
per edge, and the edgeless loop is associative. Nonassociativity is therefore
created by the graph and its overlap of vertex subsets.

The cubic extension mechanism is established mathematics. The additional
content is its order-free graph specialization, the intrinsic hyperplane
recognition needed to remove the marker from the isomorphism theorem, and the
explicit subloop and graph-operation correspondences. No priority claim beyond
the literature actually checked is made.

# Every nontrivial odd abelian right nucleus, with centerless left multiplication group

**Mira Cult**

This line-sum construction realizes every nontrivial finite odd abelian group as a whole right nucleus. The left multiplication group is centerless, so none of the nonidentity nuclear right translations belong to it. Compare the [all-central construction](abelian-central.md).

**Theorem.** Let P be a nontrivial finite abelian group of odd order u, expressed as a product of cyclic groups C_(r_i) on k≥1 nontrivial prime-power factors. Write r=exp(P) and g=gcd(r_i). For every prime q≡1 modulo r and every integer d of the form d=t(q−1)u/g with 1≤t≤(q−1)^(k−1)g/u there is a simple, nonassociative, non-Moufang left Bol G-loop of order u^(d+1) q^k. Its right nucleus is exactly P; its faithful left multiplication group has trivial center; the loop commutant is trivial. Its native exponent is r²q in all cases, and orders r² and rq both occur if k≥2; for k=1 (P=C_(p^e)) the order set is computed separately below. In all cases the right nucleus is proper and nonnormal, and the same statement holds for every loop isotope by the G-loop property.

In particular the t=1 construction always exists and for k=1 has order u^q q. For k≥2 and (q−1)^(k−1)≥u, take t=g. Conditional on the separate central realization with its m=1, there are at the **same order** u^(1+u(q−1))q^k two nonisotopic simple left Bol G-loops with the *same* right nucleus P, in one case the abelian group of its right translations lies entirely in the left multiplication group and in the other intersects it trivially. Different Z(LMlt) distinguishes even oriented loop isotopes. Suitable primes occur unboundedly, e.g. q≡1 mod u: take a prime divisor of Φ_u(M)>1 with M a sufficiently large multiple of u and of all primes already excluded. Its constant term 1 and squarefreeness of x^u−1 modulo that prime imply ord_q(M)=u. The single 1053 example with nucleus C3 external to LMlt is already obtained in Nagy's construction; this is a simultaneous general realization, not the first such single example.

## 1. A physical factorization from projective line sums

Use the additive coefficient group S=P; no division by nonunits of this abelian group is used. Let V=F_q^k. Fix the k distinct coordinate characters χ_i of P, faithful on the corresponding cyclic factor and trivial on the others, with values in F_q^×. The group P acts diagonally on V by these. All 2k characters χ_i^± are distinct since each r_i is odd, r_i>1, and the factors have independent supports. Choose any t orbits of P on the projective covector directions having every entry nonzero. Every such orbit has size u/g: a stabilizing h has all χ_i(h) equal to the same scalar, and the intersection of their cyclic image groups has exactly g elements, each with one inverse in each factor. There are (q−1)^(k−1) such directions altogether. The same count and availability hold when k=1. Let C be this union of actual directions.

Let W be the group of S-valued functions on V spanned additively by all f∘ℓ, where [ℓ]∈C and f:F_q→S has sum zero. A different representative of [ℓ] gives the same summand. The sum is **internal direct**: summing a proposed zero relation over ℓ(x)=a makes every other summand zero, as a nonproportional covector takes each value the same number of times there. The remaining summand is q^(k−1) f(a), which is zero only if f(a)=0; q is coprime to u. For k=1 there is only one summand. A zero-sum q-list has q−1 free entries; therefore |W|=u^d. With the usual action on functions by inverse affine substitution, J=V⋊P preserves W. Every nonzero element of each separate coordinate axis acts without nonzero fixed vectors: on every summand it acts as a nontrivial cyclic q-shift and a constant sum-zero column is zero. Thus the difference operator is an additive bijection, also on each of its invariant subgroups. The V-action is faithful: any one orbit of full-support covectors spans V* over F_q, since averaging its linear span with the distinct diagonal character projectors picks out every coordinate. Translation trivial on W must vanish on every chosen covector. The entire J-action is faithful by the faithful conjugation of P on V. By evaluating at 0 get a P-invariant epimorphism φ:W→P (one column represents any prescribed value at 0). **No nonzero V-invariant subgroup of W lies in ker φ**: if it did each translate of each of its functions would vanish at 0, so those functions would vanish everywhere. This certifies observability over *nonfields* and even for mixed additive subgroups, without importing irreducibility of W. A related full-grid variant uses arrays with sum zero on each coordinate-parallel line. That is exactly the maximal choice of *all* full-support projective directions here: every ridge column has that property, and the whole zero-line-sum space has |S|^((q−1)^k) entries by freely specifying the (q−1)-per-side subgrid and completing the remaining coordinates. The selected-orbit orders and same-order comparison use partial subspaces, not just this endpoint.

Take X=W⋊J and K={(w,0,φ(w)):w∈W} inside W⋊P. The P-invariance of φ gives a subgroup, regular on W, and X=KJ is an exact factorization. Both factors are core-free. J is a faithful point stabilizer on W. A normal subgroup of X inside K intersects W in an invariant subgroup of ker φ, necessarily zero. It then centralizes W, while C_X(W)=W by faithfulness; it is trivial. Also X′=W⋊V: [W,V]=W by one separate axis's difference, [P,V]=V by the nontrivial characters, and the quotient is abelian.

Use [Nagy, *A class of simple proper Bol loops*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0703919), Definition 3.1, Proposition 3.2 and Lemma 3.4. The resulting left Bol G-loop consists of the |X| marks T_x=(x,x⁻¹), acting in X² on its cosets of K×J. Its *faithful* native left multiplication group is D={(a,b):the top P components of a and b sum to 0}. Indeed the marks project onto both copies of X and T_x T_y T_xy^−1=(1,[x,y]); coordinate swapping gives the other X′ too. The true stabilizer is H=D∩(K×J), not K×J inside D. Swapping coordinates gives the signed automorphism σ. Marks are the entire σ-inverted set. See Nagy's Example IV and [dissertation, Proposition 3.12 and Example 3.15](https://real-d.mtak.hu/722/7/NagyGaborPeter_doktori_mu.pdf) for the prior single-field primitive/trace construction; we neither assume W irreducible nor use simplicity of an unspecified quotient in our theorem.

## 2. Strict native cuts without a primitiveness or irreducibility assumption

Write D=W²⋊Y with Y=(V⊕V)⋊P, top acts in the opposite orientation on the second V. Inside D, H projects onto J₀=(0⊕V)⋊P, and H∩W²=(ker φ)⊕0. Sigma swaps the two copies of W and the two copies of V and inverts the top. We use the independently reviewed complete-inverted odd-folder criterion from the signed normal-cut lemma: for all 1<C<D signed-normal (C normal in D, σ-stable), it suffices that |C∩H|<|C_C(σ)|. This is applied to the true faithful generating folder just proved; not a claim about a guessed larger X² folder.

* If C≤W², projection to the first W is invariant under a pure first-axis element v of V. On its finite projected group v−1 is bijective. Commutators with (v,1) thus put the *entire* first projection into C with zero second coordinate. Symmetrically isolate the second. The swap forces C=A×A for one nonzero X-invariant additive subgroup A≤W. The two cut sizes are |A∩kerφ| and |A|; strict by the actual evaluation separation above. In particular arbitrary noncoordinate and cross-characteristic subgroups A have not been thrown out by a semisimplicity assumption.
* Otherwise the image Y′ in Y is nontrivial and contains nonzero W′=Y′∩V²: any nontrivial top component acts nontrivially on some character axis so contributes a nonzero commutator with V². W′ is a P-invariant F_q-subspace (q is prime); the pairwise distinct 2k characters and the averaging idempotents (q∤u) yield a sum of actual separate axes. The swap ensures both sides occur. Lifting a pure first-axis element in W′, its commutators with the first W give all of W on that side (the translation component of the lift cannot obstruct this since W is abelian); analogously for the second side. Thus C contains W² and is the full preimage of Y′. Its top P₀ is proper, since P₀=P would give [P,V²]=V² in Y′ and therefore C=D. Let B=0⊕V. Then |Y′∩J₀|≤|P₀| |W′∩B| and |W′∩B|≤|C_(Y′)(σ)| by the injection b↦b+σb on the B-only entries. The actual identities |C_C(σ)|=|W| |C_(Y′)(σ)| and |C∩H|=(|W|/u)|Y′∩J₀| hold by the displayed split signed extension and actual stabilizer. Since |P₀|<u the cut is strict.

The theorem follows for all possible normal subloops, not merely the coordinatized cuts one sees in the construction. Faithfulness and a nontrivial stabilizer (at least V) rule out associativity; a simple nonassociative loop of odd order is not Moufang by odd Moufang solvability ([Foguel–Kinyon–Phillips, Proposition 6.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0208231)). We do not invoke their separate universal-center assertion.

## 3. The centerless side of the nucleus, and native orders

The projection of H onto each target factor K,J is surjective (both surject onto the top P). The normalizer of H in D is precisely D∩(N_X(K)×N_X(J)): necessity follows by these two projections; sufficiency by preservation of both factors and D under conjugation. Here N_X(J)=J, as its fixed points on W are exactly W^J=0. The projector of N_X(K) into J normalizes the top P, whose normalizer is just P because V^P=0. Conversely W⋊P normalizes K, since K is the kernel of the surjective homomorphism (w,h)↦h−φ(w) on W⋊P; thus N_X(K)=W⋊P and its quotient by K is P. The second factor J can lift any first normalizer's top with opposite sign; hence N_D(H)/H≅P. In a transitive faithful coset action the full permutation centralizer is the opposite of this normalizer quotient. A permutation commuting with every native left translation is precisely right translation R_c by a right-nuclear c, by evaluating at the identity and then testing (xy)c=x(yc). Thus the complete right nucleus is the indicated abelian P. Meanwhile Z(X)≤C_X(W)=W and W^J=0, so Z(X)=1. Since D surjects onto X in both coordinates we have Z(D)=1. In particular the nontrivial right translations just recovered are all external to D.

For clarity the *commutant* is a different intrinsic set. In any faithful Nagy factorization, a universally commuting mark T_x satisfies T_x^−1 T_y^−1 T_x T_y∈K×J for every y, so [x,y]∈K for every y. The elementary identity [x,yz]=[x,z][x,y]^z then puts all conjugates of each [x,y] into K, whose core is trivial; x∈Z(X). Conversely each central x induces multiplication by the central mark T_x in every slot, since T_x T_y=T_(xy)=T_(yx). It even belongs to the loop center: these native left and equal right translations commute with all left translations (right nuclear) and composition with an arbitrary native translation is the proper product mark (left and middle nuclear). Thus the commutant and center of this faithful Nagy construction are both the image of Z(X); in our class both are trivial. We make no priority claim about this elementary general observation.

The order of T_x as native loop element equals |x| in X: group powers T_x^a=T_(x^a) are literal marks and only the identity mark fixes the base coset. The group exponent divides r²q by the extension. For an h of order r, its action as a permutation of the q entries/directions can have orbit length smaller than r on the projective directions but on their *nonzero covectors* (choose one chosen full-support covector) it has orbit exactly r. The following direct construction puts an order-r norm into W. Partition the h-run of a chosen direction into e distinct directions, so e divides r. After these e actions the univariate coordinate has been multiplied by a scalar of exact order r/e: if a smaller positive multiple fixed a literal nonzero covector, its full-support entries force h to act trivially on all V. Start x with only that one zero-sum column nonzero, assigning a primitive value w∈P of order r at argument 1 and −w at another nonzero argument outside the multiplicative orbit of 1 under that scalar. Such an argument exists: (q−1)/(r/e) is even, since q−1 is even and r/e odd. Summing x under the r genuine powers of h, on that original direction one sees a nonzero column taking value w on the orbit of 1. Thus (x,h) has order r². For k≥2 there is a nonzero translation v in the kernel of a selected covector, fixing that whole ridge column; take any order-r member there to obtain native order rq. Hence in that case the exponent is exactly r²q. For cyclic prime-power P (k=1), the one available direction has no nonzero kernel: every nontrivial translation acts fixed-point-freely on W. An element with nonzero top in V⋊P is conjugate to its top since the single character is faithful, so all its lifts have p-power order. Every lift over v≠0 in V has order q (its norm on the sum-zero column under a q-shift is 0). Thus the *exact* native order set at k=1 is {p^i:0≤i≤2e}∪{q}, not the full spectrum of the central construction. Its exponent remains r²q, but no rq-order mark occurs. This endpoint illustrates why conditional order claims cannot be copied from the other family.

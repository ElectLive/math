# Literature positioning and a warning about a universal center claim

The [constructive realization](proofs/abelian-central.md) is separate from this comparison with earlier work. This note distinguishes the full right nucleus from the subgroup of its translations that lies in the left multiplication group. No priority claim is intended.

* [Foguel–Kinyon–Phillips, arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0208231v2), Lemma 5.16, identifies the central nuclear right translations belonging to $`D=\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)`$; it need not be the entire permutation centralizer of $`D`$. Their Problem 6.26 asks about a nonnormal right nucleus in odd order, not arbitrary prescribed nuclei. Their Corollary 6.24 asserts in particular $`Z(D)\ne1`$ for odd simple Bol loops. This **last center assertion is not used**: see the precise conflict below. [Journal publication record](https://scholarlyworks.adelphi.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/On-twisted-subgroups-and-Bol-loops/991004355494206266). The numbering and warning here refer to the version of the arXiv text actually checked. No claim that the publisher has issued an erratum.
* [Nagy, *A class of simple proper Bol loops*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0703919), Lemma 3.4 and Example IV, gives the exact-factorization loop of degree 1053; his original argument proves a simple nonassociative quotient and states the full simplicity check. [Nagy, dissertation](https://real-d.mtak.hu/722/7/NagyGaborPeter_doktori_mu.pdf), Proposition 3.12 and Example 3.15, gives a proof of full simplicity for the explicit field-trace factor. The latter text uses right Bol conventions; take the opposite to obtain the corresponding left Bol coset loop. This example already has a nonnormal whole right nucleus of order three, as checked below: we do **not** claim the first example of nonnormality or an open existence problem. Nagy's exact-factorization construction is much broader than a single loop but these cited results do not themselves give the full arbitrary-nucleus prescription.
* [Kinyon–Nagy–Vojtěchovský, *Bol loops and Bruck loops of order pq*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05785), proves a nucleus of prime order in a nonsimple two-prime class; switch left and right when comparing the opposite law. [Galici–Nagy, *An extension formula for right Bol loops arising from Bol reflections*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.09977), Theorem 1.2, computes full nuclei of its extensions, but these have a proper normal index-two copy of the base, not odd simple examples. [P. T. Nagy, *Nuclear Properties of Loop Extensions*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00025-019-1026-7), studies nuclear/Schreier extensions with an actual kernel, a different problem from prescribing a nonnormal entire right nucleus of a simple odd loop.

## The 1053 test distinguishes three different groups

Write $`Y=W\rtimes(B\rtimes P)`$, with $`W=\mathbb F_{27}^+`$, $`B=C_{13}`$ acting by square scalars, $`P=\langle h\rangle=C_3`$ acting by Frobenius. Let the first factor of the exact factorization be $`K=\{(w,h^{-\mathrm{Tr}w}):w\in W\}`$ and the second $`J=B\rtimes P`$. The field trace is invariant under $`h`$; $`K`$ is regular on $`W`$, and $`Y=KJ`$ faithfully. The sign on the trace is a harmless choice of the homomorphism in Nagy's Proposition 3.12. Since $`Y'=W\rtimes B`$ and $`Z(Y)=1`$, the group of the native left translations in $`Y^2`$ is the full subdirect fiber $`D=\{(g_1,g_2):g_1g_2\in Y'\}`$, with trivial center. Indeed, the marks $`(g,g^{-1})`$ generate both copies of $`Y'`$ by their elementary commutators and project diagonally with inverse coordinates to $`(Y/Y')^2`$. Both projections surject $`Y`$, forcing a central pair into $`Z(Y)^2`$. The group $`Y`$ is centerless directly: centralizing translations forces a pure translation by faithful linear action, and centralizing a nontrivial scalar forces that translation to vanish.

Its native stabilizer $`H=D\cap(K\times J)`$ projects onto both $`K,J`$, so $`N_D(H)=D\cap(N_Y(K)\times N_Y(J))`$ (the fiber condition is unchanged by conjugation). Here $`N_Y(J)=J`$: translating the origin must produce a fixed point of $`J`$, only the origin. Also $`N_Y(K)=W\rtimes P`$. Translations change the displacement of $`(w,h^j)`$ by $`(1-h^j)v`$, which has trace zero; Frobenius preserves the trace. On the other hand the top projection of $`K`$ to $`B\rtimes P`$ is $`P`$, whose normalizer is just $`P`$. Consequently $`|N_D(H)|=81\cdot39/3=1053`$ and $`|H|=27\cdot39/3=351`$. Thus $`N_D(H)/H\cong C_3`$, i.e. the entire right nucleus is $`C_3`$ although the left multiplication group has center $`1`$. It is nonnormal by simplicity and its proper size. This verifies failure of the *center* assertion, **not** failure of universal nontriviality of the right nucleus. We leave the latter necessity unclaimed.

One can localize the obstruction in the preprint's use of Lemma 6.22: the natural group isomorphism with the left translations of the associated Bruck loop need not identify the stabilizers. In square coordinates a mark indexed by $`a\in Y`$ sends $`y`$ to $`aya`$, hence the two-coordinate action of $`(g_1,g_2)`$ is $`y\mapsto g_1 y g_2^{-1}`$; the identity stabilizer is diagonal. The native $`H`$ contains $`(1,b)`$ for $`1\ne b\in B`$, not diagonal. This is not a challenge to the complete-inverted-set, odd-order solvability of $`\mathrm{LMlt}`$, or odd Moufang solvability results used elsewhere.

# Which groups can be the right nucleus?

**Mira Cult**

**Exactly the finite groups of odd order** occur as the entire right nucleus of finite simple non-Moufang left Bol loops of odd order. The right nucleus consists of the elements $`r`$ that satisfy $`(xy)r=x(yr)`$ for **every** pair $`x,y`$. It is a group, and its right translations commute with all left translations. Whether these permutations themselves belong to the left multiplication group is a separate question.

1. [Every odd group occurs](proofs/all-odd-groups.md), including the trivial group and nonabelian groups. The examples are G-loops: every isotope is isomorphic to the original. They have trivial left and middle nuclei and commutant. The left multiplication group in this first construction is centerless.
2. [One can prescribe the intersection as well](proofs/central-intersection.md). Given an odd group $`R`$ and any subgroup $`B\le Z(R)`$, the full right nucleus can be $`R`$ with exactly the right translations of $`B`$ lying in the left multiplication group. This necessary condition on $`B`$ is therefore sufficient.
3. [One marked translation group suffices for many targets](proofs/one-marked-group.md). For a fixed odd group $`U`$, all its subgroups occur as full right nuclei at the same loop order and with the same abstract group of signed translations and the same marked set. The point stabilizer changes. There is even a common embedded native copy of $`U`$. It does **not** follow that different stabilizers give different unmarked loops.

For nontrivial abelian targets the [product-ring construction](proofs/abelian-central.md) and the [centerless line-sum construction](proofs/abelian-centerless.md) also control loop orders and separate isotopy classes. In the former family, [commuting with just two specified elements](proofs/two-elements.md) already forces an element to be the identity, despite the prescribed right nucleus.

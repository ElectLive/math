# Optional explicit fair-bit clock for an unweighted perfect matching

Theorem 2 of [Göbel, Liu, Manurangsi and Pappik](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.00882) states expected $O(s^{15}\log^4 s)$ running time on a feasible board of side $s$. One need not interpret its arithmetic-operation count as a bit count. Here is a deliberately slower finite-precision implementation of their §6 argument giving $P(s)=O(s^{60})$ expected fair-bit operations on an ordinary bit machine with polynomial sequential bookkeeping. This is an algorithm description, not an included scalable Python backend. All bounds are for sufficiently large $s$; exact enumeration absorbs bounded small $s$.

1. Set $u=\lceil s^8\rceil$. Summarize GLMP §§6.1–6.2: $R=O(s^2\log s)$ phases estimate all $s^2$ hole weights from $S=O(s^2(\log s+u))$ independent samples of $T=O(s^7\log s)$ steps each. The initial output of each such chain can be fixed (worst-start mixing); every sample in a given phase uses the same current weight vector. When weights are good (factor 2 of ideal), GLMP's JSV mixing and concentration estimates ensure the next estimates are within $6/5$ of the previous ideal except probability at most $2^{-20u}/R$, by increasing the absolute constants in $S$. Update nonedge weights incident on one vertex by **rational** factor $3/4$ until reaching $1/s!$ (clip the final factor). This replacement of $e^{-1/2}$ in §6.1 preserves the phase proof: every configuration uses at most one incident weight, the ideal ratio changes by at most $4/3$, and $(6/5)(4/3)<2$. Retain the same order of $R$; each edge weight has $O(s\log s)$ numerator/denominator bits. A zero sample count required in a hole-weight division is declared attempt failure; it is impossible on the good estimations. On every remaining history each rational hole-weight numerator and denominator is a product of at most $R$ integers of size $\le S$ times the initial integer $s$, hence has $O(s^2\log^2 s)$ bits.

2. End a single attempt with $O(Tu)$ steps of the final chain and mark success only for a full matching using real edges. On good final weights the sampled state's TV error to stationarity is at most $2^{-20u}$ after increasing constants (GLMP §6.1). In stationarity the probability of an original perfect matching is $\Omega(s^{-2})$: near-perfect classes have weights within factor 2, and the total perfect-matching weight of configurations using a fake $1/s!$ edge is at most 1 while there is at least one original perfect matching. Conditional on output of a real perfect matching, the stationary law is exactly uniform, **independently of the actual good finishing weights**. Thus the one-attempt output restricted to its success symbols is within $O(2^{-20u})$ in total variation of some constant times uniform; the constant is $\Omega(s^{-2})$. Bad final weights have probability at most $2^{-20u}$. This argument does not require an unconditional bound on perfect-matching mass for bad weights.

3. For an actually dyadic finite experiment, use $b=\lceil 50u\rceil$ independent fair bits per random elementary proposal or Metropolis comparison. Round each binary acceptance probability down to the nearest multiple of $2^{-b}$; sample uniform proposals by dividing $[0,2^b)$ into nearly equal bins. The variation distance per elementary draw is at most $O(s^2 2^{-b})$ (there are at most polynomially many proposal neighbors) for every fixed past; the ideal rational transitions use weights as in step 1. Standard sequential coupling adds these distances over any polynomial number of draws. Failure and stopping may be padded by dummy random bits, making the total consumed length deterministic. Repeat independent attempts at most $C s^2 u$ times; if none succeeds output a deterministically found perfect matching (polynomial ordinary augmenting paths). Over this finite horizon, failure probability is $\le2^{-10u}$ at a suitable constant and the aggregate TV error to uniform of the dyadic procedure is $\le2^{-8u}$. This works also if each successful attempt is viewed as a small TV error from uniform after normalization by its success probability $\Omega(s^{-2})$.

4. Elementary bit clock for fast part: eventually $R\le s^3,S\le s^{11}, T\le s^9$ after fixed constants, so one attempt has at most $O(s^{23})$ elementary Markov transitions plus initialization/deterministic phase updates. The final $O(Tu)$ fits the same budget. At most $O(s^{11})$ attempts give $O(s^{34})$ steps (or $O(s^{35})$ with slack for implementation). One transition can calculate a ratio of current matching weights rationally: products involve at most $s$ input edge factors and a pair of hole-weight factors. These have $O(s^4)$ bits even before reducing. Computing one transition plus its $b$-bit rounded probability and compact state bookkeeping costs $O(s^{20})$ bits by schoolbook arithmetic. For sequential access one can scan the $s^2$ weight vector instead of using a unit-cost unbounded pointer; looking up/reading $O(s^2)$ entries of $O(s^4)$ bits stays below this bound. $O(s^{60})$ is deliberately loose. On this accounting all phase histogram updates/samples and copied identifiers fit the same clock.

5. Rare exact correction. Use probability $\delta=2^{-u}$ for the correction, else the preceding bounded sampler with law $Q$. On the rare branch compute the exact uniform target $\pi$ by enumerating the real perfect matchings and the exact law $Q$ by GLMP's phase-histogram principle, then sample $[\pi-(1-\delta)Q]/\delta$. This vector is a probability distribution: its smallest target entry is at least $1/s!$, while TV error $\le2^{-8u}$. The slow computation costs $\exp(o(u))$ **bit operations**, not just that many rational operations. At phase $i$ a single hole weight has at most $(S+1)^{2R}$ possible rational values, the whole vector at most $(S+1)^{2R s^2}=\exp(O(s^4\log^2 s))$. For each vector, compute a $T$-step distribution of one state by matrix powers or sequential DP on at most $(1+s^2)s!$ states. Next enumerate the $(S+1)^{s^2+1}$ possible histograms of the $S$ independent end classes; their probabilities follow by the multinomial formula and determine the updated weight vectors or failure, even when several histograms map to the same vector. For the final chain enumerate real output atoms similarly. Compute the capped independent-attempt history by a scalar finite geometric sum, not by expanding its $C s^2u$-fold path tree. Even naive scans and multiplying these displayed exponential table sizes a fixed number of times stay $\exp(O(s^4\log^2 s))$, and there are only polynomially many sequential phases. **Common denominators:** the complete bounded sampler consumes at most $O(s^{35}b)=s^{O(1)}$ fair bits with padding. Every partial law can be maintained as integer counts over the relevant identical-length random bit blocks; numerator/denominator bitlengths and all intermediate multinomial/factorial lengths are polynomial. Thus schoolbook bit arithmetic on the entries contributes only a polynomial factor to this table bound. An exact final rational choice is possible by rejection below its common denominator, using expected $O(\log d)$ fair bits for denominator $d$. The unconditional rare overhead is $2^{-s^8}\exp(o(s^8))=o(1)$. No assumption on computational complexity of real-number oracles, exact permanent evaluation on the ordinary branch, or bit length of a rare unbounded JSV trajectory is used.

## Sharper clock: $\widetilde O(s^{21})$ with lazy rounded transitions
The $s^{60}$ clock above can be reduced without invoking the printed unit-cost exponent as a bit bound. Use $u=\lceil C_0 s^4\log^2(s+2)\rceil$ in the same proof, with sufficiently large fixed $C_0$, and $b=\lceil50u\rceil$. The full histogram table sizes in step 5 are at most $\exp(C_1 s^4\log^2(s+2))$: $R=O(s^2\log s)$ and $\log S=O(\log s)$, even for this value of $u$. The asymptotic coefficient $C_1$ can be chosen independently of $C_0$ by taking a sufficiently large startup guard, since the dependence in $\log S$ is merely an additive $O(\log C_0)$. Pay a fixed power of this bound for all table scans, arithmetic and data movement by choosing $C_0$ large; the absolute rare overhead then tends to zero. The finite padded branch still has polynomial common dyadic denominator bitlength. This choice of $C_0$ affects constants, not an exponent.

For the fast branch, one whole approximate attempt takes $RST+O(Tu)=\widetilde O(s^{15})$ Markov transitions. The hard truncation at $O(s^2u)$ independent attempts is for a **finite correction law**; geometrically the **expected** number of attempts used is only $O(s^2)$, since each has success probability $\Omega(s^{-2})$. Do not compute $b$-bit divisions or generate all $b$ fair bits on the fast path. For a rational comparison with acceptance probability $a/d$, the rounded-down $b$-bit probability is implemented by revealing independent comparison bits and the consecutive binary digits of $a/d$ via doubling-and-subtracting its remainder. Stop on first unequal bit, or, if all $b$ digits tie, return false. For $a/d=1$ or 0 return deterministically. Expected digits used are at most two, uniformly over every rational input, and each next digit costs $O(\log d)$ bit operations. The finite-law interpretation still has $b$ padded bits (unused bits need not be materialized). For a uniform proposal among $h=O(s^2)$ listed choices, use bins with endpoints rounded dyadically; draw one lazy uniform comparing to the exact cumulative boundaries, using the same previously revealed fair bits at all tests and truncating the boundary digits at $b$. The probability more than $j$ digits are needed is at most $O(h2^{-j})$, so the first and second moments of the number of inspected digits are $O(\log(h+1))$ and $O(\log^2(h+1))$. One can instead use two uniform single-index choices within the same budget. All kernel errors are bounded by the same per-step $O(h2^{-b})$.

A genuine JSV local switch toggles only constantly many edges and the old/new hole-pair factor. Thus each local Hastings ratio needs only constantly many edge and hole weights, not a product along all $s$ edges of the matching. Each relevant integer has $\widetilde O(s^2)$ bits, even after all $R$ phases. Unreduced products are allowed; the transition uses $\widetilde O(s^4)$ expected bit work, including lazy precision. Sequentially scanning the complete $s^2$ hole-weight array of $\widetilde O(s^2)$ bits per entry costs at most the same, so random access to unbounded integers is unnecessary. Per-phase updates on $s^2$ hole weights and proposing neighbors fit this budget. Multiplying the $\widetilde O(s^{15})$ transitions per attempt, the $O(s^2)$ expected attempts and $\widetilde O(s^4)$ per transition gives the advertised $\widetilde O(s^{21})$ **expected bit operations**. This is our finite-law adapter derived from GLMP §6, not their published exponent in a different computation model. The numeric constants in the JSV mixing length and other proof guards remain inputs to an optimized implementation; this audit of exponents does not identify practical settings.

### An integer mixing/precision schedule for that chain
[JSV, JACM 51 (2004), Lemmas 4.1 and 4.4, pp. 683–689](https://www.math.cmu.edu/~af1p/Teaching/Markov_Chain_Mixing/Papers/permanent.pdf) give explicit canonical congestion $48s^4$ and full-flow multiplier $2+4(r+1/r)$, $r=\pi(\text{near})/\pi(\text{perfect})\in[s^2/2,2s^2]$ for factor-two hole weights. Thus the specified lazy chain has inverse gap at most $1000s^6$ for $s\ge2$. Use the exact JSV proposal (half hold; from perfect remove a uniform matched edge; from near select a uniform vertex, plugging or swapping at the corresponding hole) and Metropolis acceptance. All fake positive edges are allowed. Start **each** chain from a deterministically found real perfect matching $x_0$, so $\pi(x_0)\ge1/[(1+2s^2)s!]$. The elementary reversible-chain bound $\|P^t(x_0,\cdot)-\pi\|_{TV}\le\exp(-t/\rho)/(2\sqrt{\pi(x_0)})$ now needs no estimate of the minimum mass over all fake matchings.

One oversized choice: let $l=1+\mathrm{bitlen}(s+2)$, $u=2^{30}(s+2)^4l^2$, $L=\mathrm{bitlen}((1+2s^2)s!)$, $R$ the number of rational $3/4$ cooling phases (at most $1+4s\log(s!)+s$), and take the nonnegative integer clocks
$$
T_0=2^{10}s^6(L+20+2\mathrm{bitlen}(s)),\qquad
T_1=2^{10}s^6(L+32u),\qquad
S=2^{22}(1+2s^2)(u+\mathrm{bitlen}(R+1)+\mathrm{bitlen}(s)+1).
$$
Here $T_0$ is the preliminary mixing time per sample, $T_1$ the final mixing time and $S$ the number of independent preliminary samples per phase. $T_0$ gives TV below $1/(1000s^2)$ when weights are good, and $T_1$ below $e^{-20u}$. Every class probability is at least $1/[2(1+2s^2)]$ on these weights. Thus each preliminary sample's class mean differs from stationary by less than 1% relative. Independent Chernoff at relative error $1/50$ gives failure for any one class at most $2\exp[-S/(16000(1+2s^2))]$; even summed over $R(s^2+1)$ classes this is below $2^{-20u}$. On success the ratio of all class estimates gives the next ideal hole weights to multiplicative factor below $6/5$; the $4/3$ rational cooling ratio keeps factor two. Stationary successful real matching probability is at least $1/[2(1+2s^2)]$, and good weights occur with the just-given probability. Cap the number of independent attempts at $J=2^{10}(1+2s^2)u$. Use $b=64u+\mathrm{bitlen}(J(RST_0+T_1+2)(4s+2))+4$ dyadic bits per comparison/proposal. Its joint rounding TV loss over the full padded computation is below $2^{-60u}$. These parameters are mathematical existence witnesses, not practical defaults. An implementation using a different proposal, initialization, rounding, or correction law needs its own compatibility check. For a notationally simpler stopping implementation one can instead correct the one-attempt output including null toward a law with unchanged null mass and equal successful atoms, repeating complete independent corrected trials; the capped law above matches the bounded-horizon derivation.

For the rare multilevel enumeration at this explicit $u$, note that for all sufficiently large $s$, $R\le5s^2\log s$ and $\log S\le10\log s$; the natural log of the number of hole vectors is at most $2Rs^2\log(S+1)\le110s^4\log^2 s$. To update timing arrays, use at most a fixed polynomial number of scans of this table, enumerating both endpoints or one histogram per current vector, and use straightforward quadratic scans/accumulation if needed. Even ten nested such scans per fixed phase and a polynomial number of phase steps cost $\exp(2000s^4\log^2 s)$ for large $s$, after polynomial-bit arithmetic; $2^{30}(s+2)^4l^2\log 2$ dominates this explicit coefficient. Increasing $u$ to a slower sixth power also works with the earlier looser time exponent.

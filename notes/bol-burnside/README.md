# The Burnside questions for Bol loops of exponent three

**Mira Cult**

A loop can be generated by two elements and still be infinite even though **every element has order at most three**. Its group of translations on the Bol side behaves differently: **every finitely generated subgroup of that permutation group is a finite 3-group**. These are answers to two distinct questions, not conflicting conclusions.

1. [An infinite two-generated loop at every odd prime exponent](two-generators.md). We give an explicit multiplication, not just a presentation. The example is residually finite and, for every $`d\ge2`$, has a finite two-generated quotient of order $`p^{d+1}`$. Consequently the free right Bol loop of exponent three on $`m\ge2`$ generators is infinite; for $`m=1`$ it is cyclic of order three. Residual finiteness is proved for the example, **not** for the entire free loop.
2. [Local finiteness of the matched translation group](matched-translations.md). For every left Bol loop of exponent dividing three the left translations generate a locally finite 3-group; for a right Bol loop it is the **right** translations. Two individual translations on this side generate at most 27 permutations, and 27 occurs. This bound does not limit the number of native loop elements generated by their two underlying points.

[A. N. Grishkov's 2023 problem statement, printed p. 200](https://publications.hse.ru/pubs/share/direct/992466858.pdf#page=200), credits the free-loop question to G. P. Nagy. It calls the second group the **right** multiplication group of a right Bol loop, but its displayed map multiplies on the **left**. The [authors' journal paper, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.00808), uses the standard right-hand convention. The distinction matters: the answered named question has a positive answer, while in the displayed opposite-hand group two explicit translations have an infinite-order product.

These are research proofs with separate independent AI mathematical checks, not human-refereed or formally verified results. [Evidence, checkers and their exact scope](verification.md). The examples have a proper normal abelian subloop; they are not new simple loops. The [finite simple Bol classification](../finite-simple-bol-loops/README.md) is a separate open problem.

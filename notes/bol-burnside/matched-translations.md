# The matched translation group of every exponent-three Bol loop is a locally finite 3-group

**Mira Cult**

 No finiteness, simplicity, finite generation, radical-free condition or existence of an inversion automorphism is assumed of the starting loop.

**Theorem.** If $`L`$ satisfies the left Bol identity and has exponent dividing three, its left multiplication group $`G=\langle L_x:x\in L\rangle`$ is a locally finite 3-group: every finitely generated subgroup of $`G`$ is a finite 3-group. In fact two actual left translations generate a group of order at most $`27`$ and exponent dividing three; the bound $`27`$ is attained. The opposite statement holds for right translations of right Bol loops.

This answers the group named in [A. N. Grishkov, Mal'tsev Readings 2023, Problem 2, printed p. 200](https://publications.hse.ru/pubs/share/direct/992466858.pdf#page=200), in particular for free loops of exponent three and any generating rank, in the standard convention $`R_x(y)=yx`$ given in [Grishkov–Rasskazova–Souza dos Anjos, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.00808). The abstract's displayed operator instead has its arguments reversed. The wrong-hand assertion is false for free rank at least two, by the separate [explicit affine example](two-generators.md). Local finiteness of the matched group does **not** imply local finiteness of the loop. No historical priority claim.

## The reflection group, including the original kernel

Use rightmost-first composition in all permutation groups. The actual set $`K=\{L_x:x\in L\}`$ contains $`1`$, is closed under inverses and $`aba`$, and each of its elements satisfies $`a^3=1`$. These are left power alternativity/inverse property and $`L_xL_yL_x=L_{x(yx)}`$; see [Foguel–Kinyon–Phillips (FKP), §2 and Example 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0208231).

In $`(G\times G)\rtimes\langle t\rangle`$, with $`t`$ interchanging factors, set $`s_a=(a,a^{-1})`$, $`S=\{s_a:a\in K\}`$, and $`D=\{t s_a:a\in K\}`$. The set $`S`$ is twisted, since $`s_a s_b s_a=s_{aba}`$. Every member of $`D`$ is an involution, $`t\in D`$, and $`D`$ is closed under conjugation by each of its elements:
```math
(ts_a)(ts_b)(ts_a)=t s_{ab^{-1}a}.
```
A product of two reflections is $`s_a^{-1}s_b`$. It is conjugate to $`s_a s_b s_a`$; explicitly $`s_a(s_a s_b s_a)s_a^{-1}=s_a^2s_b=s_a^{-1}s_b`$. Hence any two **different** members of $`D`$ have product of order exactly three. We keep the full paired group; the first projection of $`\langle S\rangle`$ is the **original** matched translation group $`G`$.

An immediate route to local finiteness of $`\langle D\rangle`$ is the classical Cuypers–Hall local-finiteness theorem for 3-transposition groups, stated in [J. I. Hall, *Generating finite 3-transposition groups*, §2.1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021869322001533). The no-commuting-pair case here has the following much smaller proof.

## A finite subquasigroup does not automatically mean a finite group

On $`D`$ put $`d*e=ded`$. It is commutative since $`(de)^3=1`$ for $`d\ne e`$, is idempotent, and satisfies $`d*(d*e)=e`$. Each left multiplication for $`*`$ is an automorphism since it is conjugation in the group. Thus this is a distributive Steiner quasigroup. Anchor at $`o=t`$, let $`J(x)=o*x`$, and define $`x+y=J(x*y)`$. It is a commutative loop with identity $`o`$; $`J`$ is an involutory automorphism for $`*`$, and $`x+x=Jx`$, $`x+(x+x)=o`$. In particular
```math
 (x+x)+(y+z)=x*(y*z)=(x*y)*(x*z)=(x+y)+(x+z).
```
The displayed identity is the commutative Moufang law. Thus $`(D,+)`$ is a commutative Moufang loop of exponent dividing three, not generally an elementary abelian group.

Fix any finite $`D_0\subseteq D`$ containing $`t`$. The subquasigroup $`E`$ it generates is finite: it is contained in the subloop of the displayed Moufang loop generated by $`D_0`$, since $`x*y=J(x+y)`$ and $`Jz=z+z`$. Apply the classical local finiteness of commutative Moufang loops of exponent three, or [G. P. Nagy's stronger theorem for all Moufang loops of exponent three](https://www.acta.hu/download.phtml?id=2632). On $`E`$ the same formulas make $`+`$ a subloop (left and right translations of the finite set are injective). Moreover $`E`$ is contained in $`W_0=\langle D_0\rangle`$ as it is made from conjugations. Conjugation gives $`W_0\to\mathrm{Sym}(E)`$ with kernel **exactly** $`Z(W_0)`$: fixing all $`E`$ centralizes its generating subset $`D_0`$, and central elements fix $`E\subseteq W_0`$.

For completeness, if $`n=[W_0:Z(W_0)]<\infty`$, the center $`Z`$ is a finitely generated abelian group by Schreier. Choose a finite transversal $`(r_i)`$ and write $`g r_i=r_{g i} z_i(g)`$, $`z_i(g)\in Z`$. Then $`V(g)=\prod_i z_i(g)`$ is a homomorphism $`W_0\to Z`$ (left multiplication permutes indices); for $`z\in Z`$, $`V(z)=z^n`$. The abelianization of a group generated by involutions has exponent at most two. Thus $`z^{2n}=1`$ for all $`z\in Z`$, implying $`Z`$ finite and finally $`W_0`$ finite. Omitting the central kernel here would be unjustified.

## Recover the prime, then project

Within this finite group let $`A=tE`$ and $`P=\langle A\rangle`$. The set $`A`$ contains $`1`$. For $`d,e\in E`$, $`(td)^{-1}=t(tdt)`$ and $`(td)(te)(td)=t[d(tet)d]`$ belong to $`A`$, by closure of $`E`$ under $`*`$. Each element of $`A`$ has order one or three, so squaring is a bijection of $`A`$ (its inverse is squaring again). By [FKP, Cor. 4.22](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0208231), Cauchy's theorem for finite 2-divisible twisted subgroups, no prime other than three divides **the cardinality** of $`A`$. By FKP Theorem 4.17 (Glauberman's finite generating twisted $`\pi`$-subgroup theorem), $`P`$ is a finite 3-group. Finiteness was proved *before* invoking either statement.

Given finitely many elements of $`G`$, lift their words in $`K`$ and its inverses to words in $`S`$. Include $`t`$ and the finitely many $`ts_a`$ used in $`D_0`$. Each such $`s_a=t(ts_a)`$ lies in $`A`$. The subgroup generated by the given elements of $`G`$ therefore lies in the first projection of $`P\le G\times G`$, a finite 3-group, as asserted.

## Sharp two-translation bound

For $`a,b\in K`$ take $`D_0=\{t,ts_a,ts_b\}`$. With anchor $`t`$, the Moufang subloop above is now generated by two elements: $`+`$ and $`*`$ generate the same set, since the anchor is present. By Moufang diassociativity and commutativity this is an elementary abelian group of rank at most two. Here $`d*e=-(d+e)`$; the conjugation action of $`s_a=t(ts_a)`$ and $`s_b`$ on $`E`$ is consequently by ordinary additive translations. Their group commutator lies in the kernel $`Z(W_0)`$ above. Thus $`\langle s_a,s_b\rangle`$ has class at most two and is generated by two elements of order dividing three. The central commutator has cube one since $`[s_a^3,s_b]=1`$; collecting gives at most $`3^3`$ elements, each of exponent dividing three. The same holds after first projection. Equality is realized by two generating left translations of the ordinary Heisenberg group $`\mathrm{UT}_3(\mathbb F_3)`$ seen as an associative loop: all 27 elements occur in the regular left action.

**Boundary.** We have bounded two *group generators that are actual translations*, not the cardinality of the subloop generated by their underlying two points. The finite $`W_0`$ acts on a reflection closure; the native choice of representatives for products of arbitrary loop terms can bring in new reflections. The explicit two-generator counterexample exhibits the distinction. There is no assertion about the full multiplication group, nor about a matched one-sided local-finiteness theorem for exponents other than three.

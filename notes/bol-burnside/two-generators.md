# Two-generated residually finite Bol loops of every odd prime exponent

**Mira Cult**

The construction uses the actual multiplication, not just an action of a larger group.

## The questions and the result

On printed page 200 of [A. N. Grishkov, *Free Bol loops and corresponding groups*, Mal'tsev Readings 2023](https://publications.hse.ru/pubs/share/direct/992466858.pdf#page=200), Problem 1, attributed there to G. Nagy, asks whether the free right Bol loop on $`m`$ generators satisfying $`x^3=1`$ is finite. Problem 2 asks about local finiteness of its right multiplication group. The abstract correctly states the right Bol identity but its displayed formula for right translation multiplies on the left. The authors of the accompanying [exponent-two paper, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.00808) explicitly use right translation $`y\mapsto yx`$. We distinguish both readings; the two one-sided groups have **different answers**.

**Theorem.** For every odd prime $`p`$ there is an infinite, residually finite, two-generated left Bol loop $`Q_p`$ of exponent $`p`$. For every $`d\ge2`$ it maps onto a two-generated loop of order $`p^{d+1}`$, still of exponent $`p`$. In each case the loop is an extension of an elementary abelian group by $`C_p`$, not simple. Its opposite gives the right Bol convention. In particular the free right Bol loop of exponent three on $`m\ge2`$ generators is infinite, and the orders of its finite two-generated images are unbounded. For $`m=1`$ it is cyclic of order three.

The group generated by the *opposite-hand* translations of $`Q_p`$ is not even torsion. Therefore, in the free right Bol loop of exponent three with at least two generators, the group of literal maps $`a\mapsto xa`$ in the conference display is not locally finite. This does not give a negative answer for the usual right translations of right Bol loops. Their [positive answer has a separate proof](matched-translations.md) and is not an input to this construction.

## One formula

Put $`F=\mathbb F_p`$, $`f_i=1+it`$ for $`i\in F`$, and let
```math
R=F[t,(1-t^{p-1})^{-1}]\ \subset F(t).
```
All $`f_i`$ are units since $`\prod_{i\ne0}f_i=1-t^{p-1}`$. Use $`Q_p=F\times R`$ and define
```math
(i,u)\cdot(j,v)=(i+j,\ v+c(i,j)u),\qquad
 c(i,j)=\frac{f_{i+2j}}{f_i}=1+j a_i,\qquad a_i=\frac{2t}{f_i}.
\tag{1}
```
Each coefficient $`c(i,j)`$ is a unit, $`(0,0)`$ is the identity, and either missing factor is uniquely recovered by its first coordinate and one affine equation in its second coordinate. Thus (1) is a loop.

Let $`x=(i,u),y=(j,v)`$. A direct calculation of $`L_xL_yL_x`$ on $`(k,w)`$ gives
```math
 (2i+j+k,\ w+B+k(2a_i u+a_j v)),\quad
 B=(2+(i+j)a_i)u+(1+i a_j)v.
```
Both rational identities
```math
 a_{2i+j}(2+(i+j)a_i)=2a_i,\qquad
 a_{2i+j}(1+i a_j)=a_j
```
follow on multiplying by the denominators. Hence the displayed permutation is $`L_{(2i+j,B)}`$; evaluating at the identity identifies its subscript as $`x(yx)`$. This is the left Bol identity $`L_xL_yL_x=L_{x(yx)}`$.

Independently of Bol, repeated application of the single left translation gives
```math
 L_{(i,u)}^n(j,v)=
 (j+ni,\ v+[n+n j a_i+\binom{n}{2} i a_i]u).
\tag{2}
```
For odd $`p`$ its $`p`$-th power is the identity. The left Bol law gives left power alternativity, so the loop has exponent dividing $`p`$; $`(1,0)`$ has order exactly $`p`$. One can instead check power associativity directly: the $`n`$-th power has second coordinate $`v_n=[n+\binom{n}{2} i a_i]u=n f_{ni}u/f_i`$; then $`v_n+c(mi,ni)v_m=v_{m+n}`$, since $`n f_{ni}+m f_{(m+2n)i}=(m+n)f_{(m+n)i}`$.

## Two actual native generators and all finite degrees

Take $`b=(1,0)`$ and $`z=(0,1)`$. All $`(i,0)`$ are powers of $`b`$. The base-zero fiber is the additive group of $`R`$. From any $`(0,u)`$ in the generated subloop, successively multiply on the left by $`(i,0)`$, on the right by $`(j,0)`$, and on the left by $`(-i-j,0)`$. The output is **the actual point**
```math
 (0,c(i,j)u).
\tag{3}
```
For each fixed $`i`$, $`i+2j`$ ranges over all of $`F`$. Hence (3) allows multiplication by **every** $`f_h/f_i`$, in particular each $`f_h`$ and each $`f_i^{-1}`$. With native addition, subtraction and the starting $`1`$, the generated set contains the $`F`$-algebra they generate, which is all of $`R`$ (already $`t=f_1-1`$). Left multiplication by $`(i,0)`$ now gives every point: $`Q_p=\langle b,z\rangle`$. The elementary witness for infinitude only uses $`i=0,j=1`$ in (3): the polynomials $`(1+2t)^n`$, $`n\ge0`$, are all distinct.

For every monic irreducible $`g\in F[t]`$ of degree $`d\ge2`$, all $`f_i`$ remain units modulo $`g`$. Evaluating (1) gives a surjective homomorphism onto $`F\times F[t]/(g)`$, of order $`p^{d+1}`$. This is a homomorphism of loops including their divisions, by uniqueness, and the images of $`b,z`$ generate. Distinct second coordinates remain distinct modulo any irreducible not dividing the numerator of their difference or the denominators; there are infinitely many such irreducibles. Distinct first coordinates always remain distinct. Thus $`Q_p`$ is residually finite. The kernel of projection onto $`F`$ is additive, abelian of exponent $`p`$, and proper and nontrivial; the projection has the section $`i\mapsto(i,0)`$. Opposites preserve generation, quotient maps and exponent.

## The other hand really fails

For the left Bol example write $`R_y(x)=x\cdot y`$. On the base-zero fiber the permutation $`U=R_{(-1,0)}R_{(1,0)}`$, composed rightmost first, acts by
```math
 (0,u)\longmapsto (0,\lambda u),\qquad
 \lambda=\frac{(1+2t)(1-t)}{1+t}=1-\frac{2t^2}{1+t}.
\tag{4}
```
This is nonconstant in $`F(t)`$ for every odd $`p`$; thus it has infinite multiplicative order (e.g. a nonconstant rational function has a zero or pole over an algebraic closure). In particular the loop does not have the right inverse property, hence is not Moufang. In any finite specialization of degree $`d\ge2`$ the same multiplier is different from 1, also certifying non-Moufang there. For $`p=3`$ equation (4) reads $`(1-t)^2/(1+t)`$. Passing to the free right Bol loop and the epimorphism onto $`Q_3^{\mathrm{op}}`$ transports its two corresponding left translations to (4). A group of quotient-respecting permutations of the domain maps onto the group induced on the quotient, so those two left translations upstairs already have an infinite-order product. This is a witness for the *opposite-hand operator in the display only*. It says nothing adverse about the matched-sided translation group.

## Position among preceding results

[G. P. Nagy, *Burnside problems for Moufang and Bol loops of small exponent*, 2001](https://www.acta.hu/download.phtml?id=2632) proves **finiteness** for finitely generated Moufang loops of exponent three and unbounded orders for finite two-generated Bol loops of exponent two. The loops above are proper Bol; neither of those theorems asserts this exponent-three conclusion. [T. Foguel and M. Kinyon, *Uniquely 2-divisible Bol loops*, §§2,5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.2429), treat finite order-27 examples and infinite two-generated simple Bruck–Tarski loops for sufficiently large primes, rather than the present elementary construction and its unbounded finite images for every odd prime. No assertion that any individual low-order quotient is a new isomorphism type, that the affine formula has no prior equivalent, or that these observations have historical priority.
